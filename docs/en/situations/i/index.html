Situation i) · 7.1.2 of the thesis

How to prove a case when the evidence is in the platform's hands

i) proof in an environment of structural asymmetry

Which facts show that the evidence is concentrated in the platform's hands?

The platform holds the essential elements needed to narrate, demonstrate and reconstruct the conflict, such as logs, interface trails, alteration histories, sanction criteria, internal support communications and technical records.

What is each party responsible for showing?

On the consumer's side, a plausible presentation of the harmful experience, of its place in the ecosystem and of the accessible context elements. On the supplier's side, disclosure of trails, logs, histories, versions and technically delimited justifications.

Which rules redistribute the burden of proof?

Article 6 of the CDC1 includes the facilitation of defense among basic rights, and reversal of the burden of proof is, in many cases, the only instrument capable of making protection viable. Article 373, paragraph 1, of the CPC2 allows a different distribution of the burden of proof in the face of excessive difficulty for one party or greater ease for the other. In advertising, Article 38 of the CDC3 places the burden on whoever sponsors it.

Does redistribution dispense with plausibility?

The dynamic distribution provided in art. 373, § 1, of the CPC2 depends on a reasoned decision. Redistribution does not eliminate the need for plausibility, for argumentative chaining and for delimiting the matter in dispute. What it eliminates is the fiction of symmetry.

When it applies
When the platform holds the essential elements needed to narrate, demonstrate and reconstruct the conflict. Verifiability becomes part of the legitimate regime of supply.
Whenever the platform holds the essential elements needed to narrate, demonstrate and reconstruct the conflict, the evidentiary discipline must recognize that verifiability is itself part of the legitimate regime of supply.
Duty of supply
To preserve and disclose the minimum records necessary to reconstruct the event.
The corresponding duty is to preserve and disclose the minimum records necessary to reconstruct the disputed event.
Minimum evidence
From the consumer, a plausible presentation of the harmful experience, its place in the ecosystem and the accessible context elements. From the supplier, trails, logs, histories, versions and technically delimited justifications.
The minimum evidence required, from the consumer's point of view, consists of a plausible presentation of the harmful experience, of its place in the ecosystem and of the accessible context elements; from the supplier's point of view, disclosure of trails, logs, histories, versions and technically delimited justifications is required.
Possible legal qualification
Dynamic redistribution of the burden of proof, presumptions favorable to the consumer and reinforcement of the duty of cooperation, without prejudice to the other parameters.
The possible legal qualification involves dynamic redistribution of the burden of proof, presumptions favorable to the consumer and reinforcement of the duty of cooperation, without prejudice to the material application of the other parameters.
Institutional consequence
To prevent technical opacity from becoming procedural armor and to make open auditability and functional equivalence effective.
The institutional consequence consists in preventing technical opacity from turning into procedural armor and in making standards such as open auditability and functional equivalence effective.

The law cited on this page

  1. Article 6 of the CDC ↩

    “Art. 6. The basic rights of the consumer are: I - protection of life, health and safety against the risks caused by practices in the supply of products and services considered dangerous or harmful; III - adequate and clear information about the different products and services […]; IV - protection against misleading and abusive advertising, coercive or unfair commercial methods, as well as against abusive practices and clauses […]; VI - the effective prevention and reparation of patrimonial and moral damages […]; VIII - the facilitation of the defense of their rights, including the reversal of the burden of proof […].”

    PT“Art. 6º. São direitos básicos do consumidor: I - a proteção da vida, saúde e segurança contra os riscos provocados por práticas no fornecimento de produtos e serviços considerados perigosos ou nocivos; III - a informação adequada e clara sobre os diferentes produtos e serviços […]; IV - a proteção contra a publicidade enganosa e abusiva, métodos comerciais coercitivos ou desleais, bem como contra práticas e cláusulas abusivas […]; VI - a efetiva prevenção e reparação de danos patrimoniais e morais […]; VIII - a facilitação da defesa de seus direitos, inclusive com a inversão do ônus da prova […].”

    Wording transcribed in note 500, p. 511.

  2. Article 373, paragraph 1, of the CPC ↩

    “Art. 373. The burden of proof lies: […] § 1 In the cases provided by law or in view of peculiarities of the case related to the impossibility or excessive difficulty of meeting the burden […] or to the greater ease of obtaining proof of the contrary fact, the judge may allocate the burden of proof differently, provided it is done by a reasoned decision […].”

    PT“Art. 373. O ônus da prova incumbe: […] § 1º Nos casos previstos em lei ou diante de peculiaridades da causa relacionadas à impossibilidade ou à excessiva dificuldade de cumprir o encargo […] ou à maior facilidade de obtenção da prova do fato contrário, poderá o juiz atribuir o ônus da prova de modo diverso, desde que o faça por decisão fundamentada […].”

    Wording transcribed in note 750, p. 659.

  3. Article 38 of the CDC ↩

    “Art. 38. The burden of proving the truthfulness and correctness of the advertising information or communication falls on whoever sponsors it.”

    PT“Art. 38. O ônus da prova da veracidade e correção da informação ou comunicação publicitária cabe a quem as patrocina.”

    Wording transcribed in note 716, p. 627.
