4.2.3 Psychopolitics, self-exploitation and identity captivity

p. 351

When governance by architecture and by information stabilizes as private administration of access, visibility, permanence and sanction, it produces a power that bears not only on occasional choices, but on the conditions of economic viability and social recognition inside the ecosystem. The enjoyment that sustains the value of the IOC depends on a legible and comparable position, and this position is preserved through adherence to criteria of recognition, update rhythms and performance standards instituted by the p. 352 environment itself. Vulnerability is not exhausted in informational asymmetry or individual preferences, because permanence and belonging become governed resources, capable of producing self-adjustment, discipline and adherence as structural effects of the administered market.

The passage from the disciplinary model to a regime of performance allows contemporary subjection to be described without resorting to individual psychological analysis, since the axis of conformity ceases to depend on external prohibitions and starts operating through productive positivity and the internalization of demands. Byung-Chul Han382 argues that the positivity of power can be more efficient than the negativity of duty, so that the achievement-subject tends to be faster and more productive than the obedience-subject, although this mutation does not erase discipline, since the achievement-subject remains disciplined and carries the disciplinary stage within it Han(Han, 2015, [p. 15]). This shift helps to understand why environments governed by metrics, comparison and continuous incentive can produce stable self-adjustment without ostensive coercion, since freedom itself comes to function as a vehicle of demand. In this sense, Han describes the paradox by which freedom, when converted into a duty of unlimited performance, comes to produce coercion more efficiently than disciplinary obligation, which tends to meet limits Han(Han, 2017, [p. 8-9]). The point is that adherence to the ecosystem can be produced as a structural effect, because preserving public commensurability and effective access to recognition requires continuous recalibration under criteria defined by the environment, converting performance and permanence into a practical condition of enjoyment.

In the IOC regime, recognition, visibility and permanence do not function as side effects of consumption, since they are part of the material conditions through which enjoyment becomes valuable and sustainable over time. The platform, by administering metrics, rankings, signals p. 353 of prestige and forms of public comparison, establishes a grammar of performance that organizes what counts as success, adherence and regularity within the ecosystem. The result is functional convergence with the model described by Han.

Conformity does not depend on explicit command or frontal threat, because the environment itself makes discontinuity costly and misalignment risky, insofar as the loss of legibility, the drop in visibility and reputational downgrading directly affect the position of enjoyment that constitutes the relevant economic object. In this context, the consumer's adaptation does not express a personality trait, but a strategy of permanence in an administered market. Self-adjustment becomes rational under conditions in which value depends on preserving position in the ecosystem's internal hierarchies and on remaining eligible for validation and visibility, and the continuity of enjoyment comes to require keeping up with rhythms, expectations and standards defined by the infrastructure that controls access to the very criteria of recognition.

Adherence to the governed ecosystem need not be produced by frontal prohibition to acquire stability, because the regime of performance tends to convert freedom itself into an operator of conformity. Byung-Chul Han describes the contemporary subject as “lord and sovereign of itself”, freed from the external instance that would oblige it to work, and precisely for that reason placed in a situation in which the fall of the dominator does not lead to freedom, but makes “freedom and constraint coincide”, so that the subject “gives itself over to compulsive freedom” and to the “free constraint of maximizing performance” Han(Han, 2015, [p. 16]). Here, the decisive mechanism is the one by which discipline comes to operate in the form of achievement, initiative and autonomy, making self-adjustment compatible with the feeling of choice, even when it is structured as an obligation to remain efficient and rewardable inside the environment.

The machinery becomes clearer when the governance of the ecosystem is described as the production of adherence through incentive, exposure and reward, and not as imposition by ostensive prohibition. Han argues that a smart power does not discipline the psyche through coercion or prohibition, but approaches it and summons it to participate and to share, exploiting freedom rather than denying it, precisely because it manages to operate without presenting itself as power Han(Han, 2017, [p. 18]). The consequence is that subjection is not imposed as silence, but as continuous communication and exposure, and this active participation functions as a governance resource.

In League of Legends, this power that summons freedom to work in favor of conformity shows itself clearly in the ranked regime, because there performance is translated p. 354 into position, and position becomes a public language of value. The competitive ladder organizes the experience as a comparable trajectory, in which the individual not only plays, but measures himself, ranks himself and makes himself available to the judgment of the ecosystem itself. This structure needs no direct coercion to produce discipline, since it converts initiative into a socially intelligible obligation. What is at stake is not a “game mode”, but the continuous production of a positional portrait that comes to operate as a summarized form of identity in that world.

This capture becomes even clearer when Riot itself describes the difference between MMR and rank as an institutional translation of the subject into numbers, so that the public ranking appears as a “relatable” representation of a calculated value that remains hidden. When explaining the system, official support states that MMR is secret and that rank exists as a mechanism to translate this MMR into a form that is accessible and progressive for the player Riot Games(Riot Games, 2026h). The effect of this on the psychopolitics of performance is direct. The player's freedom is organized as a voluntary search for adequacy to the criterion that measures him, even when the criterion is opaque, because what becomes visible and socially legible is the positional result, and not the workings of the calculation.

For this reason, the ranked climb tends to transform leisure into a regime of rational self-exploitation. The platform administers symbolic risk through categories of progress, distribution and reclassification, including periodic structural changes that reposition the subject's value on the ladder and redefine the horizon of “climbing”, “holding” and “deserving” Riot Games(Riot Games, 2023d)383. Discipline, here, does not present itself as prohibition, but as a permanent summons to invest time, attention and performance so as not to lose public commensurability in the environment. The result is that conformity does not depend on an external order. It emerges as an internal need to preserve legibility, avoid falling and sustain a recognizable place in a hierarchy that the infrastructure itself produces and recalibrates over time.

This dependence, as with the other mechanisms of the ecosystem, is also not neutral from the standpoint of the contractual bond. The conditions of access and permanence are provided under a revocable and conditional license, and account termination implies loss of access to the p. 355 environment and to associated data Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e)384. Thus, the ranked ladder does not operate as detachable property. It operates as a grammar of recognition that only exists while the account and the service remain active, which makes leaving, slowing down or misalignment more costly than they would seem under an ontology of a static “product”.

Along these lines, contrasting the disciplinary panopticon with the digital panopticon, Han observes that the occupants of the new apparatus collaborate with its functioning through voluntary self-illumination and self-exposure Han(Han, 2017, [p. 13]). The conclusion is that conformity can be produced as a structural effect of an ecosystem that mobilizes freedom, performance and exposure as a grammar of permanence, reducing the need for frontal coercion while intensifying the subject's dependence on the criteria that define recognition and value.

In League of Legends, psychopolitics manifests itself not only as an incentive to “do more”, but also as an inducement to “be the right way” if the goal is to remain eligible for the full experience of the ecosystem. The Honor system condenses this logic by turning conduct into a variable of administered reputation, whose practical consequence is not limited to a moral judgment, since it reorganizes rewards, privileges and access. Official support describes Honor as a general reflection of behavior “in and out of game”, stating that it “rewards” sportsmanlike conduct with benefits and “penalizes” inappropriate behavior with reduced rewards and restriction of communication privileges Riot Games(Riot Games, 2025a)385. Discipline, here, need not appear as an explicit threat, because it settles in as the subject's own everyday prudence. The player comes to manage himself to remain rewardable and socially legible, avoiding losses that are not only symbolic, but functional, insofar as they reach what can be accumulated, displayed and enjoyed in the continuity of enjoyment.

p. 356 This shift of control to the inside of the subject becomes even clearer when the integrity policy itself connects punishments to material effects on progression and rewards. In a specific document on Honor restrictions, Riot states that sanctions such as suspension and chat restriction lower the Honor level, lock progress and make the account ineligible for rewards tied to this regime Riot Games(Riot Games, 2017b)386. In this design, conformity is produced as self-preservation: it is not a matter of obeying out of abstract fear, but of avoiding a downgrade that alters the subject's position in the reward routines and in access to certain benefits, consolidating the idea that remaining “fit” is part of the consumption experience itself. Freedom thus works as an operator of conformity because the subject anticipates the gaze of the ecosystem and adjusts his conduct so as not to lose eligibility.

This logic overflows the inside of the game and takes on the contours of identity governance, since the Terms of Service establish that the User Rules govern behavior during the game and also certain conduct outside the game, providing for disciplinary measures that may reach temporary suspension, account termination and even hardware banning Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e)387. In addition, the contract makes explicit that the User Rules may apply to streams, recorded videos and social media posts, and admits the possibility of penalties applicable to multiple Riot services, including escalated across games Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e)388. In this combination, what is observed is a regime in which the subject internalizes control as an obligation to remain eligible and acceptable before criteria that the ecosystem itself administers, extending the field of incidence of p. 357 normativity beyond the strict moment of the match. The panopticon ceases to be only an exterior gaze because the individual comes to anticipate the sanction and to adjust his own behavior to remain within the borders of the rewardable, the communicable and the participable, as an ordinary condition of permanence.

Applied to the IOC regime, this machinery reveals that the ecosystem does not administer only access to content, but governs symbolic and positional risk as a condition of permanence. Value is not sustained as a static attribute of an acquired object, since it depends on continuity of enjoyment and on the preservation of public legibility within the ecosystem, so that losing visibility, temporal pertinence and belonging, or suffering positional downgrading produces economic effects that are not reducible to subjective inconvenience.

The platform, by controlling update rhythms, events, access windows, reputation scales and forms of comparison, converts permanence into a task and transforms keeping one's place into continuous work of monitoring and adjustment. In this context, the offer is not limited to delivering a one-off utility, because it organizes a circuit in which the very possibility of continuing to be “worth something” depends on adhering to parameters of performance and presence that the ecosystem naturalizes as a condition of participation.

The management of this risk functions as an instrument of discipline without needing to present itself as an explicit threat. Frequent return, constant attention and willingness to recalibrate conduct emerge as rational choices under conditions in which the cost of misalignment is the loss of commensurability and the erosion of accumulated symbolic capital, with a direct impact on the position of enjoyment that constitutes the economic core of the IOC.

The conversion of conduct into metrics is not limited to an external management technique, because it tends to reconfigure the way the subject perceives himself and stabilizes over time, shifting identity from a narrative structure to an additive structure of data. Byung-Chul Han formulates this contrast by arguing that the promise of self-knowledge through numbers fails at the most basic level, since the accumulation of records does not produce narrative, and a sense of self depends on giving an account of a trajectory, and not on adding up dispersed indicators, which is why the logic of the Quantified Self tends to decompose the self into data until meaning itself is emptied Han(Han, 2017, [p. 50]). The point is to describe a kind of instability that is not born of subjective introspection, but of the replacement of narrative continuity by measurable tracking, making the maintenance of the self depend on indicators, goals and feedback that can be continuously recalibrated by external criteria.

p. 358 The picture thickens when the record ceases to be individual memory and starts circulating as a criterion of validation, since tracking turns into self-surveillance and connects self-exploitation, self-observation and reputation maintenance. Han states that the digitized and networked subject carries an inner panopticon, in which guard and prisoner merge, so that control ceases to depend only on an exterior gaze and comes to operate as self-surveillance Han(Han, 2017, [p. 51]). The consequence is that continuous evaluation ceases to be only the gaze of the environment and becomes an internalized gaze, turning self-adjustment into an ordinary requirement of permanence under metrics of performance and exposure that the subject himself helps to produce and to feed.

When experience comes to be organized by continuous measurement and comparison, conformity ceases to depend on explicit command and settles in as an ordinary effect of an environment that rewards predictability. Transparency operated by metrics and correlations does not function only as an expansion of available information, since it turns statistical patterns into a practical reference of normality, narrowing the space for deviation and shifting the cost of misalignment onto the very possibility of remaining visible and validated. In the IOC regime, this narrowing has a direct economic consequence, because the position of enjoyment depends on remaining commensurable and recognizable within the ecosystem, and the infrastructure controls the indicators and filters that define what will appear as adequate performance. The result is a regime of self-adjustment in which the maintenance of belonging and of positional value becomes dependent on adhering to measurable parameters that the environment measures and rewards, converting measurability into an instrument of governance.

The institutionally relevant effect of this picture can be described as identity captivity. The expression does not designate physical imprisonment nor assume an inability to act, but names a practical dependence, by which the continuity of enjoyment and the stability of symbolic identity come to be mediated by the administered ecosystem. When the position of enjoyment is anchored in signals of prestige, accumulated repertoires, performance records, visibility and belonging, the platform governs the conditions for preserving a socially recognizable place by distributing public legibility through metrics, ranking and internal eligibility criteria. In this context, leaving, slowing down or misaligning does not appear as a simple neutral choice, since it tends to imply loss of legibility and the collapse of the signals that sustain public commensurability, with downgrading of the position that gave economic meaning to enjoyment. Permanence comes to depend on continuous alignment with mutable operational p. 359 standards, and leaving ceases to be a simple substitution of service, because it involves discontinuity of prestige, comparability and belonging.

This dependence on administered recognition is legally relevant because it makes integrity and identity governed variables of enjoyment and demands, at the dogmatic level, reinforced duties of qualified transparency, contestability, auditability and restoration proportional to the kind of power exercised by the Lex Plataformae.

Dependence is consolidated because the platform concentrates the administration of metrics, recognition criteria and exposure trajectories, while freely recalibrating what counts as adequate performance and which conduct will be rewardable or punishable. The result is an exit cost that is not exhausted in a one-off patrimonial loss, since it involves the discontinuity of a narrative of self that was built, measured and validated inside the environment, besides loss of access to the very parameters that made this narrative intelligible. Identity captivity, thus, is the form in which the power of governance translates into stable subjection without ostensive coercion, because preserving enjoyment requires permanence under variable criteria and preserving symbolic identity requires continuity of recognition under signals that do not belong to the consumer, but to the infrastructure that defines and distributes them.

The exit barrier can be described as a form of social death inside the ecosystem. The expression does not operate as moral dramatization, but as a functional description of the effect produced when the platform concentrates the records that sustain belonging and relevance, that is, history, repertoire, ranked position, signals of prestige, community ties and traces of participation. Leaving the environment, under these conditions, is not like migrating from one service to another, because it implies interrupting one's very socially recognizable existence in that world, with loss of symbolic continuity and the practical erasure of trajectory marks that have become a condition of public commensurability. Leaving, therefore, does not present itself as a neutral consumption decision, since the cost is not limited to the price paid or to access to a utility, reaching the positional survival of the consumer in a grammar of recognition that the ecosystem itself monopolizes.

This effect stabilizes identity captivity through an additional mechanism, which depends not on formal coercion or direct threat, but on reputational self-preservation. If the experiential value of the IOC is anchored in signals of status, consistency of performance, belonging and social memory, any criticism that endangers the legitimacy of the ecosystem or the stability of its hierarchies tends to be perceived as a threat to the consumer's own identity investment. A circuit of defense of the environment is thus formed p. 360 in which users become agents of conformity, not out of servility, but out of the need to protect the accumulated symbolic capital and the sense of social existence that derives from it. The platform, by organizing recognition as a scarce resource and conditioning its distribution on metrics and internal rules, produces a structural incentive for the community itself to operate as an instance of surveillance and containment of dissent, raising the social cost of misalignment and reducing the space for effective contestation.

What makes this regime particularly stable is that its machinery does not depend on continuous external coercion, since it operates through the exploitation of freedom as an economic resource. Byung-Chul Han states that neoliberalism constitutes a highly efficient system for exploiting freedom and capturing practices and expressive forms associated with autonomy, such as emotion, play and communication, arguing that exploiting people against their will is inefficient and that returns are maximized when freedom itself is exploited389 Han(Han, 2017, [p. 9-10]). The governed environment need not impose itself as prohibition to obtain adherence, since it encourages, rewards and makes engagement a condition of permanence, converting initiative and participation into the raw material of conformity.

This efficiency deepens when exploitation ceases to present itself as a visible relationship between dominator and dominated and comes to be internalized as an ordinary dynamic of the subject with himself. Han describes how, in the present, each individual tends to become a p. 361 self-exploiting worker in his own enterprise, accumulating in the same figure the positions of command and submission, and that even class struggle can be lived as an inner struggle against oneself, under conditions defined by the solitude of the isolated, self-combative entrepreneur, who practices self-exploitation voluntarily Han(Han, 2017, [p. 11]). The structural effect is the neutralization of frictions that, in other regimes, would make social conflict more visible and contestable, because the pressure is lived as an intimate obligation of performance and not as an external imposition attributable to an identifiable agent.

The operational language of this process appears in the transformation of life into a regime of measurable optimization, in which weaknesses and errors must be eliminated to maximize efficiency and performance, and in which everything is made to be comparable and measurable under the logic of quantifying success in the market, not in pursuit of the good life, but because of systemic constraints Han(Han, 2017, [p. 28]). The triad composes the machinery of conformity in platform ecosystems: freedom is summoned as participation, participation is captured as productivity, and productivity is stabilized as comparability, so that the subject adjusts himself to remain rewardable and recognizable. The user becomes a practical agent of his own subjection within an environment in which a third party - not bound to any effective standards of transparency - administers metrics, visibility and permanence as “legitimate” conditions of value.

The result matters legally because it shifts the conflict from the vocabulary of choice to the plane of the governance conditions that produce, calibrate and make economically relevant what presents itself as choice. In IOC regimes, the permanence of value depends on recognition and continuity of enjoyment, and these elements do not stem from the isolated will of the consumer, since they are administered by criteria of visibility, comparison and sanction that the platform defines, applies and recalibrates. The subjection described, therefore, is not a moral trait nor an individual pathology, but a foreseeable effect of an environment in which performance, exposure and measurability function as a grammar of permanence. When the continuity of enjoyment requires self-adjustment and when the cost of misalignment manifests itself as loss of position, legibility and belonging, the environment comes to operate as a mechanism that produces vulnerability, because it transforms symbolic and positional risk into an instrument of conformity.

This shift prevents the problem from being treated as a simple informational asymmetry correctable by formal notice, or as a neutral competitive dynamic resolved by a market alternative. The platform concentrates the administration of the variables that define what will be recognized, rewarded, downgraded and excluded, and this concentration makes exploitable p. 362 the consumer's dependence on parameters he does not control and that, as a rule, he cannot verify. Thus, protection cannot be limited to inventorying clauses or to presuming that the user's choice exhausts the meaning of the supply, because the effective shaping of the object is produced by continued operations of the ecosystem that determine conditions of access, permanence, reputation and sanction. The point reached is that vulnerability, in this scenario, has institutional density and demands duties proportional to the power exercised, especially regarding the intelligibility of criteria, the minimum stability of the conditions of enjoyment and the possibility of restoration when changes, modulations or sanctions degrade the position of enjoyment that gives economic meaning to the IOC.

The cost of this permanence through continuous recalibration appears more clearly when the temporality of the environment is described as game temporality, that is, organized by immediate rewards, short cycles of validation and quick experiences of success. Han observes that games exhibit a temporality marked by immediate success and reward, but that what matures over time cannot be gamified, because what is long and lasting tends to prove incompatible with the temporality of the game, so that hunting fits the regime of rewards, while farming, dependent on slow processes and quiet growth, does not let itself be converted into scores and levels, since life cannot be reduced to permanent hunting390 Han(Han, 2017, [p. 43]). Thus, it becomes possible to argue that an ecosystem structured by comparison, performance and quick feedback tends to corrode the conditions of subjective continuity and experiential stability, demanding from the consumer incessant adjustment merely to remain legible and rewardable within the circuit that sustains the value of his IOC.

In this register, optimization ceases to function as a promise of improvement and comes to operate as a systemic requirement of permanence, since the environment values what can be intensified, measured and compared, pushing the subject toward a compulsion of p. 363 productivity that admits no interruption. Han's formulations, although purposely loaded, help to fix the point in a technically useful way, since they describe a dynamic in which the positivity of performance converts inner life into fuel for the demand to produce more, and in which the very idea of adjustment shifts from care to exhaustion.

In ecosystems that administer recognition and permanence as conditions of value, the demand to remain in the game through continuous recalibration ceases to be individual discomfort and becomes a regular mechanism of governance, because enjoyment is maintained only while the subject manages to sustain, repeatedly, signals of adherence to the criteria of the environment.

The subjection described is not a side element of the IOC regime, because it indicates objectively the practical traction that allows private rules and technical sanctions to operate efficiently in the everyday life of ecosystems. When permanence depends on recognition, when continuity of enjoyment depends on adherence to metrics and when the cost of misalignment manifests itself as loss of position and social legibility, the subject is already invested in the environment even before the explicit act of enforcement.

In this scenario, the internal norm is not limited to declaring permissions and prohibitions, since it bears on a user who needs to remain rewardable and traceable, so that discipline is realized through the expectation of visibility, the risk of downgrading and the fear of functional exclusion, without depending on frontal coercion to stabilize conformity. The consequence is that governance by architecture and by information, by producing identity captivity, prepares the ground on which the private rule becomes effectively normative, because it finds a subject willing to adjust continuously to preserve what gives economic and existential meaning to his own IOC.

The analysis must therefore advance from subjection to the normativity of the environment. Internal rules and technical enforcement do not operate only as contractual text, but as decisions implemented in filters, parameters, blocks, gradations and procedures that reconfigure enjoyment in real time. Once it is shown that adherence and self-adjustment are structural effects of the ecosystem, it becomes necessary to examine how internal policies are converted into effective restrictions and authorizations, with enough unilaterality and opacity to produce exclusion and functional dispossession. The next step, therefore, consists in describing the Lex Plataformae and its enforcement as the practical normativity of the environment, since it is at this level that private power becomes legally controllable in proportional terms.

A person playing a game, being emotionally invested, is much more engaged than a worker who acts rationally or is simply functioning. Games exhibit a specific temporality marked by immediate experiences of success and reward. But what matures over time cannot be gamified. Whatever is long, anything that lasts a long time, proves incompatible with the game’s temporality. Hunting, for instance, matches the mode of the game, whereas farming, which depends on slow processes of ripening and quiet growth, cannot be gamified at all. Life cannot be turned into hunting alone. The gamification of work exploits homo ludens. The player subjugates him- or herself to the order of domination in the very act of playing. Today, the gamification logic of ‘Likes’, ‘Friends’ and ‘Followers’ means that social communication is also being plugged into and subordinated to a game mode. The corollary of the gamification of communication is its commercialization. That said, this process is destroying human communication.” Han(Han, 2017, [p. 43], emphasis in the original). ↩
