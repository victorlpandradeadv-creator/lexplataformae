Brazilian Consumer Protection Code

    “This code establishes norms for the protection and defense of the consumer, of public order and social interest, pursuant to arts. 5, item XXXII, and 170, item V, of the Federal Constitution and art. 48 of its Transitional Provisions.”

    Section I On Immovable Goods

    Art. 79. Immovable goods are the land and everything incorporated into it naturally or artificially.

    Art. 80. The following are considered immovable for legal purposes:

    I - real rights over immovables and the actions that secure them;

    II - the right to an opened succession.

    Art. 81. The following do not lose the character of immovables:

    I - buildings that, separated from the land but preserving their unity, are removed to another place;

    II - materials provisionally separated from a building, to be reused in it.

    Section II On Movable Goods

    Art. 82. Movable goods are those capable of their own movement, or of removal by external force, without alteration of their substance or of their economic and social purpose.

    Art. 83. The following are considered movable for legal purposes:

    I - energies that have economic value;

    II - real rights over movable objects and the corresponding actions;

    III - personal rights of a patrimonial character and the respective actions.

    Art. 84. Materials intended for a construction retain their status as movables until they are used; materials coming from the demolition of a building regain that status.

    Section III On Fungible and Consumable Goods

    Art. 85. Fungible goods are movables that can be replaced by others of the same kind, quality and quantity.

    Art. 86. Consumable goods are movable goods whose use entails the immediate destruction of their own substance, and goods intended for alienation are also considered consumable.

    Section IV On Divisible Goods

    Art. 87. Divisible goods are those that can be split without alteration of their substance, considerable reduction in value, or impairment of the use for which they are intended.

    Art. 88. Naturally divisible goods may become indivisible by determination of law or by the will of the parties.

    Section V On Singular and Collective Goods

    Art. 89. Singular goods are those that, although gathered together, are considered by themselves, independently of the others.

    Art. 90. A universality of fact is a plurality of singular goods which, belonging to the same person, have a unitary purpose.

    Sole paragraph. The goods that form this universality may be the object of their own legal relations.

    Art. 91. A universality of law is the set of a person's legal relations that are endowed with economic value.

    CHAPTER II On Goods Considered in Relation to One Another

    Art. 92. Principal is the good that exists by itself, abstractly or concretely; accessory, the one whose existence presupposes that of the principal.

    Art. 93. Appurtenances are goods which, not being integral parts, are intended, in a lasting way, for the use, service or embellishment of another.

    Art. 94. Legal transactions concerning the principal good do not cover the appurtenances, unless the contrary results from the law, from the declaration of will, or from the circumstances of the case.

    Art. 95. Although not yet separated from the principal good, fruits and products may be the object of a legal transaction.

    Art. 96. Improvements may be voluptuary, useful or necessary.

    § 1 Voluptuary improvements are those of mere delight or recreation, which do not increase the habitual use of the good, even if they make it more pleasant or are of high value.

    § 2 Useful improvements are those that increase or facilitate the use of the good.

    § 3 Necessary improvements are those intended to preserve the good or to prevent its deterioration.

    Art. 97. Enhancements or additions that accrue to the good without the intervention of the owner, possessor or holder are not considered improvements.

    CHAPTER III On Public Goods

    Art. 98. Public goods are those of the national domain belonging to legal persons of domestic public law; all others are private, whatever the person to whom they belong.

    Art. 99. Public goods are:

    I - those for common use by the people, such as rivers, seas, roads, streets and squares;

    II - those of special use, such as buildings or land intended for the service or establishment of the federal, state, territorial or municipal administration, including those of their autonomous agencies;

    III - the dominical goods, which constitute the patrimony of legal persons of public law, as the object of a personal or real right of each of these entities.

    Sole paragraph. Unless the law provides otherwise, goods belonging to legal persons of public law that have been given a private law structure are considered dominical.

    Art. 100. Public goods for common use by the people and those for special use are inalienable for as long as they keep that qualification, in the manner the law determines.

    Art. 101. Dominical public goods may be alienated, subject to the requirements of the law.

    Art. 102. Public goods are not subject to adverse possession.

    Art. 103. The common use of public goods may be free or paid, as legally established by the entity to whose administration they belong.

  2. Article 8

    “Art. 8 Whoever markets a computer program, whether holder of the rights to the program or holder of the marketing rights, is obliged, in the national territory, during the period of technical validity of the respective version, to ensure to the respective users the provision of complementary technical services relating to the proper functioning of the program, considering its specifications. Sole paragraph. The obligation shall persist in the event of withdrawal of the computer program from commercial circulation during the period of validity, except for fair compensation for any losses caused to third parties.”

    PT“Art. 8º Aquele que comercializar programa de computador, quer seja titular dos direitos do programa, quer seja titular dos direitos de comercialização, fica obrigado, no território nacional, durante o prazo de validade técnica da respectiva versão, a assegurar aos respectivos usuários a prestação de serviços técnicos complementares relativos ao adequado funcionamento do programa, consideradas as suas especificações. Parágrafo único. A obrigação persistirá no caso de retirada de circulação comercial do programa de computador durante o prazo de validade, salvo justa indenização de eventuais prejuízos causados a terceiros.”
