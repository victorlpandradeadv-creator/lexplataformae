5.2 On digitally based business models and a preliminary typology of Immaterial Objects of Consumption

See this section in the PDF

This section in the layers of the site

  • Dogmatics · Sections of chapter 5

p. 482

Building on the categories already stabilized, this section begins to organize a preliminary typology of IOCs and their recurring modes of incidence, so as to allow comparison between supply arrangements and the reading of relevant losses. On the plane of technique and mediation, the techno-media environment was treated as a structure of perception and conduct, while the discussion of concrete design choices brought the governance of the environment back to the plane of technical decisions embedded in the architecture. On the plane of political economy, digital ecosystems were described as arrangements for the continuous capture of value and the production of dependence. On the semiotic plane, the commodity was also understood as a sign, so that belonging and distinction form part of its value. On the methodological and institutional plane, the requirement of controllable criteria, the operative function of concepts and the legal relevance of the normativity exercised by private powers were reaffirmed.

Without a move toward typological organization, the plurality of manifestations of the IOC tends to produce two symmetrical deviations. On one side, concrete analysis degrades into descriptive narrative, in which objects, practices and interfaces pile up as a catalog of peculiarities without any identification of relevant constants. On the other, dogmatic reconstruction loses its grip on the phenomenon by operating with overly general categories, unable to discern what, in platform-governed enjoyment, alters the acquired utility, reconfigures symbolic value or shifts the consumer's position in the ecosystem.

The proposed typology, therefore, neither performs an external classificatory function nor purports to exhaust reality through names. Its usefulness is internal and operative. It stabilizes criteria of p. 483 comparison between objects and supply arrangements, distinguishing irrelevant variations from structures that are decisive for the IOC regime. This delimitation makes it possible to treat consistently differences in dependence on account and infrastructure, intensity of lock-in, exposure to unilateral modulation, risk of gradual degradation and the identity density of the object, without dissolving what was acquired into an undifferentiated “service” or reducing the platform to a neutral distribution channel.

Typological organization, at this point, designates an instrument guided by criteria, not an effort to classify by apparent similarity. The focus is on building comparable classes that express legally relevant differences between objects and supply arrangements when enjoyment is sustained by a technical-organizational environment in continuous operation. Instead of listing items, parameters are stabilized to describe how the object exists in the ecosystem, which structural dependencies condition it and to what extent its utility and value are susceptible to modulation by the environment.

The typological criteria derive directly from the operators already formulated for the IOC regime. Semiotic vulnerability guides the identification of structural asymmetries of intelligibility and agency produced by the design and governance of the environment. Abusive teleology offers a parameter for distinguishing ordinary design variations from exploitative purposive orientations embedded in incentives, frictions and rules of permanence. Functional integrity and symbolic identity delimit the field of relevant losses beyond binary interruption, reaching degradations of enjoyment and disorganizations of the sign-value associated with belonging, distinction and recognition.

Open auditability establishes a requirement of a verifiable minimum of justification and traceability for changes and governance decisions, without imposing total transparency or unrestricted access to trade secrets. Functional value equivalence operates as a standard of restoration, preventing the formal continuity of access from being confused with the material preservation of the utility and value embodied in the object. These axes define the set of measures by which the typology differentiates objects and supply arrangements according to what actually matters for the control of the regime.

With this gain in comparability, it becomes possible to treat losses and degradations as legally relevant events even when they do not take the form of explicit cancellation, distinguishing gradual erosions of utility, changes that lower use value and alterations that disorganize symbolic value. The difference between an object conceived as isolated content and an object whose legal existence is a position of enjoyment sustained by variable rules also becomes clear. The typology makes it possible to identify when modulation goes beyond p. 484 ordinary adjustment and becomes a problem of control, and it also provides parameters for demanding minimally traceable justifications and for assessing whether the restoration offered preserves, in functional terms, the utility and value embodied in the IOC.

The focus on electronic game platforms answers to a methodological reason, not a thematic preference. These ecosystems tend to concentrate, in high resolution, the defining elements of the IOC regime, since they combine continued enjoyment, intense private governance and an offer driven by an architecture of incentives. Reward and progression systems, artificial scarcity, segmented monetization, social comparison and reputation devices make particularly visible the ways in which the object's value is produced, perceived and stabilized in the environment, while also amplifying asymmetries of intelligibility and agency. The centrality of collections, appearances, achievements and signs of belonging shows how rule changes, interface reconfigurations and alterations of visibility and display capacity can produce relevant losses without any formal interruption of access.

The development that follows begins by framing the digital ecosystems in which the IOC appears most clearly, highlighting traits common to models sustained by continued enjoyment and by governance of the environment. Next, preliminary categories of IOC on game platforms are organized according to criteria that differentiate objects and supply arrangements by structural dependence, exposure to unilateral modulation, risk of degradation and identity density. A minimal normative and institutional framing of the electronic games sector in Brazil is then presented, to the extent necessary to situate the debate. The path culminates in the preparation of the case study, in which the grid thus built will be applied to a specific ecosystem, making it possible to verify how the operators of the IOC regime manifest themselves concretely and which tensions emerge when enjoyment depends on variable rules, validation mechanisms and unilateral changes subject to requirements of verifiable justification and adequate functional restoration.

With this organization, the passage from the conceptual apparatus to empirical analysis takes place without loss of precision and without a shift of focus. The typology stabilizes vocabulary and turns dimensions of the IOC regime into a lens capable of recognizing patterns, distinguishing irrelevant variations from materially significant changes and consistently reconstructing relevant losses and minimum requirements of verifiable justification and functional restoration. The expected result is an examination anchored in controllable criteria, capable of supporting a rigorous description of the selected ecosystem and of preparing the stage of dogmatic reconstruction on the basis of organized evidence.
