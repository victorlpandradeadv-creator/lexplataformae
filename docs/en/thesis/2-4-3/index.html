2.4.3 On the survey of public statements by users

See this section in the PDF

This section in the layers of the site

  • Method · The protocol

p. 66

The survey of public statements by users is part of empirical collection with a delimited and subsidiary purpose, as a symptomatic record of recurring frictions in the ecosystem. Its objective is to observe how controversies associated with platform-governed enjoyment emerge and circulate publicly, which losses are perceived and narrated, which expectations are stated as frustrated promises and which mechanisms of sanction, restriction or modulation appear as recurring triggers of conflict. This material is not mobilized as autonomous proof of the supplier's institutional conduct, nor as a substitute for official documents, interface records, lawsuits or decisions. It is used to map recurring frictions, to identify relevant vocabularies and discursive patterns and to guide triangulation with the declared norm and with the norm in operation, under minimum selection and recording criteria that allow external verification of the material actually cited.

In this design, the verifiability of what is stated about promises, sanctions, procedures and restoration is sought primarily through documentation attributable to the p. 67 supplier, public records and traceable statements, without presupposing the building of a broad image archive of the interface. Thus, accounts, complaints and videos may operate, as the case may be, as a record of publicly perceived frictions and as useful vignettes for formulating hypotheses and testing, in an illustrative key, the robustness of the proposed categories. When, however, the argument depends on a specific institutional attribution, such as the content of an internal rule, a sanction criterion, a binding promise or a restoration procedure, the support rests on traceable and attributable sources, with public narratives used only as a starting point for locating verifiable elements.

Community and platform sources, such as forums, social networks, collaborative repositories and creators' content, are treated with a delimited and subsidiary methodological function. They are not mobilized to establish, by themselves, the content of the supplier's internal policies, its decision criteria, its moderation procedures or the existence of obligations assumed, nor to assert the occurrence of disputed institutional practices. Their usefulness is restricted to contextualizing publicly perceived controversies, to identifying recurring vocabularies and practices in the ecosystem and to locating verifiable leads that guide the search for primary documentation. Whenever the analysis depends on normatively relevant elements, priority is given to terms, policies, announcements, support materials and documentary records directly attributable to the supplier.

When mobilized, these sources are treated as a record of public perception and as a means of locating events and documents, and not as an autonomous basis for institutional attribution, so that their argumentative weight always remains subordinate to primary sources or to publicly verifiable elements.

The selection of public statements focuses on two main arenas, chosen because they function as recurring channels for publicizing conflicts in the ecosystem: YouTube, as a space for accounts and controversies in audiovisual format, and structured complaint platforms that record consumer conflicts in the user's language under thematic classification. On YouTube, priority is given to materials that address legally relevant problems for the object and that contain a verifiable reference to an event, change, sanction, account restriction, loss of items/benefits, charge, support or institutional procedure, with a record of the minimum metadata and, when necessary, a timestamp of the pertinent excerpt. For complaints, screening favors those with a sufficient description of the conflict, an indication of the request for restoration and elements that make it possible to relate the narrated friction to a promise, condition of use, support procedure, challenge or governance mechanism of the service.

p. 68 The study of public statements on YouTube is justified because it is a central space for publicizing controversies in the ecosystem, with narratives that frequently reconstruct events, point to documents and make explicit the perceived impacts on enjoyment. The interest is not to evaluate "opinions" as such, but to observe how certain conflicts become publicly knowable, which elements are mobilized as justification and which promises, losses and restorations appear in the language of the field.

The primary unit of treatment, on the axis of public statements, is the video, understood as a closed piece with traceable metadata. Internal segments are treated as an autonomous unit only when they reproduce inaccessible external material, crystallized as a quoted excerpt, in which case segmentation preserves the verifiability of the apud. Comments do not constitute an independent corpus and enter only when indispensable to understand, correct or complete the content of the video itself.

The analysis of public statements was conducted through a coding matrix oriented toward categories capable of translating the discursive material into elements relevant to the reconstructed legal problem. In both arenas, at a minimum, the following are observed: the type of problem narrated, the affective reaction and the tone of the account, the perception of loss and the elements of the supply that the user considers affected, as well as the promises and expectations mobilized, whether by explicit reference to advertising, to features, to internal conditions of enjoyment, to support and challenge procedures, or by a statement of what would be owed in terms of adequacy and restoration.

To maintain control, selection favors videos that address legally relevant problems for the object and that allow anchoring by traceable reference, with a record of the minimum metadata and, when necessary, of the point in time at which the relevant excerpt occurs. Comments are collected only when indispensable to understand the video itself, such as corrections, factual additions, retractions, updates or direct indication of public documents.

To prevent the research from expanding indefinitely as an effect of the hyperconnectivity typical of the field, the survey is limited by a non-expansion rule. External sources enter only when they are (a) official, (b) necessary to make explicit the minimum technical functioning of the object, or (c) expressly referred to by an item already included. The unit of treatment for audiovisual statements is always the video, with its own record sheet, and a "segment" is admitted only in the case of an apud record, that is, when an excerpt of an inaccessible external document or external video is crystallized within an included video.

p. 69 Reddit is part of the research design as a complementary source because of the relevance of that space for community discussions and, in some cases, for the circulation of public statements and records that help reconstruct the chain of events. Its use is conditioned by the volatility of the environment, in which content may be moderated, removed or reorganized without notice.

For this reason, Reddit is mobilized in a referenced manner, preferably on the basis of mentions and links contained in videos or in documents already included, avoiding an infinite drift of the field. Its use does not serve to infer the representativeness of opinions or the prevalence of events, but to locate chains of events, terms of reference and verifiable leads when the public environment of the ecosystem makes them relevant. The incorporation of Reddit content into the text is exceptional and subject to two limitations: it is used only when indispensable to the argument and, when used, the excerpt is presented with sufficient context to preserve the meaning of its post, avoiding undue identification of non-public users.

To enrich the technical-operational intelligibility of terms, internal structures and vocabulary of the field, and only as a complement to official sources, collaborative repositories such as Fandom are used subsidiarily. This material is used for the strict purpose of naming categories of the ecosystem, describing mechanics operationally and, as an aid, reconstructing basic relations between elements of the service when this is indispensable to translate their functioning into legally intelligible language. Because it is a collaborative environment subject to variation, its content is not used to prove institutional promises, internal decision criteria, official milestones of change or duties assumed by the supplier. When information found there is relevant to the argument, it operates as a lead for locating primary documentation or verifiable public records.

This survey is articulated with the other empirical fronts provided for in the methodology section and remains subject to cross-checking of sources. Public statements are treated as qualified indications and as verifiable leads to locate points of friction and to guide the search for primary material, without converting accounts into direct proof of the supplier's internal policies or procedures. Whenever the analysis requires institutional attribution, such as statements about rules, sanction criteria, formal moderation practices, binding promises or restoration procedures, priority goes to official documents and interface records, compared additionally, when pertinent, with the findings of the minimum institutional analysis of the disputed norm. This preserves coherence between the p. 70 subsidiary function of this survey and the dogmatic purpose of the path, which is to make consumer conflicts in continuous services under platform governance decidable.

Every item actually mobilized in the text is recorded with minimum traceability metadata (platform, author, channel or profile when public, title, URL, publication date when available and access date). The traceability of the complaints and textual statements cited is ensured according to the defined recording standard, prioritizing the indication of metadata that allow them to be located on the original platforms. Audiovisual materials are not archived in full. When mobilized, their verifiability is ensured by a complete reference and, when pertinent, a timestamp of the cited excerpt and a transcription of the relevant excerpt in the body of the text or in an explanatory note, without prejudice to the preservation of full copies of the documentary items cited for purposes of auditing the path. The protocol aims to reduce the volatility effects typical of platforms and to maintain the auditability of the path without converting the survey into an exhaustive inventory or imposing the creation and maintenance of a media archive.

As to argumentative weight, the material operates as support for intelligibility and as a source of vignettes, without replacing, when specific institutional attribution is necessary, the support in primary sources and publicly verifiable records.
