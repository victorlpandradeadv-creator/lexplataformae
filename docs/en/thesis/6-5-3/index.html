6.5.3 On the social function of intellectual property in IOC platforms

See this section in the PDF

p. 703

The social function of intellectual property operates, in IOC platforms, as a constitutional limit on the exercise of exclusivity and as a criterion for reconciling the protection of the immaterial asset with the protection of the enjoyment continuously promised to the consumer. The starting point is well known, but here it takes on a specific practical consequence. The Constitution does not give property an absolutist reading, and the very protection of industrial creations, trademarks and other IP assets is secured in view of the social interest and the technological and economic development of the country. This is enough to rule out, at the root, the idea that exclusivity would authorize opaque governance, opportunistic mutability or unilateral suppression of enjoyment in ecosystems of continuous access and modulation. Intellectual property, as a constitutionally protected species of property, exists within the limits and for the functions assigned to it, including a specific function linked to development and the social interest, which prevents its invocation as an absolute barrier against the scrutiny of practices harmful to the integrity of the supply and to the consumer's position Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 84)808.

This conclusion is reinforced when one observes, more carefully, the normative structure of Article 5, XXIX of the Constitution. The provision does not merely mention industrial property among constitutionally recognized rights. It selects its objects and imposes a purpose on them. On the one hand, it refers to the temporary privilege of industrial inventions; on the other, to the protection of industrial creations, the ownership of trademarks, company names and other distinctive signs. In this context, “industrial creations” must be read in a broad material key, p. 704 comprising industrial designs and, in the Brazilian system, articulating with the field of economic distinctive signs, including trademarks, business names and other distinctive signs protected in the market, and, finally, reaching industrial creations in the broad sense, which leads to the interpretation that, lato sensu, the rights of exclusivity structured under intellectual property in the patrimonial sense are conditioned on the constitutional purpose. One is not, therefore, facing a narrow clause aimed only at the patent, but a constitutional frame bearing on the patrimonial-industrial set of creations and signs protected in the competitive environment al.(Andrade, 2021, p. 61-66).

More important, however, is that the Constitution does not protect these legal institutions unconditionally. The phrase “having in view” performs an unequivocal finalistic function: legal protection is justified only insofar as it is oriented to the social interest and to the technological and economic development of the country. Social interest, here, is not reduced to the abstract convenience of the market nor to the private interest of the holder maximized to the limit. It designates the synthesis of the general needs of the Brazilian collectivity, measurable according to the historical, economic and social context in which protection is applied. Likewise, development is not to be confused with mere quantitative growth or rent extraction. It is a broader notion, linked to the expansion of the country's technical, social, political and economic capacities, in a manner compatible with the improvement of collective living conditions and with the material autonomy of the national community. For this reason, the patrimonial exploitation of IP finds constitutional grounding only when it remains functionally bound to this broader horizon, and not when it turns into a technique of opaque closure, relational capture and opportunistic degradation of the enjoyment already integrated into the continuous consumer relationship al.(Andrade, 2021, p. 73-81).

Hence the dogmatic consequence. Article 5, XXIX operates as a constitutional norm of conformation and limit, addressed both to the infraconstitutional legislator and to the interpreter and to those who apply the law. By saying that “the law shall ensure”, the Constitution does not merely authorize the enactment of protective norms; it conditions the very material validity of those norms and of their application on the ends it itself states. The Industrial Property Law and any other patrimonial discipline concerning industrial creations are constitutionally acceptable only to the extent that they are compatible with this finalistic frame. Outside it, what arises is not mere regulatory inconvenience, but constitutionally qualified tension. In IOC platforms, this means that exclusivity cannot be mobilized as a shield against the control of abusiveness when its concrete exercise serves to make decisions opaque, impose unilateral mutations of the experience, make minimal audit unfeasible or destroy, without restoration, the functional core of the promised enjoyment. The Constitution ensures industrial property, yes, p. 705 but it ensures it under condition. And a constitutional condition is not rhetorical ornament; it is a criterion of validity, interpretation and containment of private power al.(Andrade, 2021, p. 109-110).

This conditioning does not act as an external, accidental or exceptional brake. Functionalization is internal to the IP regime itself. It is a form of property even more conditioned, even more socially responsible and less full than other proprietary forms, precisely because the exclusive right exists to balance private interests of exploitation and public interests of access, progress and socially useful circulation of the immaterial Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 75)809. In IOC platforms, this characteristic prevents dominion over the technological or expressive infrastructure of the ecosystem from being read as authorization to organize enjoyment on bases incompatible with verifiability, trust and restoration. The power exists, but it exists functionalized.

This understanding is reinforced by the very international horizon of intellectual property. The contemporary regime does not enshrine a paradigm of metaphysically shielded exclusivity, but a balance between innovation, technological diffusion, social welfare and the distribution of rights and obligations. The protection of the holder is not an end in itself; it is legitimated precisely because it remains compatible with a broader arrangement of social reciprocity Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 662)810. In IOC platforms, the exercise of exclusivity cannot be accepted when it produces disproportionate harm to the enjoyment that has legitimately entered the consumer's sphere, above all in a context of strong technical and evidentiary asymmetry.

The social function ceases to look like a rhetorical principle and comes to function as a lock against the undue expansion of private power. It is neither a favor from the legislator nor a heteronomous intrusion upon a naturally full domain. Every subjective right is born and subsists under p. 706 this conditioning, including intellectual rights. It makes no sense to conceive of intellectual property as an enclave in which the holder could act as they saw fit, because its legitimacy already depends, from the outset, on compatibility with social values and with other legally protected interests Ascensão(Ascensão, 2007, [p. 3])811. Here, the invocation of intellectual property does not close the discussion about the manner in which power over enjoyment is exercised. It merely opens a legally more demanding discussion about its limits.

This becomes even clearer when one recalls that the exclusive intellectual right constitutes, in itself, an exception to the general freedom to use ideas and, for that very reason, must be admitted sparingly and made compatible with the collective interest. The limits of the exclusive right are neither external anomalies nor occasional concessions to the vulnerable party. They are a normal part of its conformation, a true “negation of the negation”, indispensable for keeping the channels of information, competition, culture and social communication unobstructed Ascensão(Ascensão, 2007, [p. 11; p. 13; p. 14])812. The social function does not appear ex post to denature the effects of IP. It has always existed, always integrated it, always represented its institutional limits.

This constitutional limit acquires special relevance when the exclusivity invoked is anchored in software. Since the protection of the program in Brazil is not structured under a broad patent logic over function, method or result, but under a special, predominantly copyright-based regime, it is incompatible with the Constitution to convert technical control of the infrastructure into total power over the consumed experience. Dominion over execution, updating and modulation of the environment explains a relevant part of the platform's position, but does not authorize reading any alteration, block, sanction or suppression of enjoyment as a natural and immune consequence p. 707 of ownership of the code. The social function operates, here, as a lock against this extrapolation. It prevents control over the technological infrastructure from being transformed into authorization to degrade enjoyment without restoration, block minimal verifiability or organize technical dependence as a form of capture of the consumer.

When supply is continuous and enjoyment depends on private infrastructure, the exercise of property and of IP ceases to be merely dominion over an asset and becomes, concretely, the power to organize consumption trajectories, expectations, reputational positions and possibilities of participation in the ecosystem. The constitutional economic order already makes this visible by subjecting property to the social function and by articulating this functionalization with consumer protection. In IOCs, this articulation means that the exercise of exclusivity cannot be dissociated from the functional integrity of supply and from the protection of the vulnerable party against the arbitrariness of whoever holds technical control of the environment. The social function of property and of the contract thus acts as a vector of rebalancing, preventing the ownership of exclusive rights from being converted into a tool of oppression, emptying of enjoyment or neutralization of the trust legitimately created in the relationship Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 235)813.

This functionalization also acquires a concrete consumer-law feature when one observes the position of predominance exercised by the supplier that controls, at the same time, the technological asset, the architecture of the environment and the evidence of what happens in it. The vulnerability of the consumer justifies norms limiting private power precisely to prevent factual and technical superiority from turning into the material suppression of rights. In IOC platforms, this translates into duties of clarity, verifiability and preservation of functional utility, making unlawful the use of technical exclusivity to camouflage planned obsolescence, lower the quality of performance without justification or block the auditability of contractual performance Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 274)814. The social function, here, does not replace the dogmatics of the CDC. It reinforces it constitutionally.

p. 708 The invocation of a license, industrial secret or technical prerogative is not enough, by itself, to set aside good faith, informational adequacy and control of abusiveness. In governed ecosystems, the exclusive right projects itself as a form of governance, and governance is legally attributable conduct when it affects enjoyment, access, permanence and contestation. Nor is it necessary to resort to a strong idea of the consumer's ownership of the digital good in order to demand minimal stability. That stability derives from the very structure of the consumer relationship and from the protection of legitimate expectations in continuous contracts, above all when the platform controls the conditions of intelligibility of the object and concentrates the evidence of what was altered. What is protected, then, is not a mirrored dominion of the consumer over the asset, but the functional and, where appropriate, biographical and symbolic integrity of the promised enjoyment.

It is at this point that the constructed operators appear as concretizations compatible with the social function, and not as undue aggressions against the core of exclusivity. Open auditability functions as a functional unfolding of this constitutional limit. It does not demand unrestricted opening of code, nor a broad exposure of the protected asset. It demands only that relevant alterations and sanctions with material impact be justifiable in a technically intelligible and institutionally verifiable manner, compatible with control by protection bodies and by the Judiciary. In the case of software, this is especially important, because it removes the recurring objection that any requirement of minimal transparency would amount to emptying IP itself. It does not. What the social function refuses is not the protection of the program, but the use of that protection as an absolute barrier to scrutiny of the effects of supply on the consumer's continuous enjoyment.

Functional value equivalence plays an analogous role. If exclusivity is constitutionally protected to stimulate creation and organize the market, it is fully compatible with that purpose to require that relevant updates and reconfigurations not empty the economic and existential function promised to the consumer or, when they do, that they be accompanied by adequate restoration. The social function does not freeze the ecosystem nor prevent the evolution of the service. It merely blocks the use of mutability as an instrument of extraction and normalization of losses in a context of asymmetry of information and evidence. The exclusive right may organize the market; it may not unilaterally reorganize, without control, the p. 709 functional and symbolic content of what was acquired and enjoyed under a promise of continuity.

For this reason too, Brazilian copyright protection offers, in a subsidiary way, an interesting clue against totalizing proprietary rhetoric. The moral rights of the author are inalienable and unwaivable. This fact does not make the consumer's experience a matter of authorship, nor does it transplant the moral regime of copyright into the field of the IOC. Its usefulness here is more modest and subtler. It shows that not even within the logic of copyright is the language of ownership reduced to pure, absolute patrimonial disposability. Within IP itself there is already internal resistance to the idea that every immaterial bond can be dissolved by a simple dispositive decision of the holder. This strengthens the refusal of the narrative according to which license, adhesion and technical control would suffice to turn governed enjoyment into a pure disposable permission.

This limit takes on a particularly sharp outline in cases of the cessation of activities, discontinuation of services or definitive shutdown of servers. As relevant portions of contemporary life migrate to private digital ecosystems, the formal precariousness of use licenses can no longer be read as automatic authorization for the complete sacrifice of immaterial objects of consumption incorporated into the consumer's trajectory. Histories, libraries, progressions, projected identities, reputational positions and long-term investments cannot be treated as residues without density merely because they are technically housed in someone else's infrastructure.

The social function of property and of intellectual property prevents exclusivity from operating as a sufficient title to legitimate the unilateral erasure of these collections under the pretext of business freedom, economic convenience or strategic reorientation of the supplier. The right to exploit, close or reorganize economic activity is not to be confused with a supposed right to annihilate, without duties of transition, preservation or transfer, the functional enjoyment already stabilized in the consumer's sphere.

If the shutdown of the service is unavoidable, the constitutional order requires that it be made compatible, as far as technically and legally possible, with solutions of continuity, portability, functional restoration, assignment of the operation or other measures suitable to prevent private architecture from becoming a lawful instrument of patrimonial and existential devastation of the consumer. Exclusivity may organize the market; it may not legitimate the administered destruction of someone else's enjoyment as a side effect of the very decision to leave it.

The social function of property and the constitutional teleology of IP oriented to social interest and development provide sufficient grounds to limit the exercise of exclusivity when it turns into opaque governance, harmful unilateral mutability or a p. 710 technique for capturing the consumer. In IOC platforms, this limit translates into manageable criteria: a duty of verifiable justification for relevant alterations, minimal preservation of records and trails for institutional control, a requirement of restoration by functional equivalence when there is substantial loss of the core of enjoyment, and a prohibition on the use of technical dependence as a mechanism of private exclusion without a minimal standard of control. Exclusivity remains a legitimate element in the organization of the ecosystem. What it does not do is immunize its manner of exercise against consumer protection, the social function and constitutional limits on private power.

Notes

  1. Intellectual property, as a species of the genus property at the constitutional level, does not subsist as an absolute right, being structurally bound to the realization of its social function. Each of the exclusive rights protected by the system “exists within the limits and for the functions constitutionally assigned to it”, possessing not only the social function common to all properties, but also a specific function in the design of intellectual properties, directed to the technological and economic development of the country Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 84). It follows that exclusivity is not an end in itself, but a legal instrument conditioned by the higher objectives that justify its creation and maintenance by the State. In IOC platforms, this conditioning prevents IP ownership from being invoked as an absolute barrier against scrutiny of practices that undermine the integrity of supply and the protection of the consumer. ↩
  2. The legal discipline of immaterial goods is characterized by a marked functionalization, which makes it incompatible with readings that seek to isolate the holder from responsibilities toward the collectivity and users. By its very nature and mode of constitution, intellectual property is “even more functional, even more conditioned, even more socially responsible, and surely much less full than any other form of property” Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 75). This intrinsic social responsibility stems from the fact that the exclusive right is a normative concession aimed at balancing private interests of exploitation and public interests of access and progress. Therefore, the exercise of technical and legal power over the platform's infrastructure does not take place in a regulatory vacuum, and must answer to the social function that legitimates the very existence of the intellectual property right. ↩
  3. The international regime of intellectual property reinforces the need for balance between the exclusive rights of holders and their obligations toward society, especially as regards the diffusion of knowledge and social welfare. Barbosa underlines that the TRIPs Agreement does not enshrine an absolutist paradigm, being based on a commitment to balance between the promotion of innovation and the diffusion of technology, “so as to contribute to social and economic welfare and to the balance of rights and obligations” Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 662). This global parameter of reciprocity and responsibility confirms that the protection of exclusive rights does not authorize the exercise of power resulting in disproportionate harm to the legitimate enjoyment of end recipients, justifying the application of limits and controls aimed at preserving the functional integrity of the immaterial objects consumed. ↩
  4. The social function does not constitute an external or exceptional restriction imposed on the intellectual property right, but an intrinsic condition of the legitimacy of its exercise. Every subjective right, in the contemporary legal order, is born and subsists under the aegis of this ordering principle. Ascensão is emphatic in maintaining that it is “absurd to conceive of the intellectual right as a right whose holder could exercise it as he saw fit”, noting that intellectual property is born subject to the principle of the social function, “like all rights” Ascensão(Ascensão, 2007, [p. 3]). This premise sets aside the view of IP as an enclave of private sovereignty immune to controls; on the contrary, the title of exclusivity is justified only insofar as its exercise remains compatible with social values and with the protection of others' interests equally protected by the legal system, such as the consumer's trust and the integrity of enjoyment. ↩
  5. The regimes of appropriation of the immaterial are limited from the origin and, for that very reason, fully compatible with external conditionings of social interest. The intellectual exclusive right does not present itself as a natural domain prior to the system, but as an exceptional technique of legal organization. Ascensão is categorical in asserting that intellectual right constitutes, in itself, “an exception to the general freedom to use ideas”, which is why its admission must be sparing and always guided by consideration of the collective interest Ascensão(Ascensão, 2007, [p. 11]). It follows that the limits on the exclusive right are not external anomalies, but a normal part of its own conformation. The author describes these restrictions as “the negation of the negation”, because the exclusive right itself already represents an initial restriction on common freedom; for that reason, such limits “are, for that reason, as normal as any other rules” and “are not in themselves exceptional” Ascensão(Ascensão, 2007, [p. 13]). The legally adequate balance, then, lies not in shielding exclusivity, but in making it compatible with the need to keep unobstructed “the channels of information, of competition, of culture”, that is, “the paths of communication and social dialogue” Ascensão(Ascensão, 2007, [p. 14]). ↩
  6. The social function of the contract and of property acts as a vector of rebalancing in consumer relationships, preventing the ownership of exclusive rights from being turned into a tool of oppression or of hollowing out the consumer's position. In contexts of strong asymmetry, the Law uses the social function to limit the arbitrariness of whoever holds the drafting power and technical control of the object. Marques associates this principle with the pursuit of contractual justice and balance, asserting that the social function of the contract imposes limits on freedom of contract in favor of the social interest, protecting trust and objective good faith Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 235). Applied to the semiotic governance of IOCs, this guideline requires that the supplier's exercise of intellectual property override neither the integrity of the promised enjoyment nor the protection of vulnerability, since the legitimacy of exclusivity depends on its compatibility with the social and distributive purpose of the consumer contract. ↩
  7. The exercise of intellectual property in consumer platforms must be bounded by the social function as a limit on the private power to direct the relationship. In the consumer market, the supplier that holds technological and legal control over the immaterial object exercises a position of predominance that demands normative containment to prevent abuses. Miragem observes that the vulnerability of the consumer justifies the application of norms limiting private power, founded on the social function and on good faith, to prevent factual superiority from turning into the suppression of rights Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 274). In this sense, the social function of IP in IOC platforms imposes duties of clarity, verifiability and maintenance of functional utility, making unlawful the use of technical exclusivity to camouflage planned obsolescence, lower the quality of performance without justification or block the auditability of contractual performance. ↩
