5.1.2.1 On the normative relevance of the IOC and its insertion in the discipline of consumption

p. 411

The qualification of the immaterial object of consumption within the Consumer Protection Code requires bringing platform-governed enjoyment back into a dogmatic framework that preserves the object as an object, without reducing its existence to a one-off delivery event and without dissolving it into an undifferentiated notion of service that erases what was actually acquired. Anchoring the IOC in the concept of product in Article 3, paragraph 1, of the CDC operates as a starting point because it maintains the intelligibility of supply even when enjoyment depends on technical mediations and on governance in continuous operation. The purpose is to fix operable contours for the category, so that it organizes the reading of the offer, makes it possible to identify relevant losses and prepares criteria of attribution and restoration in continuous consumption, without inaugurating an autonomous microsystem and without depending exclusively on explicit contractual commands or episodic informational duties.

The legal definition of product in Article 3, paragraph 1, of the CDC operates as a scope clause that prevents escape from protective coverage when the object does not present itself in the form of a physical medium. Nunes425 makes explicit that the CDC's choice of “product”, rather than narrower civil law categories, has the function of encompassing material and immaterial goods, noting that the designation applies to economically offered objects that are not tangible, when stating that “the designation ‘product’ is used, for example, in banking activities (loan, fixed-income investment, pledge of securities etc.). Such ‘products’ fit, then, into the definition of immaterial goods” Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 97]). This framework shifts the discussion from the medium to the function of the acquired object and to the form of its enjoyment, preserving the possibility of qualifying p. 412 relevant losses even when formal access to the content remains available. The same reasoning is reinforced when the author states that “banks sell products”, distinguishing “the immaterial ones commented on before and the material ones such as money” Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 102]), dispelling the idea that the immaterial would correspond to a legally ungraspable “non-object”.

Continuity, in turn, is not an element foreign to the CDC, nor does it prevent the dogmatic qualification of supply. Nunes maintains that services can be durable when they present continuity over time by contractual stipulation, observing that durable services are those that have continuity by reason of what was agreed and giving as examples school services, health plans and, in general, every service that the contract establishes as continuous Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 102]).

In continuing bonds, the risk of unilateral reconfigurations and interruptions is part of the very structure of enjoyment as a lasting relationship, which prevents treating utility as something already delivered and definitively beyond discussion whenever formal access persists. This trait appears directly when the author identifies as proper to contracts for continuous services those whose mark is continuity “ad infinitum as long as the consumer fulfills their obligation to pay the price” Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 508]). Continuity, thus, functions as a property of the supply bond and not as a pretext for dissolving the object into generic activity.

The distinction between product and service, when treated in an operable way, is an instrument of qualification and not a rigid dichotomy. Nunes stresses that there are cases in which the performance is correctly qualified as a service even when there is delivery of something physically identifiable, since the core lies in the supply and in the infrastructure that ensures availability. When dealing with public services, he points out that “it is important to stress this aspect of the service that is accompanied by the product, to avoid doubts regarding the public service, because even if it delivers some product (e.g., water), it continues to be characterized as a service” Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 113]), adding that “in reality it is the ‘supply’ that is the service rendered. “The assembly of the entire transmission network, piping, sanitation etc. is done so that the service may be rendered” Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 114]).

This key makes it possible to describe situations in which enjoyment depends on continuous infrastructure, but nevertheless involves the acquisition of an identifiable core of utility, preserving the intelligibility of the object and preparing to confront the product-service tension without reductionism.

p. 413 The reconstruction of the IOC as a category internal to the CDC requires, in addition to dogmatic support for qualification, a methodological foundation capable of disciplining the translation of the factual regime into operable legal language426. It is this foundation that prevents the concept from appearing arbitrary, also serving as an obstacle to the alibi of “technical necessity” as a strategy for immunizing institutional design427.

Mangabeira Unger's critique428 turns against the institutional fatalism that converts the platform's design into fatality and presents it as “technical necessity”429, that is, as something external to the field of the controllable. On the explanatory plane, Mangabeira Unger confronts both “deep structure” theories, which treat social formations as indivisible totalities governed by laws of development, and certain versions of positivist social science, which describe social life as negotiation of interests within a setting taken as given.

p. 414 Against these traditions, the author radicalizes the thesis that society is a human and contingent artifact. He maintains that prevailing institutions and beliefs are provisional settlements produced by past conflicts, and not the realization of necessary imperatives. In doing so, he places institutional design back in the field of the revisable430 and prevents the present from being confused with the only possible horizon of humanity Unger(Unger, 2004, p. xx).

This methodological brake gains special relevance when the platform justifies itself through a language of impersonal coordination431, presenting hierarchies, frictions and authorization regimes as neutral requirements of operation. Unger makes it possible to render this movement visible by indicating that, in the modern legal imaginary, different social spheres are usually organized by distinct “modes” of containing power, and that the organization of work is frequently associated with the combination of voluntary agreement and the invocation of an “impersonal technical necessity” Unger(Unger, 2004, p. 301-302).

From a critical perspective, this grammar can operate as an unjustified restriction of institutional possibilities, insofar as it shifts controversies about design and domination to the register of the inevitable. In this key, the existence of technical restrictions is not denied, but their functioning as a seal of immunity is refused. If the utility consumed depends on an environment that modulates access, visibility, updating and enforcement, p. 415 governance by architecture must remain tractable by criteria of verifiability and attribution.

The effect of inevitability is consolidated when the very structure of the regime is withdrawn from the field of deliberation and comes to appear as the “natural” backdrop of relations, ceasing to be perceived as a revisable arrangement. Unger describes this mechanism when pointing to the effort to depoliticize the basic structure of rights and economic policy and when noting that, once the original conflicts and uncertainties are forgotten, the institutional settlement takes on a misleading appearance of naturalness and necessity432, although it derives from contingent interests, illusions and tactical results Unger(Unger, 2004, p. 178). This warning prevents the practical normativity exercised by architecture from being treated as an inevitable exteriority and keeps open the field of the controllable, so that architectural decisions that shape enjoyment can be qualified, proven and attributed by minimally verifiable dogmatic criteria.

Next, the central methodological requirement that follows from Reale is that the legal category operate as integration and remain referred to the concrete conditions it organizes. In defining the norm as the “moment of integration of a class of facts according to an order of values”, the author makes explicit that the normative structure only becomes intelligible when referred to the factual and axiological factors that it dialectically integrates Reale(Reale, 2001, p. 96).

p. 416 This key makes it possible to present the IOC as an instrument of qualification that integrates a stabilized social and technical fact, namely sustained enjoyment governed by infrastructure, with normative values recognized in the protective consumer regime, and with a legal language capable of guiding the identification of the object, the recognition of relevant losses and criteria for restoration.

This operation of integration imposes limits against arbitrariness and also against the temptation to convert the concept into technological moralization. Reale maintains that, even if the idea of Natural Law is not adopted, the elaboration of legal norms cannot disregard natural data that impose themselves on everyone's ethical or scientific conscience Reale(Reale, 1994, p. 109). The same limit appears when he rejects the notion that objective law is made up of empty shells, since normative structures are not mere logical forms without content. For Reale, they are forms that derive from a concrete experience, whose dominant lines are abstracted from social reality to operate as instruments of social discipline, that is, as legal models Reale(Reale, 1994, p. 110).

This conception of the norm as a model prevents the framing from being voluntarist and requires anchoring in observable elements of the regulated phenomenon itself. The result is a double lock, because the category must capture dominant lines of the concrete experience of sustained enjoyment and infrastructural governance, and must do so through verifiable elements, preventing any ordinary friction from being converted into an unlawful act through intuitive repulsion. At the same time, it must preserve the core of value of the acquired object and make it possible to distinguish ordinary variations of the digital regime from significant degradations of the enjoyment bundle, lest it lose its disciplinary function.

The three-dimensional theory provides direct justification for treating the formulation of the IOC as a response to regularities of social experience and as a regulatory choice conditioned by facts and values, and not as a free exercise of will. Reale maintains that “the elaboration of a given and particular legal norm is not a mere expression of the arbitrariness of power, nor does it result objectively and automatically from the factual-axiological tension operating in a given historical-social conjuncture”, since it is a culminating moment of legal experience, “but power always being conditioned by a complex of facts and values, in light of which the option is made for one of the possible regulatory solutions […].” Reale(Reale, 1994, p. 61).

In complement, the idea of “legal models” is linked to the technical translation of social regularities into operable forms. Reale maintains that the very possibility of legal science presupposes the existence of relations endowed with stability and predictability and, in short, of certain “constants of action”, at least within a certain historical conjuncture, without p. 417 which it would make no sense to contemplate rules intended to govern the order of human acts Reale(Reale, 1994, p. 106). In this framework, the model does not function as a descriptive ornament, because the concept of model, in any kind of science, is linked to the idea of logical planning and to the symbolic and anticipated representation of results to be achieved by means of an ordered sequence of measures or prescriptions Reale(Reale, 1994, p. 111). What matters, for this framing, is that this representation does not arise from nothing nor is it legitimized by conceptual elegance, since it depends on identifying dominant lines of social experience and converting them into parameters that guide decisions and allow control.

In this sense, the legal model is a way of making the conflict tractable with criteria, while preserving the link with the facts and values that give meaning to the normative intervention. In environments of governed enjoyment, in which architecture, internal rules and enforcement combine to define conditions of access and permanence, the controversy tends to escape traditional categories if they do not capture what repeats and what is decisive in practice. The IOC, here, operates as a technique for representing the conflict because it organizes, in a single analytical frame, the core of value that the consumer acquires and the infrastructural conditions that sustain the continuity of this enjoyment. It thus makes it possible to describe precisely what is in dispute when unilateral reconfigurations, screenings, eligibility criteria and visibility standards alter the bundle of promised utilities, without reducing the problem to intuitive impressions or to moralizations about technology. By transforming these regularities into controllable elements, the IOC makes the conflict decidable, that is, susceptible to dogmatic qualification and legal response, without breaking the link with the concrete experience that justifies its existence.

The qualification of the IOC as an infrastructure-concept also requires an instrument to explain why dogmatics needs to stabilize expectations and produce criteria of decidability in an environment marked by structural asymmetry of intelligibility and by continuous mutation of the conditions of enjoyment.

Ferraz Jr.433 makes explicit that dogmatics does not eliminate uncertainty, but works on it in a controlled way to make the conflict decidable without breaking the binding to norms, when stating that, with the “controlled term”, one intends to signify that dogmatics “increases uncertainties”, but p. 418 does so in a way compatible with “two central requirements of the legal discipline”, which are “the binding to norms, which cannot be ignored, and the pressure to decide conflicts, since a way out must be found for them” Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 44]). The IOC, at this point, does not exist to “describe” the digital, but to bring sustained enjoyment, reconfiguration and enforcement back to a vocabulary that allows attribution and restoration without arbitrariness.

The same movement is articulated with the stabilizing function of norms as durable expectations, which resist empirical frustration counterfactually. Ferraz Jr. explains that normative attitudes are expectations whose durability is guaranteed by “non-adaptive generalization”, and concludes that norms manifest expectations “stabilized counterfactually”, since generalization “is independent of the fulfillment or non-fulfillment of the empirically expected action” Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 98]). This formulation preserves the core of promise of the object even when technical execution, curation and sanctions executed by design make enjoyment unstable and subject to unilateral reconfiguration.

In environments of structural opacity, the expectation of an ideal situation of complete information leads to a methodological error that feeds case-by-case decisions. Ferraz Jr. delimits this point by stating that decision is not “the establishment of an equitable apportionment among the alternatives”, since that would presuppose the ideal situation of a subject who deliberates “only after having all the relevant data”, being able to enumerate and evaluate the alternatives in advance. The ideal situation is denied by the very existence of the conflict, since the decision corresponds to a “complex procedure” in the face of a situation “characterized by insecurity” Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 305]). In the regime of the IOC, this prevents conditioning protection on an ideal of total transparency or on a model of proof that presupposes full intelligibility of the platform's internal functioning.

The difference between information and practical intelligibility, at the level of legal discourse, reinforces why the formal expansion of clarifications does not resolve the decisive asymmetry of platform consumption. Ferraz Jr. observes that the “absorption of insecurity” does not necessarily mean obtaining consensus and does not have the function of reducing or eliminating incompatibilities. Therefore, in terms of information theory, the absorption of insecurity “should not, then, be confused with the process of eliminating information” Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 1997, p. 44). The category needs to operate with controllable criteria that allow deciding under insecurity, without turning the absence of complete access to the functioning of the ecosystem into an impediment to attribution.

p. 419 The legitimacy of concept formation also depends on a method that preserves the internal coherence of the system and, at the same time, adherence to the object, avoiding both physicalist reductions and dissolving abstractions434.

Larenz (1997) justifies conceptual construction as a task proper to dogmatics when it needs to operate ordering structures intended to function homogeneously across multiple relations of life. This makes it possible to frame or classify individual legal relations, with attention to the general significance of these institutes for the regulation of human relations, and not to what is historically unique about them Larenz(Larenz, 1997, p. 143). This ordering function is neither neutral nor merely descriptive, since it contributes decisively to jurisprudential stability when problems of valuation are converted into problems of thought and, for that reason, can be treated with greater control through better dogmatic construction of concepts and through the explicitation and understanding of the specificity of the regulation Larenz(Larenz, 1997, p. 314). This key reinforces that the category is not a descriptive ornament, but an instrument for making representable and adjudicable what is at stake in sustained enjoyment, protecting consumer law against formalist blindness and against the abstraction that erases the object under a generic notion of “service”.

The method also makes it possible to reconstruct the legal system as a teleological and open order, still endowed with intrinsic rationality and with directive points of view. In addressing the problem of the system, Larenz maintains that the unity of law is not preserved by a system of general legal concepts, but by an axiological or teleological order structured by general legal principles. From this perspective, the principle, and not the concept, functions as an open standard that requires concretization and only becomes fully graspable in the concretizations it produces Larenz(Larenz, 1997, p. 235). This key makes it possible to integrate new situations without breaking the unity of law, because it shifts the consistency of the legal order to the internal rationality of the directive values and to the continuous work of concretization, instead of depending on a closed catalog of definitions.

In addition, the warning against solutions that promise maximum precision at the cost of emptying is especially pertinent to platform consumption. Citing Kaufmann, Larenz points out that the pursuit of maximum precision of language tends to exact as its price the maximum emptying of content and meaning, as an effect of abstraction taken to the p. 420 extreme Larenz(Larenz, 1997, p. 190). In enjoyment ecosystems governed by infrastructure, this risk appears when enjoyment is reduced to residual labels that cannot guide proof, attribution and restoration, precisely in the context in which a criterion capable of capturing the operational density and the relevant variations of the enjoyment bundle is required.

The requirement of controllable criteria for the category is not exhausted in justifying the formation of the concept, since it also sets conditions of institutional verifiability435. In continuous consumption governed by infrastructure, the risk of elasticity increases precisely where complexity and opacity increase, so that the category is only sustainable if it imposes a verifiable qualification roadmap.

The warning is completed by the critique of theoretical distinctions that, by manipulating elements assessable only at the concrete level, obstruct the application of law and eliminate the applier's burden of argumentation436 Ávila(Ávila, 2018, p. 89). The core of the IOC, therefore, must impose on the interpreter the duty to indicate the elements of the regime used, the connections between those elements and the result, and the reasons why ordinary variations were distinguished from significant degradations

The requirement of controllability is further projected in terms of legal certainty as a methodology for reducing vagueness and institutionally stabilizing decision-making standards. Ávila437 maintains that the focus should shift from “certainty of meaning” to “certainty of criteria”, so as to provide “prior and abstract parameters” that guide the resolution of later and concrete interpretive problems Ávila(Ávila, 2011, p. 71). In the same direction, legal certainty is defined as a requirement of “legal-rational controllability of reconstructive argumentative structures” and as an instrument of protection against “deception, frustration, surprise and arbitrariness” Ávila(Ávila, 2011, p. 268). The result is a rule of p. 421 calibration: the category must allow distinguishing, by controllable standards, the ordinary from the relevant and to stabilize expectations without requiring total transparency as a condition of protection.

This requirement also functions as a warning against solutions that promise justice through elasticity, but deliver arbitrariness through lack of a standard. In addressing balancing, Ávila requires prior indication of the object and the criteria, stating that, without this, balancing becomes an instrument of arbitrariness, and must be guided by criteria that are “universalizable and legally justifiable” Ávila(Ávila, 2011, p. 373).

In platform consumption, opacity and technical language facilitate both the rhetoric of “it is not possible to know” and the rhetoric of “this is obviously abusive”, and that is why the category needs to block this short circuit by imposing verifiable criteria. These criteria also need to carry an unequivocal negative edge, since the category is not activated by interface preferences, ordinary discomforts of use or a mere functional update, and does not turn the regular exercise of curation or moderation, by itself, into an unlawful act. Calculability, in this context, corresponds to the citizen's capacity to “foresee […] the limits of the intervention of the Public Power” and to know “in advance the scope of discretion” of the State Ávila(Ávila, 2011, p. 126), which requires a standard capable of distinguishing ordinary variations from significant degradations of the enjoyment bundle, preventing both dogmatic abdication and punitive voluntarism.

Under such dogmatic and methodological premises, the core of the IOC can be formulated in a lean and controllable way as an immaterial product whose mode of legal existence is not exhausted in an act of delivery, because it is realized as a position of sustained enjoyment in a technical-organizational environment that maintains, modulates and conditions the acquired utility over time. The object is not confused with isolated “content” nor with an abstract promise of access, but with a bundle of functionalities and significations that makes up the economically offered utility and that remains enjoyable through the continuity of infrastructural conditions of availability, mediation and presentation. Immateriality, in this sense, does not reduce the object to the absence of a medium, but rather identifies that the good offered is graspable by the function and by the regime of enjoyment it establishes, remaining legally qualifiable as a product.

This conceptual core remains compatible with the dogmatic indications already established on the basis of Nunes regarding the functional scope of the concept of product, the durability of continuous services by contractual stipulation and the centrality of supply even when performance depends on complex infrastructure. This set prevents two distinct maneuvers. The first is to expel the immaterial from the field of "product". The second is to use p. 422 continuity as a pretext to empty protection and confuse the promised core with a mere technical operation of delivery.

The consolidation of the core of the IOC as an immaterial product, under verifiability locks, does not correspond to the creation of a parallel regime nor to a category external to the CDC. The legal qualification of the fact is not carried out by mechanical subsumption, but by a process of concretion in which norm and factual support refer to each other until the case becomes legally graspable. Reale describes this procedure by stating that the determination of the norm “among the various possible norms” already requires “preliminary reference to the factual element”, and that the applier “not rarely goes from the norm to the fact and vice versa” Reale(Reale, 2001, p. 283). This logic prevents treating the IOC as a label and allows it to be situated as an instrument of decidability within consumer law.

The same requirement of internal integration appears, in Larenz, as a methodological commitment to the coherence of the legal order, instead of classificatory formulas that serve external systematics but fail when the dispute requires delimiting the content and limits of the institute. Criticizing the reduction of legal thought to a general-abstract concept, Larenz observes that an “abbreviated formula” may render services to systematics, but is not “appropriate as a basis for argumentation about the content and the limits” of the institute, since it would obliterate distinctions oriented to social reality and to normative function Larenz(Larenz, 1997, p. 318). In addition, certain definitions have binding meaning and only acquire practical significance “in connection with the legal propositions” in which the term is used, functioning as a normative indication for reading the institute Larenz(Larenz, 1997, p. 360). With this, the qualification of the IOC as an immaterial product presents itself as coherent integration into the CDC, preserving the object as object and avoiding both formalist refusal and dissolution into generic “service”.

Once the anchoring of the IOC in the concept of product of the CDC has been established and its core made explicit in terms compatible with sustained enjoyment, it becomes possible to operate with a category that delimits a legally recognizable object and, therefore, one susceptible to proof, attribution and restoration. The decisive consequence is to remove the conflict from the alternative between a formalism that identifies "delivery" as the one-off provision of access and an undifferentiated language of service that erases what was acquired when utility depends on continuous technical mediations. Continuity ceases to operate as an alibi for lack of protection, because the bond projected in time reinforces the need for criteria that differentiate ordinary variations from relevant degradations of the enjoyment bundle, even if nominal access remains available. Next, the category will be reconstructed in a dual function, as qualification of the object and p. 423 as an infrastructure-concept, to reorganize the reading of the offer, of relevant losses and of restoration in continuous consumption under controllable criteria.

  8. Mangabeira Unger describes the naturalization of the arrangement not as a mere theoretical error, but as a cumulative political effect. He points to the effort to “depoliticize the basic structure of rights and economic policy” and observes that, once the original conflicts and uncertainties are forgotten, the institutional settlement assumes a “specious semblance of naturalness and necessity”, although it is no more necessary than “the interests and the illusions, the tactical achievements and the tactical failures” that consolidated it Unger(Unger, 2004, p. 178). That is the form of entrapment that the radicalization of democratic experimentalism most directly undermines. The concepts of institutional and structure fetishism describe two of its more important manifestations in our ideas. They show how we become accomplices to our own enslavement. We are also entrapped, more generally, by the naturalization of the social world through repetition. Repetition in society and culture is a kind of incantation, leading us, through our inability to produce or endure permanent novelty and to be ever new, to accept a habitual form of life as a second nature. We spring the trap by changing the relation between repetition and disruption. We must so reform our arrangements and practices — including our practices of discourse in technical disciplines like legal analysis or political economy — that the very activities by which we reproduce our second nature create opportunities and provide tools for its fragmentary, but motivated and cumulative remaking.” Unger(Unger, 2004, p. cxxiii). Thus, what seems mere routine becomes an effective “institutional spell”. ↩
  9. In this framing, two works by Tércio Sampaio Ferraz Jr. are drawn upon. The first is Introdução ao Estudo do Direito: Técnica, Decisão, Dominação (2003), for its approach to law as a decision-making phenomenon and to dogmatics as a technology aimed at the decidability of conflicts. The second is Direito, Retórica e Comunicação: subsídios para uma pragmática do discurso jurídico (1997), for its pragmatic approach to legal discourse and for the way in which decision absorbs insecurity without confusing this operation with a mere accumulation of information. The approach to dogmatics as technology provides operational justification for constructing the IOC as a category capable of guiding proof and decision. ↩
  10. It is in this framing that Karl Larenz is drawn upon, in Metodologia da Ciência do Direito (1997), as technical vocabulary to maintain that, in the face of new forms of supply and of continued enjoyment, the adequate response is not to abandon the system nor to invent an “other law”, but to qualify the phenomenon by criteria internal to the legal order, preserving teleological coherence and decision-making capacity. ↩
  11. At this point, Humberto Ávila is drawn upon, in Teoria dos Princípios (2018), as a discipline of precision intended to prevent categories and standards from becoming a performative language of decision. Conceptual clarity has an institutional function by expanding means of control and preventing argumentation from descending into “capricious decisionism” Ávila(Ávila, 2018, p. 45). ↩
  12. Ávila formulates this critique in the context of the attempt to distinguish rules and principles at the abstract level. The problem, for him, is the confusion between a preliminary moment of signification (prima facie) and the conclusive moment of application (all things considered), which leads doctrine to transport into general theory elements that can only be assessed in the concrete case and, with that, to turn categories into alibis. The distinction between normative species should “relieve, by structuring it, the burden of argumentation of the applier of the law”, “reduce - eliminate, never - the need for [the duty] to be justified” Ávila(Ávila, 2018, p. 88). When, however, the classification comes to “manipulate, for the abstract interpretation of norms, elements that can only be assessed at the concrete level of application”, it “ends up obstructing it” and, “instead of relieving the burden of argumentation of the applier of the law, eliminates it” Ávila(Ávila, 2018, p. 89). ↩
  13. This framing draws on Segurança Jurídica: Entre Permanência, Mudança e Realização no Direito Tributário Ávila(Ávila, 2011), whose conceptual architecture reconstructs legal certainty as a norm-principle structured by cognoscibility, reliability and calculability. The decisive point is to shift the discussion from the promise of a fixed meaning to the requirement of prior and controllable criteria. ↩
