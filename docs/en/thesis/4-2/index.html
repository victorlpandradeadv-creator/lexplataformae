4.2 Informational domination, private power and subjectivity

See this section in the PDF

This section in the layers of the site

  • Foundations · Sections of chapter 4

p. 330

So far, enjoyment has appeared as a position sustained by signs, visibility and equivalences that exist only while the environment keeps them operative. Under this condition, acquisition depends on an ecosystem that supplies intelligibility, commensurability and the possibility of sanction, while at the same time defining, in practice, access, permanence and hierarchies. For this reason, the description of the IOC, when it seeks to support legal control, cannot remain confined to the vocabulary of price or to the psychological repertoire of preference. The core is institutional, because the platform administers the conditions of enjoyment and, in so doing, stabilizes value as a recognizable position, even when the relevant experience is reconfigured over time.

The aim is neither to build a general constitutional theory of platforms nor to transpose categories of public power automatically onto private relations, but to delimit a minimal analytical framework in which the platform is read as an instance of practical governance, with enough economic and informational power to organize access, permanence and participation at scale.

When the offer materializes as ecosystem-dependent enjoyment and value is anchored in hierarchies, criteria and sanctions produced by the environment itself, private power ceases to be merely a competitive fact and comes to act as a structural component of vulnerability, so that consumer protection must engage with limits and duties p. 331 proportional to the intensity of the control exercised, without dissolving the problem into abstractions and without reducing the platform to the classic figure of the supplier indifferent to how the very market it administers operates.

The investigation opened with the hypothesis that, in the digital environment, the platform tends to take over, by technical and contractual means, functions that were previously distributed across society, replacing public criteria of circulation and recognition with private rules of access, visibility and permanence. The formula according to which the platform “occupies the place of the collectivity without authorization” is treated as a descriptive problem to be stabilized in verifiable institutional terms, lest it remain an intuition without legal consequence.

This hypothesis must be stabilized in controllable institutional language. What matters is to identify which operations make up this occupation, what effects it produces on the enjoyment position and what asymmetries it creates between the consumer and the administrator of the ecosystem, so as to allow conduct, duties and restoration to be assessed without relying on impressionistic readings of the conflict. To this end, it must be recognized that, in the IOC regime, the platform does not merely intermediate a bilateral relationship between supplier and acquirer. It organizes, simultaneously, the normative content of the environment and the mechanisms for enforcing it.

First, it defines and updates internal rules that structure access and permanence, establishing permissions, incentives and punishments through moderation, functional restrictions or exclusions. Second, it administers visibility and reputation as economically relevant dimensions, through metrics, rankings, signals of prestige and instruments of public comparison that produce hierarchies and modulate the experience. Third, it exercises continuous curation of what appears, what disappears and what returns, controlling the horizon of expectation and reorganizing the temporality of consumption. Finally, it applies sanctions and restrictions whose effects go beyond the loss of a one-off utility, degrading symbolic position, interrupting continuity of enjoyment and reconfiguring the economic meaning of the IOC, in a register close to functional and identity dispossession.

Describing these powers requires no assumption of subjective intent, because what is at stake is a mode of operation in which control is exercised through architecture and through information. In digital environments, the relevant normativity does not manifest itself only as an explicit command, but as a configuration of choices, rhythms, frictions and incentives that makes certain conduct more likely and other conduct more costly, while defining the criteria by which the environment itself will recognize, reward or sanction the user. This governance is carried out through design decisions, moderation parameters, recommendation patterns and p. 332 access rules that operate as silent infrastructure, so that coercion need not present itself as outright prohibition in order to produce obedience, predictability and conformity.

As a result, the consumer faces not merely a contract, but an integrated set of devices that guide the experience and stabilize hierarchies while keeping the actual operating criteria opaque, creating a structural asymmetry between those who live in the ecosystem and those who control the variables that define its conditions of legibility, permanence and value.

It is in this strict sense that the expression “without authorization” should be understood, not as a generic political judgment, but as the finding that the platform performs functions of social ordering without holding a public mandate and without submitting, to an equivalent degree, to demands of institutional reciprocity. The relevant absence is not that of formal delegation, but that of minimum guarantees that function as counterweights of legitimacy, such as intelligible disclosure of criteria, the possibility of verification, decisional coherence, accessible reasoning and effective avenues of challenge.

When the environment is governed by mutable rules, selective curation and technical enforcement, the user is subjected to decisions that can alter their enjoyment position and degrade the value of the IOC without the grounds of the act being transparent, without its proportionality being open to examination and without review being practicable on terms commensurate with the economic and identity relevance of what is at stake. Consequently, private power consolidates itself as a power to administer symbolic and economic destinies at scale, while preserving a functional opacity that prevents the consumer from turning the conflict into an object of rational control, shifting the experience of enjoyment into a regime of institutional dependence not recognized as such.

Once this structure is recognized, the consequence for consumer law cannot be framed as a mere rhetorical expansion of protection, but as an adjustment of lens capable of reaching the way the offer actually materializes. If the IOC exists as a governed enjoyment position and value depends on internal criteria of recognition, protection cannot settle for reading the contract as an exhaustive statement of content, because the object is actually shaped by rules, architecture and continuous operations that go beyond the documentary form and can unilaterally alter the continuity of supply.

The question, therefore, is not merely to identify isolated abusive clauses, but to understand that the environment itself functions as a mechanism that produces vulnerability, which makes duties proportional to the power exercised relevant. The task, p. 333 then, is to prepare a conceptual basis that allows transparency, stability and restoration to be demanded on terms compatible with the regime of permanence and with the institutional density of the platform, without yet anticipating the dogmatic reconstruction developed later, but establishing that protection must reach the architectural plane on which enjoyment becomes possible and on which risk is actually administered.

To accomplish this task without generalizations, the discussion that follows will draw on references with delimited functions, as instruments of description and conceptual anchoring. With Julie E. Cohen (2012; 2019), the text delimits the specific form of power that stabilizes when information and architecture become means of practical governance, which makes it possible to treat choices, patterns and infrastructures as normative mechanisms that organize conduct and distribute possibilities. Warren and Brandeis ([201-?]) serve as a methodological model of legal reconstruction in the face of technical changes that reconfigure exposure and vulnerability, indicating that changes in the material conditions of social life require categories to be reinterpreted without moralizing the phenomenon. Byung-Chul Han (2015; 2017) is used as a lens to describe adherence and self-adjustment through performance, comparison and expectation, characterizing subjection as a structural effect of incentives and of the management of symbolic risk rather than attributing it to individual frailties. Eros Grau (2008) provides the conceptual anchor that avoids both the liberal neutralization of private normativity and its hasty transposition into the category of the State, making it possible to describe it as presupposed law endowed with collective effects and, therefore, requiring control criteria commensurate with its density.

The expected result is to establish a conceptual key according to which the platform, in the IOC regime, operates as a private power with practical normativity, because it administers not only conditions of exchange but conditions of enjoyment and recognition in a governed environment. This key moves the debate away from the simplifying opposition between freedom of contract and state intervention toward a plane on which internal rules, design, curation and enforcement constitute the economic object itself and distribute risks of loss, exclusion and degradation of value.

This prepares the transition to operational criteria that will make it possible to discuss proportional duties, transparency standards and restoration mechanisms without relying on merely formal categories, because the relevant materiality of the IOC will be understood as functional and ecosystemic materiality, and not as mere abstract ownership or one-off performance.

Governance by architecture is taken as the starting point to show how the platform constitutes a normative environment in which choices are configured and in which p. 334 informational domination is carried out by administering the variables that make enjoyment legible, comparable and sanctionable. This form of power is not confined to the economic dimension, since the technical reorganization of the conditions of exposure, participation and permanence calls for a pattern of legal reconstruction capable of dealing with new forms of vulnerability at scale, without this implying that the platform becomes a State.

In the same vein, the consumer's subjection is treated as a structural effect of mechanisms of performance, comparison and self-adjustment, when the ecosystem turns recognition into a condition of value and converts the management of symbolic risk into an instrument for shaping conduct, so that identity captivity emerges as an institutional outcome and not as an individual pathology.

The analysis then turns to the framing of private rules and enforcement as practical normativity, showing how the Lex Plataformae operates as the effective law of the environment and administers access, reputation, curation and sanctions with effects of exclusion and functional dispossession. This path closes with the figure of the consumer-citizen370 subjected to this private normativity, making it visible that consumer protection, when dealing with the IOC, must engage with the institutional impact of platform governance and prepare the way for structuring concepts that turn the description of power into an object controllable by criteria of transparency, stability and restoration.

Notes

  1. The expression “consumer-citizen” is used in a strictly analytical and functional sense. It designates the holder of an IOC, an immaterial product whose utility is realized as permanence in an ecosystem rather than as an isolated delivery. The “citizen” component does not refer to classic political-constitutional status or to nationality, but describes the practical conditions of participation, belonging and continuity within an environment governed by private rules and technical sanctions (Lex Plataformae). The point is to recognize that, in continuous digital consumption, the subject is subject to a private normative order that administers their visibility, reputation and capacity to act, making the enjoyment of the product inseparable from their position as a governed member of the ecosystem. ↩
