2 On the methodological design and the observation protocol

Epigraph

Danni Carlos, Coisas Que Eu Sei (2008). What it announces · Read the epigraph in the PDF.

p. 45

In platform economies, power tends to disguise itself as technical administration while it concentrates knowledge, organizes attention and redefines the conditions of participation. The central methodological risk, with an object of this kind, is to let the apparent neutrality of how things work stand in for an analysis of how private decisions produce practical norms and distribute asymmetries. For this reason, the work starts from a political warning that serves as the horizon of the problem: when knowledge is concentrated and everyone else is pushed into irrelevance, the answer is not contemplation, it is critical capacity and human quality.

El mundo se va a ir dividiendo [...] en los irrelevantes y los dueños de conocimiento [...]. Si nos quedamos de brazos cruzados no vamos a servir ni para que nos exploten [...]. Entonces se necesita una sociedad muy inteligente, muy capacitada [...]. Como seres humanos, no hemos evolucionado tanto como evolucionó la tecnología. Por eso hay que pelear por [...] mejorar la calidad humana. Eso se llama cultura, conocimiento y ejemplo. No me quiten la utopia. (MUJICA..., 202315)

It is on this foundation that the methodology adopted here presents itself as a legitimate taking of position, refusing to naturalize “how the platform works” as if it were a neutral method, marked by the absence of choice, and treating governance by architecture as a full phenomenon, and therefore one that can be legally described and reconstructed in dogmatic terms. The methodology represents a position toward the kind of power observed in platform economies and, therefore, toward the kind of temptation such an object creates for the researcher. In environments in which private administration and the management of conduct present themselves as technical neutrality, the central analytical risk is to naturalize decisions as if they were mere “functioning”, to reduce institutional choices to “operational details” and to attribute to the user, as autonomy, responses that have already been induced by design, communication and mechanisms of incentive, disincentive and sanction. The methodological design therefore takes as a premise that the regime under which the service actually operates emerges from the coupling of clauses, interface and institutional practices, which precludes readings that artificially separate norm, technique and culture.

The research strategy organizes collection and analysis procedures capable of reconstructing, in a controllable way, how promises are formed, how expectations become stable and how p. 46 relevant losses arise in continuous relationships. The aim is to preserve the legal intelligibility of the phenomenon without reducing its social density, since the exercise of authority in the ecosystem is distributed among contractual instruments, interface architectures and enforcement routines. The methodological consequence is a triangulation that makes it possible to trace inferences back to traceable evidence and to keep the object legally decidable, avoiding both abstraction cut off from practice and description that abdicates normative qualification.

This stance explains the choice to combine theoretical-dogmatic reconstruction with an instrumental empirical laboratory and with controlled exemplification, complementing this anchoring with illustrative applications and fit tests of the proposed keys in typical situations of continuous digital consumption. In relationships of this nature, in which conduct and expectations are administered through proprietary signs and institutional routines, the user's concrete experience is no longer external noise and becomes part of the very regulatory mechanism to be understood. This proximity does not authorize psychologizing, nor does it turn familiarity with the field into a criterion of proof. It serves to guide the gaze toward observable mechanisms of promise, friction and sanction, which must then be anchored in traceable and publicly verifiable evidence.

To keep the inferential path controllable, the literature drawn on throughout the work is handled through a methodological distinction between object and instruments16 Eco(Eco, 2007, p. 69). The object is the regime of platform-governed enjoyment and its mechanisms of promise, expectation, modulation and sanction. The instruments are the conceptual keys used to qualify this regime in language suited to the application of norms, which makes it possible to convert phenomena of architecture, communication and technical execution into operational categories compatible with legal duties, limits and consequences. In practical terms, frameworks from political economy, sociology, philosophy, the anthropology of consumption and communication theory come in to guide the identification of relevant mechanisms and to discipline inferences, without requiring an exegetical reconstruction of the works drawn on, so that the argument p. 47 can still be traced back to traceable evidence and to recognizable normative parameters Eco(Eco, 2007, p. 69-70). This distinction aims to prevent both exegetical drift and the merely ornamental use of concepts.

In this key, the choice of a non-exegetical approach does not work as a shortcut, but as a conscious delimitation of focus. When a concept is used as a tool to read an object other than the one in which it was formed, the interest lies in what it makes visible in the observed field and in how it improves the legal intelligibility of the problem, without requiring a philological reconstruction of its origin, of internal debates and of the concept's historical variants in its home domain, except where indispensable to avoid significant distortion.

The disciplined scope finds explicit support in Umberto Eco (2007, p. 70-71), who distinguishes the study of a body of thought at its origin from the study of its later uses and appropriations, and points out that, in certain cuts, what matters is what was made of the concept17. In methodological terms, this guidance allows the full genealogy of a concept to be treated as a topic only when it is needed to prevent distortion, and not as a mandatory stage of every instrumental use.

This chapter gathers the elements needed to make the investigation auditable, including the research strategy, the empirical scope and the techniques for collecting and reading the material used. This sets a standard of transparency and control capable of tracking the chain linking evidence, categories and inferences throughout the work. It thus preserves the argumentative continuity of the following sections, in which the analysis concentrates on the theoretical and dogmatic dimensions of the problem.

Notes

  1. Excerpt from an interview given by former Uruguayan President José Alberto “Pepe” Mujica Cordano to DW Brasil source(MUJICA..., 2023). ↩
  2. Eco treats the distinction between the object of research and the instruments used to make it intelligible as an organizing premise of intellectual work and as a check against turning auxiliary texts into a subject of exegesis. “A thesis studies an object using certain instruments. Often the object is a book and the instruments are other books. This is the case, for example, of a thesis on The economic thought of Adam Smith, whose object consists of the books of Adam Smith, while the instruments are other books about Adam Smith. […] In other cases, on the contrary, the object is a real phenomenon; this is what happens with theses on internal migratory movements in present-day Italy, on the behavior of a group of disabled children or on public opinion about a television program currently on the air.” Eco(Eco, 2007, p. 69). Methodologically, Eco's remark supports the view that interdisciplinary literature can operate as an instrument for qualifying the observed phenomenon without requiring an exegetical reconstruction of the works used when they are not the object of the investigation. ↩
  3. Eco offers an express formulation to justify cuts that seek not a full philological reconstruction of a body of thought at its origin, but the examination of its uses and appropriations in another context, provided the objective is clearly delimited. He gives an example: “It could, however, be a case in which the original thought interested me very little. Let us suppose I began a thesis on Zen thought in the Japanese tradition. […] Suppose, however, that, in examining the critical literature, I became interested in the use a certain American literary and artistic avant-garde made of Zen in the 1950s. Evidently, at this point I am no longer interested in knowing with absolute theological and philological exactness what the meaning of Zen thought would be, but rather in knowing how ideas originating in the East became elements of a Western artistic ideology. […] Assuming, of course, that I do not intend to show that the Californians misunderstood the original Zen, which would make comparison with the Japanese texts mandatory. But if I limit myself to presupposing that they drew freely on translations from the Japanese, what interests me is what they made of Zen and not what Zen was at its origin.” Eco(Eco, 2007, p. 70-71). The example makes explicit that the requirement to return to origins depends on the problem formulated and on the kind of claim one intends to support. ↩
