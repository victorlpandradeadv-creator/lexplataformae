The jurist's path

This way reconstructs the legal regime of the Immaterial Object of Consumption for lawyers, judges, members of the Public Prosecutor's Office and students. Each chapter starts from a problem, shows why the ordinary reading falls short and ends with what can be demonstrated and requested.

What are the chapters?

  1. How to read this doctrine

    Continuous digital consumption confronts the jurist with a conflict that the grammar of defect and accident captures poorly, because the loss usually arrives as technical normality rather than as an event. Hence the problem and the hypothesis that organize the following chapters, the normative scope in which they operate, the choice of a game as a laboratory and the weight of each kind of proof.

  2. Why technique changes the legal question

    The defense that the loss was simply how the software works treats as nature what, in a governed digital environment, is a design choice someone made. The philosophy of technique, of media and of design brings these choices back into the field of attribution, and each of its authors changes the question the jurist puts to the case.

  3. How the platform extracts value

    Consumption on platforms tends to be organized by a business model that extracts value from control of access, from produced scarcity and from the consumer's own presence, and this model explains supplier conduct that the reading of an isolated clause does not reach. Read with Varoufakis, Dowbor and Marx, this model offers the jurist conditions of imputation, and not a condemnation of the market.

  4. The Immaterial Object of Consumption

    The Immaterial Object of Consumption is the category that qualifies the utility existing only as a position of enjoyment sustained by private governance as an immaterial product, under Article 3, paragraph 1, of the CDC1. Without this qualification the case seems to have no object, and with it the jurist knows what to demonstrate in court.

  5. The law of the platform

    The law of the platform is the private ordering through which the platform creates rules, converts them into architecture and enforces them with sanctions that reach the consumer's position of enjoyment, without being either state law or a mere contract. This order takes shape and punishes through design, reaches a consumer who lives inside it and finds its limit in state law.

  6. Semiotic vulnerability

    Semiotic vulnerability is the form the consumer's factual vulnerability takes when the platform controls the signs, metrics, visibility and records through which the consumer understands what was acquired, perceives what was lost and tries to prove the loss. In these environments formally informed consent is not enough, and the category fits into Article 4, item I, of the CDC2 without creating a new vulnerability.

  7. Information, advertising and anticipation

    On platforms, the decision to buy is formed in an environment in which information, advertising, calendar and signs of prestige operate together, and the value of what is bought depends on criteria the supplier itself administers. For this reason the duty to inform is read as verifiable adequacy, the offer as controllable management of meaning, and the defense that everything was in the accepted terms loses force.

  8. Open auditability

    Open auditability is the minimum threshold of external verifiability that makes it possible to reconstruct what the supplier changed, when, why and with what effect, whenever the change or the sanction significantly affects the enjoyment of an Immaterial Object of Consumption. The requirement arises within the CDC, has a delimited scope and does not yield to secrecy or to technical complexity.

  9. Functional value equivalence

    Functional value equivalence is the criterion that judges whether the alteration of an Immaterial Object of Consumption preserved, in substantial terms, what justified the acquisition. The test rests on Article 51, item XIII, of the CDC3, reaches the appearance of an item when that is what was bought and leads to restoration.

  10. Abusive teleology and the unlawful act of architecture

    Abusive teleology is the category of unlawful act of architecture that makes it possible to impute to the supplier a program of exploitation built into the design of the service, when no clause or isolated episode is enough to show the injury. It operates through five cumulative requirements, separates aggressive business from exploitative purpose and enters the CDC through doors the law has already opened.

  11. The protected goods

    The protected goods are what the harm reaches when the platform lowers, sanctions or erases the position of enjoyment of an Immaterial Object of Consumption, even when formal access remains open, and they go by the names of personal integrity, symbolic identity and biographical continuity. They are defined by public criteria and are linked to civil personality, which is why losing a history or a title may go beyond losing an item.

  12. The constitutional order and the sectoral framework

    Platform governance is an exercise of private economic power over positions of continued enjoyment, and the jurist needs to know on what basis the legal order limits it without treating the company as the State or reducing the conflict to the contract. That basis comes from the economic Constitution, the horizontal effect of fundamental rights and the CDC, while the Legal Framework for Electronic Games and the Digital Statute of Children and Adolescents help bring the sector into view without resolving the conflict of enjoyment on their own.

  13. Intellectual property

    Intellectual property enters the regime of the Immaterial Object of Consumption as part of the infrastructure through which the platform defines what can be accessed, maintained, altered, withdrawn, audited and contested, and not as a topic parallel or superior to consumer protection. Exclusivity has come to organize competition and enjoyment, and its social function, read in light of Article 5, item XXIX, of the Federal Constitution4, makes it a field of reconciliation with the CDC.

  14. Proof

    In platform-governed consumption, the disputed fact arises and remains within the supplier's infrastructure, so whoever controls records, versions and traces of sanction also controls the conditions for reconstructing what happened. Semiotic vulnerability then turns into allegation, proof and claim, and the CDC and the Code of Civil Procedure (CPC) provide the tools to request the right record and allocate the burden.

  15. Remedies

    Remedies close the examination of a conflict over an Immaterial Object of Consumption, because they answer what to do with the injury once the object is qualified, the change reconstructed, the equivalence assessed and the proof organized. Primacy belongs to functional restoration and regime correction, and compensation comes after them, not in their place.

The law cited on this page

  1. Article 3, paragraph 1, of the CDC ↩

    “Art. 3. A supplier is any natural or legal person, public or private, national or foreign, as well as entities without legal personality, that carry out activities of production, assembly, creation, construction, transformation, import, export, distribution or commercialization of products or provision of services. § 1. A product is any good, movable or immovable, material or immaterial. § 2. A service is any activity supplied in the consumer market for remuneration, including those of a banking, financial, credit and insurance nature, except those arising from employment relationships.”

    PT“Art. 3° Fornecedor é toda pessoa física ou jurídica, pública ou privada, nacional ou estrangeira, bem como os entes despersonalizados, que desenvolvem atividade de produção, montagem, criação, construção, transformação, importação, exportação, distribuição ou comercialização de produtos ou prestação de serviços. § 1° Produto é qualquer bem, móvel ou imóvel, material ou imaterial. § 2° Serviço é qualquer atividade fornecida no mercado de consumo, mediante remuneração, inclusive as de natureza bancária, financeira, de crédito e securitária, salvo as decorrentes das relações de caráter trabalhista.”

    In the wording the thesis transcribes in note 12, p. 34.

  2. Article 4, item I, of the CDC ↩

    “Art. 4. The National Consumer Relations Policy aims at […] observing the following principles: I - recognition of the consumer's vulnerability in the consumer market; III - harmonization of the interests of participants in consumer relations […], always based on good faith and balance in relations between consumers and suppliers; VI - efficient restraint and repression of all abuses practiced in the consumer market […].”

    PT“Art. 4º. A Política Nacional das Relações de Consumo tem por objetivo […] atendidos os seguintes princípios: I - reconhecimento da vulnerabilidade do consumidor no mercado de consumo; III - harmonização dos interesses dos participantes das relações de consumo […], sempre com base na boa-fé e equilíbrio nas relações entre consumidores e fornecedores; VI - coibição e repressão eficientes de todos os abusos praticados no mercado de consumo […].”

    In the wording the thesis transcribes in note 499, p. 511.

  3. Article 51, item XIII, of the CDC ↩

    Art. 51. The following contractual clauses relating to the supply of products and services, among others, are null and void by operation of law:

    PTArt. 51. São nulas de pleno direito, entre outras, as cláusulas contratuais relativas ao fornecimento de produtos e serviços que: IV - estabeleçam obrigações consideradas iníquas, abusivas, que coloquem o consumidor em desvantagem exagerada, ou sejam incompatíveis com a boa-fé ou a eqüidade; XIII - autorizem o fornecedor a modificar unilateralmente o conteúdo ou a qualidade do contrato, após sua celebração;

    IV - those that establish obligations considered iniquitous, abusive, that place the consumer at an exaggerated disadvantage, or are incompatible with good faith or equity;

    XIII - those that authorize the supplier to unilaterally modify the content or quality of the contract after its conclusion;

    Text in force copied from the Planalto website on 27/10/2026. Later addition, outside the thesis.

  4. Article 5, item XXIX, of the Federal Constitution ↩

    “Art. 5. [...] XXIX - the law shall ensure to the authors of industrial inventions a temporary privilege for their use, as well as protection for industrial creations, the ownership of trademarks, company names and other distinctive signs, in view of the social interest and the technological and economic development of the Country [...].”

    PT“Art. 5º. [...] XXIX - a lei assegurará aos autores de inventos industriais privilégio temporário para sua utilização, bem como proteção às criações industriais, à propriedade das marcas, aos nomes de empresas e a outros signos distintivos, tendo em vista o interesse social e o desenvolvimento tecnológico e econômico do País [...].”

    In the wording the thesis transcribes in note 790, p. 694.
