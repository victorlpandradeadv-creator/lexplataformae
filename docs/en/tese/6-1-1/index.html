6.1.1 On the constitutional matrix: consumer protection under the Brazilian economic order

See this section in the PDF

p. 504

Consumer protection does not enter the Brazilian constitutional order as a moralizing appendix to the market, but as one of its internal conditions of legitimacy. The Constitution inscribes it, on one side, on the plane of fundamental rights473 and, on the other, in the principled core of the economic order474, which prevents it from being treated as lateral protection, available at the supplier's convenience or subordinated to the private grammar of contract. Economic activity, in the 1988 model, is not organized as a space of unrestricted patrimonial sovereignty, because free enterprise appears from the republican foundation bound to the dignity of the human person and to the social values that shape it475. For this reason consumer protection acts, from within, as a criterion for reading economic rationality p. 505 itself, and not as an exotic brake imposed from outside (Grau, 2010, p. 253476; Miragem, 2020, p. 977477; Silva, 2005, p. 262-263478).

This arrangement rules out from the start the caricature according to which free enterprise would mean a broad license to design markets, contracts and private infrastructures immune to material control. The Brazilian economic order aims to ensure everyone a dignified existence in accordance with the dictates of social justice, articulates economic freedom with containment of structural abuses479 and is oriented by material purposes incompatible with the naturalization of opaque private architectures. The constitutional text does not place, on one side, economic freedom, and, on the other, heterogeneous social limits; it stitches these elements together within the same normative matrix, so that social function, social justice, consumer protection and repression of abuse are not accidents of the system, but its skeleton Grau(Grau, 2010, p. 213; p. 252)480. Free p. 506 enterprise, in this framework, is not a metaphysical right of capital nor a screen for corporate sovereignty, but a category functionally conditioned by constitutional purposes and by the protection of the person Grau(Grau, 2010, p. 204; p. 208-209)481.

This reading becomes even clearer when one observes that the Republic itself is oriented toward building a free, just and solidary society and toward reducing inequalities482. There is, therefore, no constitutional neutrality in favor of private arrangements that deepen structural dependencies under the pretext of efficiency, innovation or sectoral dynamism. In platform-governed markets, in which access, permanence, visibility and contestation of enjoyment come to be administered by technical-organizational design, the containment of private power ceases to be a peripheral theme and moves to the center of the problem. The Brazilian Constitution does not idolize private power for the simple fact that it is not state power; on the contrary, it provides express language to control it when it takes a structurally asymmetrical form, especially because consumer protection shapes the legitimate functioning of economic activity and does not depend on the supplier's private tolerance Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 37)483.

p. 507 For this reason too, property and exclusivity do not appear as absolute shields. Property is subject to social function484, and this functionalization does not act as a peripheral correction after the recognition of the right, but as an internal element of its very legitimacy. Ownership, the enterprise and economic exploitation only remain constitutionally intelligible when compatible with social justice, human dignity and protection of the vulnerable party Silva(Silva, 2005, p. 283; p. 813)485. The same logic projects itself onto the economic order itself, which does not embrace a self-referential market, but a model tensioned by demands of humanization, social limit and consumer protection Silva(Silva, 2005, p. 195-196, p. 790)486. In this key, the containment of patrimonial power and the subordination of property to socially justifiable purposes are not recent extravagances, but part of a broader historical process of refusal of the private absolutization of power Comparato(Comparato, 2015, p. 70; p. 79; p. 193)487.

p. 508 On the infraconstitutional plane, this matrix finds direct concretization in the Consumer Protection Code, whose incidence does not depend on gracious recognition by the supplier. The CDC establishes consumer protection and defense norms of public order and social interest488, precisely in execution of the constitutional commands that make consumer protection a fundamental right and a principle of the economic order. There is no room, then, for the fantasy that terms of use, digital licenses or predisposed contractual architectures can select when consumerist public order will come onto the scene. It already enters the relationship before the clause, because its foundation is not private will, but the Constitution itself Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 72])489. The mandatory nature of this regime is unequivocal: its norms cannot be set aside by individual will and cannot be neutralized by contractual engineering designed to escape protective coverage (Marques, p. 509 2016, p. 678; p. 804)490; Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 92; p. 115; p. 620])491; Filomeno(Filomeno, 2011, p. 16; p. 20-21)492.

Hence consumerist public order cannot be treated as a negotiable legislative detail. It blocks both the global exclusion of the microsystem and its point sabotages, including through exoneration clauses, waiver of rights, unilateral cancellation, unilateral modification of contractual content and excessive advantages493. In a platform environment, this point is especially sensitive, because the precarization of the bond is usually recycled as the user's “acceptance” of private regimes of mutability, opacity and sanction. The consumer system, however, does not admit that structural vulnerability be disguised as consent sufficient to derogate legal protection. Where the duty arises from law, the adhesion of the p. 510 consumer is not enough to empty it Denari · Benjamin +1(Denari, 2011, p. 238; Benjamin, 2011, p. 494; Nery Jr., 2011, p. 577)494. The incidence of the CDC, moreover, makes up a true public order of protection founded on the consumer's vulnerability, which is why the rights it recognizes and the duties it imposes cannot be validly suppressed, waived or limited by predisposed negotiating arrangements Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 39; p. 380)495.

This mandatory incidence is not exhausted in the isolated literal text of the Code itself. The consumer microsystem is functionally open and operates in coordination with other normative sources bearing on the same consumer relationship496. Its openness does not serve to relativize consumer protection, but to reinforce it, allowing constitutional, civil, informational and regulatory protection to be articulated in favor of the vulnerable party, and not as a privatist escape route Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 681; p. 1120-1121)497. This same openness prevents isolationist readings of the CDC and allows it to be understood as a functional and open code, aimed at the maximum protection of the consumer, and not as an island surrounded by contract on all p. 511 sides. The contractual interpretation most favorable to the consumer498 already announces, from the outset, that unilaterally drafted ambiguities cannot convert imposed precariousness into legal normality.

The constitutional centrality of the theme also appears clearly when one recalls that the National Consumer Relations Policy expressly recognizes the consumer's vulnerability, harmonization founded on good faith and balance, and the efficient curbing of abuses499. The consumer's basic rights, in turn, include adequate and clear information, protection against misleading and abusive advertising, prevention and reparation of damages, and facilitation of the defense of their rights500. These provisions already show that consumer protection, in the constitutional order concretized by the CDC, is not limited to protecting the pocket against ostensive quality defects of a stable thing; it bears on risk, opacity, deception, evidentiary asymmetry and unfair methods of organizing the market. On platforms, this means that information, verifiability, conditions of permanence and possibilities of contestation cannot be relegated to the status of technical favors administered by the very party that concentrates the design of the environment.

For this reason, the constitutional matrix affirmed here does not authorize the regime of the Immaterial Object of Consumption to be treated as an extravagant construction or a theoretical deviation in search of a problem of its own. What it requires is precisely the opposite: that dogmatics be capable of naming, precisely, the contemporary forms of exercise of private power that bear on enjoyment. When the consumed utility depends on continuous governance, unilateral reconfiguration, technical opacity and asymmetrical distribution of the means of proof, the law is not facing an extra-systemic gray zone, but a constitutionally qualified case of containment of structural asymmetries. Equality before the law and the protection of liberty, security and property501 do not coexist with private regimes of arbitrary exclusion p. 512 disguised as contractual autonomy. Where the platform administers, at scale, access, visibility, sanction and permanence, there is private power too relevant to be treated as a simple operational detail.

Free enterprise, innovation and technological ownership do not operate as safe-conducts for unauditability, shielded irresponsibility or contractual immunization of the supplier. The Brazilian economic Constitution does not accommodate small private fiefdoms of unrestricted normative power. The constitutionally legitimate market is one crossed by dignity, social function, repression of abuse and consumer protection, and not one that tries to convert private architecture into jurisdiction without control. It is from this matrix that the dogmatic possibility derives of reconstructing, further on, platform governance as a legally controllable phenomenon and of reading the IOC not as an uncomfortable exception to the system, but as one of its hardest contemporary tests.

  4. Consumer protection, in the 1988 constitutional text, integrates the very normative structure of the economic order, as an imperative constitutional principle and a guideline shaping the functioning of economic activity. In this condition, its presence does not interrupt the constitutional logic of the market; on the contrary, it participates in the very definition of the legitimate mode of its organization, both as an instrument aimed at realizing a dignified existence and as a specific constitutional objective to be promoted even through public policies Grau(Grau, 2010, p. 253). The internal position of consumer protection becomes even more evident when it is recognized that its incidence is not limited to pathological episodes or extreme abuses, but structurally affects the exercise of economic activity in the broad sense Grau(Grau, 2010, p. 253-254). For this reason, consumer protection cannot be treated as a heterogeneous obstacle to economic rationality nor as a corrective concession after the contract. It shapes, from within, the very constitutional intelligibility of the market. Free enterprise, in the 1988 constitutional arrangement, is not confused with unrestricted contractual sovereignty nor with private freedom to design platform regimes immune to control. It is born legally crossed by demands of social justice and containment of abuses, among which consumer protection occupies a structural place. ↩
  5. The notion of product in the CDC projects itself functionally onto the utility entrusted to and legitimately expected by the consumer, anchored in the protection of trust and in the supplier's general duty to offer objects adequate to the purposes intended for them Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 523]). In products dependent on continuous services, the terms of the offer become the main criterion for assessing legitimate expectations, so that the fulfillment of the duty to inform measures the expectation of use, including for purposes of liability for quality defects Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 87]). The immaterial product provides a basis for protection to be organized from the functional correspondence between the promised utility and the enjoyment delivered, and not from a static nominal or material existence. ↩
  6. Consumer protection expressly integrates the Brazilian constitutional arrangement, both on the plane of fundamental rights and on that of the economic order. This suffices to rule out the reading according to which consumer protection would be a lateral, moralizing or merely legislative correction of occasional excesses of the market. In the constitutional system, the consumer appears as the holder of a legally qualified position, and its protection appears as a structuring principle of the economic order, which prevents treating it as a matter available at the convenience of private agents Silva(Silva, 2005, p. 262-263). The incidence of the protective regime does not depend on the supplier's contractual tolerance, because the constitutional text itself already inserts it into the normative core of economic life. This insertion responds to the recognition that consumer society and contemporary commercial traffic produce their own asymmetries, requiring adaptation of constitutional technique to this state of affairs Silva(Silva, 2005, p. 263). The containment of private power, in the field of consumption, emerges as an internal development of the economic Constitution itself. ↩

[...] Paragraph 4 The law shall repress the abuse of economic power that aims at the domination of markets, the elimination of competition and the arbitrary increase of profits” Brasil(Brasil, [2025]a). ↩
  8. The Brazilian constitutional economic order was not designed as a space of opposition between economic freedom, on one side, and social protection, on the other. Its normative meaning depends precisely on the articulated coexistence of free enterprise, free competition, social function of property, consumer protection and repression of the abuse of economic power, principles that shape one another within the same constitutional matrix Grau(Grau, 2010, p. 213). The exercise of economic activity only remains constitutionally legitimate when read within this integrated web, and not as self-sufficient private power. This same integration projects itself onto property and its social function. The simultaneous inclusion of both among the principles of the economic order does not mean mere rhetorical coexistence, but true subordination of patrimonial exercise to the dictates of social justice and to the objective of ensuring a dignified existence to all Grau(Grau, 2010, p. 252). For this reason, economic practices incompatible with the valorization of human labor, with social justice or with the general dignity aimed at by art. 170 do not represent merely debatable business choices; they become adverse to the constitutional order itself Grau(Grau, 2010, p. 197). Social justice, social function and consumer protection are part of the constitutional DNA of the market itself. ↩
  9. Free enterprise, in the constitutionalism of 1988, cannot be reduced to the old fantasy of a broad license for the private exercise of power. Its inscription as a foundation of the Republic and as a principle of the economic order does not authorize treating it as a synonym of corporate sovereignty, much less as armor against legal control of the mode of contractual organization of the market. The corresponding constitutional notion is not exhausted in economic freedom in the classical liberal sense, nor can it be read as a simple ideological affirmation of capitalism Grau(Grau, 2010, p. 204). This containment becomes even clearer when one observes that the Constitution enshrines the social value of free enterprise, and not its individualistic virtualities, besides placing it, in art. 170, beside human labor in a structure purposively oriented to the valorization of the latter Grau(Grau, 2010, p. 208). For this reason too, free enterprise is not taken as a fundamental right of the capitalist, nor as a metaphysical attribute of capital, but as a category functionally linked to business activity in a constitutionally shaped context Grau(Grau, 2010, p. 209). In the Brazilian constitutional design, free enterprise only remains intelligible when coexisting with consumer protection, social function, social justice and repression of the abuse of economic power. ↩
  10. “Art. 3. The fundamental objectives of the Federative Republic of Brazil are: I - to build a free, just and solidary society; [...] III - to eradicate poverty and marginalization and to reduce social and regional inequalities” Brasil(Brasil, [2025]a). ↩
  11. The incidence of the CDC is not subject to the private disposition of the parties, because its discipline makes up a true microsystem of public order and social interest founded on the consumer's vulnerability. The qualification of the consumer law as a norm of public order translates a public order of protection, endowed with a preferential and, above all, imperative character. To the extent that it realizes the content of a fundamental right of constitutional origin, it removes from the sphere of private autonomy the possibility of derogating it Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 39). This unavailability reaches the very structure of the consumer relationship. The rights recognized to the consumer and the duties imposed on the supplier cannot be validly suppressed, waived, limited or set aside by contract, terms of use, license or any other predisposed negotiating arrangement Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 380). Thus, the consumer's vulnerability cannot be recycled into an appearance of consent to legitimize the emptying of legal protection. Where there is a consumer relationship, consumerist public order has already entered before any contractual clause. ↩
  12. “XXIII - property shall fulfill its social function” Brasil(Brasil, [2025]a). ↩
  13. Social function acts, in the Brazilian constitutional system, as an internal element shaping private legal positions, and not as a peripheral or exceptional limitation. This means that property, enterprise and private initiative are not embraced as autonomous spheres of selfish exercise of power, but as positions whose legitimacy depends on compatibility with social justice and protection of the person. Social function, in this key, bears on the very content of the right, on its recognition and on its guarantee, introducing into the internal structure of the legal position an interest that is not necessarily confused with the isolated interest of the holder Silva(Silva, 2005, p. 283). The containment of private power should not be thought of as an external aggression against economic freedom, but as a requirement of the very constitutional mode of its existence. This reading is articulated with the centrality of the dignity of the human person in the system. The constitutional economic order is tensioned by the Constitution toward its civilization, precisely because enterprises are subordinated to the realization of a social order committed to social justice and human dignity Silva(Silva, 2005, p. 813). Dignity of the human person, social function, economic order and consumer protection belong to the same normative arrangement. ↩
  14. The economic order of the 1988 Constitution is not organized under a logic of absolute economic freedom, because it is expressly subject to the dictates of social justice and to the purpose of ensuring everyone a dignified existence. The presence, among its principles, of consumer protection, the reduction of inequalities, the protection of the environment and the pursuit of full employment shows that the constitutional text does not legitimize a self-referential market, but an economic model tensioned by demands of humanization and social limit Silva(Silva, 2005, p. 790). The consequence is decisive, since free enterprise cannot be read as unrestricted contractual sovereignty, nor as a safe-conduct for the private exercise of power detached from material control. This same logic appears in the constitutional relativization of private property. Its inclusion among the principles of the economic order does not preserve an untouched concept of individual ownership, but reconstructs it under the requirement of social function and under submission to the dictates of social justice Silva(Silva, 2005, p. 195-196). Business activity and private initiative are recognized only insofar as they are compatible with social function, dignity of the human person and consumer protection. ↩
  15. The constitutional containment of private economic power and the subordination of the exercise of property to socially justifiable purposes do not constitute an isolated innovation of recent Brazilian constitutionalism, but an expression of a broader historical process of affirmation of the centrality of the human person against patrimonially absolutized forms of power. In this key, the evolution of human rights is not exhausted in negative freedoms opposable to the State, since the very development of economic, social and cultural rights led to the recognition that, besides the Public Powers, individuals and legal entities of private law also have the duty to respect human rights of any kind Comparato(Comparato, 2015, p. 70). The incidence of constitutional limits on private centers of power, therefore, does not represent an improvised anti-liberal eccentricity, but a development of the broadening of the normative horizon of human rights. This trajectory is also revealed in the progressive social functionalization of property. In various contemporary legal systems, the attribution of a social function to private property appears founded on solidarity, so as to rule out the conception of purely self-centered ownership Comparato(Comparato, 2015, p. 79). In the same direction, the reaction of social constitutionalism to the economic exploitation of the human person was also structured against the abusive use of contractual freedom as an alibi for domination. The Mexican Constitution of 1917 appears as a landmark of the modern Social State of Law by delegitimizing practices of human exploitation previously shielded by an absolutized reading of private autonomy Comparato(Comparato, 2015, p. 193). The Brazilian constitutional economic order thus becomes more intelligible when read as part of this larger process of subordination of property, enterprise and private powers to the protection of the person and to the social interest. ↩
  16. “This code establishes norms for the protection and defense of the consumer, of public order and social interest, under the terms of arts. 5, item XXXII, 170, item V, of the Federal Constitution and art. 48 of its Transitional Provisions.” Brasil(Brasil, [2022]). ↩
  17. Consumer protection has sufficient constitutional density to prevent contract, license or private regulation from being treated as small sovereign spaces immune to the protective system. The CDC does not hover as peripheral legislation of private convenience, but as an infraconstitutional concretization of an express constitutional command. The 1988 Constitution is the origin of the protective codification of the consumer in Brazil, so that the CDC receives a true constitutional guarantee, with superior hierarchy and a character of public order, inserting consumer protection among the structuring values of Brazilian private law, beside — and in reciprocal containment of — free enterprise and private autonomy Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 72]). This reading is reinforced when the work characterizes the CDC as a law of social function, a law of economic public order and a law of “clearly constitutional” origin, precisely to explain why its norms, although they bear on private relationships, are unavailable and cannot be set aside by contract Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 115]). If the source of protection is constitutional, there is no platform license that can make it evaporate. ↩
  18. The incidence of the Consumer Protection Code on consumer relationships does not depend on acceptance by the supplier, on express provision in the negotiating instrument nor on compatibility voluntarily recognized by the platform's private architecture. It is a mandatory regime, structured as a microsystem of public order and social interest, whose application cannot be set aside by a license of use, by predisposed clauses nor by contractual modeling oriented to escape consumer protection. It is in this key that Marques states that "the Consumer Protection Code is clear, in its art. 1 in providing that its norms are directed to the priority protection of a social group, consumers, and that they constitute norms of public order, which cannot, therefore, be set aside by individual will. They are norms of social interest" Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 678). This mandatory nature prevents isolationist readings of the contractual bond, in which the supplier intends to enclose the relationship within the limits of the text drafted by the supplier itself. In a convergent sense, the author records that state intervention in consumer relationships was carried out through the imposition of "imperative norms", which limit the space previously reserved to the autonomy of will, create new rights for the consumer and protect expectations arising from trust in the contractual bond Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 804). Consequently, contract, license or private platform design do not function as an alibi to neutralize the public order of protection. ↩
  19. The incidence of the CDC cannot be set aside by contractual clause, license of use or privatist modeling of the bond, because the consumer microsystem is composed of norms of public order and social interest, unavailable to the will of the parties. This mandatory nature prevents private arrangements from functioning as a filter of entry into or exclusion from legal protection. It is generally understood that “all its norms are of public order” and, for this reason, “unavailable to the will of the parties, even by contract”, being equally “of social interest” Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 92]). The consequence is not merely symbolic: the Code is directed to the priority protection of the consumer and its norms, being of public order, “cannot be set aside […] by individual will”, and may even find application ex officio Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 115]). The supplier may write whatever contract it wishes; what it cannot do is write consumerist public order out of it. ↩
  20. Thus, the application of the CDC does not depend on the will of the parties nor can be set aside by predisposed clause, terms of use, license or equivalent private arrangement. Art. 1 of the CDC qualifies its norms as of public order and social interest, which gives them a mandatory nature not subject to derogation by the will of those concerned Filomeno(Filomeno, 2011, p. 16). The platform does not choose whether or not the consumer microsystem enters the relationship, because it already enters it by force of law. Mandatoriness is linked to the purpose of rescuing the collectivity of consumers from a structural position of fragility before economic power, ensuring them effective instruments of protection and access to justice Filomeno(Filomeno, 2011, p. 20-21). That is, the incidence of the CDC integrates a broader movement of limitation of private autonomy whenever its exercise threatens to reproduce material imbalances incompatible with the protection of the vulnerable party. ↩

Paragraph 1 An advantage is presumed excessive, among other cases, when it: I - offends the fundamental principles of the legal system to which it belongs; II - restricts fundamental rights or obligations inherent to the nature of the contract [...]; III - proves excessively onerous for the consumer [...].” Brasil(Brasil, [2022]). ↩
  22. The public-order nature of the CDC prevents not only the global exclusion of its incidence, but also the point neutralization of its effects by clauses of exoneration, attenuation of liability or waiver of the consumer's rights. The protective regime being mandatory, no validity is lent to conventions that seek to empty the supplier's legal duties or to reduce, by contractual means, the intensity of the protection ensured to the consumer Denari(Denari, 2011, p. 238). Where the duty arises from law, the consumer's adhesion is not enough to derogate legal protection. In consumer matters, individual will does not operate as a safe-conduct for the suppression of protective public order, especially when this would imply a breach of the material balance of the relationship Benjamin · Jr.(Benjamin, 2011, p. 494; Nery Jr., 2011, p. 577). Terms of use and licenses cannot recycle vulnerability into an appearance of valid consent against the protection system itself. ↩
  23. The relevant downgrading of enjoyment can be framed without difficulty within the dogmatic doors of the CDC relating to quality defects and to inadequacy of performance. The quality defect of the product or service stems from the absence of properties or characteristics that enable the performance to meet the purposes legitimately expected by the consumer, and may result even from the breach of an accessory duty, provided that this compromises, even if partially, the utility of the product or service Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 523). An absolute collapse of the performance is not necessary for the system to react. This structure is reinforced when the frustration of legitimately expected purposes, including presumable and reasonably expected utility, is treated as the axis of the quality defect, especially in services provided in disconformity with due time, place or manner, whether by reason of the offer or of advertising Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 524). The framing of the functional downgrading of enjoyment does not require a parallel category, since the CDC already offers the paths of frustration of useful interest and impairment of utility. ↩

  25. Nor is the incidence of the consumer-law microsystem exhausted in the isolated text of the CDC itself. Its normative structure is open to coordination with other protective sources, because it is not a closed and self-sufficient code, but a system functionally oriented toward the maximum protection of the consumer. This openness is particularly important in complex contexts, such as those of platforms and immaterial objects of consumption, in which adequate protection depends on an articulated reading of constitutional, civil, informational, contractual and regulatory norms. Marques addresses this point by stating that the CDC must be understood as "a subjectively specific codification, a partial codification, a functional and open codification", adding that, by virtue of its art. 7, it "lets itself be permeated by any other law protective of the consumer" Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 681). The function of this openness, however, is not to relativize consumer-law protection, but to reinforce it. That is why the author observes that, in the CDC system, art. 7 acts as an "updating interface", integrating into the microsystem the rights ensured by external legislation, "but not the limits to those rights" Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 1120-1121). Coordination among sources is thus carried out in favor of the protected vulnerability, and not as a privatist escape route. ↩
