Situation g) · 7.1.2 of the thesis

When punishing is easy and contesting is hard

g) governance by friction, sanction and asymmetric reversibility

Which facts show an opaque sanction or asymmetric reversal?

Access, permanence, visibility or contestation conditioned by opaque sanctions, selective friction, excessive costs of reaction or asymmetric reversal. Typical examples are bans, chat restrictions, automated penalties, loss of eligibility for rewards and contestation flows that lead nowhere.

Which records of the sanction should be requested?

Sanction records, the contestation flow, the criteria invoked, structural difficulties of reversal and the concrete impacts of the measure on the continuity of the experience. Disclosure may reach the category and reason for the sanction applied.

Which rules control a sanction without a reason?

Article 39 of the CDC1 prohibits abusive practices, including leaving performance of the obligation to the exclusive discretion of the supplier. The barrier clause, which hinders the exercise of a claim without plausible justification, runs counter to Article 51, item IV, of the CDC2. Depending on the case, there is a defect in the service when the legal and procedural security of enjoyment is compromised. Consumer protection, as a fundamental right and a principle of the economic order, is already enough to frame private forms of government that produce broad and repeated effects on enjoyment, even though they are not to be confused with state power.

Is the existence of the power to sanction enough?

The platform has the power to sanction, but the mere formal existence of this power is not enough. A sanction with material impact requires verifiable reasons, decisional coherence and intelligible avenues of contestation. The question is not only whether the user broke the rule, but also whether the criteria by which the deviation is produced, read and punished are legitimate.

What does the case of the irregular gifts show?

In the case of the irregular gifts, the platform reacted to payment fraud by disabling the official gifting feature on the Brazilian server for a long period, removing assets received and applying blocks and regressive restorations of accounts, including on recipients who did not knowingly take part in the fraud. The case makes it possible to formulate five allegations. The first is inadequacy of the service due to a security failure in the transactional environment, with redistribution of the risk to the consumer. The second is violation of legitimate trust, because the gifting feature was an official channel. The third is abusiveness of the private response, through an expanded presumption of bad faith and lack of individualization of conduct. The fourth is the ineffectiveness, in the concrete case, of the clauses used to make enjoyment precarious and deny restoration. The fifth is evidentiary asymmetry, which requires preserving trails, transaction histories, the origin of the removed items and the rollback criteria. The claims range from restoration of the assets, when good faith has not been rebutted by individualized proof, to disclosure of the records and correction of the information about legitimate acquisition channels.

The law cited on this page

  1. art. 39 of the CDC ↩

    “Art. 39. The supplier of products or services is prohibited from, among other abusive practices: IV - taking advantage of the weakness or ignorance of the consumer […]; V - demanding a manifestly excessive advantage from the consumer; XII - failing to stipulate a deadline for the performance of its obligation or leaving the setting of its starting date to its exclusive discretion.”

    PT“Art. 39. É vedado ao fornecedor de produtos ou serviços, dentre outras práticas abusivas: IV - prevalecer-se da fraqueza ou ignorância do consumidor […]; V - exigir do consumidor vantagem manifestamente excessiva; XII - deixar de estipular prazo para o cumprimento de sua obrigação ou deixar a fixação de seu termo inicial a seu exclusivo critério.”

    Wording transcribed in note 527, p. 520.

  2. art. 51, IV, of the CDC ↩

    Art. 51. The following contractual clauses relating to the supply of products and services, among others, are null and void by operation of law:

    PTArt. 51. São nulas de pleno direito, entre outras, as cláusulas contratuais relativas ao fornecimento de produtos e serviços que: IV - estabeleçam obrigações consideradas iníquas, abusivas, que coloquem o consumidor em desvantagem exagerada, ou sejam incompatíveis com a boa-fé ou a eqüidade; XIII - autorizem o fornecedor a modificar unilateralmente o conteúdo ou a qualidade do contrato, após sua celebração;

    IV - those that establish obligations considered iniquitous, abusive, that place the consumer at an exaggerated disadvantage, or are incompatible with good faith or equity;
