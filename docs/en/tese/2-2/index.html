2.2 On the theoretical-normative and sectoral scope

See this section in the PDF

p. 54

The theoretical-normative scope is defined to make legally cognizable conflicts proper to continuous digital services, in which enjoyment is governed by a platform and the value of what is acquired depends on private rules that operate through clauses, interface architecture and sanction practices.

The normative basis mobilized is the constitutional order of consumer protection and of the economic order, which imposes limits on the exploitation of asymmetries, on structural opacity and on forms of exercise of private power capable of reorganizing access, information and risk. On this axis, the Consumer Protection Code is preserved as the central matrix of protection, while the legislation on intellectual property rights is mobilized with a functional status, as the legal grammar of access control and licensing that, in digital markets, structures the very form of circulation and enjoyment of utilities. Licenses and technical restrictions are treated not only as contractual content, but as legal-technological infrastructure that delimits what can be used, for how long, under what conditions and under which unilateral powers of modulation, without this operating as a generic authorization to degrade promises of adequacy, comparability and restoration in consumption.

In the specific regime of software, this temporality is not only a technological fact, it is an express legal category and functions as a relevant normative boundary for the analysis of p. 55 continuous bonds. Articles 7 and 8 of Law No. 9,609/1998 articulate a duty of information about technical validity with a duty of complementary technical support for adequate functioning during that period, including upon withdrawal from circulation, which prevents treating “update”, “end of support” and “maintenance” as neutralities beyond the reach of the protection of adequacy and transparency. These provisions operate, in this scope, as points of dogmatic control for separating operational variations compatible with supply from relevant reconfigurations of the content of the performance.

In a dogmatic key, the scope recognizes that platform governance acts as continuous execution of the bond, insofar as internal sanctions, blocks, account restrictions, degradations and recalibrations of access function as forms of performance, non-performance and functional coercion incorporated into the very experience of use. The conflict ceases to gravitate around a one-off delivery and comes to be reconstructed as a dispute over continuity, functional stability and the effective content of the performance in objects administered over time, under private powers exercised at the contractual, technical and organizational levels.

At the sectoral level, interactive entertainment is adopted as a privileged field of observation because it concentrates mechanisms of permanence, artificial scarcity, reputation, continuous administration of identity and positional modulation, making visible frictions that continuous digital services produce on traditional categories of adequacy and restoration. In this context, the Legal Framework for Electronic Games is treated as an institutional reference of minimum framing for the sector, useful for situating categories, concerns and regulatory language associated with the economic and cultural recognition of the activity, without converting the path into a legislative inventory and without shifting the decisional core to the current state of the legislative effort toward its regulation.

The scope, therefore, does not have the purpose of proposing a new microsystem nor of exhausting the sectoral discipline. Its objective is to demarcate the normative boundaries within which the dogmatic reconstruction will operate, preserving the constitutional-consumer axis as the matrix of protection and mobilizing intellectual property and the regulation pertinent to the segment analyzed as structural keys for understanding enclosure, circulation and modulation of access in digital markets.

An ecosystem of game as a service and economy of virtual goods is adopted because it is a methodologically fertile environment for observing the formation of value, private governance, conflicts of enjoyment and disputes of meaning, without this turning into autonomous empirical description. The characterization of the ecosystem is distributed throughout the path, through minimal p. 56 references and illustrative vignettes, mobilized only when necessary to make the mechanisms discussed intelligible and to anchor the application of the legal categories, without any claim to exhaustive demonstration.
