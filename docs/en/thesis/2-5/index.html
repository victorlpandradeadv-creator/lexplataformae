2.5 On the procedures for reading and analyzing contractual terms

See this section in the PDF

This section in the layers of the site

  • Method · The protocol

p. 70

The institutional reading of the contract, in this work, starts from the recognition that terms of use and internal policies do not operate as static instruments for declaring intent, but as parts of a governance apparatus that integrates rule, implementation and continuous enforcement. In platform-governed ecosystems, the relevant promise is not exhausted in the contractual text, because it is reiterated and recalibrated by interface, reward routines, scarcity regimes and private sanctions. Even so, the contract remains legally central, since it is there that licenses, delimitations of access, unilateral powers and modulation clauses that sustain the enclosure and administration of the relationship are articulated. The procedure adopted consists of articulating the formal instruments with evidence of institutional functioning in order to bring the case back to the categories of the regime of the Immaterial Object of Consumption and make it legally decidable.

With the aim of making explicit how the materials consulted are organized and read to anchor the dogmatic reconstruction developed throughout the thesis, it is stated at the outset that the purpose is neither to describe the ecosystem as a factual narrative, nor to constitute an autonomous demonstrative corpus p. 71. The aim is to stabilize a controllable inferential path that makes it possible to qualify promise, delivery, loss and restoration in continuous digital services whose enjoyment depends on platform governance. Within this frame, the reference to the case operates as a locus of exemplification, used sparingly and with minimum traceability when necessary, to make relevant mechanisms visible without the field material becoming a substitute for the dogmatic foundation.

The contractual analysis takes as its reference, as a rule, the versions in force and accessible during the period in which the writing was closed (March 2026), with specific consideration of past changes only when there is a verifiable public record and when this is necessary to qualify a legally relevant modulation of the relationship.

The analysis articulates three planes as dimensions of the same phenomenon, without any claim to describe the case for its own sake or to validate it by corpus. First, an internal documentary-normative plane, composed of terms of use, policies and official support documents, in which one observes how the platform qualifies the object, defines licenses and enclosures, reserves unilateral powers and presents justifications and promises of compensation. Second, a technical-semiological plane, in which one observes how interface, metrics, visibility regimes and reward systems convert signs into value and into everyday discipline, producing operational dependencies and expectations. Third, a public experiential plane, derived from open statements, in which one observes recurring patterns of friction, perception of loss and invocation of institutional promises and procedures. These planes function as a bridge of intelligibility to anchor, in a verifiable manner, the dogmatic reconstruction.

The articulation between these planes is conducted by categories previously stabilized in the theoretical-normative scope, to prevent the material from being read as a collection of isolated cases or as loose rhetorical illustration. Public statements, observation of interfaces and marketing materials are read in light of semiotic vulnerability and of mechanisms of anticipation and steering by signs, so as to make intelligible how belonging, reputation, time and recognition form part of the promise and value of what is acquired. Contractual documents and judicial decisions are read in light of the qualification of the IOC as an immaterial product in a consumer relationship, of abusive teleology as a criterion for controlling architecture and governance, and of the parameters of open auditability and functional value equivalence as keys for dealing with opacity and mutability without neutralizing protection. The function of this procedure is to discipline the use of references, ensuring that they serve the intelligibility and contestability of the argument, without claiming for themselves the status of exhaustive empirical demonstration.

p. 72 The reference to interpretation according to contractual complexity is mobilized here as a methodological instrument for reading terms of use, internal policies and related documents as an institutional technology for governing consumption, and not as a neutral expression of private autonomy. In continuous digital services, complexity is not merely textual length. It is an indication of the distribution of normativity across layers, with reservations of power, sanction patterns and restoration mechanisms that come to form part of the very infrastructure of what is acquired. Within this frame, interpreting the contract means reconstructing the regime it organizes, including when practical performance takes place through interface, support routines and unilateral decisions applied over time.

For this reason, an interpretive technique is adopted that is compatible with highly complex contracts and with asymmetric supply contexts, in which the form of the contract and the institutional environment in which it operates are relevant elements for reconstructing essential content, legitimate expectations and typical zones of abusiveness. Complexity, here, is treated neither as a moral defect of the contract nor as a virtue in itself. It is treated as a legal sign of a supply regime that concentrates decision-making power, disperses information, makes comparability difficult and shifts the conflict to the continuous performance of the relationship.

Within this frame, the reference to Posner, Eggleston and Zeckhauser is strictly instrumental. Their perspective authorizes treating the form of the contract as an indication of the institutional environment in which it operates interpretive questions about essential content, legitimate expectations and typical zones of abusiveness in standardized and unilaterally structured contracts Zeckhauser(Posner; Eggleston; Zeckhauser, 2000, p. 92). This interpretive key serves to prevent terms of use from being interpreted as self-sufficient text and to justify reconstructing the regime on the basis of evidence of how the service functions, without replacing the dogmatic parameters of the CDC.

With the adoption of this perspective, contractual complexity functions as a roadmap for qualifying the relationship, guiding the identification of zones of informational asymmetry, of possible structural advantages and of external factors relevant to the practical functioning of the contract, above all when the logic of use of these instruments is unilaterally established by hegemonic actors Zeckhauser(Posner; Eggleston; Zeckhauser, 2000, p. 92). This key does not replace the dogmatic parameters of the Consumer Protection Code, but acts as a methodological bridge that authorizes describing the phenomenon with sufficient rigor to support the theoretical reconstruction and the dogmatic conversion of the regime.

p. 73 This caveat does not alter the control parameter, which remains dogmatic and protective, but describes a practical condition of reading in contracts that function as a governance interface and not as a complete photograph of the relationship.

Converging with this caution, Macaulay indicates that contracts, in various situations, operate less as an ordinary mechanism for resolving conflicts and more as a tool for self-planning, whose activation tends to occur as an ultima ratio Macaulay(Macaulay, 1963, p. 55). For interpretive purposes, this recommends favoring the factual situation and the conflict actually manifested, rather than presupposing that the text fully describes the relationship and its concrete expectations Macaulay(Macaulay, 1963, p. 61).

In the context of continuous digital services, this caveat reinforces the treatment of terms of use as part of a broader institutional regime, in which the practical performance of the relationship takes place through interface, support and sanction routines, requiring a reading connected to evidence of how the service functions. The orientation preserves affinity with the values of objective good faith and with the protective teleology of the Consumer Protection Code, without shifting the axis of legal control to an abstract trust in the contractual text Macaulay(Macaulay, 1963, p. 55; p. 61).
