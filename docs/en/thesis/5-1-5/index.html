5.1.5 On open auditability and functional value equivalence as criteria of legitimacy and of restoration in continuous digital services

p. 469

Platform-governed enjoyment already makes it possible to keep the immaterial object of consumption intelligible in the vocabulary of the CDC without reducing supply to the inaugural instant or dissolving what was acquired into undifferentiated performance. Even so, the most sensitive dogmatic friction appears when utility depends on continuous modulations operated under technical opacity, so that relevant alterations occur without a single event, without verifiable justification and without a common language to compare promise, performance and reconfiguration. The frustration of the enjoyment bundle tends, then, to present itself as noise, and the legal response oscillates between two equally unproductive extremes: requiring total transparency incompatible with the functioning of the technical-organizational environment or accepting mutability as a neutral datum, normalizing substantial degradations as mere operational variation.

The problem does not lie in prohibiting evolution or in denying technical complexity. The problem lies in setting minimum criteria that make the governance of supply verifiable and decidable when the object is realized as a position of enjoyment over time. This requirement can be condensed into two complementary operators. Open auditability designates the minimum level of external verifiability capable of transforming opaque modulations into matter susceptible to adversarial proceedings, proof and decision. Functional value equivalence designates the criterion of comparison and restoration by which one judges whether the utility delivered, after relevant modulations, substantially preserves the core of enjoyed utility, including when part of the value is structured by socially verifiable symbolic dimensions. The first operator makes traceable what was altered and why. The second defines what must remain equivalent and what, when downgraded, requires a legal response compatible with continued enjoyment.

A technically simple and dogmatically expressive example of this requirement of equivalence appears in updates to visual effects on old cosmetics, carried out under the label of modernizing the game. In an environment in which enjoyment depends on visual signals, legibility and social recognition, the functional value of the cosmetic is not exhausted in its formal existence in the inventory. It includes the way the item presents itself in the game space, the clarity with which it communicates an ability, the consistency of the visual “weight” the user experiences when triggering effects, and also the public distinction that the cosmetic produces in p. 470 comparison with common versions. When this set is persistently altered, equivalence is not resolved with the answer that the item “is still there”, because the core of enjoyed utility may have been reconfigured.

This problem does not transform every improvement into an unlawful act. Updates may be necessary for compatibility, security, stability and legibility in a competitive ecosystem. The relevant legal question arises when the adjustment, although justifiable under a systemic rationality, shifts enjoyment to a version that, for some consumers, no longer corresponds to what was economically stabilized at the moment of acquisition. In this scenario, auditability and equivalence operate together. Open auditability requires a verifiable narrative of what changed and by which criteria, while equivalence requires that the comparison be made by what matters for utility, and not by the generic rhetoric of “evolution”. This connection becomes even more sensitive when the contractual regime itself assumes mutability as the rule of the service, including with regard to virtual products and automatic adjustments made over time Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e)467.

The point of contact with the practical life of the service is direct. The company itself states, in a public text, that these updates aim to improve legibility, reduce visual noise and order objectives by priority, even when this implies reducing fine details considered excessive, reorganizing hierarchies and favoring clarity over other aesthetic dimensions Riot Games(Riot Games, 2022a)468. In terms of institutional structure, this means that there is a declared teleology that guides the alteration. It is understandable and, from the standpoint of the p. 471 ecosystem, may be desirable. What is lacking, from the standpoint of consumption, is a stable standard of comparison capable of recognizing when modernization alters what justified the price and the choice, especially in items whose value depends on the visual experience and on public distinction.

The practical insufficiency appears when the information made available to the consumer is, as a rule, narrative and dispersed. The typical public channel for recording changes to the game is the set of patch notes, published in regular cycles, which describe changes and communicate directions, but is not structured as a comparative mechanism aimed at the consumer who needs to demonstrate degradation of a specific visual utility over time Riot Games(Riot Games, [202-?]b)469. In an environment of high complexity, this creates a predictable imbalance. The platform holds the complete history of decisions, versions and criteria, while the consumer receives only communicational fragments, insufficient to sustain, in a common language, the comparison between the stabilized promise and the reconfigured delivery.

It is here that the legal response proposed by auditability and equivalence proves moderate and functional. It does not require freezing the service or prohibit updates. It requires an institutionally loyal way out when the platform makes a substantial change to a good acquired under a stable regime of enjoyment. If the alteration preserves the core of utility, performance continues. If the alteration relevantly downgrades what structured utility for that consumer, restoration must include, at least, mechanisms of choice that prevent captivity by surprise, with alternatives such as refund, credit, exchange for another cosmetic of equivalent value or analogous forms of recovering the investment for the consumer's reinsertion into the ecosystem, without requiring from them the impossible proof of a black box. This design keeps the dialogue open and preserves the possibility of modernization, while preventing “update” from functioning as a label to impose on the consumer a version that they had no reason to expect would be the new basis of enjoyment.

The methodological starting point of this construction lies in the replacement of the ideal of immovable fixation of meaning with a requirement of control criteria capable of operating under p. 472 indeterminacy and complexity. It is in this sense that Humberto Ávila formulates an explicit turn by maintaining that the focus should be shifted from security of meaning to security of criteria, in order to provide, in advance, prior and abstract parameters to face later and concrete interpretive problems Ávila(Ávila, 2011, p. 71). The consequence is that the protection of the IOC does not depend on total transparency of the internal functioning of the platform, nor on absolute semantic certainty about each aspect of supply. It depends on conditions of intersubjective controllability capable of exposing reasons, delimiting objects of dispute and making argumentative reconstruction verifiable.

This requirement operates through determinability and relative certainty, not through the illusion of absolute determination. Ávila makes explicit that legal certainty depends on the capacity to understand the possible meanings of the normative text from cores of signification reconstructed through intersubjectively controllable argumentative processes, which grounds the notion of determinability and relative certainty of Law Ávila(Ávila, 2011, p. 123). On the plane of argument, this functions as a lock against arbitrariness. In addressing the process of legal argumentation, the author states that certainty is based on intersubjectively controllable criteria and that, in this sense, it is, above all, rational control of argumentative arbitrariness Ávila(Ávila, 2011, p. 169). These statements authorize shifting the debate about platforms. The question is not to open the black box all the way, but to define prior and controllable criteria that make it possible to transform technically opaque modulations into legally decidable alternatives, without reducing control to automatic deference to complexity.

Open auditability, in this framework, designates a minimum requirement of verifiability applicable to supply when the acquired utility is realized as continued enjoyment governed by a platform. Its focus is not to produce full transparency of the technical-organizational environment or to impose complete disclosure of models, source code, proprietary metrics or trade secrets. What is at stake is a level of external intelligibility sufficient to reconstruct, in adversarial proceedings, the relation between promise, performance and modulation. This level must make it possible to identify what was altered, to what extent the alteration bore on the enjoyment bundle and which reasons were mobilized to justify it. In governed consumption, opacity is not an episodic anomaly; it is a structural condition of the mode of supply. Without a verifiable minimum, complexity becomes a shield, proof becomes asymmetrical to a radical degree and mutability tends to be trivialized as an “update”, even when the practical result is the persistent emptying of the core of utility.

p. 473 The usefulness of the requirement becomes evident when frustration does not take the form of total interruption, but of partial degradations, gradual reconfigurations and cumulative restrictions that erode enjoyment without producing an unequivocal event. Open auditability prevents continued performance from becoming a legally unassessable gray zone. It is not a matter of requiring the consumer to fully understand the functioning of the system. It is a matter of ensuring sufficient verifiable elements to locate what was promised, what was actually delivered and what was later reconfigured by acts of maintenance, curation, updating, enforcement and management of conditions of access and use. Without this, the conflict migrates to the plane of the unsayable and produces two degenerative effects. The supplier comes to hold the practical monopoly of the “real”, and the consumer is reduced to a bearer of symptoms, without a common language that allows comparison and control.

This requirement also operates as a negative edge against two symmetrical excesses. On one side, it prevents legal control from getting lost in the pursuit of total transparency, converting the proceedings into a dispute about internal engineering, instead of the protection of consumption. On the other, it prevents the generic invocation of “internal policy”, “security”, “improvement of the service” or “integrity of the ecosystem” from functioning as a self-sufficient justification immune to control, emptying duties of information, loyalty and adequacy in continued performance. Open auditability is the level that shifts the discussion from “trust me” to the verifiable reconstruction of what was modulated, preparing the application of a second operator that judges equivalence and guides restoration.

The dogmatic justification of this cut does not depend on a promise of total technical truth. In Tércio Sampaio Ferraz Jr., modern dogmatics presents itself as a technology oriented toward action, and not as contemplation of the world. For this reason, “dogmatic technology” becomes an interpellation of social life, “forcing it”, “concealing it” and “manipulating it”, precisely to extract from it the maximum that is operable in the form of decision Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 80]). The cut that dispenses with full transparency and, even so, requires minimum conditions of controllability is legitimized in this register. Law does not need to see everything in order to control. It needs to cut out what is relevant to make the conflict treatable, prevent opacity from functioning as shielding and allow the dispute to be organized in institutionally decidable terms.

This technological orientation is concretized in the form of doctrinal propositions, which offer schemes of orientation and technical rules. Ferraz Jr. describes this role by stating that such propositions take the form of “orientations”, as “schemes, systematizations”, or the form of “recommendations”, persuasive propositions that become rules of the type “if you want x, you must z” Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 79]). Open auditability occupies this place in the IOC p. 474 regime. It provides a scheme for reconstructing supply under opacity and a technical rule for transforming continuous modulation into verifiable criteria about what changed, how it changed and what impact was produced. The result is compatible with the institutional function of decision, which is to put an end to the conflict.

Ferraz Jr. observes that the institutionalization of conflict and of the decision-making procedure causes legal conflicts to “end”, in the sense that the decision puts an end to them by “preventing their continuation”, unlike social conflicts that may be resumed indefinitely Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 14]). In platform-governed consumption, structural opacity tends to push the dispute toward interminability, because there always remains a technical “behind the scenes” that could explain the effect. A minimum level of auditability operates as a mechanism of finitude, organizing the debate around elements that can be reconstructed and discussed in adversarial proceedings, without the fiction of total transparency.

The same logic is reinforced when control is understood as programming oriented toward conditions and means. Ferraz Jr. observes that, in conditional programming, the decision-maker's responsibility is for the “correct use and exploitation of the means”, and that the prevalence of this programming makes the legal decision a procedure in which care with formal practices and control criteria gains relevance Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 315]). Faced with complex and mutable systems, protection does not depend on knowing all the variables of the ecosystem. It depends on operating through controllable conditions, that is, through verifiable criteria that make it possible to assess the correctness of the modulation procedure, the sufficiency of the reasons presented and the causal link between alteration and degradation of the enjoyment bundle. Open auditability thus ceases to be a generic appeal for transparency and becomes a dogmatic instrument of decidability.

Platform governance does not function as a neutral background. It organizes the space of enjoyment and defines, in practice, who enters, who stays, under what conditions and by which mechanisms someone is contained or excluded. Julie E. Cohen describes this power in treating dominant platforms as agents capable of producing and controlling a type of territory of their own, which is not physical, but is constructed by protocols, data flows and algorithms, and which presents itself to the user as a space demarcated and protected by guarded borders Cohen(Cohen, 2019, p. 235). If enjoyment happens within this algorithmic territory, one cannot depend on intuition about what occurs behind the service. The border of authorization itself is already part of the mode of supply. Open auditability therefore appears as a minimum condition to map, in verifiable terms, the operational rules that configure the space of enjoyment and the reasons that justify its alteration.

p. 475 This cartography is indispensable because the apparent flexibility of the environment is managed by architectural mechanisms that selectively enable certain choices and block others. Cohen states that platform protocols impose a modular structure that enables certain kinds of flexibility and, at the same time, blocks others Cohen(Cohen, 2019, p. 42). She adds that control through protocols also enables intermediation and favors legibility, making it possible for the platform to operate according to its own priorities Cohen(Cohen, 2019, p. 42). Without minimum auditability, what modularity executes remains invisible while the user's experience is presented as free choice. The proposed requirement does not eliminate complexity. It prevents the invisibility of selective blocks from becoming practical immunity of the supplier, especially when changes of protocol, curation or legibility criteria reconfigure the enjoyment bundle.

This requirement is reinforced when one recognizes that the platform not only records data, but manufactures operational representations of users to make behavior calculable, predictable and economically exploitable at scale Cohen(Cohen, 2019, p. 67). In this scenario, treating opacity as something neutral amounts to normalizing modulation as an ordinary and uncontestable form of governance of supply. Open auditability provides the minimum necessary so that decisions of curation, ranking, access, participation and sanction can be reconstructed and discussed in adversarial proceedings when they interfere relevantly with enjoyment over time. Cohen herself formulates the need for this level by maintaining that algorithms and protocols classify subjects, shape information flows and authorize or deny access to network resources, so that any meaningful control of the surroundings depends on a baseline level of information about what these mechanisms do Cohen(Cohen, 2012, [p. 189]). Open auditability is anchored precisely in this baseline level.

On the plane of operative translation, open auditability is expressed as a standard of verifiable reasoning applicable to the relevant modulations of supply in continued enjoyment. When the platform alters access rules, reconfigures functionalities, imposes selective frictions, reorganizes regimes of visibility, applies sanctions, modifies curation parameters or carries out cumulative restrictions that substantially affect the enjoyed utility, it is not enough to invoke generalities. The reasoning needs to offer sufficient elements to reconstruct the link between change and the affected enjoyment bundle, allowing the consumer to understand what occurred, delimit the object of the conflict and submit the justification to adversarial scrutiny without depending on total technical knowledge of the system. This minimum level can be described by the identification of the act of modulation and of the temporality of its effects, by the indication of the functional impact on practical dimensions of enjoyment, by the statement of operational reasons p. 476 with purpose and criteria of application and by the indication of means of contestation and review. The effect is to shift the controversy from the plane of the unsayable narrative to the plane of a verifiable reconstruction, without turning every adjustment into litigation and without requiring exhaustive explanation.

The requirement gains institutional density when private governance is recognized as effective normativity. In Eros Roberto Grau, the contractual relationship is described as the expression of an economic relationship that, whether or not legally regulated, already operates as a relationship of will, whose content is determined by the economic relationship itself Grau(Grau, 2008, p. 61). Recognizing this normativity does not amount to accepting it as destiny. Grau formulates a two-way street by stating that presupposed law springs from society and conditions the elaboration of posited law, but that positive law, when it applies, modifies presupposed law Grau(Grau, 2008, p. 64). On platforms, terms, protocols, flows and enforcement establish a practical order that organizes expectations and distributes positions. Open auditability functions as a technique for bringing this established order back to the plane of the controllable, without purporting to fully inspect the technical design, but preventing the exercise of power from becoming immune to verification.

This organized coercion is precisely the trait that makes control legally unavoidable. In discussing the formation of the legal rule, drawing on Duguit, Grau indicates that a social norm becomes a legal norm when the mass of consciences understands that the sanction can be “socially organized” Grau(Grau, 2008, p. 73). On platforms, the sanction materializes in suspension, banning, downgrading of visibility, blocking of functionalities, restrictions on participation and multiple forms of automated or hybrid sanction, executed by architecture and by internal procedures. When sanctions and restrictions operate as a pattern, governance ceases to be mere advice and comes to function as an effective normative regime. The demand for auditability is not an aesthetic agenda of transparency, but a minimum requirement for submitting private coercion to rational control. The same intuition appears when Grau observes that the market posits its legal order by establishing an order produced by it Grau(Grau, 2008, p. 326). If an order is established and applied by organized coercion, the minimum that can be required for decidability and legitimacy is that relevant decisions be reconstructible, contestable and assessable by objective criteria.

Once this level is established, the debate changes in nature. The dispute ceases to be about what the system did and comes to be about what must remain equivalent in continuous supply. It is here that the second operator comes in. Functional value equivalence is not a promise of freezing supply or a right to an eternal version of the service. It is the criterion of substantial preservation of the core of enjoyed utility in an p. 477 intrinsically reconfigurable environment. In continued enjoyment, changes of means, method, architecture and execution may be legitimate, including for reasons of security, stability and compatibility. The limit is that these changes cannot substantially and persistently downgrade the enjoyment bundle for which the consumer kept paying, or turn adaptation into an alibi for emptying utility.

This criterion also operates through negative edges. Mere aesthetic preference for an old experience is not equivalence, nor is nonconformity with ordinary variations of the service. Equivalence is not the measure of internal states, feelings or psychological accounts of the consumer. The comparison is made by substantial preservation of functions and of positions of use that structure utility, including instrumental and symbolic dimensions when these are a constitutive and socially verifiable part of enjoyment in the ecosystem. It is precisely for this reason that equivalence requires, as a precondition, the level of auditability: without traceability of changes, without indication of impacts and without explicit criteria of application, there is no rational basis for comparing promise and delivery and for distinguishing legitimate adaptation from emptying reconfiguration.

Functional value equivalence, when applied to symbolic components of the enjoyment bundle, requires a lock against psychologism. In Eco, the symbolic dimension of value can be treated as a stabilized cultural correlation, and not as a private mental state. Thus, the objection of mentalism does not hold because one is not speaking of associations or mental images, but of correlations formulated in cultural terms, which refer to one another on an abstractly cultural plane, regardless of whether a human mind carries out this association Eco(Eco, 2000, p. 163). This makes it possible to reconstruct the symbolic as an objective datum of the system, and not as an intimate phenomenon.

Objectivity stems from the socially coercive character of the code. Eco describes the linguistic code as something established by social crystallization, the product of a statistical average fixed by use and which, once stabilized, comes to operate with coercive value, imposing a system of relations that speakers obey, often unconsciously Eco(Eco, 1997, p. 31). In digital ecosystems, signs, metrics, badges, rarities, rankings and regimes of visibility do not function as a subjective ornament added to the “real product”. They operate as coercive relations of the environment of enjoyment itself, to which the consumer is subjected through participation.

The same logic prevents treating connotation as an unpredictable nebula. Eco indicates that there are diffuse subcodes that authorize specific connotations and observes that, in certain semantic subuniverses, widely authorized connotations and axiological p. 478 marks fall upon a term, guiding predictable readings Eco(Eco, 2000, p. 162). The consequence, for equivalence, is that symbolic losses can be described by public criteria. The expectation is not idiosyncratic, but authorized and induced by code.

Eco maintains that cultural units are semiotic postulates and that the ordinary producer of signs does not see them, but uses them, while a theory of signs does not use them, but sees them Eco(Eco, 2000, p. 120). The perspective is methodologically decisive because it makes it possible to make visible, by public criteria, the rule of cultural equivalence that the consumer merely practices. Accordingly, functional value equivalence admits a controllable symbolic axis, capable of comparing what was stabilized in the system of signs with what came to be delivered after reconfigurations, including in regimes of visibility, belonging and distinction.

Once such a limit is fixed, functional value equivalence can be formulated as a criterion of comparison between the utility stabilized in the bond and the utility delivered after relevant modulations. Continued performance may justify adjustments and updates, but does not authorize the platform to preserve formal access and structurally degrade the practical content of enjoyment through silent substitution of functionalities, introduction of selective frictions, persistent reduction of reach and visibility, reordering that disfigures reputation criteria or imposition of barriers that make permanence economically or socially innocuous. Equivalence functions as a yardstick of relevance, since it transforms the discussion about change into a discussion about preservation of the core, distinguishing ordinary variation from substantial and persistent downgrading.

Consumer-law dogmatics offers direct support for this criterion because it abandons the static view of the bond and treats the contract as a process. Claudia Lima Marques states that the contract must be viewed as a dynamic relationship, which is born, lives and dies, configuring a true process that unfolds over time and radiates legal effects during its performance, before it and after it Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 222-223). If the contract is a process, equivalence does not require identity of means. It requires substantial continuity of the expected core of utility, especially when utility depends on a technical-organizational environment in continuous operation.

This premise prevents treating the interval of performance as a neutral zone. Marques rejects the idea of an empty space between the birth of the obligation and the satisfaction of the main performance, by stating that the obligational relationship is a continuous whole. In this dynamic, from the birth of the relationship, and even before, the parties are already bound by ancillary duties of conduct, imposed by good faith in performance Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 110). In platform-governed enjoyment, p. 479 preserving functional equivalence frequently requires active conduct over time, and not mere formal conservation of access.

The very structure of long-term contracts reinforces that adaptability is an expected component of the bond, but under a horizon of trust and maintenance of quality. Marques describes these contracts as based “more on trust, on reiterated interaction, on the maintenance of the economic potential and the quality of services”, as carrying “the implicit expectation of changes in social, economic and legal conditions”, and concludes that “the capacity for adaptation, for cooperation between contracting parties, for continuation of the contractual relationship is here essential, basic” Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 74). This formulation provides the fine fit for functional value equivalence: changes may be admissible and even necessary, provided they are oriented toward maintaining the potential and the quality that structure the enjoyed utility, and not toward the silent substitution of the object by a less valuable version.

The point is completed with the creative function of objective good faith as a source of new duties and of liability for professional risks that cannot be disposed of by contract. Marques, Benjamin and Miragem record that good faith performs a creative function, as a source of new ancillary duties and also as a source of liability for lawful acts “by imposing new professional risks now not disposable by contract” (2013, p. 235). The consequence is evident, since, if continued performance exposes the consumer to risks of technical obsolescence, compatibility mutations and structural reconfigurations, good faith must require care, cooperation and maintenance as duties of preservation of functional equivalence, and thus impose liability when trust is broken by reconfigurations that relevantly and persistently degrade the core of utility.

Functional value equivalence also requires an objective lock against the attempt to legitimize unilateral downgrading as “evolution of the service”. In contracts of continuous performance, permanence is the mode of existence of the bond. Rizzatto Nunes describes these contracts as relationships whose “characteristic” is continuity ad infinitum while the consumer fulfills the obligation to pay, the indeterminacy of the term being the central trait Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 508]). If continuity is the normal regime, the enjoyed utility stabilizes as an expectation of maintenance of the level of delivery, and not as an expectation of formal access to a service that may be reconfigured at the supplier's convenience. For this reason, the CDC treats unilateral modification of content or quality as a ground of nullity. In commenting on p. 480 Article 51, item XIII470, Nunes asks, in a deliberately frontal way, how it would be possible to unilaterally alter a contract and, even so, confer legal validity on the alteration Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 508-509]). The example he offers, of the supplier that reduces classes while keeping the same price Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 508-509]), functions as an objective yardstick for platforms. The reduction rarely appears as an explicit cut of “classes”, but may manifest itself as loss of reach, persistent downgrading of performance, cumulative restriction of functionalities, compression of limits, reordering of visibility or structural alterations that raise the cost of enjoying the same utility. The logic is the same. The label and the price are kept, and the delivery of value is downgraded.

With this, functional value equivalence stabilizes as an internal limit on adaptation in continued enjoyment. Changes of means and method may be legitimate, and sometimes necessary, to keep supply viable. The limit is that they cannot be used to shift structural risks onto the consumer, degrade quality, restrict relevant functionalities, erode conditions of access or reconfigure regimes of participation and recognition that were part of the economically stabilized enjoyment. Whenever the platform invokes “update”, “adjustment” or “evolution of the service” to justify persistent alterations, the core of the debate must turn on demonstrating the substantial preservation of the enjoyment bundle, and not on the rhetoric of complexity.

This point requires a methodological basis for rejecting general labels that erase functional distinctions. Larenz points out that abbreviated formulas may serve external systematics, but are not an adequate basis for arguing about content and limits, precisely because they tend to obliterate distinctions oriented by social reality and by function when one operates only with a general and abstract concept Larenz(Larenz, 1997, p. 317). In platform-governed enjoyment, reducing the IOC to “service” in a generic sense may work as an external label, but fails where dogmatics needs to work, namely in defining content, limits and criteria of comparison. The answer to this erasure is neither to inflate categories nor to replace dogmatics with moral indignation. What is required is to rebuild the boundaries through functional distinctions anchored in an explicit evaluative point of view. In this sense, for Larenz, real-normative types and legal-structural types do not hold without this guiding evaluative point of view. He shows by example how certain legal connections depend on the relation between self-interest and risk Larenz(Larenz, 1997, p. 308-309). Under governed consumption, there is a coupling p. 481 between the supplier's interest in governing the environment and the typical risk of discontinuity, modulation and downgrading of enjoyment, a risk that needs to be recognized and controlled in continued supply.

This movement also protects the construction against the objection of “invention” of Law, because the legal system is open and requires functional concretization when the factual basis shifts. Larenz describes the system as an “‘open’ and, to a certain degree, ‘mobile’ system”, always incomplete and continually called into question, whose function is to make clear the “intrinsic rationality”, the guiding values and the principles of Law Larenz(Larenz, 1997, p. 241). If technology shifts the mode of realization of consumption, preserving the internal rationality of the CDC requires concretization through functional distinctions. Open auditability and functional value equivalence occupy this place: they do not replace the system; they allow it to see, by controllable criteria, what abbreviated formulas tend to erase.

The attempt to condition the legal control of opaque ecosystems on total transparency misses the target and produces paralysis. In an ideal scenario in which “all relevant data would be known from the outset” and “all alternatives could be enumerated and evaluated”, there would remain “only the choice”, but this scenario does not describe the real legal decision, which operates where information is incomplete and premises are uncertain, having as its immediate purpose the absorption of insecurity Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 1997, p. 43).

Ferraz Jr. formulates the point in terms that serve as a rule of method: “absorption of insecurity” means that the act of deciding “transforms undecidable incompatibilities into decidable alternatives”, without promising to eliminate the conflict, but converting it into something tractable Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 312]). In platform-governed consumption, this implies that control must not be confused with the ambition of seeing everything. It must be understood as the construction of verifiable requirements that make modulation legally decidable.

Within this framework, Ávila's distinction between security and transparency provides an additional safeguard against deviations. Ávila records that transparency has its “conceptual core linked to the idea of information”, while security centers on the “rational-argumentative question”, aiming to “ensure rationality and effectiveness for Law as a whole” and not merely to “inform” Ávila(Ávila, 2011, p. 255-256). Technical opacity, by itself, does not function as a liability-free zone. It shifts the axis of control to public, contestable and auditable criteria applicable to decisions that modulate enjoyment. Open auditability defines the minimum verifiability needed to reconstruct what was done and why. Functional value equivalence defines the material parameter for judging whether utility was preserved or whether a substantial downgrade was disguised as an update.

p. 482 With this, the two operators stabilize as criteria of legitimacy and of restoration in continuous digital services. Open auditability prevents governance by architecture from operating as a power immune to verification, especially when sanctions and reconfigurations substantially affect positions of enjoyment without total interruption. Functional value equivalence prevents the inherent mutability of continuous supply from being converted into an instrument of unilateral downgrading of content and quality, including in socially verifiable symbolic dimensions, treated as cultural correlations and not as mental states. Together, the operators make controllable, under opacity and mutability, the distinction between legitimate adaptation oriented to maintaining utility and emptying reconfiguration that requires a legal response compatible with the CDC and its regime for the IOC.

In making its criteria explicit, the text includes ranked objectives and a hierarchy of importance, maintaining that one of the objectives is to “reduce overall visual noise”, for example by “reducing the ‘invasion’ of textures that have many fine and unnecessary details”, and also states that the objectives are “ranked in order of importance”, so that “the readability of gameplay mechanics will always prevail over thematic resonance” Riot Games(Riot Games, 2022a). This kind of statement is valuable because it reveals an institutional pattern of decision, not an isolated event. The point is not to contest the legitimacy of the pursuit of clarity, but to recognize that applying these criteria may, in specific cases, reconfigure the core of utility that motivated the purchase of a cosmetic, especially when part of the value is tied to the visual experience, to distinction and to the perceived “weight” in the game. ↩
  3. The official “Patch Notes” channel functions as the recurring public record of changes to the game, organized in periodic entries, by patch, with titles and dates Riot Games(Riot Games, [202-?]b). The format is useful for general information and for minimal communicative transparency, but it is not designed as a tool of comparative auditability for the consumer who needs to reconstruct, technically, a before and after of a specific cosmetic, with objective parameters of demonstration. For dogmatics, the consequence is simple. In a regime in which the supplier can update continuously and even automatically, auditability cannot depend on whether the consumer happened to archive evidence or has the technical knowledge to produce handmade comparisons. If the system does not deliver, as a standard, sufficient comparative elements, controlling functional downgrading requires either clearer public criteria for change or restoration mechanisms as automated as the alteration that dispense with impossible proof when the impact is relevant. ↩
