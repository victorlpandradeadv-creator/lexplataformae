6.2.4.3 On the operationalization of semiotic vulnerability in IOCs

See this section in the PDF

p. 605

Semiotic vulnerability acquires dogmatic usefulness only when it ceases to function as a general diagnosis of the environment and begins to operate as a scheme of attribution and control. In IOCs, this requires disciplining three distinct and interlinked planes: the mechanism alleged, the objective basis on which this mechanism can be demonstrated and the typical consequence that the CDC already allows to be drawn from it. The point is not to assert, impressionistically, that the consumer was “manipulated” or “confused”. The point is to show that the communicative regime of supply was structured so as to produce a relevant asymmetry of p. 606 understanding, comparison and contestation, and that this asymmetry can be traced back to legally controllable duties, defects and remedies.

On the first plane, what is alleged is not a failure to read, individual distraction or psychological incapacity of the consumer. The allegation falls on a communicative and architectural structure that distributes power. In IOCs, the platform controls language, framings, signals of equivalence, criteria of rarity, acceptance flows, update messages and forms of visibility of the object itself. This means that it not only informs about enjoyment, but administers the means by which enjoyment will be perceived and evaluated. The asymmetry, therefore, does not arise from a subjective deficiency of the user, but from an unequal distribution of communicative competence and of the power to define meaning688. For this reason, semiotic vulnerability can be alleged as a legal fact: the supplier organizes in advance the field of what can be understood, compared and accepted.

This structure is aggravated because, in IOC ecosystems, the governance of meaning is continuous. The platform does not limit itself to communicating once, at the moment of contracting. It permanently reorganizes the decision environment and the enjoyment environment. The legally relevant allegation, then, can take stable forms. It may consist of informational inadequacy, when the information exists but does not guide a useful decision. It may consist of a breach of good faith, when the architecture of the environment hinders the formation of free and rational will. It may consist of objective disloyalty, when relevant changes are communicated in a ritualized, dispersed manner or at an inadequate moment. And it may consist of improper management of expectation, when offer, interface and decision flow are organized to produce an asymmetric perception of advantage, risk or equivalence. It is precisely at this level that the category leaves the abstract plane and enters the real CDC, because the transparency required by the system p. 607 does not mean the mere formal existence of texts, but respect for the normal interests of the consumer and the organization of material conditions for a considered decision689.

On the second plane, operationalization requires shifting the evidence from impressionistic ground to the communicative and architectural materiality of supply. The object of proof is not the inner state of the consumer considered in isolation. What is proven is the objective form of the decision path and of the enjoyment regime as structured by the platform. This allows semiotic vulnerability to be reconstructed through external, replicable and institutionally controllable elements. The first source of support is interface trails and decision flow: dated screenshots, screen recordings, purchase, acceptance, upgrade and confirmation screens, messages about rarity, equivalence, time remaining, discount, event or change. What is relevant there is not the user's subjective memory, but the architecture actually presented. The allegation ceases to be “I did not understand” and becomes “the environment was designed this way”.

The second source of support is the history of the offer and of mutability. The advertising and institutional promise is compared with the enjoyment actually delivered and with its later changes. What one seeks to show is not generic subjective frustration, but possible relevant degradation, loss of functional equivalence or unilateral reconfiguration with material impact on the experience. In IOCs, this comparison is central because the object is not exhausted by its nominal existence. Its legal character depends on enjoyment being maintained in functionally recognizable terms. To prove a change in the environment, in the utility, in the comparability or in the intelligibility of the experience is to prove a change in the supply regime itself.

The third source of support concerns advertising and its identifying documents. Promotional pieces, posts, campaigns, influencers, disclaimers, asterisks, cascading cross-references and fine print serve to map how the expectation was produced and where the p. 608 relevant conditions were hidden, diluted or moved to zones of low visibility. The same applies to proof of informational noise. Excess becomes opacity when there is fragmentation, circular cross-references, indecipherable technical language, internal contradictions, a gap between information and the moment of decision or displacement of the essential away from the point of choice. The evidentiary core is to demonstrate not the absence of text, but the structural inadequacy of the communication for purposes of useful understanding.

This evidentiary model also requires recognizing that semiotic asymmetry projects itself into an asymmetry of evidence. Whoever controls the service controls, to a large extent, the decisive records of the relationship. Logs, version histories, transactions, interface parameters, change communications and relevant technical documentation tend to remain in the supplier's possession. For this reason, the operationalization of the category must be accompanied by requests for preservation and production of minimum documentation of the supply. Without this, the consumer is prevented from demonstrating precisely what the IOC regime makes central. This is not a procedural favor. It is a matter of neutralizing the private concentration of the means of reconstructing the case. The CDC's language for this response already exists. Consumer protection admits rebalancing techniques when the informational failure and the form of the bond reveal a material defect in the formation or performance of the relationship690.

On the third plane, semiotic vulnerability must lead to foreseeable consequences. The first group is informational. If the problem is the decisional inadequacy of the communication, the request cannot simply be “more information”. The remedy is qualified information, organized in a clear, adequate and verifiable way at the moment of decision. This may mean conspicuous highlighting of the essential, reorganization of the communication flow, prohibition of obscure cross-references or the requirement of materially useful notice about relevant changes. The second group is that of control of the offer and of practices. When the legitimate expectation was produced by ambiguity, relevant omission or semiotic management of the perception of value, correction, inhibition and restoration of the frustration produced are appropriate.

p. 609 The third group is that of the ineffectiveness of obscurity. Clauses, internal rules, cascading terms and ritual acceptances that operate as semiotic traps should not be treated as a robust manifestation of consent. When language hinders real understanding of the meaning and scope of adhesion, the consumer-law system already authorizes neutralizing the effectiveness of obscurity in the concrete case. The fourth group is evidentiary. In the face of the concentration of records, preservation and production of the trails of supply are appropriate, with a rational redistribution of the burden and presumptions favorable to the consumer when the supplier does not cooperate or keeps the evidence in a black box.

This scheme shows that semiotic vulnerability is not a decorative concept. It organizes allegation, evidence and request. What is alleged is a structural asymmetry of meaning produced by the supply regime. What is proven is the communicative and architectural materiality of that asymmetry. What is requested is the correction of the decision environment, the neutralization of obscurity, the production of records and the restoration of the affected enjoyment. The category thus ceases to be a theoretical label and comes to function as an operator. It is precisely this that prepares the passage to open auditability and to the qualification of abusive teleology as a defect of the supply regime itself. Warat helps to recall only the core of the mechanism being converted into a legal allegation: the problem lies not in an inner deficiency of the consumer, but in a structure of language and framing that unequally takes from them the power to constitute the meaning of their own relationship691.

Notes

  1. The operationalization of semiotic vulnerability does not start from the idea of the consumer's individual inattention, but from the recognition of an unequal distribution of legally relevant communicative competence. The problem is not to censure the user for not having read or understood everything, but to identify that contractual and operational communication was structured according to codes, references and forms of expression whose full apprehension is not equally distributed among the participants in the consumer relation. At this point, Gnerre functions as a subsidiary reinforcement in recalling that the linguistic registers of greatest prestige and power are not universally accessible. As the author observes, "only a part of the members of complex societies, for example, has access to a 'cultivated' or 'standard variety, generally considered 'the language', and typically associated with contents of prestige. The standard language is a communicative system within reach of a reduced part of the members of a community" Gnerre(Gnerre, 1991, p. 6). The same mechanism appears when the platform adjusts syntax, lexicon and frame of references so as to restrict the effective addressees of the message, since, as the author adds, "to reduce or broaden the range of the possible receivers of political and cultural messages it is enough to adjust the syntax, the frame of references and the lexicon" Gnerre(Gnerre, 1991, p. 21). In IOCs, this reinforces that semiotic vulnerability concerns the very communicative architecture of supply, and not a psychological failure or mere negligence in reading. ↩
  2. The operationalization of semiotic vulnerability, in the sphere of IOCs, requires bringing it back to the concrete decision plane of the CDC, that is, to the ground on which the asymmetry of meaning is converted into legally relevant informational inadequacy, breach of objective good faith and grounds for requests to correct the supply regime itself. Marques offers decisive support in stating that good faith, made concrete in the consumer relation, "means mandatory transparency toward the contractual partner, a mandatory respect for the normal interests of the other contracting party, a positive action by the stronger contractual partner to allow the weaker contractual partner the conditions necessary for the formation of a free and rational will", adding that ensuring information, protection against the pressures of sales methods and time for reflection are legal objectives of this pursuit of a rational decision by the consumer Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 812). In a convergent sense, the author argues that the possibility of prior knowledge of the contractual text and of the obligations it contains is an "essential condition for the formation of a truly free, conscious, 'rational will", which is why Article 46 allows the consumer to request in court release from the bond due to a failure of the supplier's duties of information Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 898-899). This allows semiotic vulnerability to be translated into allegation, evidence and request within the real CDC. ↩
  3. The CDC does not limit itself to recognizing, in theory, that the consumer's freedom of choice may be compromised by inadequate contractual and informational structures; it provides for concrete corrective consequences precisely to rebalance the decision environment when the formation of will proves materially defective. Marques formulates this reasoning by stating that, in the face of the structural "pressures" that unbalance contracting in consumer society, there arises the need for a "rebalancing legal response", exemplified by control of the content of the contract, by "a new informative formalism" and by the right of withdrawal without cause, all this "with the aim of rebalancing or supplying the 'factual failure' in the freedom or will of the weaker contracting party." Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 287). The author also highlights the case-law tendency to declare the ineffectiveness of contractual clauses and even of entire consumer bonds on the basis of arts. 46 and 54, § 4, of the CDC, precisely because of problems of form and contractual drafting revealing insufficient information and warning to the consumer Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 1304). This authorizes structuring corrective requests aimed not only at later reparation, but at the refinement of the supply regime itself. ↩
  4. Warat offers a bridge for tracing back the mechanism underlying semiotic vulnerability. The relevant asymmetry is not founded on a psychological incapacity of the consumer, but on an unequal distribution of power through language, communicative framing and ritualization of meaning. The author warns that the function of domination should not be analyzed “from a psychologistic point of view”, since senders and receivers are institutionally situated and their discourses operate as supports of a mediatized ideology Warat(Warat, 1984, p. 67). The inequality, therefore, lies not in a supposed subjective deficiency of the addressee, but in the way the communicative structure is organized in advance to favor certain poles of definition, interpretation and legitimation. This key is reinforced when Warat shows that the producer of discourse can secure for himself “the monopoly of the constitution of the denotative field”, selecting designative properties for persuasive purposes and leaving the receivers “asleep to the discussion of the persuasively proposed designative criteria” Warat(Warat, 1984, p. 70). It is precisely this kind of communicative disciplining, and not an inner failure of the consumer, that can be traced back to the legal allegation of semiotic vulnerability in IOCs. ↩
