Situation c) · 7.1.2 of the thesis

When the information exists but gets buried in excess

c) inadequate information as functional opacity

Which facts show information that exists but offers no guidance?

Textual excess, strategic dispersion, fragmentation of informational paths and merely performative transparency. Essential rules sit in long documents, cross-references, FAQs and update histories that the consumer can hardly locate and connect to the impact they suffered.

How do you demonstrate that the meaning was not knowable?

What was shown, at what moment, with what salience and through which channels, and whether that organization allowed the consumer to understand what was relevant to join, stay or react.

Which rules require clear and conspicuous information?

Article 31 of the CDC1 requires correct, clear, precise and conspicuous information in the offer and in the presentation. Article 46 of the CDC2 releases the consumer from obligations when there was no real opportunity to learn of them beforehand or when the wording hinders understanding. Paragraphs 3 and 4 of Article 54 of the CDC3 require clear adhesion contracts and highlighted limiting clauses. Article 47 of the CDC4 requires clauses to be interpreted in the way most favorable to the consumer. This rule connects to the control of abusiveness provided for in Article 51, item IV, of the CDC5.

Is it enough to prove that the text was there?

The decisive point is not to prove that the text was there, but to assess whether the legally relevant meaning was knowable in real terms. Depending on the case, the failure qualifies as an informational quality defect, as an abusive practice by obscuring or as a basis for the invalidity of clauses.

PTA prova mínima exigível requer identificação do que foi mostrado, em que momento, com qual saliência, por quais canais e em que medida essa organização permitia ao consumidor compreender o que era juridicamente relevante para aderir, permanecer ou reagir.

This is precisely what art. 54, under paragraphs 3 and 4, of the CDC makes explicit. PTAs cláusulas contratuais serão interpretadas de maneira mais favorável ao consumidor.

For this reason, the new function of contract law places the principle of interpretation in accordance with good faith back at the center, so that art. 47 of the CDC4, together with the general clause of art. 51, IV, allows the pre-drafted clauses to be interpretedp. 605

PTPor isso, a nova função do direito contratual recoloca em posição central o princípio da interpretação conforme a boa-fé, de modo que o art. 47 do CDC , em conjunto com a cláusula geral do art. 51, IV, permite interpretar as cláusulas predispostas

Institutional consequence
Correction of the communicational arrangement, reinforcement of useful transparency, disclosure orders and restoration of the affected enjoyment.

The law cited on this page

  1. art. 31 of the CDC ↩

    “Art. 31. The offer and presentation of products or services must ensure correct, clear, precise, conspicuous information in the Portuguese language about their characteristics, qualities, quantity, composition, price, warranty, expiration dates and origin, among other data, as well as about the risks they present to the health and safety of consumers.”

    PT“Art. 31. A oferta e apresentação de produtos ou serviços devem assegurar informações corretas, claras, precisas, ostensivas e em língua portuguesa sobre suas características, qualidades, quantidade, composição, preço, garantia, prazos de validade e origem, entre outros dados, bem como sobre os riscos que apresentam à saúde e segurança dos consumidores.”

    Wording transcribed in note 681, p. 601.

  2. Article 46 of the CDC ↩

    “Art. 46. Contracts governing consumer relationships shall not bind consumers if they are not given the opportunity to become aware of their content beforehand, or if the respective instruments are drafted in a way that hinders understanding of their meaning and scope.”

    PT“Art. 46. Os contratos que regulam as relações de consumo não obrigarão os consumidores, se não lhes for dada a oportunidade de tomar conhecimento prévio de seu conteúdo, ou se os respectivos instrumentos forem redigidos de modo a dificultar a compreensão de seu sentido e alcance.”

    Wording transcribed in note 682, p. 601.

  3. Article 54 of the CDC ↩

    “§ 3 Written adhesion contracts shall be drafted in clear terms and with conspicuous and legible characters, so as to facilitate their understanding by the consumer. § 4 Clauses that imply a limitation of the consumer's rights shall be drafted with prominence, allowing their immediate and easy understanding.”

    PT“§ 3º Os contratos de adesão escritos serão redigidos em termos claros e com caracteres ostensivos e legíveis, de modo a facilitar sua compreensão pelo consumidor. § 4º As cláusulas que implicarem limitação de direito do consumidor deverão ser redigidas com destaque, permitindo sua imediata e fácil compreensão.”

    Wording transcribed in note 710, p. 621.

  4. art. 47 of the CDC ↩

    “Art. 47. Contractual clauses shall be interpreted in the manner most favorable to the consumer.”

    PT“Art. 47. As cláusulas contratuais serão interpretadas de maneira mais favorável ao consumidor.”

    Wording transcribed in note 498, p. 511.

  5. art. 51, IV, of the CDC ↩

    Art. 51. The following contractual clauses relating to the supply of products and services, among others, are null and void by operation of law:

    PTArt. 51. São nulas de pleno direito, entre outras, as cláusulas contratuais relativas ao fornecimento de produtos e serviços que: IV - estabeleçam obrigações consideradas iníquas, abusivas, que coloquem o consumidor em desvantagem exagerada, ou sejam incompatíveis com a boa-fé ou a eqüidade; XIII - autorizem o fornecedor a modificar unilateralmente o conteúdo ou a qualidade do contrato, após sua celebração;

    IV - those that establish obligations considered iniquitous, abusive, that place the consumer at an exaggerated disadvantage, or are incompatible with good faith or equity;

    XIII - those that authorize the supplier to unilaterally modify the content or quality of the contract after its conclusion;
