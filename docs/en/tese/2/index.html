2 On the methodological design and the observation protocol

Epigraph

Danni Carlos, Coisas Que Eu Sei (2008). What it announces · Read the epigraph in the PDF.

p. 45

In platform economies, power tends to mask itself as technical administration while it concentrates knowledge, organizes attention and redefines the conditions of participation. The central methodological risk, faced with this type of object, is to let the appearance of neutrality of the functioning replace the analysis of the way in which private decisions produce practical norms and distribute asymmetries. For this reason, it starts from a political warning that functions as the horizon of the problem: when knowledge is concentrated and the rest is pushed into irrelevance, the answer is not contemplation, it is critical capacity and human quality.

El mundo se va a ir dividiendo [...] en los irrelevantes y los dueños de conocimiento [...]. Si nos quedamos de brazos cruzados no vamos a servir ni para que nos exploten [...]. Entonces se necesita una sociedad muy inteligente, muy capacitada [...]. Como seres humanos, no hemos evolucionado tanto como evolucionó la tecnología. Por eso hay que pelear por [...] mejorar la calidad humana. Eso se llama cultura, conocimiento y ejemplo. No me quiten la utopia. (MUJICA..., 202315)

It is on this foundation that the methodology adopted here takes itself to be a legitimate stance, refusing the naturalization of “how the platform works” as if it were a neutral method, marked by the absence of choice, and facing governance by architecture as a full phenomenon, and therefore one legally susceptible to description and reconstruction in dogmatic terms. The methodology represents a positioning before the type of power observed in platform economies and, therefore, before the type of temptation that such an object produces in the researcher. In environments in which private administration and the management of conduct present themselves as technical neutrality, the central analytical risk is to naturalize decisions as if they were simple “functioning”, to reduce institutional choices to “operational details” and to attribute to the user, as autonomy, responses that have already been induced by design, communication and mechanisms of incentive, disincentive and sanction. The methodological design, therefore, assumes as a premise that the regime of the service in operation emerges from the coupling between clauses, interface and institutional practices, which precludes readings that artificially separate norm, technique and culture.

The research strategy organizes collection and analysis procedures capable of reconstructing, in a controllable manner, how promises are formed, how expectations are stabilized and how p. 46 relevant losses are produced in continuous relationships. The objective is to preserve the legal intelligibility of the phenomenon without reducing its social density, since the exercise of authority in the ecosystem is distributed among contractual instruments, interface architectures and enforcement routines. The methodological consequence is a triangulation that makes it possible to bring inferences back to traceable evidence and to keep the object legally decidable, avoiding both abstraction disconnected from practice and description that abdicates normative qualification.

This stance explains the option of combining theoretical-dogmatic reconstruction with an instrumental empirical laboratory and with controlled exemplification, complementing this anchoring with illustrative applications and adherence tests of the proposed keys in typical situations of continuous digital consumption. In relationships of this nature, in which conduct and expectations are administered by proprietary signs and by institutional routines, the user's concrete experience ceases to be external noise and comes to form part of the very regulatory mechanism to be understood. This proximity does not authorize psychologization, nor does it convert familiarity with the field into a criterion of proof. It functions as an instrument for guiding the gaze to identify observable mechanisms of promise, friction and sanction, which must then be anchored in traceable and publicly verifiable evidence.

To keep the inferential path controllable, the literature mobilized throughout the work is treated through a methodological distinction between object and instruments16 Eco(Eco, 2007, p. 69). The object consists of the regime of platform-governed enjoyment and its mechanisms of promise, expectation, modulation and sanction. The instruments are the conceptual keys used to qualify this regime in language suited to normative application, which makes it possible to convert phenomena of architecture, communication and technical execution into operational categories compatible with legal duties, limits and consequences. In practical terms, frameworks from political economy, sociology, philosophy, anthropology of consumption and communication theory enter to guide the identification of relevant mechanisms and to discipline inferences, without imposing an exegetical reconstruction of the works mobilized, so that the argument p. 47 remains traceable back to traceable evidence and to recognizable normative parameters Eco(Eco, 2007, p. 69-70). This distinction aims to prevent both exegetical drift and the merely ornamental use of concepts.

In this key, the option for a non-exegetical approach does not operate as a shortcut, but as a conscious delimitation of focus. When a concept is mobilized as a tool to read an object distinct from the one in which it was formed, the interest falls on what it makes visible in the observed field and on how it improves the legal intelligibility of the problem, without this imposing a philological reconstruction of the origin, internal debates and historical variants of the concept in its domain of birth, except when indispensable to avoid relevant distortion.

The discipline of scope finds explicit support in Umberto Eco (2007, p. 70-71), who distinguishes the investigation of a thought in its origin from the investigation of later uses and appropriations and points out that, in certain cuts, what matters is what was made of the concept17. In methodological terms, the orientation authorizes treating the complete genealogy of the concept as a topic only when it is necessary to prevent distortion, and not as a mandatory stage of every instrumental mobilization.

Here are gathered the elements necessary to make the investigation auditable, including the research strategy, the empirical scope and the techniques for collecting and reading the material mobilized. This sets a standard of transparency and control capable of following the chain between evidence, categories and inferences throughout the work. Thus, the argumentative continuity of the following sections is preserved, in which the analysis concentrates on the theoretical and dogmatic dimensions of the problem.

Notes

  1. Excerpt from an interview given by former Uruguayan President José Alberto “Pepe” Mujica Cordano to DW Brasil fonte(MUJICA..., 2023). ↩
  2. The distinction between the object of research and the instruments mobilized to make it intelligible is treated by Eco as an organizing premise of intellectual work and as a measure of control against the transformation of auxiliary texts into a topic of exegesis. “A thesis studies an object using certain instruments. Often the object is a book and the instruments are other books. This is the case, for example, of a thesis on The economic thought of Adam Smith, whose object is constituted by the books of Adam Smith, while the instruments are other books about Adam Smith. […] In other cases, on the contrary, the object is a real phenomenon; this is what happens with theses on internal migratory movements in present-day Italy, on the behavior of a group of disabled children or on the opinions of the public regarding a television program currently being broadcast.” Eco(Eco, 2007, p. 69). Methodologically, Eco's comment sustains that interdisciplinary literature is capable of operating as an instrument for qualifying the observed phenomenon without this imposing an exegetical reconstruction of the works mobilized when they do not constitute the object of the investigation. ↩
  3. Eco offers an express formulation to justify cuts in which what is sought is not a full philological reconstruction of a thought in its origin, but the examination of uses and appropriations in another context, provided that the objective is clearly delimited. The author gives an example: “It could, however, be a case in which the original thought interested me very little. Let us suppose I began a thesis on Zen thought in the Japanese tradition. […] Suppose, however, that, in examining the critical literature, I became interested in the use that a certain American literary and artistic avant-garde made of Zen in the 1950s. Evidently, at this point I am no longer interested in knowing with absolute theological and philological exactness what the meaning of Zen thought would be, but rather in knowing how ideas originating in the East became elements of a Western artistic ideology. […] Assuming, of course, that I do not intend to demonstrate that the Californians misunderstood the original Zen, which would make comparison with the Japanese texts mandatory. But if I limit myself to presupposing that they drew freely on translations from the Japanese, what interests me is what they made of Zen and not what Zen was in its origin.” Eco(Eco, 2007, p. 70-71). The example makes explicit that the requirement of returning to origins depends on the problem formulated and on the type of statement one intends to sustain. ↩
