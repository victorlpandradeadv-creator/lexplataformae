5.1.3 On abusive teleology and the unlawful act of architecture on platforms

p. 444

Platform governance has already been described as a form of private regulation that combines terms, technical architecture and continuous execution of rules, producing an environment in which the acquired utility is realized under permanent modulation. In continuity with the recognition that, in IOCs, architecture and governance are not “exteriorities” of the object, but a material dimension of supply itself, the next step consists in bringing this description back to the plane of legally relevant conduct, distinguishing institutional diagnosis from applicable dogmatic criterion.

The Lex Plataformae operates as a descriptive category to identify the mode of exercise of power and of production of normativity in the ecosystem, that is, the scenario in which the legal p. 445 phenomenon manifests itself. Abusive teleology, in turn, must operate as a controllable category of attribution. Its role is to qualify exploitative purposes materialized in the very architecture of supply, without being confused with moral judgment about technology and without becoming an expansive label indifferent to limits. As a result, legal control can fall on the purpose and structural arrangement of supply, without being limited to isolated conduct and without anticipating, at this point, the exhaustive fit into quality defects, safety defects, abusive practices and abusive clauses.

Governance by architecture can be described as the capacity of centralized protocols and controls to actively define the space of possibilities in which enjoyment takes place, including through structural choices that enable certain flexibilities and block others Cohen(Cohen, 2019, p. 42). In this arrangement, the supplier's power is not exhausted in initial access, since it is realized in the continuous modulation of the environment and of its conditions of use over time. This modulation operates as permanent adjustment through information flows and, for that reason, can be understood as a shift from disciplinary frames to less crude and potentially more effective forms of control by continuous modulation Cohen(Cohen, 2012, [p. 171]). When oriented toward exploitative ends, the arrangement bears directly on agency and autonomy, because the information flows themselves can be designed to induce patterns of motivation and response at pre-deliberative levels, under a regime of instrumentarian power and data-driven agency, with ubiquitous anticipation Cohen(Cohen, 2019, p. 77). Within this frame, the architecture of supply becomes legally treatable as relevant conduct, since the direction of the ecosystem manifests itself as a pattern of organization of possibilities, frictions and dependencies in the very performance of the bond.

The objectivity of this modulation is not metaphorical. On certain platforms, the consumer's experience is so dependent on the ecosystem that unilateral alteration imposes itself with the same materiality and naturalness with which, at Hogwarts, the staircases rearrange their paths, or with which the “Room of Requirement” reconfigures itself to serve the purpose of whoever seeks it450. The decisive difference lies in the source of the reconfiguration. In the fictional universe, it p. 446 presents itself as an autonomous property of the environment. On the platform, it results from choices of architecture, curation criteria, sanction mechanisms and layers of opacity that convert the shifting of the "ground" into an act of government, even though they are presented to the user as the simple functioning of the world. For this reason, abusive teleology is not located primarily in an isolated event.

It is not exhausted in the one-off irregularity of a clause or of a specific decision. In regimes of continued enjoyment governed by a platform, the decisive conduct manifests itself as an architectural program, composed of design choices that define possibilities of use, introduce frictions, stabilize dependencies, operate sanctions and continuously reconfigure the practical conditions of enjoyment. It is at this level that it becomes possible to speak of purpose, because purpose appears materialized in the way the environment is structured and in the type of behavior that it repeatedly induces and reinforces. Abusive teleology designates this structural deviation oriented by an identifiable exploitative purpose, in which the architecture of supply ceases to be mere technical mediation and comes to operate as an instrument of extraction and control, reproduced as a pattern in the performance of the bond.

For this qualification not to derive from moral reproach or from rhetoric of convenience, it is necessary to make explicit why technical choices carry normative direction and can be read as conduct. This requires setting aside the idea of the neutrality of design and recognizing that technical decisions incorporate preferences and sediment interests as a standard of functioning. Feenberg maintains that technical designs that appear neutral may, in practice, incorporate unconscious preferences Feenberg(Feenberg, 2017, p. 5). Further, directionality ceases to be a side effect and comes to be understood as a socially situated orientation of technical progress itself, insofar as technological development is overdetermined by technical and social criteria of progress and, for that reason, may follow different directions depending on the prevailing hegemony Feenberg(Feenberg, 2002, p. 143). If architecture can branch according to hegemonies, then it is not merely a means of execution. It is the place in which values and interests become operative as an organization of possibilities, costs, frictions and rewards. Within this framework, abusive teleology does not describe a metaphysical wrong of technology, but a form of legal qualification of a pattern of design and of governance of supply.

The passage from institutional diagnosis to a controllable dogmatic category requires methodological legitimation that authorizes reading structural patterns of supply as legally relevant facts, without converting this reading into loose moralism. In Reale, the p. 447 legal norm is not reduced to a logical proposition. It operates as an integration between facts and values and, for that reason, requires simultaneous reference to these two factors. This formulation supports the view that, in platform-governed consumption, legal qualification can and must capture the integration between a factual regime of architectural execution and the values that structure the protection of consumption, instead of limiting itself to carving out an isolated episode. The very formation of Law presupposes the observation of social regularities and constants, from which it becomes possible to discriminate and classify behaviors as lawful and unlawful. This axiological dimension does not authorize voluntarism and imposes equivalent methodological restraints on the dogmatic operation of incidence. Thus, abusive teleology is justified as the legal qualification of a verifiable pattern inscribed in the architecture of supply, oriented by controllable values and duties of the system, without depending on free moral judgments.

This requirement of controllability is reinforced by the technological status of dogmatics, oriented toward decidability and not toward the exhaustive description of the world. Ferraz Jr. observes that, unlike zetetic theories, dogmatic ones, being concerned with the decidability of conflicts, do not have absolute logical rigor as a priority. What is essential is to make a cut in reality, isolate the problems relevant to decision-making and divert attention from the others Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 81]). This cut does not eliminate uncertainties, but makes them operable under normative binding and decisional pressure. In the same sense, in addressing control, the author clarifies that the term controlled indicates that dogmatics increases uncertainties, but does so in a manner compatible with central requirements of the legal discipline. Increasing uncertainties, therefore, is not merely creating doubts, but producing them in a manner oriented toward the action of man in society Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 44]). For this reason, the expectation that qualification depends on knowing all relevant data from the outset becomes inadequate, as already established. The decision absorbs the insecurity proper to opacity and contingency by converting blockages into institutionally acceptable outcomes, since the act of deciding transforms undecidable incompatibilities into decidable alternatives, and decisions absorb insecurity not because they eliminate the conflict, but because they transform it Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 306]). Abusive teleology, therefore, needs to be constructed as an instrument for making that cut, one that transforms diffuse suspicions into verifiable hypotheses, without requiring full transparency of the ecosystem or imposing impossible proof on the consumer.

In this same movement, the qualification cannot lose sight of the fact that a relevant part of the value offered on platforms is semiotic, and that the architecture exploits signs, metrics, visibility and belonging as components of supply itself. The possibility of treating symbolic dimensions as legally relevant depends on setting aside, from the outset, p. 448 the confusion between sign and private “feeling”. In Eco, as already established, the sign operates materially in social life as a force that structures practices and effects, and meaning need not be reduced to “a mental image”, since it is “this imposition, exercised by the code upon the speaker”, that allows it to be understood as a relation instituted by the system, and not as a psychic event, avoiding the “dangerous ‘mentalist’ concession” Eco(Eco, 1997, p. 24).

Umberto Eco's logic makes it possible to affirm that symbolic promises and semiotic effects integrated into the enjoyment bundle can be reconstructed in a controllable way as objective components of utility and legitimate expectation, which is decisive when architectural modulation reconfigures, without total interruption, the practical meaning of use, the consumer's position in the ecosystem and the value of permanence.

With the premises and their respective limits established, abusive teleology can be defined, at this point, as the structural deviation of platform-governed supply in which the architecture and governance of the environment come to operate in a manner oriented by an identifiable exploitative purpose, producing and reinforcing patterns of modulation that degrade, reconfigure or condition enjoyment beyond the ordinary risk of a continued performance.

The category does not depend on imputing subjective psychological intention or on locating a single act as a sufficient cause, because its object is the architectural program as a set of reiterated choices that organizes possibilities, introduces frictions, manages visibilities, distributes sanctions and stabilizes dependencies in the performance of the bond. For this qualification to remain controllable and not turn into an elastic label, it must take the form of a composite test made up of cumulative elements, capable of bringing the discussion back to verifiable requirements and of delimiting the edges of application.

The first requirement is inscribed teleology. It must be possible to reconstruct that the exploitative purpose is not merely in an occasional side effect, but appears incorporated in design choices, terms of use, authorization mechanisms and enforcement strategies that structure the environment of enjoyment. Inscription is identified when the architecture systematically organizes possibilities and exclusions, introduces selective frictions, asymmetrically distributes visibilities and sanctions, or conditions functionalities so as to steer behaviors and reduce alternatives relevant to the user.

The second requirement is reproduction as a pattern. The deviation cannot be an isolated episode, a contingent error or an operational exception. It must reappear as a regularity of execution, replicable at scale, whether because it applies to classes of users or because it is repeated p. 449 in cycles of updating, adjustment or curation, revealing a consistent directionality of the ecosystem.

The third requirement is the exploitation of structural vulnerabilities. The exploitative purpose must bear on asymmetries of intelligibility and agency that are part of the very arrangement of governed consumption, such as semiotic vulnerability, dependence through captivity, switching costs and the loss of investments accumulated in reputation, networks or history. Exploitation is characterized when the architectural pattern mobilizes such asymmetries to extract permanence, attention, data or economic advantage, not as an inevitable effect of performance, but as the dominant logic of supply.

One of the most characteristic forms of inscription of the exploitative purpose, in this third requirement, stems from a trait peculiar to the laboratory studied here and to the ecosystems resembling it: the supplier is, above all, an entertainment company, and this allows it to extend the ludic frame beyond the product supplied.

The game ceases to be merely what is acquired and comes to contaminate the consumer relationship itself, so that acquiring, converting currency, deciphering price, following event windows and trying to obtain a good become, themselves, game mechanics. The difficulty of understanding how much one spends, previously described as an asymmetry of intelligibility, is here re-presented by the platform as part of the fun, as if understanding the internal economy were a legitimate puzzle of the contracted entertainment. The problem is that this transposition is not symmetrical.

Unlike a level of the game, whose rules exist so that the player can, in theory, beat them, the game embedded in the consumer relationship is designed and refereed by the supplier itself, which defines the probabilities, sets the unit of account, recalibrates the internal exchange rate and establishes the deadlines, systematically reserving for itself the position of the bank. The house, in this arrangement, wins by structure, not by chance.

It is precisely this shift that constitutes the abusive inscription, because the ludic form is mobilized not to entertain, but to naturalize as play what, outside this frame, would be immediately recognized as tying, price opacity or value capture. That is why the claim that "it is a game" does not operate, here, as a justification, but as a symptom: when the ludic mechanic is applied to the contracting and to the enjoyment themselves, and not to the content delivered, it comes to indicate the purposive deviation instead of dispelling it.

This mechanism becomes especially clear, because it makes the exploitative impulse visible as access design, in the introduction of the Sanctum as a way of obtaining p. 450 Exalted skins. In this arrangement, access to the good ceases to operate through direct acquisition and comes to be conditioned on a drawing system mediated by a currency of its own, with rarity and a guarantee by attempts, which redesigns consumption as a path of probabilistic extraction Riot Games(Riot Games, 2025d).

Abusive teleology appears forcefully when one observes that the system not only bars the direct purchase of the main good, but converts the acquisition into a functional tying sale with a set of items and internal currencies that, in most consumption trajectories, do not correspond to the player's interest. This economic binding does not take place through a declared obligation of joint purchase, but through the design of the path, in which most outcomes do not deliver the desired good and come back as intermediate rewards, especially currencies and collateral cosmetics, which come to function as symbolic cushions for the spending and as an incentive to keep trying Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024a)451.

The specific problem of the Sanctum, at this point, is that it forces the consumer to finance a diffuse package of other lower-value IOCs along the way, including chromas and cosmetics intended for champions who may not be part of their gameplay, in addition to accumulating Mythic Essence in variable amounts, which is a currency aimed at another internal storefront Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024a). This currency, in turn, is described by support itself as a way of acquiring rare items in the Mythic Shop and, from 2025 on, as a reward primarily associated with the Sanctum itself, which reinforces the nature of a coupled circuit and of a deviation of economic purpose Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024n).

This design makes explicit a relevant loss of consumer autonomy, since the consumer no longer chooses the route of access to the good and instead comes to “pay to try”, with accumulated progression and a foreseen spending cap, in a logic that is only completed through continued consumption within the system itself Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024a). In this key, the innovation does not operate as a parallel alternative, but as a substitution of the mode of acquisition, conditioning the enjoyment of the main good on the acceptance of an exploitative architecture of calculated risk452 Riot Games(Riot Games, 2025d). It would not be a problem if the option of buying the skin directly at a proportional and reasonable price were maintained. However, by making the path mandatory, the company comes p. 451 closer to an abusive practice of tying than to the creation of an entertainment experience that genuinely cares about the expectations and desires of its consumer users.

At the same time, the contractual frame makes it possible to understand why this architecture can be implemented as an ordinary operation of the service, without the need to formally remove content from the ecosystem. This happens precisely because the structure of supply presents and justifies itself as a license to access virtual content and, at the same time, as a prerogative of alteration, modification, restriction and evolution, including through patches453 Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e). Thus, although abusive teleology is not reduced to the contract, the contract contributes to stabilizing the scenario in which the platform reorganizes by design the form of access and enjoyment, shifting the consumer onto tracks of consumption and permanence that serve primarily the supplier's rationality Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e).

The existence of this type of architecture does not warrant the conclusion that every deep reprogramming of the service constitutes abusive teleology. There are structural reforms that rewrite the environment of enjoyment and yet are not organized as exploitation, precisely because they neither impose relevant loss nor convert the continuity of use into a new mandatory spending route. A classic example is the reform of the Runes system, which replaced the previous regime with a new model of pre-game choices, significantly altering the way the player prepares and specializes Riot Games(Riot Games, [201-?])454.

In this case, the institutional communication emphasized that the new set of runes would be made available without direct charge, which, in itself, shifts the qualification of the episode outside the exploitative core that characterizes the purposive deviation Riot Games(Riot Games, [201-?]). At the same time, the company described the reform as a design project aimed at restructuring choices and strategies, showing that the teleology of the change was openly assumed as experience engineering, and not as economic coercion of access Riot Games(Riot Games, 2018a)455.

p. 452 The contrast is useful because it makes the distinguishing criterion visible. If the reform had extinguished the prior investment without an equivalent route of restoration, and if the new system required compulsory purchase or prevented the user from preserving the position of enjoyment that motivated their past spending, the modulation would cease to be compatible with the purpose of consumption and would come to constitute purposive deviation. This hypothesis makes it possible to separate governance of a service, however intense, from abusive teleology properly so called, which presupposes relevant loss and exploitative redirection.

The same logic can be illustrated, hypothetically, by an even more transparent example. If a package is sold as a structurally complete acquisition, with an objective promise to include future content, and the platform later alters the system and retroactively removes this functionality, requiring a new purchase to maintain the same level of enjoyment, the design ceases to be evolution and comes to operate as economic coercion by architecture. In this configuration, the platform not only changes the environment, but reconstructs the relationship to extract again what had already been incorporated into the consumer's expectation.

However, the relevant loss that characterizes abusive teleology is not limited to functional utility. In immaterial goods, especially when their enjoyment is publicly visible, utility is inseparable from status, rarity and social recognition, which function as part of the enjoyment bundle acquired. Hence, the governance of the ecosystem controls not only technical access to the good, but also its circulation and, with it, the distinctive value that sustains the consumer's experience as a position. It is on this plane that the regime of Prestige skins becomes a particularly revealing laboratory, since, at least at the time of its introduction, the value of the item rested not only on the cosmetic itself, but on its institutionally managed scarcity and on the perceived cost of the path to obtain it.

In the initial cycle, the specialized press described the means of obtaining it as conditioned on a long list of missions and on the circuit of events, which consolidated rarity as an internal marker of distinction relevant to the ecosystem, and not as a simple ordinary aesthetic variation Marshall(Marshall, 2018). The point in question is important because the “investment”, in this sense, does not translate only into pecuniary value, but also includes time, adherence to events and conformity with a specific path. Status, in this design, is produced by the architecture, because the platform decides who can obtain, when they can obtain and under what conditions the good circulates.

The conflict appears when the platform retroactively reprograms the regime of circulation of the good and allows items previously treated as rare to return to the market under a new acquisition system. In this scenario, the injury perceived by the original holders does not stem p. 453 from subjective preference, but from the alteration of the foundation that sustained part of the qualitative expectation of consumption, namely the public signal of exclusivity as a position. The institutional announcement of the mythic content reform itself recognizes the difference in exclusivity and difficulty of obtaining older Prestige skins456 and provides for their reintroduction within a new regime Riot Games(Riot Games, 2022j). From then on, what is at stake is not the “right to change the game”, but the use of the power of architecture to rewrite the semiotic value of the good, downgrading the originally managed rarity and, with that, emptying a relevant portion of the utility of the initial investment.

The institutional response is, methodologically, the most important part of the conflict, because, in the case at hand, it can confirm, from the company's own statements, that status is a material component of enjoyment and that, when the architecture changes, restoration may be needed. In a public notice, the developer states that, when old Prestige skins returned to the store, the original owners would receive exclusive modified versions “in recognition of the investment”, and that these modified versions will not be available for purchase with the new currency then introduced into the ecosystem, Mythic Essence Riot Games(Riot Games, 2022j)457. This is a symbolic restoration aimed directly at the point of loss, preserving a public mark of distinction for the original holders. In practice, this solution reveals the dogmatic threshold that matters to abusive teleology: when a change threatens to produce a relevant loss of position and recognition, the platform acknowledges that it is not enough to “evolve the system”, and tries to rebuild, by design, a minimum equivalence of enjoyment so as to avoid the perception of injury.

In sum, the episode stands out because it demonstrates that, in IOCs with high semiotic density, the content is not merely “access to a cosmetic”, but access to a socially p. 454 legible position. When the platform reorganizes the circulation of these goods, it reprograms the very distinctive value that formed part of the consumption decision. Abusive teleology becomes identifiable when this reorganization operates as a relevant loss without an equivalent route of preservation, or when the restoration offered is insufficient to maintain the core of enjoyment that justified the acquisition. The Prestige case is useful precisely because it shows, with institutional documentation, that governance itself acknowledges this dimension and acts to manage it when the redesign threatens to break the equivalence of the investment.

In parallel, the Eternals regime lends itself to serving as a test case for delimiting the threshold between legitimate modulation and abusive teleology, not because it is already abusive, but because the institutional offer itself describes the pass as an acquisition with a projection of future completeness, that is, as a good whose utility is not exhausted by present content. In presenting the Series Pass, the company makes explicit that the purchase would cover existing champions as well as champions released later, building continuity into the acquired enjoyment bundle Riot Games(Riot Games, 2022f)458.

Within this frame, abusive teleology would arise only in a specific scenario, namely if the system's architecture were redesigned to retroactively remove this structural attribute, reprogramming the product to require a new purchase in order to preserve the same completeness previously promised. The point would not be the creation of new series or ordinary adjustments to the ecosystem, but a redesign that turned continuity, previously communicated as a component of the package, into compulsory recurrence, shifting the practical purpose of the acquisition Riot Games(Riot Games, 2022g).

Should this removal occur, the relevant loss would not be merely economic. It would also be a loss of equivalence, because the consumer would have acquired a good whose utility included future projection, but would come to be compelled to finance again, by design, the p. 455 same level of enjoyment that had already been incorporated into the expectation legitimately formed by the supplier's communication Riot Games(Riot Games, 2022f).

The contractual frame helps to understand how a rupture of this type could be implemented without formal withdrawal of the service, since the contract normalizes the mutability of the environment, frames virtual content as a revocable license and admits alterations, restrictions and updates that may affect even virtual products Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e)459. This reinforces the criterion: the identification of the purposive deviation cannot be left to the fact that the environment is mutable, but would depend on the finding of relevant loss without equivalent restoration precisely when the utility projected into the future was presented as the core of the good acquired.

The fourth requirement is opacity combined with low contestability. It is not enough for the system to be complex or to operate with metrics. It is necessary that the mode of operation structurally hinder the understanding and challenge of relevant decisions, reducing the possibility of effective contestation, verifiable review, controllable justification or proportional reversal of effects. Opacity functions as a condition of effectiveness of the program when it prevents an informed reaction and limits the ordinary demonstration of the causal link between modulation and degradation of enjoyment.

The fifth requirement is a legally relevant effect on the enjoyment bundle. Abusive teleology must produce a substantial and persistent impact on the practical conditions of use and value of the acquired object, even if there is no total interruption. This effect may take the form of significant degradation of quality, accessibility, functionalities, conditions of participation and visibility, or of unilateral reconfigurations that alter the content and quality of performance to the consumer's detriment, including through cumulative restrictions that erode utility over time. The requirement prevents the category from being triggered by any fluctuation of the service and requires delimiting the core of the affected utility, distinguishing ordinary variations from those that structurally reconfigure enjoyment.

This definition and its test fulfill their function only if they are brought back to the dogmatic language of the CDC for long-term relationships, in which utility is realized over time and the supplier's relevant conduct manifests itself in performance. Claudia Lima Marques frames these arrangements as mass contractual relationships that produce complex and prolonged p. 456 legal relationships, marked by the consumer's position of captivity or dependence Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 77). In this type of structure, the contract cannot be treated as a static act, since it unfolds over time and radiates legal effects during performance, before and after it Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 223). For this reason, the expectations proper to these relationships rest more on trust and on maintaining the economic potential and quality of the service, requiring capacity for adaptation, cooperation and continuity of the relationship Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 106). Taken together, this makes it possible to qualify architectural modulations not as terrain indifferent to the contract, but as a plane of duties and expectations stabilized in performance.

On platforms, alteration frequently manifests itself as architectural reconfiguration, reordering of visibilities, cumulative restrictions and selective frictions that preserve formal access while degrading the enjoyment bundle. Abusive teleology fits exactly here, since it makes it possible to qualify as legally relevant conduct the architectural pattern that exploits continuity and dependence and converts the capacity for adaptation into a systematic instrument of extraction and control.

The cumulative structure of the test must, finally, be accompanied by a standard of controllability that prevents the flexibility of complex environments from turning into argumentative arbitrariness. Abusive teleology cannot function as a moral wild card. It must depend on the rational demonstration that the architecture is oriented, in a dominant way, toward the exploitation of vulnerability and the disproportionate capture of value, with persistent opacity and asymmetry, and not toward legitimate purposes of the service, such as security, stability and balance of the ecosystem.

With abusive teleology delimited as a criterion of attribution, the next step is to make explicit which relevant losses it produces in enjoyment and in the consumer's position in the ecosystem.
