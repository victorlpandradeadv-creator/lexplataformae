Your rights

What problem are you facing?

  • My account was blocked or banned. What about what I bought?When the account is blocked or banned, which is permanent expulsion, you also lose access to everything that was in it, including what you bought.
  • What I bought changed in an update. What now?An update, which is the change the company makes to the program after the sale, altered what you bought.
  • The service got worse, and the price is still the same. Can I complain?You pay the same as before, but you get less, because the service lost functions, tightened limits, got slower or started demanding more to deliver the same thing.
  • An item I received as a gift was removed from my account. What now?You received an item as a gift through the game's official function, and later it disappeared from your account because there was fraud somewhere along the way.
  • The service is going to end. What happens to what I bought?The company announced that it will shut down the game, the app or the service, or turn off the servers, which are its computers where everything runs.
  • The company says I only have a license. So I didn't buy anything?You paid, the button said buy, and the contract calls what you received a license, which is a permission to use that can be withdrawn.
  • What was advertised is not what I received. What now?You bought after seeing previews, countdowns, rarity badges or promises of prominence, and what arrived later does not match what all of this led you to expect.
  • I paid a lot for something exclusive, and it came back cheaper. Is that allowed?You paid more because the item was rare, a special edition or a last chance, and later it went back on sale for less, in another store or in another currency.
  • I paid for a draw or a loot box. What do I need to know?You paid to try your luck, in a loot box or an item draw, and what came out was almost never what you wanted.
  • The service's currency now buys less. Is that allowed?You bought internal currency with real money, such as a game's coins or an app's credits, and now the same item costs more coins.
  • The offer said last chance. Was I pressured into buying?The countdown, the limited edition or the notice that the item would disappear made you decide quickly, perhaps before understanding everything you were buying.
  • The rule that harmed me was buried in the terms of use. What now?The company says the rule was in the terms of use, which are the contract you accept when creating the account or when buying.
  • The records are with the company. How do I prove what happened?In most conflicts with platforms, it is the company, not you, that keeps the records of what happened.
  • I complain, and the response never fixes anything. What should I do?Customer service is kind, answers quickly and asks you to wait, but the problem persists, and you end up letting it go.
  • I want to leave, but I would lose everything I built there. Does that count?You think about leaving the game, the network or the service, but leaving means losing the rank, the reputation, the history and the ties that exist only inside it.
  • I lost years of history. Is that more than an item?A ban (permanent expulsion from the account), a progress reset or an erasure took away years of history, inventory, achievements and reputation.
  • The service requires a program that runs on my device. Can I question it?Some services only work if you install a program that runs in the background of your computer and starts up with it, even with the game closed.
  • My child spent money in a game. What does the law say?Your child spent money in a game, on small repeated purchases, on loot boxes or on offers that came back with every event.
  • The artificial intelligence assistant changed or got it wrong. What can I demand?You used an artificial intelligence assistant, the kind of program that chats, summarizes and gives guidance, and the answer turned out to be wrong or the service started working differently.
  • The online store hides who paid for placement and who is trustworthy. Does that count?In an online store with many sellers, the so-called marketplace, you choose among what the platform put up front, with the badges, ratings and highlights it organizes.
  • My reach dropped or I was punished on a social network. Is there anything I can ask for?On a social network, what you see and how many people see what you post, the so-called reach, depend on the platform's own rules.
  • I work through an app, and the rule changed. Is there anything I can ask for?You do deliveries or rides through an app and, after a rule change nobody explained, you got fewer requests, your earnings dropped or you were hit with a restriction.
  • I sell on a platform. Does consumer law protect me?You sell in an online store or use the platform to earn money, and you heard that consumer law does not apply to you because you have a business.
  • It happened to a lot of people. Can it be solved all at once?The same problem affected many people on the same service, and each person stayed on their own, thinking it was bad luck.

Where can you complain when the platform won't fix the problem?

A word of caution. None of this is a ready-made formula or a guarantee of results, because each case depends on what happened, on what can be shown and on how the person deciding reads the facts. What changes is how the conflict is told, which stops being an isolated episode and becomes a matter of how the platform supplies what it sold.
