4.2.6 Semiotic vulnerability and the demand for operational and institutional criteria

See this section in the PDF

Concepts in this section

  • Semiotic vulnerability

  • Foundations · Sections of chapter 4

p. 386

If the platform simultaneously governs meaning, access, reputation and sanction, the starting point is not “information” understood as content communicated to the consumer, but the regime of intelligibility of the ecosystem itself. What is in dispute, in the consumption of IOCs, is the possibility of enjoyment existing as a stable and recognizable position over time. This position depends on a grammar of signs that makes the experience legible and comparable, on access paths that make certain practices possible and others impracticable, on circuits of visibility that convert recognition into a condition of value and on devices of enforcement that reconfigure the continuity of enjoyment through restrictions, downgrades and exclusions. When these four dimensions are administered by the same decision center, vulnerability ceases to be merely an asymmetry regarding contractual data and becomes p. 387 structural dependence on the way the environment produces value, distributes belonging and decides permanence.

Semiotic vulnerability arises because, in this kind of supply, the object is not “worth” something only for the content delivered, but for the socially operable meaning that the ecosystem manages to stabilize around the user's position. The environment decides which signals will be sufficient to produce prestige, which metrics will define performance, which categories will organize comparability and which marks of trajectory will be preserved as recognized memory. Thus, the consumer faces not only the lack of access to information about price, risk or limitations, but the practical impossibility of mastering the vocabulary and the criteria that attribute economic meaning to what was acquired. The deficit is semiotic because it falls on the production of legibility and commensurability of the object itself; it is institutional because this production is concentrated, mutable and scarcely verifiable for those who bear its effects.

This vulnerability becomes more acute when the governance of meaning is coupled with the governance of access. The platform not only describes what the user “can” do, but implements the range of the possible through architecture: permissions, frictions, interface patterns, verification requirements, calendars, availability windows and interaction sequences. The consequence is that the consumer's formal autonomy turns into administered autonomy. Deviant conduct need not be prohibited; it suffices that it be made expensive, slow, invisible or technically impracticable. Access, here, is a normative-operational category: it defines what exists as a real possibility of enjoyment and, therefore, is part of the very content of the supply.

Next, the platform governs reputation and visibility as variables of value. The user's position in the ecosystem is not merely “being inside”, but being found, being considered eligible, being comparable and remaining recognizable under signals that circulate publicly. Rankings, performance metrics, signals of prestige, recommendations and forms of highlighting operate as the economic language of the environment: they convert attention into opportunity and convert recognition into permanence. In this layer, vulnerability is not an individual weakness, but rather the fact that the positional value of the IOC depends on internal criteria of classification and exposure that can be unilaterally recalibrated, without sufficient publicity of the grounds and without proportional means of contestation. Reputation, therefore, is not a side effect of consumption, but rather a governed component of the enjoyed object.

p. 388 The integration of these dimensions is completed by enforcement. Sanctions, downgrades and restrictions do not function as an exceptional response to a fully intelligible infraction, since they operate as gradual instruments of everyday governance. A decisive part of enforcement occurs through visibility and eligibility: remaining “inside”, but less findable, less rankable, less recommended and less recognized may amount to a relevant loss of the object in its functional and symbolic value. The sanction, in this arrangement, need not present itself as clear punishment to produce functional dispossession. It suffices to reorganize the conditions of access, reputation and continuity of enjoyment so as to degrade the acquired position, maintaining the formal bond while altering the practical core of what was enjoyable.

In view of this design, legal control cannot be reduced to a checklist of abusive clauses, because the injury may occur without textual alteration and without evident contractual breach. The effective shaping of the IOC is produced by continuous operations of the ecosystem, and the central risk is the unilateral reconfiguration of enjoyment by opaque operational criteria. Clauses are relevant, but do not exhaust the phenomenon, since the decisive power lies in the capacity to define and execute, through infrastructure, the parameters of legibility, comparability, eligibility and sanction. The focus of control needs to fall on the practical rationality of the regime: which criteria exist, how they are applied, how they can be verified and which avenues of contestation are effectively usable.

Hence the demand for operational and institutional criteria. Qualified transparency does not mean “more information” in the abstract, but intelligibility of the criteria that structure enjoyment: classification standards, recommendation parameters, minimum reasons for downgrades and restrictions, explanation of the type of conduct or signal that triggered the measure, and communication capable of enabling an informed response. Effective contestability requires that the challenge not be a mere ritual, but an avenue with deadlines, stages, access to minimum elements of the grounds and a real possibility of review. Verifiable reasoning is not justificatory rhetoric; it is the minimum form of transforming decisions into examinable objects, making it possible to control coherence, proportionality and non-discrimination.

Open auditability comes in as a requirement of external verifiability and of control over “how” the decision was produced. It is not a matter of demanding full exposure of trade secrets, but of making decisive categories auditable, including logs and records of measures applied, decision trails, general eligibility criteria, error statistics, correction mechanisms, and guarantees that the system does not operate by irrecoverable arbitrariness. Auditability is “open” because it needs to be compatible with scrutiny beyond the unilateral p. 389 account of the platform itself, lest contestation remain blind and the conflict remain trapped in the result.

Functional value equivalence operates as a yardstick to capture the point at which the platform preserves the formal appearance of the supply, but degrades its real utility. If the IOC is an enjoyment position in a governed environment, reducing functionalities, limiting relevant access, imposing asymmetric frictions, downgrading eligibility or reorganizing visibility may substantially alter what the consumer is able to do, even without “removing” the nominal object. Functional equivalence makes it possible to frame these mutations as a possible loss of the object in its economic core, requiring restoration when the enjoyment delivered ceases to correspond, in operational terms, to the legitimately expected level.

Integrity and identity complete the path because, in the IOC regime, they are not mere subjective adornments, but rather structural dimensions of value when the ecosystem governs belonging and continuity through reputation, social memory and recognized trajectory. Integrity, here, concerns the minimally consistent preservation of the conditions of enjoyment and participation that allow the consumer to remain legible and operative, without arbitrary reconfigurations that destroy the practical unity of the experience. Identity, in turn, designates the recognizable continuity of a position built through records, repertoire, signals of prestige and bonds, whose destruction through downgrades or exclusions may produce functional dispossession and collapse of commensurability. When these elements are administered by the infrastructure, they become legally relevant as part of the protected object: the platform administers not only access to utilities, but the possibility of continuity of a socially recognized position that was economically built within the ecosystem itself.

Thus, the transition from semiotic vulnerability to operational criteria is not rhetoric of protective expansion. It is a reconstruction of the point of incidence of control: from the textual promise to the effective regime of value production, in which meaning, access, reputation and sanction are integrated as a mechanism of private governance. If the power that reconfigures enjoyment is exercised through architecture and through information, the legally adequate response requires parameters that also operate at this level: intelligibility of criteria, verifiability, contestability, auditability, functional equivalence and mechanisms of restoration proportional to the density of the dominion exercised.
