7.2.7 From trust administration to the neutralization of conflicts by architecture

See this section in the PDF

This section in the layers of the site

  • Platforms · Platform family

p. 780

The application of the regime of the Immaterial Object of Consumption to environments of trust administration starts from a structural fact that usually escapes the more traditional legal reading. In platform-governed ecosystems, the conflict does not begin when the user decides to litigate, complain or formalize disagreement. In many cases, it is governed before that. The architecture of the environment, the interface language, the response protocols, the communicational tone, the signs of closeness and the ordinary management of expectation p. 781 can operate as mechanisms for the prior absorption of critical resistance, the accommodation of loss and the practical neutralization of contestation. The utility supplied, in these cases, is not limited to the main object of consumption. It also includes a functionally administered regime of trust, by which the environment organizes the way the user interprets friction, metabolizes frustration and decides whether or not the experience suffered deserves institutional translation.

This point is important because it prevents the analysis of the supply from remaining confined to the written contract, to the formally provided information or to the isolated clause governing a given aspect of the relationship. On platforms, trust is not a spontaneous by-product of good performance of the service. Frequently, it is produced, calibrated and maintained by design. The cordial interface, the assistive tone, the diffuse promise of care, merely performative transparency, the appearance of communicational horizontality and the calculated personalization of language materially make up the environment of enjoyment. They do not function merely as brand ornament. They function as a technique for governing perception, organizing the threshold from which loss will be perceived as acceptable, abuse will be read as a minor failure and contestation will be perceived as unnecessary, premature or disproportionate.

That is why, in this family, trust administration must be treated as a functional dimension of the supply itself. The user does not consume only access, utility, content or performance. They consume an environment that presents itself as intelligible, assistive, responsive and, to some extent, worthy of practical credit. This trust need not take the solemn form of an express guarantee to produce relevant legal effects. It is enough that it act in a stable way on the ordinary interpretation of the experience and on the user's willingness to endure friction, accept vague justifications, normalize losses and remain in the cycle of use even when enjoyment has already been functionally degraded. The platform, in this context, does not administer only initial adherence. It administers tolerance of harm and the user's energy to react.

This administration becomes especially sensitive in environments of continuous consumption, in which the relationship is not exhausted in a single act of acquisition and in which small frustrations can be absorbed, reinterpreted and reintegrated into the ordinary flow of the experience. Loss ceases to appear as an exceptional event and comes to be metabolized as part of the normal functioning of the system. The same happens with friction. Instead of promptly triggering a reading of inadequacy or abusiveness, it can be reconfigured by the platform as a technical adjustment, a temporary limitation, an understandable inconvenience, a security measure, a necessary update or a simple stage of use. The language of the environment, in this process, does not p. 782 merely describe the frustration. It administers it. And, by administering it, it redefines the very time at which the conflict emerges institutionally.

In this key, trust ceases to be a psychologically vague element and becomes a precise dogmatic problem. What matters is not whether the user “felt” more or less welcomed, but whether the platform structured signs, routines and protocols capable of shifting the reading of loss, reducing the critical force of the experience and weakening the probability of intelligible contestation. Persona, operational cordiality, messages of closeness, excessively reassuring simplifications, answers with an explanatory appearance and service circuits designed to absorb disagreement without effective reconstruction of the problem can operate, together, as a technique of demobilization. Trust, thus, is no longer merely subjective credit deposited by the user. It is a private mechanism for organizing the reception of harm.

The central vulnerability of this subtype is, once again, semiotic vulnerability, but now intensified by the management of trust as a form of modulating resistance. The user is not only facing an opaque environment or criteria they do not control. They are facing an ecosystem that produces signals of intelligibility and care sufficient to induce accommodation, even when the materiality of enjoyment has already been compromised. The result is relevant. Contestation can be postponed, demobilized or dissolved before it acquires institutional form. The conflict does not disappear. It is governed in its pre-legal phase, through mechanisms that turn loss into tolerable noise, opacity into normality and asymmetry into absorbable experience.

This point calls for caution so as not to turn all corporate cordiality into unlawfulness. The problem does not lie in the use of accessible language, in polite customer service or in the legitimate attempt to maintain a functionally stable relationship with the user. The problem begins when these elements come to form, structurally, part of a regime of practical neutralization of conflict. The platform may, for example, offer a sense of transparency without delivering real intelligibility, produce an impression of listening without opening effective avenues of contestation, mobilize a human tone without assuming proportional duties of reasoning or justification and distribute signs of care that function less as adequate information and more as semiotic cushioning of criticism. In these cases, trust administration ceases to be a quality of the service and becomes a technology for containing resistance.

The evidence compatible with this subtype requires reconstruction of the discursive and procedural environment in which frustration becomes legible or, on the contrary, is diluted. This includes analysis of the interface language, the customer service flows, the response templates, the p. 783 explanation routines, the way the platform frames loss and the stages through which the user is led to accept, wait, relativize or close the disagreement. It also requires attention to recurring blind spots. The first is the tendency to consider the discursive dimension of the supply irrelevant, as if only clauses and formal acts produced legal effects. The second is the tendency to treat trust as a private feeling of the user, and not as a functional arrangement of the environment. The third is the tendency to suppose that the conflict begins only when an external complaint or litigation arises, ignoring that the platform can govern precisely the moment at which loss becomes institutionally perceptible.

The possible dogmatic consequence, in this subtype, is not reduced to a classical informational defect nor exhausted in misleading advertising in the strict sense, although it may engage with both. The problem tends to gravitate around qualified informational insufficiency, abusive practice through opaque administration of expectation and, in denser cases, abusive teleology when the neutralization of contestation proves to be integrated into the ordinary functioning of the ecosystem. The legal response compatible with this framework tends to require more than simple retrospective reparation. It may call for correction of customer service flows, reinforcement of intelligibility, prohibition of functionally misleading discursive routines, procedural duties of reasoning and the design of avenues of contestation that serve not merely to absorb disagreement, but to make it effectively processable and verifiable.

The usefulness of the subtype lies precisely in making visible a form of governance that usually escapes the radar of traditional control. In many environments, the platform does not need to formally prevent conflict in order to reduce it. It is enough to administer the way it is perceived. It is enough to organize tolerance, semiotic pacification and the metabolization of friction within the very cycle of enjoyment. The control of the IOC, therefore, cannot remain tied to formal information or to the written contract. It must reach the way the environment produces meaning, distributes trust and governs the very practical emergence of contestation. It is on this plane that the neutralization of conflict by architecture ceases to look like a mere communicational detail and reveals itself as a material element of the governed supply.
