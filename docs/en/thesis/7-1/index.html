7.1 On the parameters of controllability of the IOC

See this section in the PDF

p. 714

The passage from the regime of the Immaterial Object of Consumption to its incidence in different families of platform requires an operative grid capable of preserving dogmatic unity in distinct contexts. When continuous immaterial consumption is organized by private architectures of access, visibility, permanence, curation and ordinary mutation, the conflict cannot be resolved by the appearance of the sector nor by the degree of social familiarity with the technology involved. It is necessary to bring the case back to parameters dense enough to stabilize institutional expectations, guide the formation of evidence and allow consistent normative consequences.

This requirement stems from the very nature of the bond. In platform-governed ecosystems, enjoyment is not exhausted in a one-off act of delivery, nor can it be understood as a neutral reality on which only lateral contractual clauses bear. What is offered to the consumer is an experience legally shaped by continuous mechanisms of private organization, whose effects fall on access, intelligibility, equivalence, permanence and contestation. For this reason, the controllability of the regime does not depend on inventorying technologies or classifying markets, but on establishing criteria that make legible the power structure that modulates enjoyment. Without this mediation, application degrades into descriptive casuistry or impressionistic judgment, oscillating between uncritical fascination and generic aversion to technology.

The parameters synthesized at the end of the preceding dogmatic reconstruction then take on a properly operative function. They no longer present themselves as mere moments of theoretical p. 715 elaboration, but as references applicable to the institutional reading of the conflict. Their usefulness lies in reducing dependence on the interpreter's subjective repertoires and in containing the tendency, recurrent in digital litigation, to decide on the basis of vague impressions about innovation, technical complexity or business freedom. The digital condition, by itself, decides nothing. Nor does the sectoral label of the service. What decides, legally, is the way in which the environment organizes private power, redistributes capacities of understanding and contestation and produces relevant frictions on the consumption experience.

The application of these parameters is guided by a repeatable sequence. One starts from the predominant governance mechanism and the type of friction it produces. The central vulnerability triggered in the bond is identified. The standard of evidence compatible with the structure of the conflict is established. Finally, the possible dogmatic consequence under the IOC regime is indicated.

The sequence works as a controllability lock. Its function is to prevent both the decomposition of the conflict into isolated clauses, one-off events or contingent failures, as if the relationship could be understood from fragments detached from a structurally governed experience, and the replacement of legal analysis by a diffuse impression about technology, innovation or technical complexity, in which the interpreter oscillates between excessive deference and generic distrust without managing to convert the environment examined into normatively tractable facts. Both distortions result from the same error of perspective. They ignore that, in continuous digital services, enjoyment is not an external consequence of the contract, but a permanent space of private shaping of the experience.

The first task, therefore, consists in recognizing the presence of an immaterial object of consumption subject to continuous, reconfigurable or modulable provision. This recognition does not stem from the name of the service, the social image of the sector or the mere presence of a digital interface. It stems from the legally relevant way in which access, permanence, intelligibility, equivalence and contestation are organized throughout enjoyment. Once this regime is identified, it becomes possible to qualify semiotic vulnerability not as a subjective attribute of the user nor as an imprecise environmental label, but as a mechanism of power bearing on the bond. It is this qualification that makes it possible to reorganize duties and unlawful acts around informational adequacy, advertising, legibility and governance standards of the environment, without reducing the question to individual cognitive insufficiency or to a moral defect of technology.

On this basis, the legitimacy filters built on the dogmatic plane come into operation, with open auditability, functional value equivalence and p. 716 abusive teleology at their center. These filters make it possible to distinguish legitimate modulations of provision from changes that structurally degrade enjoyment, obscure the intelligibility of the bond or incorporate an exploitative orientation incompatible with the limits of the CDC and with the constitutional containment of private power by architecture. Their relevance lies in providing verifiable attribution criteria, capable of organizing decisional reasoning without requiring exhaustive technical mastery of the ecosystem by the judge. This is not a matter of freezing innovation, nor of requiring absolute transparency about all of the supplier's internal processes. It is a matter of imposing sufficient parameters of institutional translation so that governance, mutability and friction can be brought back to discernible duties, unlawful acts, evidentiary burdens and remedies.

The requirement, consequently, is not the exhaustive observation of all contemporary digital markets, but the demonstration that the regime retains a capacity for controllable incidence when confronted with diverse arrangements of continuous supply by platform. What one seeks to verify is whether these parameters manage to produce legal qualification, organize evidence and guide normative consequence in distinct environments, without losing precision and without depending on intuitive impressions about the sector examined. Therein lies their relevance and also their pertinence. They do not have the power to replace the complexity of the real, but they prevent this complexity from being used as an alibi for irresponsibility over architecture.
