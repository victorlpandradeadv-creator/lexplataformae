6.1.2 On the horizontal effect of fundamental rights and the public relevance of the powers of the Lex Plataformae

p. 512

The horizontal effect of fundamental rights ceases to look like an abstract theoretical question once one observes that, in contemporary private relations, relevant subjection no longer comes only from the State. It also arises from private centers of power capable of organizing access, permanence, visibility, reputation, evidence and contestation on a scale sufficient to affect, structurally, the practical freedom and material equality of the person subject to the relation. In such contexts, private autonomy cannot serve as a screen shielding materially asymmetric arrangements, because the Constitution does not merely restrain public power but also radiates effects onto bonds between private parties when the concentration of power becomes legally relevant Sarmento(Sarmento, 2010, p. 51; p. 258; p. 285)502. The private relation remains private, but the fantasy vanishes that this formal p. 513 qualification, by itself, would make it immune to the normative force of fundamental rights Sarlet(Sarlet, 2018, p. 528-529)503.

This reinforced application does not require conceptually turning the platform into a state entity, nor confusing company with public power. The legally relevant point lies in the material density of the power exercised and in the concrete effects it produces on the sphere of the other party to the relation. When a private agent concentrates enough economic, technical, informational and organizational power to condition choices, restrict freedoms and reconfigure the user's practical position, the private nature of the bond is no longer enough to push the Constitution off the stage Sarmento(Sarmento, 2010, p. 61; p. 209; p. 298)504. It is precisely for this reason that fundamental rights apply more intensely in relations between private parties in settings of structural asymmetry, in which contractual freedom no longer expresses any minimally real parity and comes instead to cover the material dependence of the weaker side Santos(Santos, 2019, p. 15; p. 112; p. 132)505.

p. 514 It is within this framework that the Lex Plataformae must be understood. It cannot be reduced to the contract, nor does it merge with an isolated technical instrument, because it designates the coordinated set of rules, procedures and operational standards through which the platform structures the continued enjoyment of the object consumed. Terms of use, internal policies, interface, reputational systems, updates, support and enforcement do not appear there as loose pieces, but as a private regime that orders the consumption experience in practice. The platform, therefore, does not merely offer utilities; it governs the very conditions of access, permanence, equivalence, intelligibility and contestation of enjoyment, distributing information, evidentiary capacity and powers of unilateral modulation asymmetrically. This is enough to dispel the naive image of the contract as a neutral meeting of equivalent wills and to put the problem back where it really lies: in the legal containment of a private power structurally preponderant over the consumer Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 42; p. 162)506.

The clauses of the ecosystem examined are themselves what make this phenomenon too empirical to be treated as an abstraction. Riot reserves the power to terminate or suspend accounts on the basis of criteria it formulates itself, including with regard to the “best interest” of its community or its services507.

p. 515 It also provides for bans, suspension, account deletion, hardware bans, content removal, progress resets and functional restrictions508, extends its discipline to off-platform uses linked to game content509 and radiates penalties from one service to another within the same ecosystem510.

What we face, therefore, is not mere technical entertainment infrastructure, but a private center of governance that disciplines permanence, history, visibility, progression and contestation across an integrated network. The public relevance of this power does not arise from grandiloquent metaphor, but from the brute fact that it organizes, on a continuing basis, the consumer's practical position in the digital environment.

For this reason, the horizontal effect of fundamental rights does not enter here as an artificial graft, but as a constitutional response to the intensity of the private power actually exercised. Private autonomy, read constitutionally, is not an abstract safe-conduct for the material subjection of the user, above all when inequality of power turns formal freedom into an elegant fiction Sarmento(Sarmento, 2010, p. 183; p. 286)511. For the same reason, the reinforced p. 516 application of fundamental rights in the private sphere does not depend on any rite turning the private party into a “quasi-State”; it is enough to recognize that the threat to freedom, to material equality and to dignity may come from holders of social and economic power intense enough to produce relevant subjection Sarlet(Sarlet, 2018, p. 527; p. 529; p. 530)512. Put simply: the platform remains a private party, but that does not give it a constitutional license to operate as a small jurisdiction immune to control.

The point gains density when one recalls that the Constitution does not tolerate forms of marginalization, blocking and discrimination merely because private agents practice them. The promotion of the good of all without discrimination513, the protection of honor, image and the personal sphere514, the inviolability of intimacy, private life, honor and image515, the right of access to the courts516 and the prohibition on deprivation of property without due process517 form, here, a decisive constitutional framework. In platform environments, exclusions, suspensions, removals, reputational erasures and invasive monitoring cannot be treated as technical noise or simple “internal administration of the service”, because they affect the consumer's biographical trajectory, informational integrity, practical permanence in the ecosystem and very capacity to respond to the power that governs them. The problem p. 517 is not only patrimonial; it is also one of personality, of practical freedom and of the controllability of private power.

In the digital environment, this reading gains even more density because the exercise of private power also runs through surveillance and the collection, processing and exploitation of personal data. Data are not neutral matter for market circulation, but legally relevant projections of personality, whose protection is tied to human dignity and to informational self-determination Laprano(Canavez; Andrade; Laprano, 2021, p. 155-156)518. The information society itself, by intensifying private structures of surveillance and behavioral analysis, called for new fundamental rights to be made explicit and for data protection to be reinforced in response to the practical erosion of privacy Mendes(Canavez; Santos; Mendes, 2022, p. 69-70; p. 78-79; p. 82- 83)519. When private agents accumulate the capacity to monitor, infer and modulate behavior in depth, what we face is no longer simple, neutral business freedom, but a socially relevant form of power over fundamental goods of the person.

Data protection and the protection of personality rights help show, in contemporary Brazil, that the reinforced application of fundamental rights to digitally mediated private powers is no longer a remote hypothesis. The point is to recognize that private structures of digital governance bear on privacy, personality, freedom p. 518 and self-determination with enough intensity to justify reinforced legal containment Laprano(Canavez; Andrade; Laprano, 2021, p. 141; p. 152; p. 155)520. Along the same lines, massive data exploitation, behavioral analysis and corporate surveillance show that these private powers now operate over central spheres of autonomy to a constitutionally relevant degree, calling for a more robust normative response Mendes(Canavez; Santos; Mendes, 2022, p. 70; p. 72; p. 78)521. The Lex Plataformae governs not only access and use; it may also govern the user's perception, predictability, exposure and margins of self-determination.

All this explains why the horizontal effect of fundamental rights, although indispensable, is still not enough on its own as an operational grammar for the problem. This constitutional containment must be carried back into a dogmatic language of control capable of reaching, concretely, architecture, practices, information, sanctions and evidentiary regimes. That is where consumer law comes in, not as a sectoral appendix, but as normative infrastructure for containing the asymmetries the Lex Plataformae produces. The CDC starts precisely from the recognition that the supplier holds a structurally preponderant position in defining the terms of the bond, organizing information, modulating performance and allocating risks, which is why vulnerability, good faith and balance operate as a concrete p. 519 normative translation of the constitutional requirement to limit private power in the consumer relation Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 274; p. 324)522.

Read this way, the consumer contract is no longer seen as a neutral space of self-regulation and comes to be understood as a field for containing the supplier's dominant position. Objective good faith does not enter as decorative moralism, but as a technique for limiting the abusive exercise of private prerogatives, especially when they are projected onto a structurally vulnerable consumer Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 111; p. 221)523. The platform may shape its service, but it may not turn that shaping into private sovereignty over the user's position. Likewise, the CDC's control of abusive clauses and practices already offers enough language to bring the problem of platform governance down to the ground of positive law, typifying abuses, repressing exaggerated disadvantages and weakening the fiction that everything accepted en masse has become legally legitimate Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 741; p. 370)524.

p. 520 This consumer-law grammar comes clearly into view once one looks at the normative doors opened by the statutory provisions themselves. Strict liability for defects in the service and for insufficient or inadequate information525, the rules on quality defects that render the service unfit or diminish its value526 and the prohibition of abusive practices that take advantage of the consumer's weakness, impose excessive advantage or leave performance of the obligation to the supplier's sole discretion527 show that the problem of the Lex Plataformae need not be framed only as a lofty conflict between Constitution and market. It can already be grasped, operationally, as defect, inadequacy, abuse, exploitation of vulnerability and unilateral manipulation of the conditions of enjoyment. This shifts the focus from isolated clauses to structural patterns of governance, verifiability and reasoning.

The methodological consequence is decisive. The public relevance of the powers of the Lex Plataformae does not authorize confusing platform with State, but it prevents its governance from being read as an internal, legally neutral business detail. When a private supplier organizes, through design and enforcement, the conditions of access, permanence, equivalence, visibility, sanction and contestation of an ongoing consumption experience, legal control cannot remain a prisoner of the contractual surface. It must reach the architecture of the relation and reintroduce into it criteria of controllability, reasoning and verifiability. Without this, opacity, unilateral change and systematic obstacles to contestation no longer appear as pathologies and come to be naturalized as the ordinary condition of digital consumption. It is precisely this naturalization that is rejected here.

The public relevance of the powers of the Lex Plataformae thus also becomes evident because private surveillance and mass data processing already operate, in the contemporary informational environment, as phenomena capable of justifying reinforced constitutional containment. ↩

Data protection, in this key, ceases to be a purely market question and comes to operate as a form of defense of the person against private forms of interference in their informational and decision-making life. In this direction, the protection of personality rights is not exhausted in their enactment in the Civil Code, also having the stature of fundamental rights, which allows data protection itself to be understood as a form of defense of these rights Laprano(Canavez; Andrade; Laprano, 2021, p. 152). The same reasoning is reinforced when the problem is situated in the tension between an impersonal market and the protection of the fundamental rights of the natural person submitted to such influences, in a perspective of repersonalization of private law Laprano(Canavez; Andrade; Laprano, 2021, p. 141). In cases of justifiable vulnerability, even the immediate use of the fundamental norm, in its pure form, is admitted to make possible the effective rebalancing between the parties Laprano(Canavez; Andrade; Laprano, 2021, p. 155). The limitation of digitally mediated private powers can be built through the articulation of personality rights, data protection, vulnerability and horizontal effect. ↩
  20. Digitally mediated private powers produce sufficient impact on privacy, behavior, personality and autonomy to justify reinforced legal containment in light of fundamental rights. When private structures accumulate the capacity to infer, monitor and exploit such deep dimensions of individuals' lives, one is no longer facing mere neutral business freedom and moves onto the terrain of the exercise of socially relevant power over fundamental goods. This reading deepens when one recognizes, in dialogue with Shoshana Zuboff, that the power derived from the extraction and analysis of personal data reaches the capacity to shape human behavior, requiring responses of containment and regulation of surveillance capitalism Mendes(Canavez; Santos; Mendes, 2022, p. 72). In addition, the existence of abuses committed by technology companies reinforces the need for more robust protection of data processing relations, including in digital media Mendes(Canavez; Santos; Mendes, 2022, p. 78).   21. The containment of private powers in the consumer environment does not depend only on abstract constitutional theory, because it already finds operable language within the CDC itself. The consumer's subjective right to contractual balance stems directly from consumer-law principles, in particular good faith, vulnerability and balance itself. Because of the consumer's vulnerability before the supplier, who holds a dominant position in the contract, the CDC establishes a series of norms limiting the supplier's private power, through clear State legislative intervention in contractual autonomy, aimed at protecting the interests of the vulnerable party Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 274). This reading gains density when one recognizes that, in contemporary society, the consumer relation presents itself less as an expression of contractual freedom and more as the fulfillment of a need to consume. In this context, the notion of abuse translates the prevalence of the supplier's dominant position over the consumer's vulnerability, good faith being the central legal brake on the exercise of this negotiating superiority Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 324). The public relevance of the powers of the Lex Plataformae, therefore, need not remain suspended in a theoretical cloud: it can already be grasped in a key of structural vulnerability, good faith, balance and limitation of abuse within the real CDC. ↩
  22. Objective good faith, in the consumer contract, does not act as a moral ornament, but as a concrete technique for containing the abusive exercise of private prerogatives, especially when these are projected onto a structurally vulnerable party. In contexts of informational asymmetry, practical dependence and unilateral predetermination of the rules of the relationship, consumer protection requires that the exercise of contractual powers be subject to standards of loyalty, reasonableness, economic and social purpose and respect for the trust legitimately placed in the relation. This is exactly the point Marques develops when she states that "the notion of objective good faith, as a new principle guiding the conduct of the contracting parties in captive contracts, means a new and important limitation on the exercise of subjective rights", so that the exercise of the right will be abusive when diverted from its economic and social function or exercised in disloyal circumstances Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 111). The author adds that good faith has a limiting function, "reducing the freedom of action of the contractual partners" and controlling abusive conduct and clauses Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 221). In a platform environment, this prevents the freedom to shape the relationship from being converted into private sovereignty over the consumer's position. ↩
  23. The control of abusive clauses and practices functions, in the CDC, as a direct technique for containing structural asymmetries between consumer and supplier. Arts. 39 and 51 make up protective mechanisms designed precisely to equalize a factually unequal relation, typifying abusive practices and sanctioning abusive clauses with nullity by operation of law, in a clear mitigation of pacta sunt servanda Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 741). Abusiveness, in this key, does not appear as a peripheral deviation or a moral correction of occasional excesses, but as a legislative instrument for repressing the disloyal exercise of dominant private positions. This control thickens when one perceives that abuse of right, in consumer law, corresponds above all to the abuse of a dominant legal position of the supplier in the consumer market. It is the exploitation of this superior position over the consumer's vulnerability that qualifies certain practices and stipulations as abusive, whether on the plane of conduct or on the plane of contractual content that imposes exaggerated disadvantage on the adhering party Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 370). In practical terms, the containment of the platform need not be built only through lofty constitutional mediation: good faith, abuse of dominant position, exaggerated disadvantage and the nullity of abusive clauses already offer sufficient grammar to bring it down to the ground of the CDC. ↩
