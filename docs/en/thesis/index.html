Do regime jurídico do Objeto Imaterial de Consumo (OIC) sob a Lex Plataformae: a tutela da vulnerabilidade semiótica do consumidor à luz da ordem constitucional e econômica do Brasil

Download the full text as PDF

Abstract

This thesis investigates the interactive entertainment industry, organized through platform ecosystems and continuously provided electronic games, as a techno-legal infrastructure with significant impact on everyday life. It identifies the emergence of a private ordering grounded in architecture, curation, sanction, and unilateral mutation, here termed Lex Plataformae, whose operation challenges the traditional framework of the Brazilian Consumer Law, still oriented by a material ontology centered on discrete products and services, one-time deliveries, and static standards of conformity. It argues that such a framework fails to adequately capture the risks of platform-governed continuous enjoyment, since, in these markets, the consumed good does not appear as a stable thing, but rather as a functional and semiotic arrangement continuously modulated by the supplier. The thesis proposes the category of the Immaterial Object of Consumption (IOC) as an infrastructural concept capable of giving concrete form, within platform environments, to the notion of immaterial product set forth in Article 3, paragraph 1, of the Brazilian Consumer Protection Code as a governed enjoyment bundle. From this formulation onward, legal protection no longer focuses solely on the formal regularity of performance, but comes to encompass the functional integrity of the acquired object and the personal and symbolic integrity of the consumer. Within this framework, factual vulnerability is also reinterpreted in its semiotic dimension, insofar as the asymmetry involves a structural deficit in the consumer’s capacity to understand, anticipate, and control the regimes of signs, interfaces, functional promises, and symbolic rewards that compose the value of the IOC. On this basis, the thesis formulates the doctrine of the unlawful act of abusive teleology, under which abusiveness may arise not only from episodic failures, but from the very purpose inscribed in the architecture of the business model when designed to exploit the consumer’s semiotic vulnerability. In addition, it defends open auditability and functional value equivalence as criteria for legitimacy, controllability, and redress regarding unilateral mutations imposed over time. It further argues that the problem exceeds a strictly contractual and consumer-law grammar and reaches the constitutional level, insofar as it involves infrastructural private powers capable of affecting legally relevant consumer positions within environments of social participation governed by corporate actors. Methodologically, the research combines theoretical-constitutional reconstruction, consumer-law dogmatics, and a functional reinterpretation of intellectual property, in dialogue with critical theory of technology, platform sociology, political economy, anthropology of consumption, and semiotics. The Riot Games: League of Legends ecosystem is used as a locus of referenced exemplification through documentary analysis and situated observation of the functioning of the service and its ecosystem, without any claim to exhaustive empirical demonstration. In conclusion, the thesis argues that interactive entertainment IOCs must be recognized as legally protectable objects in contexts of continuous enjoyment and as loci of social participation under private infrastructure, thus becoming subject to a protective regime oriented by the object’s functional integrity, the consumer’s symbolic integrity, constitutional consumer protection, and the social function of intellectual property.
