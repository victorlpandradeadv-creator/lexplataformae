6 On the protection of the consumer's semiotic vulnerability in light of Brazil's constitutional and economic order

See this section in the PDF

This section in the layers of the site

  • Dogmatics · Opening of chapter 6

Epigraph

Zélia Duncan, Diz Nos Meus Olhos (Inclemência) (2005). What it announces · Read the epigraph in the PDF.

p. 501

The legal protection of continuous digital consumption demands more than the mechanical extension of consolidated categories to new realities. It demands identifying, with dogmatic precision, what kind of power operates when the enjoyment of an immaterial object depends on mutable private architecture, permanent curation and regimes of signs that organize perception, expectation and behavior. The problem no longer lets itself be satisfactorily grasped by the classical grammar of the product or the service, conceived under assumptions of stability, one-off delivery and a defect knowable by relatively direct inspection. In platform ecosystems, the experience is administered in flow, value is modulated over time and loss may arise not as an exceptional failure, but as an ordinary effect of design.

Thus, semiotic vulnerability is not to be confused with a psychological trait of the consumer, a generic synonym of digital fragility or a mere environmental description of networked life. It is a structural mechanism of power, bearing on the production of meaning, the intelligibility of the offer, the comparability between alternatives, the verifiability of enjoyment and the very conditions for contesting loss. When the architecture of the environment organizes what can be perceived, promised, inferred and proven, the asymmetry ceases to reside only in missing information and comes to operate also in the form, in the context and in the regime of visibility in which that information appears. The central question is no longer only whether something was informed, but whether it could be understood, contrasted and effectively verified in terms compatible with consumer protection.

The proposed reconstruction starts from this mutation. Instead of fitting the immaterial object of consumption into prior categories by hasty analogy, it seeks to bring back into controllable dogmatic language what is frequently naturalized as technical necessity, a rule of the system or an inevitable consequence of the infrastructure. With this, private choices of modulation, curation, opacity and sanction cease to figure as a neutral backdrop to the consumer relationship and come to be examined as a qualifiable exercise of private economic power. The constitutional order, the Consumer Protection Code and the functional limits of intellectual property together offer the criteria for this reconduction, provided they are read in light of continuous enjoyment, of the functional integrity of the object and of the centrality of the consumption environment as a space of normative incidence.

The stage now beginning thus develops a decisive shift: from the appearance of technical neutrality to the legal knowability of design; from the mere making available of p. 502 information to the requirement of legibility and verifiability; from abusiveness understood as an isolated episode to abusiveness incorporated into the purpose of the environment. It is along this path that both the need for criteria such as open auditability and functional value equivalence and the figure of abusive teleology become visible, the latter understood as the incorporation, into the very operating logic of the object, of dynamics of inducement, extraction and normalization of losses. What is at stake is not merely correcting peripheral excesses, but delimiting, in a constitutional and consumer-law key, how far the private governance of the consumption experience may go when it comes to structure the very field of the possible for the consumer.
