5.2.1 On games-as-a-service, marketplaces and other IOC ecosystems

See this section in the PDF

  • Dogmatics · Sections of chapter 5

p. 485

Dividing the field by digitally based business models requires delimiting, first of all, the type of ecosystem in which the immaterial object of consumption manifests itself most clearly. In environments sustained by continued enjoyment, the acquired utility is not realized as an isolated event, because it depends on a technical-organizational arrangement in permanent operation, in which infrastructure, interfaces, rules of use, validation mechanisms and sanction instruments are part of the very experience of enjoyment. In this configuration, the consumed object does not present itself as a detachable thing, but as a situation of enjoyment whose practical stability is conditioned by private governance and by ordinary modulations of the environment.

The framing has a strictly operative purpose. It seeks to fix minimum parameters for comparing distinct objects and arrangements of supply in digital ecosystems, preventing the empirical analysis from degrading into a catalog of peculiarities and preventing the dogmatic reconstruction from resting on excessively general categories, incapable of discriminating relevant losses, asymmetries of agency and materially significant variations of utility and value. For this reason, the description that follows is deliberately guided by recurring structures, with no claim to exhaustiveness and no attribution of generic intentions to platforms.

An IOC ecosystem can be described as a technical-organizational arrangement that sustains and conditions enjoyment over time, integrating continued operation, private governance and update cycles that define, in practical terms, what counts as admissible use, recognized performance, displayable collection and preserved or degraded position in the system. In these terms, the consumed object is not reduced to isolated content nor to an abstract promise of access. Its utility depends on conditions of enjoyment built and maintained by the ecosystem itself.

The first structural trait of this type of ecosystem is continued enjoyment. It shifts the focus from one-off delivery to maintaining the conditions of use over time. The acquired utility is realized through a sequence of acts of operation, updating, curation and enforcement of rules, so that continuity of the bond and minimum predictability of the conditions of enjoyment form part of the material content of supply. This structure changes how relevant losses are recognized: instead of depending on a single event of “access cut off”, the loss may occur through cumulative erosions of utility, functional downgrades and gradual shifts in the cost of remaining, even if access remains formally available.

The second decisive trait is the private governance of the environment of enjoyment. The platform does not merely enable access. It defines, operationally, the conditions of remaining and the p. 486 forms of use of the object. Conduct policies, eligibility criteria, performance parameters, ranking mechanisms, visibility rules, validation procedures and sanction regimes make up a normative set incorporated into the architecture of the environment. This set does not regulate only externalities, since it determines, directly, how utility manifests itself, is maintained and can be restricted, and concentrates centers of practical decision that modulate enjoyment under structural asymmetries of information.

The third common trait is the constant modulation of access and value. In digital ecosystems, update cycles, interface reconfigurations, parameter adjustments and revisions of internal policies are an ordinary part of the functioning of the environment. Under these conditions, access is not expressed merely as a binary of availability. The way of enjoying depends on criteria that can be changed without the object formally ceasing to exist. Changes in rules of use, in eligibility criteria, in validation mechanisms and in visibility devices reorganize the content of enjoyment, defining what it is possible to do, display, accumulate or convert into utility within the system. Modulation, moreover, can reconfigure the value associated with the object, even when access remains intact, through changes in balancing, rarity, criteria for obtaining and reputational metrics.

The fourth trait is the structural dependence of the consumer on the ecosystem. Account, credentials, servers, use policies, validation mechanisms and internal visibility criteria constitute conditions of possibility of enjoyment and remain under the supplier's control. For this reason, the object is not fully appropriable outside the technical-organizational arrangement that sustains it. The intensity of this dependence varies according to the degree of lock-in, the centrality of collections tied to the account, the relevance of the usage history, the weight of reputation metrics and restrictions on transfer, interoperability or reconstruction of what was acquired outside the platform. These variables tend to operate cumulatively, causing the permanence of the bond and the practical acceptability of unilateral changes to become components of the material content of what was acquired.

These traits manifest themselves, with particular clarity, in three digital business models: games operated as a service, marketplaces and artificial intelligence services supplied by platform.

In games as a service, enjoyment is sustained by an environment in continuous operation in which the utility of the object depends on account, inventory, progression parameters, events, updates and rules of coexistence. What is acquired is composed of layers administered by the ecosystem, such as access to modes of enjoyment, content made available by season, items p. 487 tied to the profile, permissions of use and criteria of permanence. The governance of the environment acts as a constitutive component of enjoyment. In this sense, parameter adjustments, store reconfigurations, revisions of internal policies and balance changes can reconfigure utility and the cost of remaining without formal interruption of access.

Furthermore, games as a service frequently incorporate an intense symbolic dimension, in which distinction, belonging and recognition are realized through visibility devices, reputation metrics, signs of rarity and forms of displaying the collection. For this reason, changes in rarity criteria, in display conditions, in rules of use and in validation mechanisms can produce relevant losses linked to symbolic identity, even when enjoyment remains formally available.

In digital marketplaces, the layer of mediation governed by the platform materially conditions the consumer's appropriation of value, whether the immediate object is material or immaterial. Curation, ranking, reputation, dispute policies, display criteria, delivery parameters and verification mechanisms structure the possibility of finding, comparing, contracting and validating what is acquired. This is not to say that every digital mediation automatically converts any good into an IOC. The point is to recognize that, in certain arrangements, the layer of mediation functions as a continuous technical-organizational environment that forms part of supply, because it conditions practical predictability, the intelligibility of the offer and the capacity to validate the result. Modulations in visibility algorithms, reputational metrics, recommendation patterns, return policies, anti-fraud rules and dispute parameters can reconfigure risks, transaction costs and the practical viability of choices without taking the form of explicit interruption. Structural dependence manifests itself through the concentration, in the platform, of transaction records, reputational credentials and decision-making instances, intensifying asymmetries of intelligibility and agency.

In artificial intelligence services supplied by platform, the consumed object is configured as a functional capacity made available under technical and normative parameters administered by the supplier. The utility of what is acquired depends on the continuous functioning of the system, its versions, its models and its use policies. Usage limits, authorization criteria, content filters, forms of performance prioritization and rules of interaction integrate the practical existence of the object, which is realized as an enjoyable capacity under variable conditions of governance. Modulation is constitutive, given that version changes, parameter adjustments, policy revisions, performance variations and reconfigurations of safeguards can alter results, restrict functionalities or reorganize costs of enjoyment without formal cancellation of access. Structural dependence becomes p. 488 evident through the practical impossibility of replicating outside the ecosystem elements that condition the utility of the object, such as computational infrastructure, model updating and moderation criteria, reinforcing asymmetries of intelligibility and agency as to the reasons for and impacts of successive changes.

The approximation between these three models stems from a common structural core: in all of them, enjoyment depends on the continuous operation of a privately governed technical-organizational environment, in which rules, interfaces, metrics and validation procedures integrate the content of supply and remain susceptible to modulation over time. This convergence explains why relevant losses are not necessarily expressed as formal interruptions: modulation can downgrade use value, shift costs of remaining, alter conditions of visibility and reconfigure forms of validation without suppressing access, while structural dependence intensifies asymmetries of intelligibility and agency.

With the framing concluded, the next stage consists of converting the identified structural pattern into comparable classes guided by criteria, capable of expressing how the object exists in the ecosystem and to what extent its enjoyment is sensitive to unilateral changes. This step will make it possible to organize preliminary categories of IOC on game platforms and to proceed, with discipline and controllability, to the study of the legal regime applicable to the ecosystem studied.
