Situation f) · 7.1.2 of the thesis

When the update took away the value of what was bought

f) relevant mutability and functional value equivalence

Which facts show that utility has been hollowed out?

Nerfs that hollow out acquired utility, removal of relevant features, interface changes that disrupt ordinary use and redefinitions of rules that downgrade the promised equivalence. The consumer still has the account and access, but the utility enjoyed has been substantially downgraded. The loss may affect functional integrity, tied to performance and the utility delivered, or symbolic integrity, tied to belonging, reputation and distinction, when these elements are part of the performance as offered.

How do you compare before and after the alteration?

The utility previously incorporated into the relationship, the change that occurred, the concrete functional impact and the insufficiency of the restoration offered. In practice, one compares before and after, examines the relevant versions and identifies the core of utility affected.

Which rules control unilateral modification?

Article 51, item XIII, of the CDC1 makes null any clause that authorizes unilateral modification of the content or quality of the performance after contracting. Articles 18 and 20 of the CDC2 open the door to the quality defect, which does not require total uselessness. Articles 421 and 422 of the Civil Code3 limit contractual freedom by the social function of the contract and require good faith in performance as well.

Is every mutation unlawful?

Mutability is not unlawful in itself. The parameter does not require identity between versions nor does it freeze the service. Predominantly aesthetic alterations, as a rule, are left out, except when they are part of a convergent set of signs that supports recognizing an unlawful act of architecture through structural indications of abusive teleology. It is not necessary to demonstrate full ownership of the object. The response prioritizes functional restoration, technical reversal, equivalent substitution and correction of the way updates are made. The decisive question is not whether the item still exists in a formal sense, but whether the practical equivalence of what was acquired remains recognizable. That is why empty symbolic substitutes are not enough as restoration.

When it applies
When the alteration degrades, hollows out or substantially diverts the function that justified the acquisition.
Whenever the platform alters the experience so as to degrade, hollow out or substantially divert the function that justified the acquisition, there will be a rupture of functional value equivalence.
Duty of supply
To preserve, in substantial terms, the functional, relational and, when legally relevant, symbolic role of the object.
The corresponding duty of supply is to preserve, in substantial terms, the functional, relational and, when legally relevant, symbolic role of the object in the consumer's experience.
Minimum evidence
The utility previously incorporated into the relationship, the change, the concrete functional impact and the insufficiency of the restoration offered.

Possible legal qualification
Quality defect of the product or service, relevant inadequacy of supply and, where there is coordinated exploitation of mutability, an additional element of abusive practice or abusive teleology.

The law cited on this page

  1. Article 51, item XIII, of the CDC ↩

    Art. 51. The following contractual clauses relating to the supply of products and services, among others, are null and void by operation of law:

    PTArt. 51. São nulas de pleno direito, entre outras, as cláusulas contratuais relativas ao fornecimento de produtos e serviços que: IV - estabeleçam obrigações consideradas iníquas, abusivas, que coloquem o consumidor em desvantagem exagerada, ou sejam incompatíveis com a boa-fé ou a eqüidade; XIII - autorizem o fornecedor a modificar unilateralmente o conteúdo ou a qualidade do contrato, após sua celebração;

    IV - those that establish obligations considered iniquitous, abusive, that place the consumer at an exaggerated disadvantage, or are incompatible with good faith or equity;

    XIII - those that authorize the supplier to unilaterally modify the content or quality of the contract after its conclusion;

    Text in force copied from the Planalto website on 27/10/2026. Later addition, outside the thesis.

  2. Articles 18 and 20 of the CDC ↩

    “Art. 18. Suppliers of durable or non-durable consumer products are jointly and severally liable for quality or quantity defects that make them unfit or inadequate for the consumption for which they are intended or reduce their value […].”

    PT“Art. 18. Os fornecedores de produtos de consumo duráveis ou não duráveis respondem solidariamente pelos vícios de qualidade ou quantidade que os tornem impróprios ou inadequados ao consumo a que se destinam ou lhes diminuam o valor […].”

    Wording transcribed in note 609, p. 557.

    “Art. 20. The service supplier is liable for quality defects that make services unfit for consumption or reduce their value, as well as for those arising from disparity with the indications contained in the offer or advertising message […].”

    PT“Art. 20. O fornecedor de serviços responde pelos vícios de qualidade que os tornem impróprios ao consumo ou lhes diminuam o valor, assim como por aqueles decorrentes da disparidade com as indicações constantes da oferta ou mensagem publicitária […].”

    Wording transcribed in note 526, p. 520.

  3. Articles 421 and 422 of the Civil Code ↩

    “Art. 421. Contractual freedom shall be exercised within the limits of the social function of the contract.”

    PT“Art. 421. A liberdade contratual será exercida nos limites da função social do contrato.”

    Wording transcribed in note 620, p. 561.

    “Art. 422. The contracting parties are bound to observe, both in the conclusion of the contract and in its performance, the principles of probity and good faith.”

    PT“Art. 422. Os contratantes são obrigados a guardar, assim na conclusão do contrato, como em sua execução, os princípios de probidade e boa-fé.”

    Wording transcribed in note 618, p. 561.
