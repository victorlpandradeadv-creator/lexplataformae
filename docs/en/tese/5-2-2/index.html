5.2.2 On the categories of immaterial objects of consumption on game platforms

See this section in the PDF

  • Dogmatics · Sections of chapter 5
  • Platforms · The laboratory of origin

p. 488

Once the ecosystem of continued enjoyment has been delimited, it becomes possible to organize a typology that stabilizes comparability between objects and arrangements of supply on game platforms. In these architectures, the acquired utility is realized over time and under private governance of the technical-organizational environment. The object of consumption, therefore, does not present itself as a detachable thing, but as a position of enjoyment conditioned by rules, interfaces, validation mechanisms and ordinary modulations.

So that the analysis does not degrade into a catalog of peculiarities, and so that the dogmatic reconstruction does not operate with shorthand that collapses heterogeneous arrangements, an instrument is needed that can consistently distinguish what varies irrelevantly from what materially alters the enjoyment and value of what was acquired, including when the loss manifests itself through gradual degradation, functional downgrading or disorganization of symbolic value without formal interruption of access.

p. 489 This typology is deliberately internal to the games laboratory and is not offered as a general classification of platforms. Its function is to allow the analysis, within an ecosystem that concentrates hybrid objects and dense governance, to recognize patterns of mutability and of loss through verifiable equivalences, without depending on episodic descriptions. The transposition of the IOC regime to other families of platforms will be developed in a dedicated stage of adherence tests, in which the dogmatic parameters already stabilized will be confronted with typical situations of continuous digital consumption in distinct ecosystems. It is thus established that the typology proposed below is internal and strictly operational to the model studied.

The first category is that of cosmetic-ornamental objects. These are IOCs whose utility is not exhausted by functional performance attributes, since it incorporates symbolic value realized as distinction, belonging and recognition within the ecosystem. Enjoyment depends on a tie to the account, the inventory and the mechanisms of visibility and display that make what was acquired visible to third parties. Changes in display criteria, inventory organization, rules of admissible use, criteria for obtaining items and rarity regimes can materially depreciate enjoyment even when the item remains available. In these cases, the relevant loss appears as disorganization of the sign-value incorporated into the object, and restoration is not exhausted by undifferentiated compensation, but must be assessed by functional value equivalence compatible with the symbolic nature of what was acquired.

The second category is that of progression objects. Here the object is realized as a trajectory governed by rules, parameters and validation mechanisms administered by the ecosystem. What is acquired is not to be confused with a mere record of time invested, but with an enjoyable position built through goals, requirements and rewards defined by internal criteria. Unilateral modulation bears on the parameters for obtaining items, eligibility requirements, difficulty calibrations and criteria for recognizing performance. Successive changes can reorganize the relationship between effort and result, raise the costs of staying or make rewards impracticable, producing functional degradation without explicit cancellation. The requirement of controllability falls on reconstructing what changed and which material effects were produced, as well as on assessing restorations according to the preservation of the bundle of utility incorporated into the trajectory.

The third category is that of access and permanence objects. These are IOCs marked by intense dependence on account, credentials and eligibility, so that enjoyment takes on, in practice, the form of a revocable position. The object is not reduced to the initial act of enabling, since it involves maintaining the conditions that make it possible to remain in the ecosystem and to use, on an ongoing basis, the functionalities, modes of enjoyment and collections tied to the profile. p. 490 Policy modulations and governance decisions, such as restrictions, suspensions and partial limitations, can degrade the consumer's position and send cascading effects through the collection and the trajectory of enjoyment. In this category, open auditability takes on a central role, since the minimum required is verifiable justification and traceability of the measure, with an intelligible delimitation of what was decided and of its material effects, without imposing total technical transparency.

The fourth category is that of functionality objects. These are instrumental capacities made available to the consumer under technical and normative parameters administered by the supplier, such as usage permissions, convenience utilities, configuration options and conversion capacities that form part of the ecosystem's bundle of utility. Unilateral modulation manifests itself through reconfiguration of parameters, usage restrictions, interface changes and redistribution of capacities among modes of enjoyment. Relevant losses appear as functional degradation of the bundle of utility, with increased operational friction, reduced capacity and downgrading of conveniences incorporated into what was acquired, even if access remains intact. Adequate restoration must be assessed by functional value equivalence, taking into account the capacity actually enjoyed and the materiality of the change.

The fifth category is that of time objects. It covers temporal structures that condition when, for how long and within which windows enjoyment can occur, such as validity regimes, seasons, availability cycles and frequency limits. In this class, time forms part of the material content of supply. Changes to windows, redefinition of deadlines, reorganization of cycles and recalibration of limits can reconfigure utility, the cost of staying and the practical viability of enjoying what was acquired, without suppressing access. Relevant losses take the form of lost opportunity and material downgrading of the time invested, especially when changes shift the balance between effort, reward and permanence.

The sixth category is that of reputation objects. They correspond to positions of recognition instituted by the ecosystem through metrics, validation criteria and visibility devices. Reputation is not reduced to stored data, since it depends on calculation rules, display criteria and validation systems that make it socially intelligible and comparable. Modulations of ranking criteria, display changes, governance decisions and sanctioning measures can reorganize reputational position without interrupting access. Relevant losses manifest themselves as symbolic harm and disorganization of the place of recognition, with repercussions on the very utility of the environment. For this reason, the requirement of auditability is particularly sensitive, and changes and decisions that affect reputation p. 491 must offer a verifiable minimum of justification and traceability, while restorations must be assessed by functional equivalence compatible with the reputational nature of the object.

Applying this grid directly to concrete objects from the laboratory shows how typological categories can correspond to distinct modes of supply and conditioning, without reducing the ecosystem to an inventory of items. A first example, typical of a progression object, is the event pass, in which the acquisition is directed not at a finished result, but at access to a metered trajectory of milestones and rewards that is realized through tasks, game routines and internal validations. The mechanics are structured so that progress occurs over the course of the event and so that a significant share of the rewards depends on purchasing the pass, so that the consumer buys not merely “content”, but a form of active permanence that makes enjoyable what was made available as potential Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024b)471.

This design is consistent with the very contractual framework for access to virtual content, in which the supplier acknowledges that the license may be obtained not only by purchase but also by earning it, including through “game missions or tasks”, which makes it methodologically controllable to describe the pass as a progression object in the strict sense, that is, as an acquired and governed trajectory, and not as a simple one-off consumption Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e).

As an example of a time object, the platform deploys regimes of availability and return to produce windows, cycles and rarities that form part of the material content of enjoyment. In operational language accessible to the public, certain categories of skins are treated as “legacy” or “limited”, with variations as to whether they return to the store and under what conditions, so that the symbolic utility of what was acquired comes to depend also on an institutionally administered temporality Riot Games(Riot Games, 2025h)472.

p. 492 In this case, the contractual framework that grants the supplier the power to exclude, alter, move, remove and reprice virtual content at any time, with or without notice, provides the minimum declaratory axis for reading the administration of availability as an ordinary variable of supply, which shifts the legal problem to the assessment of relevant losses through lost opportunity, recalibration of windows and reconfiguration of rarity, without confusing formal continuity of access with material preservation of the value enjoyed Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e).

Finally, an example of a reputation object can be seen in the way the ecosystem makes utilities and loot conditional on metrics of conduct and eligibility. The honor system operates as a mechanism of comparable and socially intelligible recognition, but also as an everyday discipline, since behavior penalties can lock reputational progression and, in practice, affect access to rewards associated with crafting loot, such as keys and capsules Riot Games(Riot Games, 2017a).

Reputation thus appears not as an extra-legal ornament of consumption, but as a condition of enjoyment administered by rules of conduct and by sanctioning decisions with cascading patrimonial and symbolic effects, not least because closing an account may entail loss of access to purchases and to associated virtual content Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e).

The proposed categories neither eliminate the complexity of the ecosystem nor purport to reduce concrete objects to a single type. They provide a comparable language for breaking hybrid arrangements down into controllable dimensions and for locating precisely which aspects were affected by a relevant modulation. This breakdown prevents formal continuity of access from serving as an alibi for material downgrades and allows losses and degradations to be treated as legally relevant even when they manifest themselves through gradual erosion, reconfigurations of rarity, changes in display capacity or changes in reputational criteria.

With this preliminary typology, the analysis no longer depends on episodic descriptions and comes to operate through stable parameters to recognize recurring patterns of platform-governed enjoyment, locate asymmetries of intelligibility and agency, and identify when changes go beyond ordinary variation and become a problem of control. This gain is intentionally obtained within the games ecosystem, as a laboratory of intensive observation, without turning the categories above into a general taxonomy of platforms. The next stage consists of situating the electronic games sector within the Brazilian normative and institutional landscape only to the extent necessary to contextualize the debate, preserving the operative function of the typology and preparing the application of the grid to the case study.
