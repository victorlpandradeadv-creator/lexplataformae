4.1.5 Controlled semiosis and semiotic vulnerability

See this section in the PDF

p. 320

Under the regime described, the good is not sustained as an isolated “thing”, but as a position of enjoyment dependent on intelligibility, comparison and recognition in a governed ecosystem. The structural consequence of the regime described is to observe consumption through the prism of delivery and proof, for the relevant exposure takes the form of a loss of verifiability of the link between promise and enjoyment in a governed ecosystem. In this environment, adjustments of visibility, relevance criteria, calendar rhythms and signals of prestige may alter, without an evident marker, what sustains the position of enjoyment as valuable. The center of the p. 321 problem is the practical difficulty of demonstrating what was promised as position and what remained as delivery when the conditions of value are administered as a flow.

Semiotic vulnerability designates the consumer's structural exposure to the platform's unilateral control of the conditions that make the promise hold as delivery when the good is a position of enjoyment and not an autonomous thing. It appears when intelligibility, visibility, equivalence and temporal cadence cease to be background and come to make up the very mode of existence of the object, so that value depends on codes of reading, on public hierarchies, on markers of recognition and on rhythms that the environment continually administers. For this reason, the core lies not in ignorance of a clause or in an information deficit, but in the loss of the capacity to verify and demonstrate the link between promise and enjoyment in a governed ecosystem, in which what counts as “delivery” can be shifted without clear notice and without a visible rupture of the object.

Nor is this vulnerability to be confused with a moral judgment of the consumer who “did not read” or “did not understand”, because the problem is not inattention to a text, but asymmetry over the bases that make the promise operative and over the possibility of reconstructing them with proof. Nor is it reducible to a psychologization of behavior, as if everything were explained by impulse, desire or “addiction”, since permanence is produced by an architecture of comparison and by the governance of time, and not by mere spontaneous affect. Nor is it an ornamental culturalism that treats signs as makeup over a neutral substance, because, in immaterial consumption, the symbolic is a material part of the offer when it defines recognition, commensurability and socially legible position. The point, in all cases, is structural, for it concerns the governance of the regime of signs, of scene and of time as a condition of value and as the source of a specific loss of verifiability.

When the good is positional, the everyday confirmation of delivery tends to shift from the promise-enjoyment link to a promise-signal link, because what the ecosystem makes visible and circulable as recognition comes to function as a practical criterion of fulfillment. Markers of rarity, belonging, prestige, being current, participation in an event and other labels stabilize the position as a legible and comparable difference. These signals may remain intact even when the relevant enjoyment has been degraded or reconfigured, maintaining the public appearance of continuity and making the loss harder to convert into rupture. This shift is the heart of the problem, because it transforms the conflict into a dispute about criteria and equivalences, and not only about “the item” itself.

p. 322 Semiotic vulnerability does not result from an isolated asymmetry, for it derives from a matrix of layers that combine in the same ecosystem and reinforce the shift from promise-enjoyment to promise-signal. There is an asymmetry of meaning, linked to control over codes of reading, categories and classifications that define what counts as relevant difference. There is an asymmetry of visibility, linked to control over the field of what appears, what is highlighted and what becomes comparable as prestige. There is a temporal asymmetry, linked to control over cadences, windows and cycles that make value depend on continuous presence. And there is an evidentiary asymmetry, linked to control over records, metrics, versions and internal criteria that determine what can be demonstrated later.

These layers couple together as a mechanism and produce a structural loss of verifiability: the central problem is not only knowing, but being able to prove.

The layer of meaning operates when the platform governs the intelligibility of the good without needing to alter the item technically in any overt way, because reclassifying, recategorizing, renaming attributes and reordering repertoires alters what the object “is” for the ecosystem. An IOC may remain accessible and, even so, lose the status that made it economically operative. The rare becomes common, the distinctive becomes ordinary, the “event” item becomes noise, the signal of belonging becomes indifferent, the difference in status that justifies the acquisition is lost over an undisclosed period and without any notice. This kind of shift is not peripheral, because, in immaterial consumption, the identity of the good is partly constituted by this shared grammar that allows recognition, comparison and validation. When the platform controls this grammar and recalibrates it unilaterally, it also controls the semantic stability of the acquired position. For this reason, it is able to produce relevant losses without producing a clear event of “breakage” of the item.

An empirical case that makes this semantic instability explicit is the controversy surrounding the Soul Fighter Samira skin, released in the Ultimate tier in 2023361. The legally relevant point is not to discuss “taste” or to turn community criticism into a measure of truth, but to observe how the ecosystem itself comes to depend on a unilateral redefinition of what the sign of the tier promises. When the platform treats the Ultimate category as a label whose definition can be rewritten over time by more open criteria, it alters, through p. 323 language and classification, what counts as delivery, without needing to admit any non-fulfillment of what was offered362.

The consumer, up to that point, was still faced with the label and the catalog's top price, but felt the loss of a stable standard of auditability of the promised content, because comparison ceases to rest on a historically verifiable technical repertoire and comes to depend on the institutional framing of what would be “special” for that champion. The practical effect is to shift verification outside the point of purchase and outside the supplier's self-description, producing a regime in which proof of the promise-enjoyment link becomes litigious, socially disputed and informationally costly, precisely because what was sold as a category comes to operate as an elastic sign administered by the supplier itself363.

The layer of visibility reinforces the mechanism because positional value depends on what can be exposed, compared and confirmed as relevant difference in a public circuit. The platform does not merely present an offer; it organizes the field of comparison by defining showcases, highlights, apparent metrics, orderings and display patterns that turn reputation and social proof into value infrastructure. Enjoyment, then, includes occupying a recognizable place before others, and the good also derives its value from the way it lets itself be seen, measured and validated. For this reason, permanence ceases to be a behavioral detail and comes to function as a practical condition for maintaining value: without return and presence in the flow, the position falls out of date, loses legibility and loses social effectiveness. Even without any technical alteration to the item, the mere reconfiguration of what appears, of how it appears and of what counts as relevant can demote or elevate the IOC, shifting the value of the position without necessarily generating a clear marker of non-performance.

The layer of equivalence manifests itself when the ecosystem is able to preserve the appearance of fulfillment through signals that keep the good commensurable and recognizable, even though the relevant enjoyment has been reconfigured from within. The platform preserves the label, class, frame of rarity, form of display and comparative place of the item, p. 324 ensuring that it continues to “read” as equivalent in the internal hierarchies, while functions, restrictions, modes of access, performance conditions and practical effects may be altered, degraded or emptied. Value remains operative because the circuit of recognition keeps working. The good remains validatable as difference, even if the experience that justified that difference has changed. This does not depend on individual psychological deception, since it is a mode of operation in which equivalence is produced by internal criteria and by stable signals, capable of sustaining public continuity without necessarily preserving the position of enjoyment in its relevant elements.

The temporal layer closes the circuit when time is governed as a program and the promise ceases to behave as a one-off event, becoming the administered sequence typical of games-as-a-service. Seasons, passes, tracks, events, roadmaps, availability windows, returns and relaunches are not “extra content”, they are devices that organize relevance and access, convert expectation into present value and turn “being up to date” into a practical criterion of belonging and being current. Permanence becomes a requirement because the position remains operative only if the consumer keeps up with the cadences and milestones that the ecosystem imposes, and because the experience itself is anchored in continuous decisions about what appears, what disappears and what returns. This intensifies semiotic vulnerability by multiplying barely legible points of recalibration and by shifting delivery to a field of administered temporal choices, in which “maintaining value” depends on the rhythm and the framing that the platform governs.

When these layers operate together, permanence and recurrence cease to be a “side effect” attributable to individual habit and become the very economic form of immaterial consumption, because the position is worth something only while it remains validated in the ecosystem. Return becomes an input because the environment transforms recognition into a recurring task, transforms being current into a condition of belonging and transforms absence into a risk of visible demotion, so that repeating and remaining are not psychological excesses, but practical requirements for preserving socially operative value. The teleology of extraction stabilizes precisely when consumption ceases to be an episode and becomes a regime in which meaning, visibility, equivalence and time are governed to produce continuity as necessity, and not as preference.

The state of risk becomes palpable when the platform unilaterally alters the attributes that sustain a sign of value already incorporated as identity and as social position. The nerf is a prime example in this scenario, since it is not merely a neutral technical adjustment, but a reconfiguration of what counts as power, relevance and prestige within the p. 325 ecosystem, carried out by whoever controls the “physics of the world” in the form of code. When an item, an ability, a character, a category or a status marker is rescaled, demoted or redefined, it is not only a performance that is altered. What is altered is the public meaning of the investment and, in many cases, the very intelligibility of the acquired position.

Symbolic debasement is relevant because it produces loss through mutation of the internal rule of value, and not through an overt functional failure. The consumer may continue to have “access” to the same nominal item, the same inventory and the same ownership marker, while losing, from within, the practical effects and the symbolic effects that made that position hold as delivery. Contestation becomes difficult because the platform does not break the object. It goes further, redefining the very world in which the object holds value. In evidentiary terms, rupture ceases to be an event and becomes a recalibration, and it is precisely this form of loss that demands parameters of traceability and of substantive equivalence, to prevent the mutation of the sign from functioning as a normalized path of demotion without demonstrable consequence.

Under these premises, the notion of adequacy cannot be decided by the binary “works/does not work”, because it is perfectly possible to maintain technical access and, even so, lose what made the position economically operative. If the relevant unit is position, circuit and sequence, adequate delivery depends on the good remaining recognizable, comparable and validatable on the terms that sustained its value in the ecosystem, and not only on continuing to be “present” as a nominal item in the inventory. Reading the good as a closed unit fails because it ignores that, in immaterial consumption, a decisive part of value is produced by governed conditions of intelligibility, visibility and cadence, which can be recalibrated without an identifiable event of rupture. For this reason, minimum functioning does not serve as a sufficient yardstick: it confuses technical continuity with preservation of the position of enjoyment that made the acquisition hold as delivery.

A typical risk of this regime is merely nominal restoration, in which label, category and public markers of continuity are preserved, while the conditions that constituted the position of enjoyment are altered from within. The platform keeps the good legible for the showcase and for social comparison, with declared rarity, frame of prestige, belonging and internal commensurability, and, at the same time, recalibrates access, restrictions, effects, performance conditions, modes of display and relevance criteria that sustained the acquired experience. Rupture is neutralized because the circuit of recognition continues to produce social proofs of delivery, even when the relevant content of enjoyment has been p. 326 shifted. The result is a loss that tends to be absorbed as a normal adjustment of the ecosystem, for the continuity of the signal takes the place of the continuity of the position, which makes it difficult to transform internal reconfiguration into demonstrable non-performance.

For this reason, the relevant object of proof also changes. It is not enough to demonstrate that “the item exists” or that “access remains”. What must be capable of reconstruction are the conditions that sustained the promise at the moment of acquisition and that constituted the position of enjoyment as operative in the ecosystem, that is, which functions, restrictions, modes of access and parameters of recognition made that position valuable, comparable and validatable. The evidence required ceases to be mere inspection of the current state of the good and becomes reconstruction of the link: which classifications, metrics, visibility criteria, cadences, rules and equivalences were in force when the promise was assumed and how they were altered afterward. Without this change of focus, the dispute remains trapped on the surface of “appearing-as-delivery”, precisely where nominal continuity and public signals are most efficient at concealing relevant losses in enjoyment.

Auditability, in this context, is the minimum requirement of verifiability in a market in which delivery depends on opaque and adjustable layers. It means the possibility of reconstructing, in a traceable way, the conditions that made the promise hold as delivery at the moment of acquisition and the relevant alterations that affected those conditions over time, without depending on the self-validation produced by the platform itself. What must leave a trace is not a generic “trade secret”, but what is material to the acquired position of enjoyment, such as criteria for classifying and reading the item, parameters that organize visibility and comparison, rules and metrics that sustain recognition and belonging, and temporal decisions that shape access, cadence and relevance.

Without this minimum, the dispute tends to be resolved by surface and impression, because the continuity of signals functions as self-justifying proof while the consumer cannot demonstrate what changed, by which criteria and with what effect on the acquired position. Auditability, therefore, enters not as a regulatory luxury or a moralization of the symbolic, but as a practical condition for the promise-enjoyment link to be demonstrable in markets of continuous governance. Even so, tracing is not enough in itself: a criterion is also needed to judge whether what was preserved after the alteration corresponds, in fact, to the relevant value of the position, and it is precisely there that the requirement of functional value equivalence imposes itself.

Functional value equivalence is the criterion that prevents restoration from being reduced to continuity of name, label, declared rarity or public symptoms of p. 327 “sameness” when what was acquired was a position of enjoyment. To be equivalent, here, does not mean to appear equivalent for the showcase, but to preserve, in a substantive way, the relevant elements that made that position hold as delivery, including practical functions, applicable restrictions, modes of access, performance conditions and effects that structured the acquired experience. Since, in the IOC, part of the value is materially produced by recognition and commensurability in the ecosystem, the “functional” also covers this component only insofar as it was an operative condition of the position bought, that is, that without which the good ceases to function as recognizable difference on the relevant terms of the acquisition. This does not authorize turning any symbolic value into an indeterminate legal obligation; on the contrary, it requires isolating what was relevant to that specific position and assessing whether the restoration preserved that core or merely maintained signals of continuity while shifting, from within, what mattered. The effect of the parameter is to separate legitimate adjustment from opportunistic substitution: if the system preserves appearance and label, but substantially demotes functions, restrictions, access, performance or the social operability that defined the position, there was nominal restoration and not preservation of delivery. Restoration thus ceases to be arbitrated by the platform as “equivalent by declaration” and becomes controllable by a substantive yardstick, compatible with a reconfigurable market, but hostile to the zone of impunity of the sign.

These parameters do not target the symbolic, because the symbolic has already been recognized as a material part of the mode of existence of the IOC as a position of enjoyment in the ecosystem. The problem appears when the continuity of signals comes to function as a substitute for substantive preservation and as an evidentiary shortcut, allowing “appearing-as-delivery” to absorb internal demotions as if they were simple adjustments. Auditability confronts this risk by requiring demonstrable traceability of the conditions that sustain the promise and of the alterations that reconfigure it, reducing dependence on evidence produced by the circuit itself. Functional value equivalence confronts the same risk in content, by requiring that restoration preserve, in a verifiable way, the relevant elements of the acquired position, instead of maintaining only label and public commensurability. The result is not to deny signs, but to reduce the space in which the continuity of the signal replaces proof of delivery when the platform preserves appearance and shifts, from within, what made delivery hold.

Taken together, the mechanism closes as a single gear: administered semiosis makes the good intelligible and desirable as a position; hierarchized visibility turns comparison and social proof into value infrastructure; equivalence by signals allows the promise to remain operative even when the relevant enjoyment shifts; and governed p. 328 time serializes the promise, making the to come an economic resource that converts permanence into a requirement. In this scenario, delivery is not reduced to technical access, and the conflict tends to concentrate on the verifiability of the link between promise and enjoyment, precisely because the environment can preserve signals of continuity while recalibrating the criteria and conditions that sustained the value of the acquired position.

A privileged way of visualizing this loss of verifiability is the nerf, understood as the technically deliberate weakening of attributes, abilities or items, by means of a patch, to make something less powerful in the practical functioning of the game League of Legends Wiki(League of Legends Wiki, [202-?]m)364. The operation appears as an “adjustment”, but its relevant consequence, from the perspective of the IOC, is the practical reclassification of what counts as power, efficiency and prestige. The platform preserves access and the continuity of the good's label, while demoting, from within, what made the position count as delivery, which shifts the core of enjoyment without necessarily producing a clear external marker that would allow the consumer to reconstruct the promise-enjoyment link.

This effect is neither collateral nor exceptional, for the contractual regime of the ecosystem itself incorporates the nerf as an ordinary power to govern the service, including over virtual products Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e)365. At the same time, virtual content is qualified as a limited and revocable license, setting aside the language of ownership and reinforcing that permanence of access does not mean substantive preservation of the value enjoyed Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e)366. The fit is methodologically revealing, since the nerf is not merely a p. 329 numerical alteration, but a technique for administering the meaning of value that preserves the appearance of continuity while altering the functional content of the position, making it harder to demonstrate, in verifiable terms, what was actually lost.

The public language of balancing, in turn, tends to operate as a legitimizing narrative for this governance by continuous modulation. In the patch notes, the “nerfs” are presented as necessary corrections to keep the environment “healthy” and to stabilize experiences, while at the same time describing concrete reductions of power that reconfigure what counts as advantage, viability and performance Riot Games(Riot Games, 2026d)367. The discourse produces intelligibility for the alteration and offers technical justification for the loss, but does not resolve the central problem. The question that matters is not whether the game can be adjusted, but under what conditions the platform can treat as “compatible with the promise” a substantive demotion of delivery368, when the very criterion of what counts as delivery remains under unilateral administration and with traces whose form and sufficiency are also defined by the same authority.

In this key, semiotic vulnerability ceases to look like a problem of “consumer sensitivity” and appears as a structural risk of a market in which equivalence can be proclaimed by signals. The nerf is the limit case because it shows, with precision, how the continuity of name and access can function as self-justifying proof369, while the relevant delivery shifts in what the environment counts as power and utility.

p. 330 Semiotic vulnerability thus names this type of structural loss of demonstrability that occurs when meaning, scene, equivalence and time materially integrate what is put into circulation and remain under the unilateral administration of the ecosystem. The question ceases to be whether changes are possible and becomes under what conditions they can be treated as compatible with the promise and with the preservation of the relevant value. In reconfigurable markets, altering the catalog, reordering visibility, redesigning cycles and recalibrating signals make up the regular mode of governance, but this normality cannot function as a license to preserve continuity of appearance while the core of the position of enjoyment is shifted from within.

Mentioning the term does not serve to legitimize closing the analysis in favor of the platform, but to make visible the type of language that, in case of conflict, tries to absorb substantive losses as if they were internal variations without legal relevance, which aggravates the loss of verifiability of the promise-enjoyment link. ↩
