Section 7 · pp. 713 to 714

7 On the typology of the models of incidence of the Immaterial Object of Consumption in different functional categories of platform

See this section in the PDF

This section in the layers of the site

  • Platforms · The test

Epigraph

Marisa Monte, Infinito Particular (2006). What it announces · Read the epigraph in the PDF.

p. 713

The stabilization of the legal regime of the Immaterial Object of Consumption allows a further movement. It is no longer a matter of justifying its normative existence, but of testing its capacity for incidence in ecosystems other than the one in which its formulation was originally densified. The legally relevant point does not lie in asserting that every continuous digital provision is subject to the same framing, nor in expanding the concept on the mere ground that economic life has been digitized. What matters is to verify in which functional contexts certain recurring arrangements of private governance, operative opacity and ordinary mutability reorganize, in a structurally similar way, the conditions of access, permanence, intelligibility and contestation of enjoyment, producing an environment apt to receive the same regime of control.

The typology mobilized here is neither sectoral nor merely technological. Its axis is functional and structural, in an analytical key and without claim to exhaustiveness. It does not serve, for example, to dwell on classical strict functional IOCs815, such as digital tools for editing images, audio or video, whose qualification tends to be more evident when the contracted utility is organized as an enjoyment bundle sustained over time, dependent on account, access, updatable functionalities, libraries, files and continuous modulation by the platform. It seeks, rather, to recognize typical models of incidence from the recurrence of mechanisms that, although they appear in diverse markets, perform legally comparable functions in the organization of the consumption experience.

The test, therefore, is not satisfied with superficial affinities. It requires showing that categories such as semiotic vulnerability, open auditability, functional value equivalence and abusive teleology remain operable when moved to other platform environments, retaining the aptitude to guide the reading of the facts, the organization of evidence and the determination of legal consequences. Without this, the concept would degrade into a classificatory p. 714 metaphor. The operation that follows is, thus, expansive without being inflationary. The underlying question is not how many environments can be nominally drawn into the orbit of the IOC, but in which contexts the combination of continuous supply, private governance and structural mutability produces a specific form of legally protectable enjoyment, whose discipline can no longer be adequately resolved by the traditional categories of the one-off circulation of goods and services.

The analysis then turns to typical models of continuous digital supply and platform governance, with special attention to contemporary ecosystems in which opacity, mutability and private direction of the experience are ordinary conditions of the provision. Its aim is to make visible the recurrence of structural forms of organization of continuous immaterial consumption and to verify to what extent the regime already formulated retains, in these environments, its force of qualification, evidence and remedy.

Notes

  1. Here, classical strict functional IOCs means the immaterial objects of consumption whose main utility presents itself in a more directly operational way, such as programs, accounts, libraries, files, tools and digital functionalities exploited under continuous provision. Their identification as IOCs stems from the very organization of the supply as an enjoyment bundle sustained over time, subject to access, updating, interoperability, functional preservation and unilateral modulation by the supplier. Thus, the removal or relevant degradation of an essential functionality in editing software, the unjustified restriction of access to an account that concentrates files and libraries, or the substantial alteration of the conditions of use of contracted tools may affect the very object enjoyed, and not merely an accessory convenience of the provision. The reference to such IOCs, at this point, serves only to distinguish them from the less intuitive models examined in the typology, in which the incidence of the IOC depends on recognizing structural functions of governance, intermediation, visibility, reputation, productivity or trust. ↩
