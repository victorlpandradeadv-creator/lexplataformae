Situation d) · 7.1.2 of the thesis

When the storefront promised more than was delivered

d) advertising and offer as controllable management of expectation

Which facts show advertising inside the offer?

Enjoyment preceded by curated cycles of anticipation, promise, suggested scarcity, symbolic distinction or insinuated equivalences. A typical case is an item sold as exclusive that later reappears in an alternative store, in a special currency or at a much lower price, although the time limit of the exclusivity was never disclosed in an understandable way at the time of purchase.

How do you connect the promise to the later enjoyment?

The promotional environment, the communication pieces, the symbolic salience, the context in which the object is presented and the relation between the prior promise and the later enjoyment. Useful evidence includes promotional pieces, posts, campaigns, influencers, notices, asterisks, cascading cross-references and fine print. The questions are how the expectation was produced, at what moment the relevant conditions were revealed, with what prominence they appeared, what the sequence between promise and acceptance was and whether there were real means to understand and compare the offer.

Which rules make the offer binding?

Article 30 of the CDC1 makes all sufficiently precise information binding, and the presentation in the digital storefront may constitute an offer. Article 35 of the CDC2 allows the consumer to demand specific relief, accept an equivalent performance or rescind the contract and claim damages. Article 38 of the CDC3 places on whoever sponsors the advertising the burden of proving that the information is true and correct. In the curated anticipation of the offer, relevant omission appears when what is essential is moved outside the focus of the decision. Advertising is misleading when the utility or equivalence built by the promotional environment finds no verifiable correspondence in enjoyment. And it is abusive when the design systematically exploits vulnerability, compressing deliberation through artificial urgency, symbolic reward and fragmentation of the relevant conditions.

Is insistent advertising enough?

Insistent advertising is not enough. What counts is the link between the prior promise and the later enjoyment. Communication becomes an element of abusive teleology only when it is tied to a broader exploitative program. Not every persuasion technique is unlawful, because the market may promote, dramatize and organize interest. But unlawfulness may lie in the way elements that seem neutral on their own are combined, and not only in a false advertisement or an isolated clause.

The law cited on this page

  1. Article 30 of the CDC ↩

    “Art. 30. All sufficiently precise information or advertising, conveyed by any form or means of communication regarding products and services offered or presented, binds the supplier that conveys it or makes use of it and becomes part of the contract that may be entered into.”

    PT“Art. 30. Toda informação ou publicidade, suficientemente precisa, veiculada por qualquer forma ou meio de comunicação com relação a produtos e serviços oferecidos ou apresentados, obriga o fornecedor que a fizer veicular ou dela se utilizar e integra o contrato que vier a ser celebrado.”

    Wording transcribed in note 442, p. 433.

  2. Article 35 of the CDC ↩

    “Art. 35. If the supplier of products or services refuses to honor the offer, presentation or advertising, the consumer may, alternatively and at their free choice: I - demand specific relief of the obligation […]; II - accept another equivalent product or provision of service; III - rescind the contract […], and to losses and damages.”

    PT“Art. 35. Se o fornecedor de produtos ou serviços recusar cumprimento à oferta, apresentação ou publicidade, o consumidor poderá, alternativamente e à sua livre escolha: I - exigir o cumprimento forçado da obrigação […]; II - aceitar outro produto ou prestação de serviço equivalente; III - rescindir o contrato […], e a perdas e danos.”

    Wording transcribed in note 573, p. 540.

  3. Article 38 of the CDC ↩

    “Art. 38. The burden of proving the truthfulness and correctness of the advertising information or communication falls on whoever sponsors it.”

    PT“Art. 38. O ônus da prova da veracidade e correção da informação ou comunicação publicitária cabe a quem as patrocina.”

    Wording transcribed in note 716, p. 627.
