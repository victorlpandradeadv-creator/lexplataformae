5.1.2.4 On continued enjoyment and the usefulness of the product-service dichotomy in the CDC

See this section in the PDF

p. 435

The passage from one-off consumption to platform-sustained enjoyment exposes a structural tension in the vocabulary of the CDC. Supply is no longer described as a simple p. 436 event of delivery, nor as an episodic performance exhausted in initial execution. When the acquired utility depends on a technical-organizational environment in continuous operation, the object remains identifiable as an enjoyment bundle, but its legal existence is realized over time through acts of maintenance, modulation, curation and execution of rules.

In this context, the product-service dichotomy tends to become a true semantic trap, given that at times it paralyzes the analysis as if the classification settled the regime, and at other times it dissolves the object into a generic “service” and renders invisible relevant losses that do not manifest themselves as total interruption. The task, therefore, is to put the dichotomy back in its functional place, preserving the intelligibility of what was acquired as an immaterial product and recognizing, at the same time, that continued enjoyment requires a reading of supply capable of capturing duties and conduct that are projected over time.

As a result, the axis of the problem ceases to be the abstract framing of the object and becomes the interpretive operationalization of supply in a durable regime of enjoyment. What matters is a mode of reading that makes it possible to capture duties, conduct and relevant losses when utility is not realized as an inaugural instant, but as permanent execution administered by a platform. The product-service dichotomy, here, is only useful if it functions as a tool for calibrating effects, without reducing what was acquired to instantaneous delivery and without dissolving it into undifferentiated performance.

At this point, consumer doctrine offers well-known instruments for dealing with long-term relationships, successive execution and duties projected over time. Claudia Lima Marques445, in multiple reference works, reconstructs the incidence of the CDC on contract theory, emphasizing objective good faith, protection of trust, socialization of the contract and the centrality of adhesion bonds, so as to maintain that protection is not exhausted in formation and is projected onto execution.

Platform-governed continued enjoyment brings supply closer to consumer relations that are not explained by instantaneous purchase and sale, since they are structured as complex long-term bonds and tend to place the consumer in a position of practical dependence vis-à-vis the supplier and its chain. Claudia Lima Marques describes this p. 437 structure when dealing with “new contracts or contractual relations” that, under mass contracting methods, form “complex long-term legal relations” marked by the “position of ‘captivity’ or ‘dependence’ of clients, consumers” Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 77).

Updating this diagnosis for the digital environment reinforces that dependence is not limited to the economic cost of switching. It may arise from the very technical and informational design of the service, which makes migration costly and, at times, unrealistic. In this sense, when examining vulnerability by captivity in the digital sphere, Marques and Mucelin observe that, in contracts of successive performance, the search for security and for a future performance reduces the consumer to the position of “captive”, and that this dependence is understood in the context of current relations446 Mucelin(Marques; Mucelin, 2022, p. 24). When the bond is prolonged and utility is realized over time, the product-service distinction only remains useful if it can see enjoyment as continuous supply and, within it, locate duties and conduct that do not fit in a description centered on the initial instant.

This long-term structure also prevents treating the interval between contracting and result as a legally irrelevant void. Marques proposes visualizing the contract as a dynamic relationship that is born, lives and dies, constituting a true process that develops over time, in which legal effects are produced during performance, before it and after it Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 222-223). This processual reading makes it possible to maintain, in a controllable way, that the supplier's relevant conduct is not reduced to the initial “yes”, but is projected onto execution and onto the continuous administration of the conditions of enjoyment.

The legal usefulness of the product-service dichotomy is lost when it is treated as a dispute over labels capable of exhausting, by itself, the description of supply. In the regime of the IOC, this reduction is particularly costly because it shifts the focus away from what matters: preserving the acquired object as an enjoyment bundle and identifying the conduct that, in continued execution, maintains, degrades or reconfigures platform-governed utility. When enjoyment is read p. 438 only as service in a generic sense, what was acquired tends to disappear from the language, and with it disappear relevant losses that do not manifest themselves as total interruption, but as significant degradation of the core of utility. At the opposite extreme, when supply is compressed into the paradigm of one-off delivery, the characteristic dimension of governed consumption, that is, the permanent administration of the conditions of access and use, ceases to be captured as part of performance itself. What is required is to prevent the dichotomy from producing methodological blindness and replacing the reconstruction of supply with a classification that blocks analysis, weakens the attribution of losses and compromises restoration compatible with continued enjoyment.

The first step in putting the dichotomy back in a functional key is to recognize that the CDC does not tie “product” to materiality, precisely to prevent economically relevant offers from escaping protection through formalism.

Once the breadth of “product” is recognized, the distinction from “service” remains relevant as a technical tool of attribution, provided it is not converted into a rigid ontology. Nunes observes that, in certain situations, even if it is possible to detach the service from the product, the distinction must be preserved because differences in the liability regime derive from it. At the same time, the author admits hybrid zones, in which the product is part of the service and may even be confused with it Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 102]). In a convergent key, Marques indicates that central expressions of the CDC require different readings depending on whether it is a contract for the provision of services or for the supply of products and that the very concept of quality defect assumes different meanings depending on the nature of the performance. The author also stresses that, although the discipline of formation and of control of contractual balance is the same in both modalities, the code does not ignore that the execution of these contracts is differentiated and characteristic Miragem(Marques; Benjamin; Miragem, 2013, p. 472). The result, here, is methodological. The dichotomy does not govern the regime of the IOC as a totalizing label; it calibrates specific effects, preserves the intelligibility of the acquired immaterial product and makes it possible to identify, in continued execution, relevant duties and conduct, without dissolving the object nor reducing supply to the initial instant.

Continued enjoyment, however, shifts the center of gravity of supply. In forms of consumption in which utility is realized over time and depends on permanent administration of the environment, protection is not played out only in the initial act nor exhausted at the instant in which utility becomes accessible for the first time. The product-service dichotomy can produce relevant effects, but these effects are concentrated where time matters: in the structure of duties and expectations that are stabilized during execution and in the way the system p. 439 responds to partial degradations, gradual reconfigurations and localized interruptions of enjoyment. The usefulness of the distinction, in this context, lies not in declaring which label “wins”, but in preventing the reading of the offer and of the duties of adequacy from being flattened as if supply were an event.

The coexistence between the acquired object and continued execution appears clearly when the “product” sold is presented as a premium item, but its utility depends on routines of updating, compatibility and performance that only exist while the service remains technically operational. This is what happens with skins, especially those structured as evolving models that change form during the match itself. The consumer does not buy merely an “image” coupled to the character. They acquire a position of enjoyment that is only realized if the game environment remains capable of rendering models, effects, animations and interfaces that are reconfigured with each cycle of patches and system adjustments Riot Games(Riot Games, 2022e)447.

The dogmatically relevant point is not to turn this item into a “service” by classificatory will, nor to pretend that the purchase is exhausted in the delivery of a static package. What matters is to perceive that, in objects of this type, utility is a bundle that depends on continuous execution of software. The form, the visual reading, the logic of transformation and the aesthetic experience happen as repeated performance in each match. Therefore, gradual alterations of the graphics engine, of the game's memory budget, of loading routines and of technical balance itself may, out of necessity of performance and compatibility, modify the way the item presents itself, limit behaviors, adjust features and, in extreme cases, require replacements and recompositions to preserve the integrity of the service Riot Games(Riot Games, 2022k)448.

p. 440 This type of example puts the product-service dichotomy back in the role the argument had already been demanding. The skin remains identifiable as an acquired object, not least because it is priced and communicated as a premium and distinctive item. At the same time, it does not exist as an enjoyable utility without the service of updating and maintaining the environment, which administers compatibility, fixes and adjustments. Riot's contractual framework itself assumes this structure by treating “virtual products” as elements that may evolve with fixes, graphical improvements and game rebalancing, and by declaring that updates and modifications may alter the service and restrict content and functionalities over time Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e)449.

In terms of attribution, this means that relevant losses may occur without total interruption. Frustration may manifest itself as localized degradation of the aesthetic experience, loss of consistency of the evolving model, alteration of features that were part of the practical promise of enjoyment, or reconfiguration that preserves the name of the item, but does not preserve the core of the utility that justified the purchase decision. In these cases, the product-service dichotomy helps precisely because it prevents two symmetrical blindnesses. The first blindness is to reduce everything to “delivered product” and treat subsequent technical modulations as an irrelevant exteriority. The second blindness is to dissolve the acquired object into “generic service” and, with that, make unsayable what, concretely, was lost, even if the bond continues formally in execution. The insertion of this empirical laboratory serves to show, with a case understandable even to those who do not live in the ecosystem, that the acquired and the executed coexist, and that protection needs to see this coexistence so that the protection of trust and the reading of adequacy are not flattened.

When the relationship is ongoing, the calibration of effects requires attention to elements that do not appear with the same clarity in one-off exchanges. Time projects duties of continuity and loyalty in execution, reorganizes the perception of adequacy, shifts the focus from “delivery” to “maintenance of utility”, and connects the protection of trust less to an isolated initial declaration and more to the consistency of performance throughout the bond. The product-service distinction can guide the reading of liability and warranty regimes without reducing the problem to materiality, and can help locate the core of non-performance when p. 441 enjoyment is frustrated not by total absence of performance, but by relevant loss of quality, accessibility, functionalities or conditions of use. Calling everything a product as if it were instantaneous delivery may blind the analysis to the processual character of supply; calling everything a service in a generic sense may dissolve the acquired object and hinder the identification of what was lost.

The dichotomy, therefore, only retains usefulness if it is treated as a tool subordinate to the reconstruction of supply over time. It contributes to mapping effects and consequences, but does not by itself define the structure of the regime when utility materializes as sustained and governed enjoyment. This shift requires methodological justification: the refusal of the totalizing label is not conceptual relaxation, but a requirement when the regulated reality demands functional distinctions that general-abstract vocabulary tends to erase.

Once this shift is legitimized, the product-service tension comes to be read as a symptom of change in the mode of realization of supply. In platform-governed consumption, utility is not exhausted in making content accessible for the first time; it depends on the permanence of a technical-organizational environment that sustains, organizes and conditions enjoyment over time. The object remains intelligible as an immaterial product, an acquired enjoyment bundle, while performance manifests itself processually through continuous acts of maintenance, updating, curation, reconfiguration and execution of rules that modulate the experience of use.

Supply ceases to be described only by the inaugural instant and comes to be recognized as a durable practice of administering conditions of enjoyment, in which “quality” and “adequacy” become temporal attributes, subject to gradual degradations, cumulative restrictions and persistent alterations that do not take the form of total interruption. The dichotomy only retains usefulness if it follows this movement and allows identifying, without reductionism, the coexistence between an acquirable object and a continued execution that keeps it alive as a legally relevant utility.

This recognition finds explicit dogmatic support in the discipline of long-term contracts. Claudia Lima Marques states that “the obligational relationship is a continuous whole”, in which, “from its birth (and even before)”, the parties are bound “by a series of ancillary duties of conduct (Nebenpflichten), imposed by the general principle of good faith in the performance of obligations” Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 110). Protection cannot treat time as an empty interval, because it is precisely in execution that duties of cooperation, loyalty and protection of trust are stabilized, able to capture conduct that, in regimes of continued enjoyment, manifests itself as permanent administration of the conditions of access and use.

p. 442 The reconfiguration deepens because long-term contracts carry structurally distinct expectations. Marques records that the contract is long-term, of successive and protracted execution, and therefore brings expectations different from those present in contracts of immediate execution, based more on trust, on repeated interaction and on the maintenance of the economic potential and the quality of services Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 106). In this framework, the contract is organized as a process, and platform-governed enjoyment can be described as a supply regime whose relevant conduct manifests itself in continued execution. The result is not to dilute the object, but to make explicit that the acquired utility presupposes permanent acts that are part of performance itself and that the product-service dichotomy only produces useful effects when it follows this processual character, without reducing protection to the inaugural instant.

With these premises, the product-service dichotomy can be stabilized as a rule of reading, and not as a classificatory dispute. Product fixes the intelligibility of what is acquired in the IOC, preserving the object as an economically valued and legally consumable enjoyment bundle, even if immaterial and dependent on technical mediations. Service, in turn, ceases to mean “there is no object” and comes to designate the mode of continued execution of supply, composed of practices of maintenance, organization and modulation of the conditions of enjoyment that are projected over time. The functional reading requires recognizing the coexistence of these dimensions without reciprocal reduction. This formula prevents two symmetrical distortions: it does not allow the supplier to escape protection by dissolving what was acquired into a generic performance and does not allow protection to be compressed into the paradigm of instantaneous delivery, incapable of capturing gradual degradations, persistent reconfigurations and relevant losses in the quality of enjoyment.

The understanding of supply as continued execution finds direct support when dogmatics shifts the focus from the isolated good to the activity of organizing, facilitating and sustaining access to an economically valued utility. In the Comentários ao Código de Defesa do Consumidor, Claudia Lima Marques makes explicit that the field of the CDC encompasses all legally consumable, useful and economically valued services and doings, even if they consist in simple use, simple organization, simple intermediation, simple information or ancillary conduct linked to a purpose valued in the market Miragem(Marques; Benjamin; Miragem, 2013, p. 186). In the same sense, in defining the verb to supply, she records that to supply means to provide, stock, furnish, give, administer, facilitate and afford, qualifying it as an activity independent of whoever holds ownership of the goods that may be used to provide the service and of its ancillary duties Miragem(Marques; Benjamin; Miragem, 2013, p. 179). When the core of the relationship lies in the administration of a structure p. 443 that makes enjoyment possible, the legally relevant conduct is located in the very organization of access, and not in the material ownership of the means employed.

This reading is completed when it is shown that, in complex and lasting relationships, the supplier may be precisely the organizer of the chains that make consumption viable, even if the final performance is executed by third parties. Marques describes this structure by stating that these are services generally carried out with the use of third parties to achieve the true contractual objective, which leads to the organization of invisible chains of direct and indirect suppliers Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 76). In a convergent key, discussing network activities, Nunes maintains that, in reality, the service provided is the supply and that the assembly of the entire network exists to make the realization of that supply viable Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 114]). It thus becomes consistent to read platform-based continued enjoyment as supply that is realized through infrastructure and the governance of access. The product-service dichotomy remains in the system as a tool for specific effects, without erasure of the object, and the immaterial product remains identifiable, merely complemented by the service dimension, understood as a continuous activity of organizing and administering the conditions of enjoyment.

Repositioning the dichotomy as an instrument of reading requires, finally, justifying why dogmatics needs to operate with controllable cuts in contexts of complexity and continued enjoyment. From the methodological perspective already established, the dogmatic operation is a technique aimed at decidability, which increases uncertainties in a way compatible with the binding to norms and with institutional pressure for decision. In platform-governed consumption, insisting on generic labels or rigid classifications tends to paralyze protection, because the vocabulary needs to cut the phenomenon so as to preserve what is relevant for attribution and restoration. The conceptual engineering of the IOC, in this framework, is not a luxury, but a condition of possibility of non-arbitrary protection in environments of opacity and mutability.

In regimes of continued enjoyment, the counterfactual stabilization of normative expectations, already established as a methodological lock of the regime of the IOC, makes it possible to treat as legally relevant duties of continuity, adequacy and loyalty even when the technical environment admits constant reconfigurations. The conflict ceases to be only the event and comes to be the resistance of normative expectations to the frustration produced by the very mode of functioning of supply.

Finally, in scenarios of opacity and insecurity, legal decision cannot be presented as the elimination of conflict, but as its conversion into operable alternatives, p. 444 since decisions absorb insecurity not because they eliminate the conflict, but because they transform it Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 306]). Therefore, the product-service dichotomy, when treated as a totalizing label, tends to produce blindness or impossibility of decision. When treated as a calibrating instrument, on the contrary, it helps make it possible to decide what, under rigid classifications or undifferentiated terms, would remain undecidable.

In this way, the dichotomy is preserved in the system without coming to govern the system. It remains useful for calibrating effects and locating specific regimes, but ceases to function as a totalizing answer for a supply whose utility is realized over time and under infrastructural governance. The immaterial product preserves the intelligibility of what was acquired as an economically valued enjoyment bundle. Service describes the continuous activity of organizing, facilitating and administering the conditions of access and use, where ancillary duties, expectations of continuity and conduct relevant to adequacy and quality are installed. The consequence is a rule of reading that makes the phenomenon decidable without flattening it. Initial delivery does not exhaust performance, and continued execution does not erase the object.

With the dichotomy repositioned in this instrumental role, the next step ceases to be classificatory and becomes one of attribution. It is necessary to identify when the very architecture of supply, in its permanent administration, manifests exploitative purposes and patterns of modulation that exceed the normal risk of the bond and are converted into a controllable legal criterion. It is this transition that requires advancing to the category of abusive teleology, capable of bringing platform governance back to the plane of legally relevant conduct and of preparing, next, the delimitation of relevant losses and the standards of control and restoration compatible with continued enjoyment.
