7.1.1.5 On the parameters of open auditability for control without indiscriminate opening of code

See this section in the PDF

p. 726

The controllability of the Immaterial Object of Consumption requires that relevant alterations of the service, of access, of the conditions of enjoyment and of the very objects incorporated into the ecosystem can be institutionally reconstructed in case of controversy. This requirement does not stem from technical curiosity about the internal workings of the platform, nor from a maximalist claim to full transparency. It stems from the elementary need to prevent the p. 727 structural opacity of the environment from operating as a mechanism of practical immunization against legal control. Whenever enjoyment depends on continuous private governance, the discipline of the relationship presupposes some degree of verifiable intelligibility about what was altered, when, for what reasons it was justified and what relevant effects it produced on the consumer's experience.

It is in this context that open auditability is asserted as a parameter of legitimacy. The expression does not designate unrestricted opening of source code, full exposure of the technological architecture or a generalized waiver of business secrets. Its core is more precise and institutionally more defensible. Open auditability means the existence of minimal conditions of verifiability about relevant aspects of supply, so that protection bodies, the Judiciary, experts and, to a certain extent, consumers themselves can reconstruct the relation between promise, alteration, sanction, functional impact and the justification presented by the supplier. What is demanded is not to strip the whole system bare, but to make controllable, at a sufficient level, whatever legally interferes with enjoyment.

This parameter breaks down a false dichotomy that often contaminates the debate. On one side, it is argued that any requirement of transparency would compromise industrial secrecy, innovation or business autonomy. On the other, it is assumed that control would only be effective if the platform's internal workings were fully opened. Both positions are bad. The first turns technical complexity into an alibi for irresponsibility. The second confuses legal protection with total invasion of the productive environment. Open auditability rejects this binary theater. It starts from the premise that exclusivity, complexity and secrecy can coexist with minimal duties of institutional intelligibility, provided these bear on the elements relevant to understanding the conflict and to reconstructing the disputed supply.

The requirement is therefore selective and functional. What must be auditable is not everything, but what matters to make the controversy decidable. Relevant alterations of access rules, reconfigurations with functional impact, version changes that affect utility or performance, sanctions bearing on permanence or visibility, ordinary modulations of the environment and decision criteria with material repercussions on enjoyment cannot remain in a zone of undemonstrability shielded by the mere claim of technicality. In these cases, the platform must be able to preserve and exhibit trails, records, minimal logs, operational justifications and contextualizing elements sufficient to allow bounded institutional verification. It is not a matter of demanding a self-congratulatory narrative from the supplier, but of imposing objective conditions for control.

p. 728 This point is especially important because, in platform-governed ecosystems, opacity does not function only as a technical characteristic of the environment. It functions as an asymmetric distribution of power within the relationship. Whoever controls the criteria of alteration, the records of what was done, the memory of the system and the language in which these events can be described also concentrates the capacity to define what will be considered demonstrable. Without minimal auditability, the conflict tends to be degraded into an unequal confrontation between the consumer's subjective experience and a formally self-justified corporate version. Private governance then ceases to be only a power to modulate enjoyment and becomes also a power to control the institutional conditions of evidence. It is precisely against this practical immunity that the parameter is directed.

Open auditability also performs a function of containing litigation. Paradoxically, contrary to what is often claimed, the minimum of institutional verifiability does not indefinitely expand technical judicialization. It rationalizes it. When there are preserved trails, identifiable relevant versions, minimally statable reasons for alteration and basic elements for reconstructing the conflict, the judicial discussion is prevented from turning into an endless war of opaque technicalities, in which each side speaks a different language and the decision ends up handed over to intuitive deference to the more organized party. The parameter does not seek to hypercomplexify the proceedings. It seeks to create sufficient grounding for the controversy to be delimited and for expert examinations, document production and orders for clarification to bear on knowable objects, and not on self-immune corporate abstractions.

The intelligibility required by this parameter does not coincide with absolute explainability. Law does not need to understand, in their microscopic totality, all the internal flows of the system in order to exercise legitimate control over the consumer relationship. What it needs is sufficient intelligibility to identify the occurrence of a relevant alteration, relate it to the promised enjoyment, verify whether there was a justification compatible with the legal regime and ascertain whether the sanction, the change or the modulation respected minimal standards of predictability, proportionality and contestability. The parameter is calibrated precisely to make control possible without demanding technical omniscience from the judge or disrupting the economic operation of the ecosystem. In governed environments, this is what separates legitimate complexity from obscurity turned into a technique of power. Not total transparency, but sufficient transparency. Not absolute revelation, but minimal verifiability. Not a sweeping search of the system, but refusal of the practical immunity produced by structural opacity.

This formulation has a relevant consequence for the application of the CDC in platform environments. Business secrecy, economic exclusivity and the complexity of the p. 729 service cease to operate as absolute vetoes on control and become elements to be reconciled with duties of minimal verifiability. The platform remains the holder of its intangible assets and its internal architecture, but can no longer invoke them to prevent entirely the institutional reconstruction of events that affect the consumer's enjoyment in a legally relevant way. Structural opacity thus ceases to be a zone naturally immune to protection and becomes a legal problem that can be addressed through preservation orders, bounded document production, restricted expert examination, verifiable reasoning and other instruments compatible with the procedural and administrative rationality of the protective system.
