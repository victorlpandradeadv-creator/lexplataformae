5.1.2.2 On the functional qualification of the IOC and its systematic coherence with the CDC

See this section in the PDF

p. 423

The anchoring of the immaterial object of consumption in the concept of product of Article 3, paragraph 1, of the CDC ensures the dogmatic insertion of the phenomenon and preserves the intelligibility of what is acquired in platform-governed enjoyment, without reducing the relationship to a one-off event of delivery or dissolving it into an undifferentiated notion of service. Even so, qualification as an immaterial product does not settle the central problem of the IOC regime. In continuous consumption, utility is not realized as isolated “content”, but as a position of enjoyment sustained in a technical-organizational environment that maintains, modulates and conditions the enjoyment bundle over time through internal rules, curation, reconfiguration and enforcement executed by design. The next step is to reconstruct the IOC as an infrastructure-concept, capable of operating simultaneously as a form of qualification of the object and as an interpretive key to the supply regime, reorganizing the reading of the offer, the identification of relevant losses and the orientation of restoration when degradation manifests itself as erosion of the enjoyment bundle, even without total interruption of formal access.

The methodological justification for this operation finds in Karl Larenz (1997) an especially adequate vocabulary. Conceptual construction is not presented as a classificatory ornament, but as an instrument for making recurring conflicts decidable in a stable and verifiable way, without requiring the solution to depend on moral impressions, on episodic readings of clauses, or on mechanical subsumptions insensitive to the contemporary form of supply. The question is explicit when it is stated that dogmatics “makes ‘a decisive contribution to jurisprudential stability, when its problems of valuation are resolved into problems of thought and, as such, can be better mastered, in the language familiar to it, through a better dogmatic construction of the concept, through uncovering, understanding and mastering the specificity of the regulation’” Larenz(Larenz, 1997, p. 313-314).

In the regime of the IOC, this “specificity of the regulation” corresponds to the fact that the acquired utility is administered over time by infrastructural conditions of availability, visibility, circulation, permanence and sanction, so that the controversy is clarified neither by a snapshot of delivery nor by a generic label of performance.

p. 424 This reconstruction demands, however, an additional caution. The conceptual effort must remain internal to the CDC and to its protective teleology, without inaugurating an autonomous merely “digital” vocabulary and without opening space for an elasticity that replaces criterion with rhetoric. The dogmatic risk of dissolving abstraction, already warned of by Larenz, appears when a category serves external systematics but fails precisely where density is required to discuss content and limits. In platform consumption, this erasure produces a direct effect. The object disappears from the analysis, the offer loses its delimiting function and restoration becomes erratic when degradation occurs as persistent erosion of the enjoyment bundle, without absolute blocking of formal access.

The symmetrical critique is equally relevant. The pursuit of precision can buy apparent certainty at the price of emptying the concept, producing a category that is formally clean and materially blind — a risk already pointed out by Larenz regarding abstraction taken to the extreme. For the IOC, this means that insistence on rigid oppositions or on undifferentiated labels delivers a language incapable of seeing relevant losses in the concrete case and incapable of sustaining proof, attribution and restoration in continuous consumption. The reconstruction of the IOC as an infrastructure-concept aims to block these two falls without abandoning the unity of the system, preserving the intelligibility of the object and, at the same time, incorporating infrastructure as a constitutive dimension of the mode of existence of enjoyment.

The systematic coherence of this operation presupposes understanding the legal system as a teleological order able to integrate new situations by internal criteria, oriented by function and by meaning — a characteristic already formulated by Larenz as a structural trait of the internal system. The consequence is that the relevant question is not limited to verifying whether there was initial enabling of access, but brings supply back to the promise that is realized over time and to the infrastructural conditions that make it enjoyable, because it is on this plane that the content of what was acquired and the scope of its protection under the CDC are decided.

The requirement of conceptual reconstruction also depends on an institutional justification aimed at decidability under uncertainty. In platform-governed consumption, protection cannot be held hostage to an ideal of full intelligibility of the ecosystem, nor can it require total transparency as a condition for recognizing relevant non-performance. From the already established perspective that legal decision operates as a complex procedure in the face of insecurity, and not as a rational choice in a scenario of complete information, the reconstruction of the IOC as an infrastructure-concept assumes the function of a dogmatic technology of decidability, by providing a grammar to recognize relevant losses and guide attribution and restoration even when the execution of supply remains partially opaque.

p. 425 This same requirement appears at the level of legal communication. In environments marked by structural asymmetry of intelligibility, shifting the axis to the promise of control through accumulation of information tends to produce the opposite effect, turning protection into a hostage of an idealized situation of deliberation. Ferraz Jr. criticizes this approach by maintaining that linking decision and consensus immediately turns the “rational” decision into an idealized model. In this model, all the relevant data would be available from the outset and all the alternatives could be enumerated and evaluated in advance, which is incompatible both with non-controllable variables and with the very structure of the conflict Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 1997, p. 43). In the regime of the IOC, dogmatics must operate with previously structured criteria for qualifying the object and reading the enjoyment bundle, on pain of turning opacity into an alibi of indeterminability or of reducing the debate to generic labels such as “service”.

The counterfactual stabilization of normative expectations, already established earlier in a methodological key, operates, in platform consumption, as a block on two recurring maneuvers. The first reduces delivery to the formal provision of access and treats the nominal persistence of content as eliminating the possibility of relevant non-performance. The second dissolves the object into a vague and reconfigurable performance, converting restoration into an oscillation between shallow restitutions and case-by-case assessments. When the promise is recognized as a normative reference, the category can maintain the core of value of what was acquired as an anchor for identifying relevant losses, even when enjoyment is modulated over time by curation, visibility, ranking, internal rules and sanctions executed by design.

At this point, the regime of the IOC requires recognizing that enjoyment does not occur “despite” the ecosystem, but through a layer of governance that operates as practical normativity. Here, the contribution of Eros Roberto Grau makes it possible to describe this layer with legal precision and without dissolving the argument into a general sociology of private power, because it shifts the criterion of the legal from the state monopoly to the presence of functional coercion exercised by a social organization over a delimited group.

On platforms, this coercion is not exhausted in the clause. It is realized in the combination of internal rules, interface architecture and enforcement mechanisms that condition access, permanence, visibility and utility of what was acquired, converting the functioning of the environment into a regime of conduct. What matters, for the analysis of the IOC, is that this normativity is part of the very mode of execution of the promise, because it defines what counts as possible, stable and recognized enjoyment within the ecosystem.

p. 426 Recognizing practical normativity, however, is not equivalent to declaring every technical decision unlawful, nor to turning ordinary interface frictions into abuse. The objective is to prevent technique from functioning as automatic shielding against attribution when the design and execution of the ecosystem significantly reconfigure the promised enjoyment bundle, shifting risk to the consumer under the appearance of a simple “update” or “operational adjustment”.

On such bases, the functional qualification of the IOC can be formulated in an operable way. Infrastructure does not appear as a mere technical context accompanying a previously delivered object. It is part of the form in which the promise of enjoyment is fulfilled, because it is through it that the conditions of availability, presentation, circulation, permanence and sanction that make the acquired utility effectively enjoyable are maintained and modulated over time. Reconstructing the IOC as an infrastructure-concept means qualifying the object according to its legal-economic mode of existence in continuous consumption, preserving the intelligibility of what was acquired without reducing supply to a snapshot of delivery or dissolving it into an undifferentiated generic performance.

From this operator, the object can be described as a bundle of functionalities and significations administered throughout the relationship, coupled with permissions and limits of use, regimes of visibility and ordering, metrics and signals of recognition, and mechanisms of curation and enforcement executed by design. Enjoyment ceases to be a simple initial enabling and becomes a sustained position in the ecosystem, whose utility depends on the continuity of infrastructural conditions that organize expectations and distribute consequences. The category does not indefinitely expand the unlawful act nor moralize technical choices. It stabilizes a grammar to identify the core of the promised enjoyment bundle and to distinguish, by controllable criteria, between ordinary modulations of the regime and legally relevant significant degradations, including when nominal access persists.

Closing the item also requires stating clearly the negative edge of the reconstruction. The reconfigurability of the environment does not turn every update, interface adjustment, change in ranking or ordinary curation choice into unlawfulness. What becomes legally relevant is significant, persistent degradation recognizable by criteria, that which alters the core of the economically offered utility or structurally lowers the conditions of enjoyment that gave meaning to the acquisition. The discussion thus ceases to oscillate between formalism and abstraction and comes to require parameters of recognition, evidence and limits of application, so that the legal response remains proportional, verifiable and stable. Once this functional qualification is established, it is possible to move on to the criteria of p. 427 incidence, proof and restoration of the regime of the IOC, now with a conceptual operator capable of keeping the object as object and, at the same time, incorporating infrastructural governance as a legally relevant dimension of supply.
