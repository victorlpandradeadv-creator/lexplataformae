2.2 On the theoretical-normative and sectoral scope

See this section in the PDF

p. 54

The theoretical-normative scope is defined so as to make legally cognizable the conflicts typical of continuous digital services, in which enjoyment is governed by a platform and the value of what is acquired depends on private rules that operate through clauses, interface architecture and sanction practices.

The normative basis is the constitutional order of consumer protection and of the economic order, which sets limits on the exploitation of asymmetries, on structural opacity and on forms of private power capable of reorganizing access, information and risk. Along this axis, the Consumer Protection Code is kept as the central matrix of protection, while intellectual property legislation is drawn on with a functional status, as the legal grammar of access control and licensing which, in digital markets, structures the very form in which utilities circulate and are enjoyed. Licenses and technical restrictions are treated not merely as contractual content but as legal-technological infrastructure that determines what can be used, for how long, on what terms and under which unilateral powers of modulation, without this operating as a blanket authorization to degrade promises of adequacy, comparability and restoration in consumption.

In the specific regime for software, this temporality is not just a technological fact. It is an express legal category and serves as a relevant normative boundary for the analysis of p. 55 continuous relationships. Articles 7 and 8 of Law No. 9,609/1998 link a duty to inform about technical validity with a duty to provide complementary technical support for proper functioning during that period, even upon withdrawal from circulation, which rules out treating “update”, “end of support” and “maintenance” as neutral matters beyond the reach of the protection of adequacy and transparency. Within this scope, these provisions operate as points of dogmatic control for separating operational variations compatible with supply from relevant reconfigurations of the content of the performance.

In a dogmatic key, the scope recognizes that platform governance acts as the continuous execution of the relationship, insofar as internal sanctions, blocks, account restrictions, degradations and recalibrations of access function as forms of performance, non-performance and functional coercion built into the very experience of use. The conflict no longer revolves around a one-off delivery. It is reconstructed as a dispute over continuity, functional stability and the actual content of the performance in objects administered over time, under private powers exercised at the contractual, technical and organizational levels.

At the sectoral level, interactive entertainment is taken as a privileged field of observation because it concentrates mechanisms of permanence, artificial scarcity, reputation, continuous administration of identity and positional modulation, making visible the frictions that continuous digital services create for traditional categories of adequacy and restoration. In this context, the Legal Framework for Electronic Games is treated as an institutional reference that provides a minimum framing for the sector, useful for situating the categories, concerns and regulatory language associated with the economic and cultural recognition of the activity, without turning the inquiry into a legislative inventory and without shifting the decisional core to the current state of legislative efforts to regulate it.

The scope, therefore, is not meant to propose a new microsystem or to exhaust the sectoral rules. Its aim is to mark out the normative boundaries within which the dogmatic reconstruction will operate, keeping the constitutional and consumer-law axis as the matrix of protection and drawing on intellectual property and the regulation relevant to the segment analyzed as structural keys for understanding enclosure, circulation and the modulation of access in digital markets.

A game-as-a-service ecosystem with an economy of virtual goods is adopted because it is a methodologically fertile environment for observing the formation of value, private governance, conflicts over enjoyment and disputes over meaning, without this becoming an autonomous empirical description. The ecosystem is characterized gradually throughout the inquiry, through minimal p. 56 references and illustrative vignettes, used only when needed to make the mechanisms under discussion intelligible and to anchor the application of the legal categories, with no claim to exhaustive demonstration.
