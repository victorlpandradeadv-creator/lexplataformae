6.2.1 On product, service and factual vulnerability under the CDC

See this section in the PDF

p. 550

The starting point of the regime of the IOC does not lie in resolving, with taxonomic solemnity, whether continuous digital supply would be “more product” or “more service”. The distinction exists in the system, has real dogmatic utility and will remain relevant for specific purposes of p. 551 periods, quality defects, remedies and calibration of consequences597. The error lies in turning it into an ontological prison. In governed digital ecosystems, consumption is no longer organized according to the paradigm of the one-off delivery of a stable utility, but according to the paradigm of the continuous maintenance, updating and modulation of enjoyment under private control. Within this framework, product and service remain operative, but cease to function as the exclusive key of intelligibility of the conflict. The center of the problem becomes the relationship of administered enjoyment.

This derives from the very way the CDC was built. The final recipient remains the subjective point of reference of the system598, and the supplier remains identified by its professional insertion in the consumer market. But the legal concepts of product and service were formulated in a deliberately open way, precisely to prevent narrow readings that leave out immaterial supplies, hybrid arrangements and complex structures of performance. A product is any good, including immaterial, and a service is any activity supplied in the market for remuneration, which reveals from the outset that the system was not designed to protect only corporeal things and simple performances Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 194; p. 193; p. 272])599. Therefore, it is not a matter of forcing an extension of the legal text; it is only a matter of reading it free of any conceptual claustrophobia.

This is also why the distinction between product and service cannot be conducted as an exercise in dogmatic taxidermy. In consumer matters, the utility of categories depends on their capacity to solve the protective problem, and not on satisfying a classificatory purism indifferent to the way supply really operates. Consumer law works with more open and functional concepts because they only acquire meaning p. 552 in light of the problem to be solved, and it is this key that prevents using the product-service dichotomy as an exclusionary filter of protection in continuous and governed digital supplies Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 219-220; p. 149-150)600. The point is not to abolish the distinction, but to refuse its fetishization.

This reading becomes even clearer when one observes that the CDC itself starts from relational categories and not from isolated essences. Consumer, supplier, product and service only make dogmatic sense within a concrete supply relationship, and not as watertight compartments capable of deciding everything alone. The system was designed to capture the economic dynamics actually in place, not to reward whoever best plays at classifying the world into elegant little boxes Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 31; p. 139])601. In p. 553 complex digital supplies, this is decisive: the central question is not whether the object fits comfortably into a civilist ontology inherited from another technological environment, but whether there is a final recipient, a professional market agent and a utility supplied in a context of structural dependence.

It is in this respect that factual vulnerability manifests itself as the true interpretive infrastructure of the item. The CDC does not protect the consumer despite their fragility; it protects them because this fragility is the constitutive fact of the consumer relationship. Vulnerability is the starting point of the system and militates in favor of the final recipient precisely because the market is organized by asymmetries that the consumer does not control Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 168; p. 118; p. 166])602. In other words: the incidence of the CDC does not arise from the classificatory purity of the relationship, but from the presence of a vulnerable party before a supplier that unilaterally structures the conditions of supply.

This vulnerability, of course, is not limited to possible economic insufficiency. It is also technical, informational, organizational and practical. The consumer enters the market without taking part in the definition of the productive models, without controlling the processes that structure the offer and without mastering the criteria that organize the functioning of the good or service supplied. They receive models unilaterally designed by the supplier and are often not even in a position to fully understand their logic of operation Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 72; p. 457])603. In continuous digital environments, this asymmetry intensifies because supply p. 554 does not end at the initial acquisition: it projects itself over time through infrastructure, updating, curation, restriction and continued intervention by the supplier in the user's experience.

The same holds for understanding the service and the consumed utility. Performance does not always present itself in a linear, simple and isolable form. There are composite services, utilities dependent on multiple agents, instrumental activities articulated with each other and performances whose enjoyment only exists because an entire technical, communicational and relational architecture keeps running in the background Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 97; p. 198])604. This observation is particularly useful here because the IOC is not to be confused with a “digital file” nor with “momentary access”. Its enjoyment depends on an ecosystem that maintains, alters and administers it. For this reason legal control cannot remain stuck at the abstract instant of availability; it must reach the regime of maintenance of the acquired utility.

This was, moreover, already the material spirit of the CDC from its classical basis. The notion of service was conceived as any utility supplied in the market to the consumer, and the legislative choice for broad categories of goods and services pointed precisely to the real objects of interest of consumer relationships, and not to conceptual barriers intended to narrow protection Filomeno(Filomeno, 2018, [p. 110])605. The system was born oriented to the utility concretely p. 555 supplied to the final recipient, and not to terminological disputes disconnected from the protective function of the Code. This fact matters because it prevents regression to formalisms that the CDC itself had already overcome at the start.

Likewise, vulnerability was recognized early on as a structural presupposition of consumer-law protection, linked not only to the absence of bargaining power, but to the consumer's lack of control over products, services, information and functioning criteria defined by the supplier. In this field, then, the old dogmas of autonomy of the will in its pure version, of abstract equality and of contractual intangibility give way, since they presuppose a real parity that simply does not exist when the market is unilaterally organized by whoever supplies Filomeno(Filomeno, 2018, [p. 61; p. 346])606. In a continuous digital environment, this perception ceases to be a theoretical detail and becomes the very key for reading the bond.

Contracting by adhesion, predisposed general conditions, network organization, practical dependence of the adhering party and difficulty in understanding the internal logic of the service are clear indications of factual vulnerability, even when the consumer has technical training or works professionally in another field. In long-term bonds, dependence may deepen to the point of frustrating relevant expectations if the relationship is interrupted or unilaterally reconfigured, which makes even less defensible a reading centered only p. 556 on superficial classificatory labels Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 613; p. 109)607. This is why the IOC must be read from the standpoint of governed enjoyment, and not only from the formal appearance of an isolable performance.

In this context, factual vulnerability is not merely a backdrop; it is a criterion of interpretation. Consumer law belongs to the rights of protection precisely because it seeks to restore material equality in situations of real inequality, and this function is only achieved when the reading of the legal categories remains functional, broad and faithful to the structure of the market Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 34; p. 122])608. In other words, product, service, consumer and supplier only make sense within a system built to respond to p. 557 vulnerability. When the analysis forgets this, it becomes high-level conceptual bureaucracy, but of low utility. A very refined specialty, but not very effective.

Offer, adequacy, information, trust, reparation and restoration must be read from the standpoint of governed enjoyment, and not only from the literal content of a detachable performance. The consumed object is inseparable from the ecosystem that sustains, modifies and administers it, which is why the center of control must shift to standards of controllability of the environment, verifiability of changes and minimum stability of the acquired experience. The product-service dichotomy remains relevant, but as an instrumental variable to calibrate specific consequences, not as a totem defining the entire regime.

In cases of unilateral downgrading of enjoyment, the door of the quality defect remains available609, precisely because relevant inadequacy need not take the crude form of total uselessness. It may appear as an objective decrease in the functional value of the enjoyment promised and maintained over time. In IOCs, this means that the discussion about quality defects cannot be captured by traditional images of a defect perceptible by direct inspection. Functional equivalence comes to operate as a ruler better suited to the problem: what matters is whether the acquired experience was degraded in a legally relevant way by the continuous regime of private governance.

The distinction between product and service exists, remains operative and will be dealt with more surgically further on. But, for the regime of the IOC, the true interpretive foundation lies in the factual vulnerability of the final recipient before an immaterial, continuous supply governed by a third party. It is this fact that authorizes rereading the CDC from the standpoint of administered enjoyment, and not of the old scene of the stable thing delivered once and for all.

The concepts of product and service in the CDC were formulated in a deliberately open way, precisely to prevent a narrow reading that leaves out of consumer-law protection immaterial supplies, complex structures of performance and hybrid market arrangements. Paragraph 1 of Article 3 defines product as “any good, movable or immovable, material or immaterial”, which allows it to be understood beyond the narrower civil categories and to encompass goods consumable physically or not, new or used, fungible or non-fungible, principal or accessory Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 194]). The same openness projects itself onto the concept of service. Paragraph 2 of Article 3 defines it as “any activity supplied in the consumer market, for remuneration”, with concise and intentionally open wording, precisely to ensure the inclusion of a wide number of providers and arrangements of performance within the field of incidence of the CDC Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 193]). The Manual further stresses that the legislator did not build rigid concepts, but adopted a true “typological statement, flexible par excellence”, capable of reaching mixed acts, immaterial supplies and economically complex relationships Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 272]). ↩
  4. The reading of the categories of product and service within the CDC cannot be conducted as an exercise of rigid taxonomy, alien to the protective function of the microsystem. The distinction remains legally relevant, but must not operate as a classificatory jail capable of neutralizing the incidence of the consumer-law regime whenever supply takes technically hybrid, procedural or continuously governed forms, as happens with immaterial objects of consumption. In consumer matters, the dogmatic utility of categories depends on their capacity to solve the protective problem posed, and not on satisfying an ontological purism that, in the end, only serves to leave the consumer alone in the middle of the labyrinth. It is in this direction that Marques observes that consumer law moves away from the strict positivist ideal and adopts a problem-oriented style of thought, with concepts “more open, more functional, delimited without so much logical rigor”, precisely because they “only take on meaning in light of the problem to be solved” Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 219-220). This functional key prevents the product-service distinction from being converted into an exclusionary filter of protection, above all when the bond involves continuous digital supplies, modulated over time by private architecture, updates, restrictions of use and unilateral reconfigurations. The point is not to abolish the categories, but to refuse their fetishized use against the purpose of the system. Marques argues precisely that the rules of the CDC must be interpreted according to the protective logic of the Code itself and in accordance with its normative purpose, which requires a teleological and not merely formal reading. In dealing with the definition of consumer, the author states that “the rule of art. 2 must be interpreted in accordance with the special protection system of the Code and in accordance with the purpose of the norm”, concluding that “only a teleological interpretation” allows the field of protection of the CDC to be adequately defined, including through openness to cases in which vulnerability proves legally relevant, even if certain traditional classificatory fits do not present themselves in their purest form Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 149-150). Applied to the present problem, this orientation authorizes privileging the protective function of the microsystem over excessive classificatory formalisms, so that the distinction between product and service continues to serve Law, and not the other way around. ↩
  5. Bruno Miragem is particularly important at this point, because he makes quite clear that the CDC operates on the basis of functional and relational categories, and not of rigid ontological compartments. The author observes that the Brazilian legislator did not directly define the “consumer relationship”, preferring to conceptualize its subjects — consumer and supplier — and its object — product or service. For that very reason, these are relational and dependent concepts: there will only be a consumer if there is also a supplier, as well as a product or service, so that these definitions do not stand in isolation, and must appear jointly to trigger the application of the Code Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 139]). The application of the CDC does not depend on excessively formalistic or classificatory fits, but on the concrete verification of a relational dynamic in which a vulnerable final recipient becomes bound to a market agent around a supplied utility. This same reading appears when Miragem states that the scope of the consumer law microsystem was defined precisely on the basis of the notion of the subjects of the consumer relationship and of its object, these being, once again, relational concepts, that is, legal positions that only reveal themselves within a concrete relationship Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 31]). The point is especially useful because it shows that consumer, supplier, product and service are not watertight categories, designed to artificially limit the CDC's field of application, but keys of functional reading capable of capturing the economic reality actually at hand. It is precisely this key that matters to the present item. In complex digital supplies, the central question should not be whether the object fits comfortably into old civil-law taxonomies, but whether there is, in practice, a final recipient, a market professional and a utility offered in a context of vulnerability. Miragem thus helps to show that the CDC already has sufficient language to reach sophisticated techno-digital relationships, provided that its categories are read functionally. The gain is great: less classificatory fetish, more fidelity to the way the consumer relationship really happens. ↩
  6. Vulnerability is not a lateral element of the consumer relationship, but a structuring presupposition of the very protective application of the CDC. The system does not protect the consumer despite their fragility; it protects them precisely because it recognizes that fragility as a constitutive datum of the consumer market. The Manual recovers Antonio Herman Benjamin's formulation according to which vulnerability is the “fundamental piece” and the “starting point” of the entire application of consumer law, especially in contractual matters, which is why, in light of arts. 2 and 4, I, a true presumption of vulnerability operates in favor of natural persons who are final recipients of products and services Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 168]). The STJ case law cited by the work goes in the same direction when it states that the starting point of the CDC is the principle of the consumer's vulnerability, understood as a mechanism intended to guarantee formal-material equality between the subjects of the consumer relationship and to illuminate the application of the protective and rebalancing norms in pursuit of equitable justice Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 118]). Thus, the legal consumer relationship is not defined merely by the abstract presence of persons at its poles, but by the presence of a vulnerable party and of a supplier, which may justify the application of the CDC even in relationships between legal entities Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 166]). ↩
  7. The consumer's vulnerability, in Rizzatto Nunes, does not appear as a lateral or occasional element of the consumer relationship, but as a structural presupposition of the very protective application of the CDC. The decisive point lies not only in possible economic inferiority, but in the technical asymmetry that separates consumer and supplier within the productive process. The consumer enters the market without taking part in the definition, elaboration or control of the goods and services offered to them, which is why they occupy a position of objective fragility before productive models unilaterally organized by the supplier. As the author states, the consumer is “the weak element” of this market formation, precisely because they are “admittedly vulnerable as a recipient of the production models unilaterally defined and imposed by the supplier”, being moreover a “mere spectator at the spectacle of production” Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 72]). This finding deepens when vulnerability is also read in its informational dimension. Nunes stresses that the consumer not only stands outside the productive system, but also lacks the technical conditions needed to understand its functioning or to assess, on equal terms, the products and services presented to them. The consumer's fragility thus stems from the lack of command over the processes, criteria and information that structure the offer, which justifies the corrective intervention of consumer law as an instrument for the material realization of equality. Along these lines, the author notes that the law recognizes the fact that the consumer “not only has no access to the productive system but has no means of knowing how it works”, which is why they must be understood as “the weak party of the legal consumer relationship” Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 457]). ↩
  8. Rizzatto Nunes's reading is particularly useful at this point because it puts the classificatory problem back in its proper scale: the core of art. 3 of the CDC lies not in restricting the reach of protection to corporeal objects or simple performances, but in ensuring that consumer-law protection applies to every supply relationship economically organized for the market. On this view, the legal concept of product was deliberately formulated in comprehensive terms, precisely to prevent escape zones from the protective regime, also reaching immaterial goods and incorporeal utilities made available to the consumer. As the author summarizes, the law adopted concepts “as generic as possible” because “the aim is that nothing escapes it”, so that even performances traditionally perceived as intangible can already be framed as immaterial goods for purposes of the CDC's application Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 97]). The same logic projects onto the concept of service. Nunes observes that not every consumer performance presents itself in a linear or isolated form, there being simple services, composite services and structures in which the final utility depends on the articulation between multiple agents, instrumental activities and even aggregated products. For that reason, supply is not exhausted in the one-off act of delivery, also encompassing the maintenance of the functional architecture necessary for the enjoyment of the contracted utility. In the author's words, there are services “composed of others” and also cases in which the performance is organized “through the use of products” Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 198]). This framing is especially relevant for immaterial supplies in a digital environment, in which the consumer experience depends less on an isolated thing and more on the operational continuity of a technical, communicational and relational infrastructure set in motion by the supplier. ↩
  9. Commenting on paragraph 2 of art. 3, Filomeno recalls that service is “any activity supplied in the consumer market, for remuneration”, observing that the Code covers “each and every type of service”, understood as a utility enjoyed by the consumer and provided by a given supplier, in a facere Filomeno(Filomeno, 2018, [p. 110]). The formulation is especially useful because it takes the legal concept back to its simplest material matrix: service, in the consumer-law regime, is the provision of utility in the form of a doing, without this authorizing narrow readings or formalistic classifications aimed at withdrawing protection. This basis is reinforced when Filomeno recalls that, in the genesis of the Code, the terms “goods and services” were chosen as unequivocal and generic expressions precisely to point the applier to the real objects of interest of consumer relationships, while at the same time excluding, by contrast, relationships of another nature, such as tax relationships Filomeno(Filomeno, 2018, [p. 110]). The value of his observation lies in showing that the CDC is born committed to a material reading of the consumer relationship, centered on the economic utility concretely supplied to the final recipient. Before any later sophistication about the product-service dichotomy, the system's starting point is already settled on broad, functional categories oriented toward consumer protection, and not on conceptual barriers designed to narrow it. ↩
  10. Filomeno treats the consumer's vulnerability as a structural presupposition of consumer-law protection. The author observes that, for evident reasons, among them the consumer's absolute lack of control over the products and services placed on the market and the absolute disproportion between their bargaining power and that of suppliers, one starts from the presupposition that the consumer is the vulnerable party in the consumer relationship, which justifies unequal legal treatment between naturally unequal parties and direct protective governmental action Filomeno(Filomeno, 2018, [p. 61]). The application of the CDC is not explained by classificatory formalism, but by a material finding of structural fragility that calls for normative correction. This vulnerability, moreover, is not reduced to possible economic insufficiency. Filomeno describes it as the consumer's fragility before the supplier also in informational and technical aspects, that is, with regard both to purchasing power and to the information made available and the specialized knowledge held by the supplier itself Filomeno(Filomeno, 2018, [p. 61]). For that very reason, the recognition of this vulnerability has a direct impact on contractual dogmatics: as the author notes, the finding that the consumer is the vulnerable party in consumer relationships causes the classical dogmas of full autonomy of the will, abstract equality and contractual intangibility to yield, precisely because such principles would only hold in full if the parties had equal real power of decision Filomeno(Filomeno, 2018, [p. 346]). Consumer-law protection is already born, in its classical basis, founded on vulnerability as a structural datum of the relationship and not as an episodic exception. ↩
  11. The interpretation of the consumer contractual relationship, especially in contexts of complex, continuous or technically governed supply, must start less from abstract formal framings and more from the consumer's material vulnerability and from the way the supply is actually organized and operates. This means that the application of the CDC cannot be held hostage to superficial classificatory appearances, nor to an idealized image of parity between the parties, when the reality of the bond reveals mass contracting, unilateral pre-drafting of terms, organizational opacity and the adhering party's practical dependence on the supplier's structure. Marques makes this point explicit when she observes that adhesion contracts and general contract conditions work as a strong indication of factual vulnerability, even when the contracting party, in theory, has technical training or a professional activity. In the author's words, “most banking contracts are concluded through the use of general contract conditions and adhesion contracts. These methods of mass contracting […] serve as an indication of the co-contracting party's vulnerability. Even if the co-contracting party is a lawyer, even if a merchant or farmer, factual vulnerability will almost always be present” Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 613). This insight becomes even more relevant when the supply does not end in a one-off act but extends over time as an organized, repeated relationship functionally closed around the consumer. In these cases, vulnerability is not a mere episodic accident of the contractual moment, but a structural condition of the user's insertion into a regime of dependent enjoyment. Marques describes this logic when discussing services provided through organized networks, whose internal functioning often escapes the consumer's awareness and which, in the contemporary context, tend to bind the consumer ever more captively to the supplier or chain of suppliers. As the author notes, these are services that “bind the consumer in such a way that, over the years of the complex contractual relationship, they become a ‘captive’ client of that supplier or chain of suppliers, becoming dependent even on the maintenance of that contractual relationship or seeing all their expectations frustrated.” Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 109) Applied to the present problem, this reading reinforces that the legal qualification of the bond must take into account the real form of the supply and the vulnerability it concretely produces, and not merely formal labels that, used. ↩
  12. Factual vulnerability is a structural presupposition of consumer-law protection. Miragem observes that consumer law belongs among the so-called protective rights, whose raison d'être necessarily rests on a situation of factual inequality, which they seek to redress by means of the protective norm Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 34]). The application of the CDC is thus explained not by a formal abstraction of categories, but by the material finding that the consumer relationship is marked by a real asymmetry between its parties, which calls for normative intervention aimed at restoring balance. The principle of vulnerability is the basic principle that grounds the existence and application of consumer law. The very existence of consumer law is justified by the recognition of the consumer's vulnerability in the consumer market, and it is precisely this vulnerability that requires the law to concern itself with the consumer's protection Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 122]). The point is central because it shows that vulnerability is not an accidental, peripheral or complementary element of the microsystem, but its cornerstone. When product, service and complex digital supplies are at issue, the center of the analysis should not be a somewhat bureaucratic cult of classification for its own sake, but the protective function of the CDC in the face of a structurally unequal relationship. Miragem thus helps to recall an important obvious point: consumer, supplier, product and service make dogmatic sense only within a system built to respond to vulnerability. And it is precisely for this reason that these categories must be read in a functional and broad way, faithful to market reality, not a narrow or ornamental one. ↩
