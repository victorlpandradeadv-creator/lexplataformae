## Style rules for site prose (not for the thesis full text)

- No semicolons, no colons, no em dashes or en dashes in site prose. Use short separate sentences, or join with and, but, because, when, while, so that. Literal thesis text keeps its own punctuation.
- Information mode speaks to a lay reader in the second person (you), colloquial but human. Sentences linked by coordination and subordination, average 17 to 20 words, none above 37.
- Doctrine speaks to lawyers. Didactic, complete, may use longer sentences, still without colons, semicolons, dashes.
- No didactic imperatives (Think, Imagine, Notice, See) unless the Portuguese has them.
- Never promise outcomes. "Asking is no guarantee of getting."
- Never conclude that a company or platform is abusive. Keep every modal and caveat ("may", "can", "possibly"). Never upgrade a possibility to a certainty.
- The site does not attribute the doctrine to "the thesis" outside Science and the full text. Do not add such attributions.
- Keep the thesis title in Portuguese where it appears as a bibliographic title, followed by the English title in brackets only where the Portuguese page shows the title as running text.
  English title. On the legal regime of the Immaterial Object of Consumption (IOC) under the Lex Plataformae. The protection of the consumer's semiotic vulnerability in light of Brazil's constitutional and economic order.

## What stays in Portuguese

- Bibliographic references (ABNT entries, obra titles, publisher data). Translate only connective words of the site around them ("Disponível em" becomes "Available at", "versão" becomes "version", "Acesso em" becomes "Accessed on").
- Proper names, song titles, game names, URLs, email addresses.
- Names of works cited inside data attributes.
- Personal names of the author (Victor L. P. Andrade).

## Quoted authors inside the thesis

When the thesis quotes a foreign author in Portuguese translation (Postman, Han, Zuboff and others), translate the Portuguese into English. Do not try to recall the author's original English wording. The site marks every literal passage as a free translation.
