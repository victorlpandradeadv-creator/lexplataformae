Situation h) · 7.1.2 of the thesis

When loss is a routine part of the design, not an accident

h) abusive teleology and unlawful act of architecture

Which facts show mechanisms operating together?

Microfrictions, normalized losses, fabricated urgency, artificial scarcity, strategic opacity and elastic equivalences operating in a coordinated way. Also pre-checked options, hostile cancellation routes, sanction regimes and proof arrangements that work together as a method.

How do you demonstrate the pattern, and not just the episode?

The pattern, its replicability, the coordination between mechanisms and the relevant impact on practical autonomy, intelligibility or utility. Proof is not confined to the micro-event and combines evidence of flow, documentation of campaigns, ecosystem rules and records of the decision path. The case does not need to show that a single consumer was psychologically defeated by the system.

Which rules reach the whole arrangement?

Article 39 of the CDC1 prohibits abusive practices, such as taking advantage of the consumer's weakness. Article 51, item IV, of the CDC2, read in light of the barrier clause, makes it possible to control entire procedural arrangements designed to produce asymmetric friction. Article 84 of the CDC3 supports specific relief, and in the face of a repeated pattern the system also calls for injunctive relief. Article 29 of the CDC4 reaches those exposed to the platform's practices. Framing under the CDC goes through three doors. The first is the quality defect, when the program produces a relevant loss of adequacy, continuity or equivalence of enjoyment. The second is abusive practices, when artificial scarcity, fabricated urgency and asymmetric friction operate in a predictable and repeated way. The third is abusive clauses, when the program takes shape in pre-drafted terms, policies and acceptances. There is no need to leave the CDC or invent a parallel law of platforms.

Which requirements must be present together?

The five requirements are cumulative. They are the teleology inscribed in the design, reproduction as a pattern, exploitation of structural vulnerabilities, opacity combined with low contestability and the legally relevant effect on the enjoyment bundle. The category does not depend on psychological intent and does not work as a moral wild card. The analysis must demonstrate the dominant orientation of the arrangement toward exploiting vulnerability and the disproportionate capture of value, distinguishing it from the legitimate purposes of the service, such as security, stability and balance of the ecosystem.

The law cited on this page

  1. art. 39 of the CDC ↩

    “Art. 39. The supplier of products or services is prohibited from, among other abusive practices: IV - taking advantage of the weakness or ignorance of the consumer […]; V - demanding a manifestly excessive advantage from the consumer; XII - failing to stipulate a deadline for the performance of its obligation or leaving the setting of its starting date to its exclusive discretion.”

    PT“Art. 39. É vedado ao fornecedor de produtos ou serviços, dentre outras práticas abusivas: IV - prevalecer-se da fraqueza ou ignorância do consumidor […]; V - exigir do consumidor vantagem manifestamente excessiva; XII - deixar de estipular prazo para o cumprimento de sua obrigação ou deixar a fixação de seu termo inicial a seu exclusivo critério.”

    Wording transcribed in note 527, p. 520.

  2. art. 51, IV, of the CDC ↩

    Art. 51. The following contractual clauses relating to the supply of products and services, among others, are null and void by operation of law:

    PTArt. 51. São nulas de pleno direito, entre outras, as cláusulas contratuais relativas ao fornecimento de produtos e serviços que: IV - estabeleçam obrigações consideradas iníquas, abusivas, que coloquem o consumidor em desvantagem exagerada, ou sejam incompatíveis com a boa-fé ou a eqüidade; XIII - autorizem o fornecedor a modificar unilateralmente o conteúdo ou a qualidade do contrato, após sua celebração;

    IV - those that establish obligations considered iniquitous, abusive, that place the consumer at an exaggerated disadvantage, or are incompatible with good faith or equity;

    XIII - those that authorize the supplier to unilaterally modify the content or quality of the contract after its conclusion;

    Text in force copied from the Planalto website on 27/10/2026. Later addition, outside the thesis.

  3. Article 84 of the CDC ↩

    “Art. 84. In an action whose object is the performance of an obligation to do or not to do, the judge shall grant specific relief for the obligation or order measures that ensure a practical result equivalent to that of performance.”

    PT“Art. 84. Na ação que tenha por objeto o cumprimento da obrigação de fazer ou não fazer, o juiz concederá a tutela específica da obrigação ou determinará providências que assegurem o resultado prático equivalente ao do adimplemento.”

    Wording transcribed in note 574, p. 540.

  4. art. 29 of the CDC ↩

    “Art. 29. For the purposes of this Chapter and the following one, all persons, determinable or not, exposed to the practices provided for therein are deemed equivalent to consumers”

    PT“Art. 29. Para os fins deste Capítulo e do seguinte, equiparam-se aos consumidores todas as pessoas determináveis ou não, expostas às práticas nele previstas”

    Wording transcribed in note 817, p. 779.
