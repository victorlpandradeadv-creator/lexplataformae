3.1.1 Autonomy of technique and the reorganization of social life

See this section in the PDF

This section in the layers of the site

  • Foundations · Sections of chapter 3

p. 92

Ellul's notion of the autonomization of technique helps explain how efficiency comes to operate as the dominant criterion of social organization. Technique no longer figures as a mere instrument subordinate to external ends and becomes a system of rationalization capable of conditioning practices, expectations and institutions. In this shift, even Law comes under pressure from criteria of performance, operability and functional adaptation.

This key appears, on a smaller scale, in the way platform-operated games normalize continuous updates, recalibrations and reconfigurations of value. The unilateral alteration of a cosmetic item or the redesign of a character does not arise as an extraordinary event, but as an ordinary manifestation of a system that adjusts itself according to internal criteria of efficiency, balance and retention. Ellul is drawn on43 to explain why this logic tends to shift the debate from the realm of ends, that is, what must be preserved and the expectations that were created, to the realm of method, that is, what “works best” for the system. Technical design thus reveals itself as a locus of power and as a relevant dimension for the subsequent legal qualification.

In Ellul, the conversion of technique into environment stems from the modern shift of technique from a set of instruments to a totalizing rationality, present in methods of organization, procedures, decision-making patterns and ways of administering reality. Unified by measurable efficiency, technique comes to operate less as a means subordinate to substantive ends and more as a regime capable of reorganizing values, expectations and institutions, reconfiguring the very grammar of what counts as “adequate”, “better” and “possible” in everyday life. For this reason, the technical phenomenon cannot be reduced to machines or material p. 93 apparatuses, since it includes organizational factors and utilitarian modes of thought that structure the social Ellul(Ellul, 1968, p. 1-5).

In developing the characterology of technique44, Ellul describes an evolution marked by the pursuit of efficiency, the expansion of automatism and the formation of self-augmenting structures, traits that distinguish modern technique from earlier forms. In this picture, technique presents itself as indifferent to moral judgment and breaks with the belief that technical progress would, in itself, amount to human progress Ellul(Ellul, 1968, p. 117). The distinction between ancient and modern technique makes this shift explicit: while the former remains tied to empiricism and to slow development, the latter is characterized by universality, objective rationality, artificiality, automatism, self-augmentation, indivisibility and autonomy, with effects that can reach every aspect of human life, including the reduction of man to the condition of an object Ellul(Ellul, 1968, p. 66-80).

The rationality of modern technique operates by applying logic to the pursuit of maximum efficiency, which tends to demote values that cannot be translated into measurable performance. In this context Ellul describes the automatism of technical choice as a blind search for the “best way” to perform a task, with the loss of a holistic criterion of adequacy and a growing indifference to the social, moral or aesthetic consequences produced by the method Ellul(Ellul, 1968, p. 82-88). Once this automatism sets in, the question of what must be preserved gives way to the question of what best keeps the system running.

In the League of Legends ecosystem, the match management algorithm makes this autonomy especially visible, because matchmaking presents itself as an optimization problem, and not as a deliberation on the best possible experience for each individual. The company describes the need to simultaneously balance “fair matches”, “position preference” and “fast queues”, admitting that, under real constraints, the system shifts weight among these criteria and relaxes parameters so that the match can take place Riot Games(Riot Games, 2026a). In this grammar, “fairness” no longer operates as moral language and instead functions as a statistical target, tied to an approximate 50% probability of victory for each team, produced by averaging MMR45 Riot Games(Riot Games, 2026a). Ludic enjoyment does not disappear, but it is compressed p. 94 by a method that must operate under scarcity of supply, schedules, an unequal distribution of roles and variations in skill, turning the player into a performance variable in a self-adjusting architecture.

This point is reinforced when the company itself explains that MMR is a hidden number, designed to operate in back-end46 systems and to guide how each person is placed in balanced matches, while the visible rank works as a pedagogical and progressive translation of that measure47 Riot Games(Riot Games, 2026c). Here, technical autonomy lies in the shift of the relevant decision-making procedure to a non-transparent layer, which defines what counts as balance and continuously adjusts matchmaking criteria to preserve the stability of the ecosystem. When queue time becomes the decisive constraint, matchmaking rules tend to loosen, and the system begins to seek the “next best” instead of the “ideal”, because the method requires that the match take place within an acceptable time horizon48 League of Legends Wiki(League of Legends Wiki, [202-?]i)49. The result matches the effect Ellul describes: a technical choice presented as inevitable because it “works best” for the system, shifting the debate from ends to the continuous adjustment of method.

This normalization of technical mutability also appears in the contractual frame by which the service authorizes itself to evolve continuously. In the Terms of Service, the user agrees that Riot may change, modify, update, suspend, “nerf” or restrict access to features and parts of the service, including through automatic background updates, with or without prior notice50 Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e). The vocabulary that legitimizes this plasticity is that of constant p. 95 improvement, evolution and security, and the user's experience comes to be treated as an adjustable dimension of systemic functioning. In an Ellulian key, technique ceases to appear as a means subordinate to the end of entertainment and asserts itself as a self-justified environment, capable of absorbing enjoyment as a management variable to preserve the stability of the ecosystem.

As regards artificiality, Ellul asserts that modern technique stands opposed to nature and constitutes a system made up of artificial means, in which technical intervention reduces the real to a “logical scheme” and reorganizes facts and phenomena according to internal demands of rationalization Ellul(Ellul, 1968, p. 82). This environment breaks with traditional grounding, since technique rejects tradition and comes to rely on the combination and accelerated evolution of earlier technical processes, at a pace too fast for full integration into the accumulated cultural fabric Ellul(Ellul, 1968, p. 13).

The economy, in this picture, becomes progressively dependent on technique and its methods, to the point at which technique ceases to function as a subordinate economic instrument and comes to dictate the conditions of the economic game itself Ellul(Ellul, 1968, p. 152-162). Along these lines, the economy becomes intimately dependent on technique, seeking to adapt to its demands and using its methods to achieve objectives.

The relationship between technique and the State reinforces this institutional diagnosis. Ellul characterizes technique as a fundamental instrument of state power, especially in the modern State, increasingly dependent on technical procedures to administer, regulate, control and wage war Ellul(Ellul, 1968, p. 233-238). This dependence shapes the state structure, favors centralization and bureaucratization and alters the very nature of the State, which comes to operate as a technical organism, limited by the forms of perception and decision that its administrative rationality makes available Ellul(Ellul, 1968, p. 257-260).

In examining techniques directly aimed at man, oriented toward modifying behavior, needs and subjective dispositions, Ellul identifies psychological, sociological and biometric techniques as expressive manifestations of attempts to control and manipulate the human person Ellul(Ellul, 1968, p. 325-331). The author also observes that the technical modification of space itself can condition behavior, which extends the problem beyond isolated instruments and reaches the environmental organization of human action Ellul(Ellul, 1968, p. 331-335). This reflection directly concerns digital ecosystems, p. 96 in which environments of interface, reward, punishment and visibility are designed to guide conduct, modulate expectations and reduce practical margins of freedom.

The Ellulian reference to “inevitability” must be taken as a critical description of a system effect51, that is, of the tendency toward naturalization and self-justification of the technical, and not as a normative thesis according to which history would be closed to contestation. What matters, above all, is the mechanism by which technique presents itself as destiny and removes from public visibility the choices and costs embedded in its procedures. This hermeneutic caution preserves Ellul's diagnostic force without converting critique into fatalism, making it possible to place back into the design of infrastructures the disputes, alternatives and ends that the language of efficiency tends to obscure.

At the end of his work, Ellul questions the belief that technical progress is an objective synonym of human progress and appeals to individual and collective responsibility in the face of technique Ellul(Ellul, 1968, p. 441-445). In his view, society must seek effective forms of control over technical development in order to orient it toward the human good, even as he recognizes that man cannot fully adapt to the technical phenomenon.

This set makes it possible to identify the point of passage to the legal problem: by reducing the real to a “logical scheme” and refusing external judgments when the problem is presented as technical, technique compresses the space for deliberation on ends and shifts the decision to the language of efficiency Ellul(Ellul, 1968, p. 82; p. 136). What cannot be rationalized tends to be treated as irrelevant, impracticable or external to the functioning of the system. Hence the importance of placing technical choices back in the field of attribution, since the appearance of neutrality may conceal ends, costs and distributions of risk that directly affect the consumption experience.

With this, technique comes to organize the very perception of the social world. When the economy, public administration and entertainment operate under this rationality, the individual is recoded into indicators of performance, available time, engagement and risk. p. 97 Technique ceases to be an accessory of social life and comes to define what can be seen, measured, governed and contested.

This reorganization also becomes institutionally entrenched. At the level of operative rationality, the shift appears when, to escape “arbitrariness and subjectivity” and “ethical judgment”, technique demands that reality be reduced to number Ellul(Ellul, 1968, p. 17). Law is affected by this pressure not only because it comes to deal with technically complex objects, but because its own practice can be absorbed by criteria of efficiency. For the “technician of law”, the decisive reference tends to shift from the normative end to the efficiency of application, since “all law depends on its efficiency” and “a law that is not applied is not a law” Ellul(Ellul, 1968, p. 299). Under this pressure for performance and operability, normative conflicts tend to be recast as problems of management and compliance, which makes it harder to deliberate on ends when technical autonomy narrows the room for choice over collective means and directions Ellul(Ellul, 1968, p. 143).

Transposed to digital consumption, this diagnosis makes it possible to interpret electronic games, streaming platforms and social networks not as isolated applications, but as specific manifestations of this technique-environment. Interfaces, algorithms, reward systems and mechanisms of artificial scarcity are organized under criteria of efficiency, retention and continuous governance. What is prepared here under the category of Immaterial Object of Consumption emerges precisely from this operation: it is an object of functional and continued existence, inseparable from the technical infrastructure that gives it access, utility, stability and practical meaning.

For this reason, a strictly material reading of consumption does not adequately capture the phenomenon. When the object is inseparable from the technical architecture52 that sustains it, the central problem ceases to be only the existence of a quality defect in an isolated product and comes to include the way technique organizes possibilities of use, permanence, loss or degradation over time.

Ellul is not called upon to sustain a generic critique of technological modernity, but to make intelligible the technical order in which the IOC is formed. In digital ecosystems, the consumer relationship is not reduced to the exchange of a performance for a price; it is installed in an environment organized by metrics, automatisms, access regimes and procedures of p. 98 continuous updating. The enjoyment of the object becomes inseparable from the functioning of the technical ambience, which is why architecture and governance cannot be treated as externalities of supply, but as material dimensions of the consumer relationship itself. Next, this ambience will be combined with the media reconfiguration of experience, with Postman, and with cultural and advertising desymbolization, with Vanderburg, preparing the passage to the Lex Plataformae and to semiotic vulnerability.
