6.3.2 On functional value equivalence as a parameter of the lawfulness of alterations

View this section in the PDF

  • Functional value equivalence

p. 648

Functional value equivalence operates as a criterion of the lawfulness of unilateral alterations made in continuous digital services that administer Immaterial Objects of Consumption. The starting point is the feature whereby, in IOCs, the legal problem is not solved merely by asking whether the object still “exists”, whether the account remains accessible or whether some formal trace of the acquisition is still standing. What must be controlled is whether the change made by the supplier substantially preserves the core of enjoyment that made the acquisition rational and legitimizes the consumer's remaining in the relationship. In continuing relationships, protection is not satisfied by the nominal survival of the performance, because the minimum basis of the contract also includes the legitimate expectations of utility and continuity built up over the course of performance734. It is precisely for this reason that functional value equivalence presents itself as a more suitable parameter than a reading centered only on abstract ownership or on the formal permanence of the asset: what the system must protect is the integrity of enjoyment, and not the mere appearance that “something is still there”.

This formulation does not require rigid equality between before and after. Functional value equivalence does not mean freezing the object, literal repetition of attributes, absolute p. 649 maintenance of layout, stats735, parameters or isolated micro-characteristics. The standard does not prohibit the evolution of the service, the adaptation of the ecosystem, the balancing of mechanics or the ordinary innovation inherent to continuous platforms and games. What it requires is substantial preservation of the performance, the practical utility and the role played by the object in the governed environment, taking into account the objectifiable reasons that justified the acquisition and the consumer's remaining in the relationship736. In other words, the legally relevant question is not whether everything remained identical, but whether the object continues to serve, at a functionally equivalent level, the ends that legitimately justified entering into the contract and maintaining the relationship Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 521; p. 523]).

This equivalence, however, is not exhausted by narrow technical functionality. In IOCs, functional value may comprise ecosystemic and, in certain cases, symbolic dimensions, provided they are objectifiable within the regime of enjoyment itself. This includes, for example, capacity for use, performance, access and compatibility, but it may also reach the role of the object within the ecosystem, its relational relevance, its position of use, its density of rarity, its function of distinction or the way it integrates trajectory, status and recognition stabilized in the environment737. The point is not to turn every subjective preference into a legal claim, nor to open the door to touchiness disguised as dogmatics. The point is to recognize that, in governed ecosystems, certain symbolic and positional dimensions are not external p. 650 adornments, but part of the very utility for which the consumer joins, remains and invests in the object. When these elements verifiably form part of the promised core of enjoyment, their degradation may be legally relevant even if the object remains formally accessible Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 328; p. 246]).

The dogmatic premise of this construction lies in the treatment of continuing and dependent relationships. The parallel with long-term captive contracts does not enter here as an automatic transplant or as academic ornament, but as disciplined interpretive infrastructure. In relationships of this nature, what is protected is not only the initial delivery, but the minimum stability of the performance program and the trust legitimately built over time738. The consumer does not adhere merely to an isolated utility, captured in a contractual snapshot of the first day. He binds himself to a relationship whose value depends on the minimally loyal preservation of the functional program of the performance. For this reason, readaptations and mutations are legally admissible, yes, but only insofar as they are compatible with the maintenance of the expected utility and with the non-frustration of the legitimate expectations that sustained joining and remaining in the relationship Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 101). The platform may change. What it may not do is convert mutability into a vehicle for the opportunistic downgrading of enjoyment under the pretext of mere evolution of the service.

It is at this point that the theory of objective good faith in lasting relationships gains a practical function. The lawfulness of the change is not measured by its mere abstract provision in a contractual clause, nor by the formal permanence of the object after the alteration. In prolonged relationships, change remains legitimate only when compatible with the functional continuity of the performance, with loyalty in performance and with the trust built by the supplier's prior conduct739. Unfavorable and harmful surprise, the sudden break with patterns p. 651 previously tolerated and the abrupt reconfiguration of the expected utility may amount to a breach of trust and a violation of the contractual program as actually lived, even if the contract remains nominally in force Martins-Costa(Martins-Costa, 2018, p. 45; p. 401). In less pompous and more useful language: it is not enough to leave the shell of the relationship intact if the content enjoyed has been hollowed out.

This requires recognizing that contractual balance, in long-term relationships, is not only the balance of the initial moment of agreement. There is also a dynamic synallagma, that is, a functional balance that accompanies the relationship over time740. In IOCs, this observation is pure gold, because the object is not consumed only at the moment of purchase; it is consumed along the iter of enjoyment, amid continuous updates, reconfigurations and mediations. Hence, the lawfulness of the alteration must be assessed in light of the functional unity of the relationship, and not merely of the abstract existence of a generic authorization to change. If the modification significantly breaks this functional unity, downgrades the expected utility and disrupts the trust that has been built, the formal permanence of the relationship no longer suffices as a criterion of legitimacy Martins-Costa(Martins-Costa, 2018, p. 75; p. 232).

Functional value equivalence, then, acts as a material test of legitimate mutability. Its field of application opens whenever there is an alteration capable of significantly affecting the practical function, the ecosystemic position, the symbolic density or the continuity of enjoyment of the IOC. The trigger is not any petty change, any visual touch-up or any marginal fluctuation typical of the normal life of the service. The standard lights up when the platform brings about a downgrade of use, limitation of access, loss of compatibility, alteration of essential parameters, removal of functionalities, structural rebalancing, discontinuation of an ecosystemic role, degradation of relational position or hollowing out of attributes with objective weight in the rationale of the acquisition741. Unilateral p. 652 alteration is already, in itself, legally sensitive under the CDC; and this sensitivity is heightened when the change is not neutral, but results in a material reduction or impairment of the consumer's interest in the performance Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 387; p. 425).

To make this standard operable, it is advisable to structure it as an application test. The first step consists of identifying the functional core of the object. What practical utility justified the acquisition? What role did the object play in the ecosystem? Where pertinent, what symbolic, reputational or relational density was objectively incorporated into the enjoyment? Here, the analysis must be neither psychologizing nor folkloric. It does not matter whether the consumer “loved” that item. What matters is whether there was a verifiable core of utility, role and meaning that formed part of the legitimately formed expectation742. The offer and the trust it awakens provide a direct basis for this identification, since the consumer contracts in view of the advertised advantages and qualities, which gives rise to the legal duty to maintain the conditions offered or, at least, to substantially preserve their utility Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 424).

The second step is to reconstruct the alteration and the impact. What exactly was modified? What is the before and after in terms of use, performance, access, compatibility, ecosystemic function, recognition, rarity, trajectory or continuity of experience? This examination cannot remain trapped in the formalism of “it still exists” or “it is still available”. Consumer-law inadequacy also sets in when the performance remains formally present, but no longer corresponds, in legal terms, to the legitimately expected quality, functionality or conformity. The CDC works with the functional defect precisely to reach nonconformities that frustrate the consumer's legitimate expectation without requiring the total disappearance of the object Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 246; p. 328]).

p. 653 The third step is to measure the significance of the loss. Was there a marginal loss, part of the ordinary evolution of the service, or was there a substantial hollowing out of the functional core of enjoyment? Not every reduction is unlawful. Platforms are not wax museums. But there is a point beyond which the alteration ceases to be ordinary adaptation and begins to operate as a material downgrading of the use value acquired. It is here that the idea of the minimum basis of the relationship becomes central. If the fundamental legitimate expectations that sustained the contract are excluded, the imbalance is no longer a detail: it becomes structural Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 292). Functional equivalence serves precisely to separate tolerable wear from legally intolerable corrosion.

The fourth step is to examine justification and restoration. Even when there is a relevant loss, the mutation may still be legally tolerable if there is verifiable justification and adequate restoration. Did the platform demonstrate, in a minimally auditable way, the technical or systemic reason for the change? Did it offer real alternatives, reversibility, a parallel version, equivalent functional substitution, correction of parameters, preservation of compatibility, restoration of status or another mechanism capable of substantially preserving enjoyment? This point matters a great deal because the CDC is not content with the mere finding of the problem. It structures remedies of material restoration, specific relief and a practical result equivalent to performance, both in the case of a quality defect and in the case of nonconformity with the offer Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 338; p. 408; p. 597]). Thus, it is not enough merely to acknowledge the change and assert one's own power to carry it out. If the change was substantially relevant, restoration must necessarily be part of the discussion.

This test depends directly on the preceding standard of open auditability. There is no way to assess substantial preservation of functional value without a verifiable minimum about what was altered, when, why and with what effects. If the platform prevents the impact from being reconstructed, opacity is not neutral. It becomes a dogmatic aggravating factor, because it makes it impossible to control the adequacy, the trust and the very legitimacy of the mutation. Functional equivalence without auditability becomes an elegant guess. With auditability, it becomes an institutionally manageable criterion.

The legal consequences of this construction are significant. When there is no substantial preservation of functional value, the alteration tends to constitute a relevant inadequacy of supply. Depending on the structure of the case, this may be treated as a quality defect in the product or service, or, in more extreme situations of harm or risk, come close to the logic of the safety defect. The main point, however, is that the nominal permanence of the object does not exclude the application of the protective regime. If the legitimately expected utility has been hollowed out, the inadequacy is already p. 654 established, even if the account remains active and the item is still listed in the inventory Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 521; p. 523]).

In these cases, the priority form of protection must be functional restoration. The consumer-law system was not designed to convert every relevant frustration automatically into residual monetary compensation. In an IOC environment, the most legally appropriate remedy is often to restore equivalence of enjoyment: reversal of the alteration, an alternative version, equivalent functional substitution, restoration of attributes, correction of parameters, preservation of trajectory, maintenance of compatibility or other measures capable of reestablishing, as far as possible, the position of enjoyment that justified the acquisition. Monetization pure and simple may be useful in certain cases, of course, but treating it as the default answer is precisely the kind of dogmatic limitation that this thesis strives to overcome.

This logic also applies to the control of unilateral alterations as such. The CDC does not look kindly on clauses and practices that authorize the supplier to modify on its own the content, the quality or the essential parameters of the performance after contracting743. The problem does not lie in every change considered in the abstract, but in the change imposed without the consumer's effective participation, without preservation of the balance of the relationship and without respect for the functional core of enjoyment. A relevant unilateral modification, when detached from restoration and from substantial preservation of use value, ceases to be mere management of the service and begins to approach structural abusiveness Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 655; p. 495-496]).

This point connects directly to the theme of trust. In continuing relationships, the protection of trust is not theoretical perfume. It is the criterion that prevents the platform from treating the consumer as a hostage to an object whose function can be redefined at any moment for the exclusive benefit of the supplier744. In captive contracts, the so-called supervening p. 655 “consensus” is often no more than forced adherence to the new state of affairs, especially when the consumer is already invested in the relationship and depends on the ecosystem to keep enjoying what he acquired. Alterations that hollow out the utility, the quality or the material position of the consumer in the relationship may constitute, even under the appearance of continued acceptance, a form of de facto unilateral variation of the contract incompatible with good faith Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 1225; p. 1251; p. 1319-1320).

For this reason, functional value equivalence is a criterion of stability of the performance program, not a requirement that the object stand still. It makes it possible to recognize the sophisticated obvious: continuous digital services change, but they cannot change in just any way; IOCs evolve, but they cannot be hollowed out under the label of evolution; platforms administer ecosystems, but they cannot use this administration to shift unilaterally onto the consumer the cost of relevant downgrades of enjoyment. The standard does not prevent the life of the service. It prevents the life of the service from being used as a screen for the material breach of what was functionally promised.

The example of updates that degrade accessibility and compatibility in software or firmware helps to visualize the structure of the problem. In these cases, no one seriously argues that every update is unlawful. The question is whether, under the pretext of improvement, the update significantly impairs the use, performance or compatibility of goods still within their expected durability, without adequate information, without effective consent and without mitigation options. The legal structure is the same in IOCs: an update, rebalancing or rule change is not illegitimate merely because it exists, but may become unlawful when it substantially degrades the functional core of the acquisition without verifiable justification and without adequate restoration. The mutability of the ecosystem does not absolve the supplier of responsibility for preserving, in substantial terms, the utility that gave meaning to consumption.

On the methodological plane, the advantage of the standard is clear. It avoids both the extremism of “it changed, therefore it is unlawful” and the complacency of asserting that “change is inherent, so everything is lawful”. Instead of such caricatures, it offers a finer criterion: did the alteration substantially preserve the functional, ecosystemic and, when relevant, symbolic core of enjoyment? Was there verifiable justification? Was there adequate restoration? If the answer is no, mutability has ceased to be a legitimate attribute of the service and has turned into an p. 656 instrument for hollowing out enjoyment, with its own consequences under the CDC regime Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 424]).

This is what justifies asserting, with some firmness, that functional value equivalence is not just one more appealing concept but the parameter of material lawfulness of alterations in IOCs. Without it, the supplier will always be able to hide behind the formal permanence of the object or the rhetoric of continuous innovation. With it, it becomes possible to ask what really matters: did the platform change the service while substantially preserving the functional value of what the consumer acquired, or did it use its position of governance to opportunistically downgrade, suppress or hollow out the promised enjoyment? It is precisely this question that functional value equivalence makes it possible to answer.

The quality defect arises from the absence of properties or characteristics that enable the performance to meet the ends legitimately expected by the consumer, and may even result from the breach of an ancillary duty, provided this impairs, even partially, the utility of the product or service Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 523]). In continuous and digitally governed consumer relationships, the mutation made by the platform is not lawful merely because something continues to exist; the parameter is whether what remains still serves, in a functionally equivalent way, the ends that legitimately justified entering into the contract. ↩
  4. The inadequacy of the performance, under the CDC regime, is not limited to the physical nonexistence of the object or to its total suppression. Consumer-law dogmatics reaches situations in which the performance continues to exist formally or materially, but no longer corresponds, in legal terms, to the legitimately expected quality, functionality or conformity. A product may be considered legally unfit for failing to comply with a regulatory presentation requirement, even though it meets immediate practical needs Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 328]). The functional defect is a nonconformity that prevents or limits the function for which the good is intended, and may extend even to irregularities of lesser apparent gravity concerning details that frustrate the consumer's legitimate expectation Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 246]). If the relevant enjoyment was downgraded, if the expected functionality was impaired or if the trust legitimately incorporated into the relationship was frustrated, consumer-law inadequacy has already set in. ↩

The theory of the relational contract contributes “to a new understanding of the trust awakened by the activity of suppliers and to the acceptance of a constant readaptation of long-term relationships so as not to frustrate the legitimate expectations of the parties, despite the limited will initially manifested.” Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 101). The material test of legitimate readaptation lies in whether the modification preserves the expected utility that the relationship promised to deliver to the consumer and in which he legitimately trusted. ↩
  6. The lawfulness of contractual mutations in prolonged relationships is not measured only by the formal permanence of the object nor by the abstract existence of a clause permitting alteration. In lasting relationships, change remains legally tolerable only when compatible with objective good faith, with the functional continuity of the performance and with the trust legitimately built in the course of performance. Objective good faith functions expressly as a yardstick for assessing lawfulness in the exercise of rights derived from legal transactions, including to set the limits on the exercise of termination in lasting and continuing-performance contracts and to put brakes on the ius variandi in specific cases Martins-Costa(Martins-Costa, 2018, p. 45). What proves legally relevant is the unfavorable and harmful surprise produced by unjustified hesitations, successive alterations or an abrupt change of previously tolerated patterns, in breach of the trust generated by the preceding conduct Martins-Costa(Martins-Costa, 2018, p. 401). A mutation that significantly hollows out the promised enjoyment and breaks the contractual program as actually lived without sufficient restoration goes beyond the field of legitimate adaptation and enters that of the violation of trust. ↩
  7. In prolonged relationships, the lawfulness of alterations cannot be assessed only by the formal subsistence of the performance, because the relationship is structured not only by the initial synallagma, but also by its functional continuity over time. Contractual balance is not limited to the moment of agreement; it also corresponds to the “dynamic or functional synallagma”, that is, the one that accompanies the contractual relationship over the course of its duration Martins-Costa(Martins-Costa, 2018, p. 75). The transformations that affect the relationship during this iter must be traced back to the very structural and functional unity of the relationship Martins-Costa(Martins-Costa, 2018, p. 232). The relationship can bear changes only to the extent that they remain compatible with its concrete purpose and with the functional unity built in the contractual experience. If the mutation significantly breaks this functional unity, downgrades the expected utility and hollows out the trust actually built in the course of the relationship, the merely formal permanence of the relationship no longer serves as a sufficient criterion of lawfulness. ↩
  8. A relevant unilateral alteration of the performance must be read as at once a problem of structural abusiveness and of material impairment of adequacy. Clauses that authorize the supplier to unilaterally modify the content or quality of the contract after its conclusion are abusive, precisely because they concentrate in the supplier the power to direct the relationship and reduce the consumer to a mere spectator of the course of the transaction Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 387]). If unilateral alteration is already enough, in itself, to characterize abusiveness, this characteristic is aggravated when the content of the change results in the reduction or impairment of the consumer's interest in the performance Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 425]). If the platform changes the game on its own in a way that preserves the shell but hollows out the utility the consumer trusted he would receive, there is a breach of legitimate expectation and legally relevant inadequacy. ↩
  9. The protection of the consumer's trust provides a direct basis for requiring substantial preservation of the utility enjoyed. The right to the maintenance of the originally contracted conditions derives from the application of good faith and from the binding effect of the offer, which results in the prohibition of unilateral alteration of the terms of the contract Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 424]). The consumer makes his decision to contract in view of the advantages and qualities relating to the object or to the manner of contracting offered by the supplier, which gives rise to the legal duty to maintain the conditions offered, out of respect for the motives that led to the contract and for the contractual cause itself Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 424]). In relationships of continued enjoyment, what must be preserved, or adequately restored, is the functional core of the advantages, qualities and utilities that motivated the contract. Functional value equivalence is an offshoot of good faith, of the binding effect of the offer and of the protection of trust within the CDC. ↩
  10. Unilateral alteration clauses and practices are treated in the CDC directly from the perspective of contractual imbalance and abusiveness. Any modification of the consumer contract must observe bilaterality and the absence of abusive imposition, so that a clause that arms the supplier with the prerogative to innovate unilaterally in the relationship departs from contractual balance, its validity being undermined by Article 51, item XIII Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 655]). The unilateral modification of adjustment indices or formulas amounts to an iniquitous, abusive and excessively disadvantageous imposition on the consumer, prohibited by the abusive clauses of Article 51 Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, [p. 495-496]). Relevant unilateral alterations are legally problematic not because every change is prohibited in the abstract, but because a mutation imposed without the consumer's effective participation and without preservation of the balance of relevant enjoyment amounts to abusiveness and authorizes material restoration of the performance. ↩
  11. The control of unilateral modifications, in captive contracts, requires assessing whether the alteration materially hollows out the consumer's position in the continuing relationship, beyond the apparent form of the change. In relationships marked by practical dependence and a strong preponderance of the supplier, the so-called supervening “consensus” is often no more than forced adherence, amounting to a rescission contrary to good faith and a fraud on the law because it permits the de facto unilateral variation of the content, the performances, the quality or the price Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 1225). Clauses that authorize the supplier to unilaterally modify “the content of the contract or the quality of the contract” after its conclusion are abusive, as are practices that leave the alteration of performances or of the price over time to its discretion Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 1251; p. 1319-1320). The control of the lawfulness of alterations must reach every modification that, while keeping the relationship standing on the nominal plane, erodes the utility, the quality or the material position of the consumer built up in the course of the relationship. ↩
