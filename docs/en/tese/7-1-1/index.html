7.1.1 On the operational statements of controllability of the IOC

See this section in the PDF

p. 716

The dogmatic stabilization of the Immaterial Object of Consumption requires a further step of condensation for application. It is not enough for the previously constructed parameters to be conceptually consistent. They must be able to circulate in a sufficiently dense, precise and citable form, capable of sustaining legal argument in diverse institutional settings. Whenever a dogmatic construction seeks to guide pleadings, decisions, legal opinions, administrative filings and protection routines, it must offer operable language without sacrificing theoretical density or turning into an empty formula. It is at this point that the parameters take the form of operational statements, which do not replace legal reasoning with ready-made formulas, but keep the incidence of the IOC from depending, in each new conflict, on an exhaustive return to the reconstructive itinerary that grounded its elaboration.

The statements should not be read as a horizontal inventory of concepts. Their function is to organize a graded decision sequence, in which each parameter prepares the incidence of the p. 717 next one and narrows the range of interpretive variation of the following step. The analysis starts from the qualification of the object and of the enjoyment regime, without which the rest of the analysis falls apart. Next, semiotic vulnerability comes into play as a mechanism of governance of meaning and of the intelligibility of the bond. Only then do the duties of information and advertising become legible in a dogmatically useful way, understood as structural forms of organizing expectation, choice and contestation. From this core, the standards that structure the institutional controllability of the conflict come into play. Open auditability, functional value equivalence, abusive teleology and the rational distribution of evidentiary burdens operate as criteria of verification, containment and attribution. Reconciliation with intellectual property and the primacy of functional restoration appear, at the end, as necessary operational closures, defining, on the one hand, the limit of private exclusivity in the face of consumer-law protection and, on the other, the legally adequate form of response to the conflict.
