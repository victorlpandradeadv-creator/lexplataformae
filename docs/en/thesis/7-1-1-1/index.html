7.1.1.1 On the parameters for qualifying the Immaterial Object of Consumption as an immaterial product under governed enjoyment

p. 717

The application of the regime of the Immaterial Object of Consumption depends, before anything else, on the correct qualification of what is offered to the consumer. This qualification cannot be resolved by clinging to the formal appearance of the relationship, nor by repeating the traditional categories with which the market describes its own activity. In platform ecosystems, the controversy is not adequately organized around the rigid opposition between product and service, as if it were necessary to locate, on one side, a static thing and, on the other, an instrumental activity performed from outside. The legally relevant fact lies elsewhere. What matters is to recognize situations in which the utility economically enjoyed depends structurally on an environment administered, modulated and changeable by private governance, in such a way that the consumed object only remains accessible, intelligible and functionally usable while certain conditions of infrastructure, curation, internal rule and enforcement persist.

In these cases, the qualification as an Immaterial Object of Consumption operates as the entry key to the legal regime. It is not a neutral terminological description, but a qualification with a precise dogmatic function. It shifts the focus from the civil-law ontology of the good to the continuous regime of maintenance, provision and modulation of enjoyment. What comes to matter is not the search for a stable support, autonomously inspectable and graspable outside the environment of circulation, but the identification of a bundle of immaterial utilities that is only realized under conditions continuously organized by the supplier. Enjoyment, here, does not p. 718 appear as an accidental effect of a formally concluded contract, but as the material core of the consumer relationship.

The change of perspective is necessary because it prevents the analysis from being captured by a reifying grammar, ill-suited to objects whose use value depends less on their nominal existence than on the concrete conditions of permanence, integration and intelligibility in the ecosystem that hosts them. The consumer, in these relationships, does not enjoy a presumably stable good whose content can be isolated from the architecture that sustains it. What the consumer enjoys, rather, is a use experience continuously conditioned by technical infrastructure, access curation, update systems, visibility criteria, interaction rules and private enforcement mechanisms. In such contexts, formal ownership, a use license or the mere nominal availability of the object do not exhaust the legal problem. The central point becomes determining the conditions under which enjoyment remains possible, comprehensible and functionally preserved over time.

The consequence of this qualification is relevant on two planes. On the first, it prevents legal control from being neutralized by the supplier's descriptive strategies, especially by the claim that there is only the provision of a service or a mere content license, as if these formulas sufficed to naturalize the precariousness of the relationship and set aside the protective regime that bears on enjoyment. On the second, it redefines the axis along which the CDC is read. Instead of operating primarily from the contract as a formal statement, the discipline comes to be structured around enjoyment as a governed relationship. This does not mean denying the relevance of the contract, but refusing its explanatory sufficiency in environments in which the economic content of the relationship is realized continuously by mechanisms that go far beyond the contractual text.

Under this key, the qualification of the IOC as an immaterial product does not require denying the performance dimension of the relationship. On the contrary, it requires recognizing it in its contemporary form. What is asserted is that, whenever the consumed utility presents itself as a position of enjoyment sustained by continuous private governance, there will be an immaterial product in a legally relevant sense for the application of Article 3, paragraph 1, of the CDC. The performance does not disappear, but appears as the mode of sustaining, updating and shaping the object of consumption itself. For this reason, the old question of whether the case involves a product or a service, taken in an exclusionary key, loses much of its analytical usefulness. The problem becomes the structure of supply and the way it organizes, preserves or degrades the acquired experience.

p. 719 This parameter thus performs the function of a trigger for the others. Without the correct qualification of the object as governed immaterial enjoyment, the subsequent parameters tend to lose adherence, because auditability, functional equivalence, abusive teleology and restoration remedies presuppose precisely the existence of a relationship in which the consumed utility depends on the continuous administration of the environment. It is this initial qualification that makes it possible to understand why alterations of rule, of access, of visibility, of integration or of performance are not mere peripheral accidents of a service external to the object, but potentially central interferences with the very experience that entered the user's sphere of consumption.

The criterion, therefore, is neither the materiality of the support nor the nomenclature employed by the supplier, but the structural dependence of enjoyment on a governed environment. Whenever this dependence proves constitutive of the acquired utility, the relationship must be read as a case of an Immaterial Object of Consumption, with all the dogmatic consequences deriving from it. This makes it possible to abandon the false security of watertight classifications and to recognize that, in platform economies, the core of the conflict lies less in the possession of a good or in the isolated performance of a service than in the legal discipline of an enjoyment maintained, modulated and potentially made precarious by private architecture. It is on this plane that the CDC regains its explanatory and corrective force.
