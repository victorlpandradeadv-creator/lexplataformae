4.1 Semiotics, spectacle and simulacrum in attention economies

See this section in the PDF

  • Foundations · Sections of chapter 4

p. 264

The platform is an economic architecture, an arrangement that depends not only on price, but on permanence, repetition and continuous governance to extract value. Thus, the next step is to explain how this extraction becomes normalized and desirable in the consumer's experience, in a stable and recurring way. In immaterial consumption, capture is not sustained only by a clause or by a technical barrier. It is sustained by a regime of presentation, visibility and production of meaning that transforms choice into conduct and transforms enjoyment into permanence. For this reason, the description of the phenomenon must incorporate the semiotic dimension, since a decisive part of value is produced and stabilized in the way the offer presents itself, circulates and becomes recognizable as “delivery”.

In the field of IOCs, the object does not appear to the consumer as an isolated “thing”, nor as a simple accessed file. It appears as a position of enjoyment within a governed ecosystem, and this ecosystem already delivers the object wrapped in language, status and expectation. The offer, here, is always double, because it is composed of a narrative promise and an infrastructure of support. It is narrative because it presents meaning, rarity, belonging and prestige as if they were natural properties of the item. It is infrastructure because it exists only while the techno-media environment maintains the intelligibility of that meaning and the possibility of living it before others.

For this reason, the sign does not function as a communicational ornament of the product, but as a material component of the immaterial good itself. It is not a matter of an “image” superimposed on a neutral substance. It is a form of existence of the object, in which what is acquired is defined also by how it shows itself, by how it circulates and by how it can be recognized as valuable.

This mechanism acts in a practical and continuous way. The promise is not limited to the moment of purchase, nor is it exhausted in the formal description of the item. It operates as a script of conduct, because the signs that surround the IOC organize desire, urgency, comparison and belonging in real time. Enjoyment becomes, simultaneously, use and performance. The p. 265 value of the good is not only what it “does”, but what it authorizes one to say about whoever holds it, where it places the subject within visible hierarchies and which symbolic doors it opens or closes within the community. Thus, identity, reputation and social integration cease to be side effects of consumption and come to make up the very circuit of the object. In this type of market, the platform governs not only the technical delivery of the good, but the symbolic conditions that make that delivery desirable, recognizable and continually renewed.

Under these conditions, the vulnerability that matters cannot be reduced to lack of information or to the old model of the consumer who “did not read” or “did not understand” the contract. The point is more structural. The consumer decides and remains within a field of meaning that he cannot audit, because the correspondence between the sign and the delivery depends on curations, metrics and continuous adjustments that are beyond his reach and, often, beyond even the formal text of the offer. The asymmetry lies not only in knowledge of clauses, but in the capacity to verify what really sustains the promise and under what conditions it can be altered, degraded or replaced without this appearing as an explicit breach. Hence the expression semiotic vulnerability. It is not a rhetorical adjective, but the name of a type of exposure in which the regime of signs governs experience and value, while the consumer's access to the bases of this governance is opaque, partial and reconfigurable.

If the regime of signs participates materially in the offer and in the very mode of existence of the IOC, legal control cannot be limited to examining formal declarations or to measuring harm as a simple failure of technical performance. The consequence is that the problem comes to require criteria of verifiability capable of reconstructing, with some degree of demonstrability, which conditions sustained the promise and how they operated in enjoyment. In this direction, auditability designates the requirement of traceability of the conditions that make the promise operative, allowing the link between expectation, architecture and experience to be reconstructed without depending only on the platform's self-description. Likewise, functional value equivalence designates the need to separate merely nominal restoration from effective preservation of the acquired position of enjoyment, since, in positionally defined goods, maintaining label and appearance may not preserve relevant functions, restrictions and effects. These names do not function as moralism against the symbolic, but as an anticipation of the type of control that will become necessary when the sign can operate as a shortcut of continuity and, with this, obscure proof of what was effectively delivered.

The semiotic block is organized as a tripod, but not as three contributions of the same weight and function. In Eco, the grammar of the problem is fixed, for the sign is treated as a structural and administrable component of intelligibility, an objective condition for the p. 266 promise to exist socially and for the link between expectation and enjoyment to be reconstructed and demonstrated. In Debord, the mechanism is described by which this grammar is put into operation as a regime of visibility and comparison, making reputation, social proof and legible hierarchies part of the economic circuit of permanence. In Baudrillard, it is made explicit how equivalence can be stabilized by signals of delivery and by internal commensurability, allowing nominal restorations that preserve the public appearance of fulfillment without preserving the position of enjoyment in its relevant elements. The objective is not to multiply vulnerabilities by author, but to decompose, in layers, the conditions under which the governance of meaning is converted into governance of evidence and, with this, into a problem of verifiability amenable to protection by Law.
