6.5.2 On the tension between exclusivity, consumer protection and the integrity of IOCs

See this section in the PDF

  • Dogmatics · Provisions cited

p. 695

The tension between exclusivity, consumer protection and the integrity of IOCs arises when the platform tries to convert an intellectual property title into unrestricted freedom of governance over enjoyment. The problem lies not in the abstract existence of exclusivity, but in the undue leap between having an IP right and assuming, from it, total power to alter conditions of use, reconfigure the experience, impose private sanctions, suppress accumulated trajectories and block the institutional reconstruction of what was done. In continuous digital ecosystems, this leap is especially dangerous because the technical and legal control of the environment projects directly onto access, permanence, progression, reputation and contestation. The collision, here, is not theoretical. It is a practical conflict of private governance over continuously supplied enjoyment.

This excess often begins in the vocabulary itself. The language of “intellectual property” tends to suggest full and unitary dominion over the immaterial, as if ownership were enough to justify any mode of exercising power. But this equation is misleading. Intellectual exclusivity is a delimited normative construction, not metaphysical sovereignty over all the projections of the ecosystem. Ascensão is right to point out that qualifying intellectual rights under the sign of property favors the tendential elimination of their limits and that this operation has a clear ideological charge, not a descriptive one p. 696 Ascensão(Ascensão, 2007, [p. 3; p. 5])791. Consequently, revocable license, unilateral mutability, banning or erasure of trajectory cannot be treated as self-justified effects of the mere ownership of IP.

This containment becomes even clearer when one recalls that exclusivity in intellectual matters is not born of the natural scarcity of the immaterial good, but of its artificial legal rarefaction. The law creates exclusion and, with it, the economic advantage of scarcity. For this very reason, its content is predominantly negative: it confers the power to exclude third parties, not unlimited positive sovereignty over every experience that gravitates around the protected good Ascensão(Ascensão, 2007, [p. 12-13])792. This fact prevents turning exclusivity into a general safe-conduct to legitimize any form of mutability, sanction, precarization of enjoyment or blocking of restoration.

Nor can one infer, from mere ownership, the full legitimacy of the concrete mode of exercising power over enjoyment. The exclusive right remains subject to external conditions, including the social function, and does not authorize its holder to exercise it as they see fit. The very idea of intellectual property coexists with internal and external limitations that make it absurd to treat it as an unrestricted authorization for the use and government of the ecosystem Ascensão(Ascensão, 2007, [p. 3; p. 13])793. This matters because it shifts the analysis from the abstract p. 697 level of the existence of the title to the concrete level of its exercise over the consumed experience.

The same conclusion appears, in a Brazilian key, in the reading of Denis Borges Barbosa. Exclusivity in intellectual property exists within the limits and for the functions constitutionally assigned to it, not as a general authorization to govern without restraint the entire enjoyment of the environment. The principle remains that of freedom; exclusivity is a functionally delimited exception Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 84; p. 537; p. 547)794. In other words, the existence of an IP right does not automatically convert unilateral mutability, broad private sanction or opacity of governance into effects legitimized by nature.

This point deepens when one observes that ownership does not, by itself, shield the concrete mode of exercising power. The very exercise of copyright can be abusive, and the patent, as an instrumental monopoly, coexists with containment mechanisms and with the logic of exhaustion, which prevents endless control over all subsequent forms of enjoyment of the good already placed on the market Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 110; p. 639; p. 671)795. Not even within the classical core of IP does the title authorize indefinite dominion over everything that results from the circulation of the good. Proprietary overreach, therefore, is not sustained even by the field's own foundations.

On the contrary. Intellectual property is perhaps even more functional, conditioned and socially responsible than other forms of property, which makes p. 698 any absolutist reading of exclusivity untenable. Not even the international regime enshrines a totalizing paradigm; it works, on the contrary, with a balance between incentive and diffusion, between protection and limits Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 75-76; p. 662)796. This overturns the claim to use IP as a terminal argument that would close, even before it began, the discussion about good faith, restoration, verifiability and control of private power.

In the case of software, this tension takes on an even more precise contour. The platform often acts as if the legal protection of the technological infrastructure conferred on it practical sovereignty over the entire consumed experience, when the Brazilian regime does not deliver this. Exclusivity over delimited technical means does not become a monopoly of the result, the effect or the experience enjoyed by the user. The industrial result, in itself, remains outside patent protection, which prevents treating the utility perceived by the consumer as if it were entirely captured by the technological title Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010b, p. 1128)797. This is a direct dogmatic limit to the platform's rhetoric.

The same containment applies to use and enjoyment after the product is placed on the market. Exclusivity over technology does not amount to freedom to fully control all forms of use, enjoyment, access or modification by the end recipient. Once in commerce, certain subsequent effects escape the reach of exclusivity, and insisting on controlling them as if everything were still a natural extension of the title may constitute abuse of right Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010b, p. 1145; p. 1139)798. This means that control of the code, of execution and of updating p. 699 does not authorize, without more, blocking verifiability, neutralizing the assessment of functional loss or reducing enjoyment to a disposable permission administered ad nutum.

Of course, the protection of software explains an important part of the platform's technical power. License, control of execution and the discipline of updating are legally recognized instruments for organizing this power. The formal contractual relationship with the user has become a central mechanism for structuring access, and adhesion contracts often intensely limit the freedom to use the program Barbosa(Barbosa, 2017, p. 2011; p. 2018)799. But recognizing this does not amount to accepting, without control, unilateral mutability of enjoyment, technical opacity or private sanction incompatible with consumer protection.

Nor does exclusivity over software automatically immunize the way this technical power is exercised against external limits. The use of IP rights to dominate markets, limit access or structure exclusions incompatible with the legal order may constitute abuse. Exclusivity does not operate as a safe-conduct to turn technical infrastructure into an unrestricted instrument for the material suppression of the utility of the contract or for the neutralization of control over its effects Barbosa(Barbosa, 2017, p. 2025; p. 2030; p. 2026)800. This is the exact point at which IP and the CDC meet: not in the denial of exclusivity, but in the control of the way it is used in the consumer relationship.

The same applies to contracts and trade secrets. They may legitimately organize the economic exploitation of the immaterial, but they do not become, by themselves, a shield for structural opacity. The know-how contract and the protection of secrecy explain specific forms of organizing power between the parties, but their legitimacy depends precisely on obligational structuring and on the concrete delimitation of what is meant to be kept secret (Barbosa, p. 700 2018, p. 361; p. 376)801. This means that the supplier may protect its secret, but may not use the word “secret” as a magic fog to make everything it does over enjoyment incontestable.

Absolute shielding is also incompatible with the very regime of secrecy, which requires specification of what is protected and concrete delimitation of the secret content for legal protection to make sense. Where there are effects on enjoyment, restoration or contestation, the protection of secrecy coexists with a minimum duty to make controllable what one intends to assert against the consumer and to the system of protection Barbosa(Barbosa, 2018, p. 392-393)802. Industrial secrecy is not a license for a total black box when the problem has ceased to be only the asset and has become the impact of its use on the supplied experience.

In this scenario, the language of the revocable license does not save the platform either. In consumer relations, the binding force of the contractual text is not measured only by formal acceptance, but by its compatibility with good faith and balance. The will expressed in a contract is no longer an isolated decisive factor, and clauses that seek to turn continued enjoyment into a revocable favor, emptying its legal density, are not self-justified merely because they were predisposed in an adhesion contract Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 1004; p. 1080-1081)803. Calling it a license does not erase the structure of continued supply nor eliminate the material scrutiny of the CDC.

p. 701 Moreover, the contractual form chosen by the supplier is not enough to rule out the incidence of the consumer-law microsystem. The CDC applies to consumer contracts in their substance, and not according to the business name given to them. License, exclusivity or technological secrecy do not remove the relationship from the consumer-law regime when there is continued supply under exploitable vulnerability Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 471; p. 481; p. 612)804. The platform may organize its ecosystem through licenses and reservations of rights; this does not place it outside the field of balance, adequate transparency and control of abusiveness.

This same point reappears clearly in the doctrine of Bruno Miragem. The language of the license and of technological dependence does not dissolve the protection of the concretely promised enjoyment. When the utility of the product depends on software, failures in this supply can remove all its value, and the captivity created by continuous updates further broadens the relevance of the duty to inform in advance of the terms of these mutations. Restrictions of use and relevant alterations must be clarified in the offer; otherwise, they may give rise to an information defect, abusiveness and frustration of legitimate expectations Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 86; p. 79])805. The name of the contractual technique does not change the density of the harm.

Likewise, consumer protection acts to prevent private directive power from materially emptying the utility of the performance. The supplier's dominant position requires limits to the exercise of this power, and clauses or practices that give it intense unilateral direction over the course of the relationship, to the point of diminishing or suppressing the utility of the consumer's interests, are materially abusive Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 274; p. 387])806. This reasoning p. 702 applies with special force when governance is anchored in software and in technical control of the environment, precisely because there the platform usually presents as a “need of the system” what is, strictly speaking, a management choice regarding the supply.

There is also a useful point of dogmatic inversion internal to the field of IP itself. The Brazilian copyright regime is not exhausted in the patrimonial facet of exploitation. It also maintains a core of moral rights expressly qualified as inalienable and unwaivable807. This provision does not enter here to make the consumer's experience a matter of authorship nor to transplant, without mediation, the moral logic of copyright into the field of the IOC. Its role is more restrained and more elegant: to show that the very language of IP is not reduced to absolute patrimonial disposability and already knows, from within, limits to the complete dissolution of relevant immaterial bonds. This reinforces the argument that license, adhesion and technical control are not enough, by themselves, to erase all the legal density of the memory, traceability and continuity of enjoyment constituted in the governed ecosystem.

When the platform invokes exclusivity, integrity of the system, operational necessity or software protection to justify blocks, reconfigurations, suppressions or erasures, the legal problem does not end with the abstract validity of the prerogative invoked. It shifts to the control of the concrete mode of exercising this power over governed enjoyment. It is at this level that minimum verifiability, the requirement of reasoning, the assessment of functional loss, the possibility of contestation and, where appropriate, the restoration of relevant effects must apply. IP may explain the organization of the ecosystem. It cannot immunize the way this ecosystem is exercised against duties of verifiable transparency, functional equivalence and control of abusiveness.

This containment does not derive only from objective good faith, exhaustion, the prohibition of abuse or the doctrinal critique of proprietarian absolutism. It derives from the Constitution itself. In dealing with industrial property, Article 5, XXIX does not grant a self-justified patrimonial prerogative, nor does it protect exclusivity as an end in itself. On the contrary, it expressly subordinates the legal guarantee of the privilege over industrial inventions and of the protection of industrial creations, trademarks, company names and other distinctive signs to the social interest and to the technological and economic development of the country. The infraconstitutional discipline of patrimonial intellectual property therefore does not receive a constitutional blank check; it receives a purposive frame. Thus, the mere ownership of an IP right is not enough to p. 703 legitimize, by itself, unilateral mutability of enjoyment, decisional opacity, incontestable private sanction or the destruction of utilities incorporated into the continuous consumer bond. Where the concrete exercise of exclusivity collides with the constitutional ends that justify it, there is no proprietary shielding, but a problem of constitutional compatibility of the very mode of exercising private power al.(Andrade, 2021, p. 61-66).

Notes

  1. The existence of an intellectual property right does not authorize, by itself, the automatic expansion of exclusivity to the entire concrete governance of enjoyment. The language of “property”, when applied to the immaterial, tends to suggest full and unitary dominion where the legal order has in fact instituted a delimited legal position surrounded by limits. It is in this key that Ascensão criticizes the presentation of intellectual rights under the sign of property, observing that such an operation favors the “tendential elimination of the limits that the laws establish for intellectual rights” and concluding that “all this argumentation is however false” Ascensão(Ascensão, 2007, [p. 3]). The author adds that this qualification has an “ideological origin, in the bad sense of the word”, since one qualifies “not to attend to the nature of the being, but to obtain results that are of interest” Ascensão(Ascensão, 2007, [p. 5]). Consequently, license, revocability, unilateral mutability, banning or erasure of trajectory cannot be treated as self-justified effects of the mere ownership of IP. ↩
  2. Exclusivity in matters of intellectual property does not express natural sovereignty over all the utilities and functional projections of the immaterial good. It is an artificial normative construction, produced by rules that withdraw from the generality what, in itself, is not naturally scarce. Ascensão is direct in stating that the legal rule “artificially rarefies goods that are not scarce” and that “this is the technique of the exclusive right” Ascensão(Ascensão, 2007, [p. 12]). For this very reason, the content of the intellectual right is predominantly negative: it does not confer on the holder unlimited positive power over the object, but a faculty of exclusion before third parties. Hence the statement that the intellectual right “gives the holder nothing he did not already have: it merely does not extend to him the prohibition that reaches all others” Ascensão(Ascensão, 2007, [p. 12-13]). The very example of the patent confirms the point, since it “attributes nothing positively”, manifesting itself “in the power to prohibit, but never comes to have the power to use” Ascensão(Ascensão, 2007, [p. 13]). This prevents converting exclusivity into a general safe-conduct to legitimize any form of governance of enjoyment. ↩
  3. Ownership of intellectual property does not amount to the full legitimation of the concrete mode of exercising power over enjoyment. The exclusive right remains legally conditioned, including by limits external to the regime of the immaterial itself. Ascensão observes that it would be “absurd to conceive the intellectual right as a right whose holder could exercise it as he saw fit”, adding that property is born already subject to the principle of the social function, “like all rights” Ascensão(Ascensão, 2007, [p. 3]). This limitation is not merely abstract. In examining the unlawful intellectual work, the author shows that the right may subsist and, even so, its ordinary exploitation be legally prohibited, leaving the holder, in essence, the negative aspect of exclusion, without positive legitimation for full use following from it. For this reason, he concludes that this right “does not positively give faculties of use of the work” Ascensão(Ascensão, 2007, [p. 13]). From the mere ownership of IP one cannot infer the full legitimacy of all concrete governance of enjoyment in the IOC ecosystem. ↩
  4. The existence of an intellectual property right does not automatically legitimize the entire governance of the ecosystem in which the immaterial good circulates. Exclusivity is a functional legal construction, constitutionally delimited and bound to the reasons that justify its existence, not unrestricted power over all the projections of the good. Barbosa formulates this directly when he states that each of these exclusive rights, although it constitutes property at the constitutional level, “exists within the limits and for the functions constitutionally devoted to it”, having not only the common social function, but also a specific function in the design of intellectual properties Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 84). This structural limitation is reinforced when the author places exclusivity back within an economy of freedom: the principle is that of freedom, the norm that excludes certain creations from common use being exceptional Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 537). In intellectual property, “everything that is not protected is free to be copied, even slavishly” Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 547). The existence of an IP right does not convert, by itself, exclusivity into a safe-conduct for unilateral mutability, unrestricted private sanction or total control of enjoyment. ↩
  5. Ownership of intellectual property does not automatically translate into full legitimation of the concrete mode of exercising power over enjoyment, access or use. The title is not enough to shield any form of concrete governance, because the exercise of the right remains subject to external legal control. Barbosa is explicit in recognizing that the very exercise of copyright may constitute abuse, precisely because it is subject to the constitutional limitations inherent to the social function and to restrictions arising from collision with other protected interests Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 110). The patent, as an “instrumental monopoly”, does not subsist as sovereign arbitrariness, admitting means of containment such as compulsory licensing and price control Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 639). Moreover, under the doctrine of exhaustion, once the economic benefit has been obtained by placing the good on the market, the holder's rights over that copy cease, leaving only the exclusivity of reproduction Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 671). Not even within the classical core of IP does the title authorize endless control over all subsequent forms of enjoyment. ↩
  6. The discipline of intellectual property structurally coexists with external limits and, for this very reason, does not authorize an absolutist reading of exclusivity. Barbosa maintains that it is “even more functional, even more conditioned, even more socially responsible, and surely much less full than any other form of property” Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 75). This formulation prevents the reasoning according to which the mere presence of an IP right would close the discussion about the limits of its exercise, since even absolute rights are technical categories dependent on normative configuration and not metaphysical expressions of sovereignty Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 75-76). The rejection of absolutism is explicit in the international regime, since the TRIPs Agreement does not enshrine an absolutist paradigm, being based on a balance between the promotion of innovation and the diffusion of technology Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010, p. 662). Exclusivity in IP exists within a functional legal arrangement, incompatible with any reading that seeks to shield, in advance, the concrete mode of exercising power over enjoyment. ↩
  7. Protection over technological infrastructure does not automatically become unrestricted power over the consumed experience. Patent exclusivity falls on delimited technical means, and not on the ends, effects or experiences to which they may give rise. Barbosa makes this point explicit when reconstructing the doctrine according to which the means of the invention correspond to the technical elements that solve a given problem, while “in relation to the result, it is not patentable” Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010b, p. 1128). The containment appears even more directly in the statement that “the industrial result will never be covered by the patent, always remaining free of the monopoly” Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010b, p. 1128). The existence of protection over certain technical means does not legitimize the claim to unilaterally reconfigure the consumed experience, block verifiability or treat the enjoyed utility as a disposable permission subject to the holder's arbitrariness. ↩
  8. Exclusivity over technology does not amount, by itself, to freedom to fully control use, enjoyment, modification or access by the user. Once the product is placed on the market, “any subsequent use is outside the right of intellectual property”, the relationship between holder and user coming to lie at the ordinary level of civil or commercial law Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010b, p. 1145). Trying to enforce the exclusive right against the end user after exhaustion “would go beyond the reach of the patent” and would constitute “abuse of right” Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010b, p. 1145). This containment reinforces that the technical knowledge intrinsic to the patent remains freely usable for “any intellectual purpose, whether scientific or non-industrial” Barbosa(Barbosa, 2010b, p. 1139). Exclusivity over technology is a functionally delimited legal fiction, and not natural sovereignty over all forms of experience and use by the recipient. ↩
  9. License, control of execution and the discipline of software updating constitute legally recognized forms of organizing the holder's technical power over the infrastructure. Given the material ease of reproduction, practice came to rely on a “formal contractual relationship between the user and the holder of the program”, instead of depending only on abstract enforceability Barbosa(Barbosa, 2017, p. 2011). The license does not appear as a peripheral detail, but as a central instrument for organizing access; adhesion contracts tacitly bind the user and limit their freedom of use, prohibiting, for example, reproduction or separation of components Barbosa(Barbosa, 2017, p. 2018). The protection of software legally explains how the supplier structures technical power, but this recognition does not mean admitting, without control, unilateral mutability of enjoyment, technical opacity or private sanction incompatible with the CDC. ↩
  10. The protection of software does not automatically immunize the mode of exercising the holder's technical power against external limits or controls over its effects. In Brazilian law, the use of intellectual property rights to dominate the market or limit access may constitute an infringement of the economic order, in light of the constitutional protection of freedom of competition Barbosa(Barbosa, 2017, p. 2025). Exclusivity does not function as a safe-conduct to turn technical infrastructure into an unrestricted instrument of exclusion, since “abuse of exclusive rights” is also prohibited, and the legal efficiency of the exercise of power must still be examined Barbosa(Barbosa, 2017, p. 2030). No state agent may give effect to abusive or restrictive clauses in a license agreement, provisions frontally incompatible with the legal order being inadmissible Barbosa(Barbosa, 2017, p. 2026). ↩
  11. Contracts and trade secrets may legitimately organize the economic exploitation of the immaterial, but this legitimacy does not convert them, by itself, into a shield for structural opacity or unilateral precarization of enjoyment. The know-how contract allows the holder to structure a relationship in which it remains master of the competitive capacity, a dominion that does not derive from erga omnes sovereignty, but from contractual organization: the supplier is “the master between the parties” Barbosa(Barbosa, 2018, p. 361). The protection of the secret depends on the externalization of the intention to keep it secret in legally structured relationships, which needs to be “regulated by obligation” Barbosa(Barbosa, 2018, p. 376). Such arrangements explain the platform's organizational power, but do not immunize it against external controls over the mode of its exercise. ↩
  12. Industrial secrecy and contractual confidentiality clauses do not authorize absolute shielding against minimum duties of transparency, contestation and control of the effects of the supply. Both in the regime of secrecy and in that of patents, legal protection bears only on certain and determined elements, requiring that the subject of the secret be concretely delimited to allow verification of the legal hypotheses of protection Barbosa(Barbosa, 2018, p. 392). The absence of specification of the cause of action regarding the secrets allegedly exploited impairs the finding of unlawfulness; delimitation is required of what would be common knowledge and of what would correspond to effective novelty of a confidential nature Barbosa(Barbosa, 2018, p. 393). Where there are effects on enjoyment or restoration, the protection of secrecy coexists with a minimum duty of specification and with the need to make controllable what one intends to be enforceable. ↩
  13. The supplier's invocation of a revocable license or a broad contractual reservation of powers is not enough to neutralize consumer protection nor to downgrade the governed enjoyment of the IOC to the condition of a disposable permission. In consumer relations, the binding force of the text is not measured only by formal acceptance, but by its compatibility with good faith and contractual balance, since “the will of the parties freely expressed in the contract is no longer the decisive factor for the Law” Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 1004). Article 51, IV, of the CDC functions as a general prohibitory clause against provisions that establish iniquitous obligations or ones incompatible with equity Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 1080-1081). Adhesion clauses that seek to empty the legal density of enjoyment or turn continued consumption into a favor revocable ad nutum cannot be taken as self-justifying. ↩
  14. The contractual form chosen by the supplier is not enough, by itself, to rule out the material incidence of the CDC on the consumer relationship. The consumer-law microsystem establishes “a new legal regime for all kinds of contracts [...] involving consumers and suppliers of goods or services”, applying to all types of contracts, if consumer contracts Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 471; p. 481). To characterize the consumer contract, the decisive factor is not the existence of a special law or the name given to the transaction, but the presence of a consumer in a materially protectable position Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 612). License, exclusivity or technological secrecy do not remove the relationship from the consumer-law regime when, in substance, there is continued supply and exploitable vulnerability. ↩
  15. The language of the license and of technological dependence does not dissolve the consumer-law protection of the concretely promised enjoyment. When the utility of the product depends on software, failure in this supply can remove all its value, generating captivity linked to continuous updates that may modify content or accelerate obsolescence Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 86). For this reason, the terms of the update must be disclosed in the offer, failing which they do not bind the consumer or give rise to a claim for frustration of legitimate expectations Miragem(Miragem, 2020, [p. 86]). Restrictions of use in digital products reinforce the duty of pre-contractual clarification; its breach may constitute an information defect and abusiveness, being further subject to the scrutiny of proportionality Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 79). Calling it a license does not erase the breach of legitimate expectation nor save the opportunistic precarization of enjoyment. ↩
  16. Consumer protection is not limited to policing the external form of the contract; it acts to prevent private directive power from materially emptying the utility of the performance. The consumer's vulnerability before the supplier — which exercises a dominant position — causes greater factual power over the course of the relationship to remain concentrated in the supplier, requiring norms limiting private power Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 274). Potestative clauses are abusive precisely because they confer on the supplier a power of direction so intense that it diminishes or suppresses the consumer's possibility of usefully pursuing their interests Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 387). If the unilateral direction of the bond reduces or suppresses the utility of the contract for whoever paid to enjoy it, the problem is no longer only one of structure; it is one of restoration of the enjoyment concretely protected by the CDC. ↩
  17. “Art. 27. The moral rights of the author are inalienable and unwaivable.” Brasil(Brasil, [2021]). ↩
