6.3.3 On the mechanisms appropriate to enabling procedures of open auditability and assessment of functional equivalence

See this section in the PDF

p. 656

In IOCs, open auditability and functional value equivalence become legally useful only when they can be converted into concrete mechanisms for making supply controllable. Without this, they remain standards that are correct in theory but harmless in practice, especially in environments in which the platform concentrates architecture, records, criteria for alteration and means of proof. The problem, therefore, is not only to recognize that enjoyment is governed, but to determine how the consumer-law system can react when this governance becomes opaque, unilaterally reconfigures the acquired utility and, on top of that, monopolizes the reconstruction of what happened. The answer does not require creating a new microsystem or importing an autonomous regulatory regime for platforms. It can be built from the combination of objective good faith, the duty of adequate information, relational cooperation, specific relief, production of documents and redistribution of the burden of proof already admitted in the CDC and the CPC Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 1242)745 Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 856)746.

p. 657 If the platform can alter, modulate, remove, recalibrate, sanction and reinterpret enjoyment on an ordinary basis, then legal control needs to shift from the abstraction of the “power to change” to the verifiability of what was changed, of how this was communicated and of the impacts it produced on the utility legitimately expected by the consumer. In other words, the law does not need to forbid every mutation of the service; it needs to prevent relevant mutations from being pushed onto the consumer as a fait accompli, without a reconstructible trail, without a minimally intelligible explanation and without a real possibility of challenge. It is at this point that open auditability and functional equivalence cease to be merely material criteria and come to require their own procedural instruments: qualified communication, preservation of evidence, production of records, expert examination delimited by functional impact and remedies oriented toward restoring enjoyment, and not only toward the later payment of losses and damages747 Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 111; p. 385; p. 783).

The first of these mechanisms is the duty of reasoning and qualified communication in relevant alterations. In continuous digital services, it is not enough for the platform to “publish an update”, “release a patch note” or bury the modification in layers of text, links or generic notices. When the change is capable of impacting the core of enjoyment of the IOC, good faith requires communication oriented to the essential, hierarchized and timed appropriately to the moment when the consumer can still understand the impact, react, decide and, if necessary, challenge. A relevant alteration cannot be communicated as noise or as a peripheral detail, because this turns mutability into a semiotic trap. The very relational logic of good faith prevents the supplier from imposing unnecessary or malicious expedients that make the exercise of the right a documentary via crucis or a scavenger hunt. If breaching the duty of cooperation and loyalty already means nonperformance, even if p. 658 partial, then communication designed to neutralize the useful understanding of the change also violates the regime of the relationship Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 1242).

This duty of qualified communication also prevents the platform from turning the consumer's silence, passivity or mere remaining in the environment into fictitious acceptance of relevant mutations. In continuing relationships, a substantial alteration of the performance cannot be implemented by a generic notice, a hidden cross-reference or a notification structured to go unnoticed and later be used as an alibi of consent. When the company modifies content, quality, parameters or relevant conditions of enjoyment, it must offer abundant, intelligible and timely information, precisely because the consumer is inserted in a relationship of practical dependence in which the cost of exit, adaptation or challenge is not neutral748 Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 1319-1320). The point is elementary, but it tends to be solemnly sabotaged by platform practice: if the change is important enough to affect enjoyment, it is important enough to be communicated in a way the consumer can understand without having to do digital archaeology.

The second mechanism is the preservation of minimum technical records as a condition of effective control. In IOCs, the supplier controls not only the object and the environment; it also controls the institutional memory of supply. It knows what was shown, when it was shown, how it was altered, which version was in force, which interface trail led to the contracting, which parameter was applied in a sanction, which transaction occurred and in what technical context. When all these elements remain under the platform's monopoly, the governance of the service also becomes governance of proof. For this reason, the minimum preservation of records ceases to be an administrative detail and becomes a condition of controllability. What is required here is not a total search of the system, but a minimum documentary core: history of relevant versions; essential records of alterations with functional impact; trails of consent and of the decision flow; history of relevant transactions; communications of change; and, when there is enforcement, basic records of the decision event, such as the category applied, the reason and the consequence749 Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 131; p. 145; p. 184).

p. 659 This design is entirely compatible with the critique of performative transparency already developed. If the platform can appear to have informed without really making the information useful, it can also appear to have governed legitimately without preserving minimum trails that allow an institutional reconstruction of what it did.

Open auditability is not limited to an abstract duty to “be transparent”; it requires a minimally documented regime of reconstruction of supply. The consumer does not need to know the total engineering of the system, but needs to be able to demonstrate — and protection bodies need to be able to verify — what occurred in a legally relevant way. Without this, the technical mutability of the service becomes an evidentiary safe-conduct, and the duty to inform degenerates into archival performance: a lot of paper for little verifiability.

The third mechanism is properly procedural: preservation of evidence, production of documents and dynamic redistribution of the burden of proof. When proof is born and remains under the platform's control, the procedural system cannot feign neutrality. The CPC already offers quite clear instruments to deal with this. Article 373, paragraph 1750, permits a different distribution of the burden of proof when there is excessive difficulty for one party to discharge the burden or greater ease for the other to produce proof of the contrary fact Brasil(Brasil, [2025]b). In disputes over IOCs, this is precisely the normal configuration: logs, interface trails, alteration histories, sanction criteria, internal support communications and relevant technical records are concentrated in the supplier. The dynamic redistribution of the burden, therefore, does not arise as extravagant judicial benevolence, but as a technically adequate response to the structural asymmetry of proof Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 145; p. 184).

In the same vein, the regime for the production of a document or thing provided for in Articles 396 to 400 of the CPC offers the most direct path to operationalize open auditability. The judge may order the party to produce a document or thing in its possession (Article 396751), provided the request indicates, as completely as possible, the document or record sought, its evidentiary purpose and the circumstances showing that it is with the opposing p. 660 party (Article 397752) Brasil(Brasil, [2025]b). This allows surgical requests, not procedural fishing expeditions.

Instead of generically requesting “all the data in the system”, the consumer may seek, for example, the history of relevant versions of a given item, the trail of screens displayed along the purchase path, the minimum records of the specific transaction, the content of the communication of alteration or the category and reason for the sanction applied. If the supplier denies possessing the document, Article 398753 allows the requesting party to demonstrate by other means that the denial is untrue; if the refusal is unjustified, Article 400754 authorizes the judge to admit as true the facts that the document would serve to prove Brasil(Brasil, [2025]b). In platform-related disputes, procedural guarantees satisfy the need to react to generic answers such as “we no longer have such information”, “this is internal-use data”, “this information is not accessible to the public” or “we are unable to reconstruct the procedure”.

This evidentiary arm connects directly with the duty of real clarification already recognized in consumer law. The supplier does not fulfill its role merely by throwing information into the environment; it also needs to demonstrate, when questioned, that it informed adequately, that it properly exercised its power of alteration and that the impact produced was compatible with the promised performance. Demonstrating compliance with these duties forms part of its own burden of proof Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 412).

When there is greater risk without sufficiently conspicuous information, an informational defect or a relevant mutation whose intelligibility depends on data controlled by the company, it will fall to the company to demonstrate the nonexistence of the defect or nonconformity, and not to the consumer to prove in the dark what happened inside the environment they do not control Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 488). Without this adjustment, the platform becomes simultaneously master of the fact and of the process, concentrating in itself both control of the occurrence and control of the institutional means of its reconstruction.

p. 661 The fourth mechanism is technical expert examination oriented to functional impact, and not to the total engineering of the ecosystem. When the dispute requires specialized examination, the expert examination need not and should not turn into an endless audit of the platform, nor into a demand for full disclosure of source code, architecture or trade secrets. Its object must be delimited by the legally relevant effects of supply: what changed, and to what extent this affected the utility, the compatibility, the continuity, the ecosystemic position or the functional equivalence of the IOC. The expert examination comes in as a tool for completing the reconstruction, operating on minimum records, relevant versions, preserved trails and verifiable functional parameters. Thus both absolute opacity and the fantasy of total transparency are avoided. The proceeding does not need to become a complete autopsy of the system; it only needs to be able to answer, with method, the legally right question.

This delimitation is also important to reconcile the standards with the protection of software and with trade secrets. Open auditability is not oriented toward the unrestricted disclosure of the supplier's technical infrastructure, but toward minimum documentation and the verifiability of the legally relevant effects of supply. What is protected here is not the consumer's technological curiosity, but the institutional possibility of reconstructing alterations and impacts that affect their enjoyment. There is, therefore, compatibility between preserving secrecy and requiring verifiability. Trade secrecy may justify restraint in exposure; it cannot function as an absolute veto on reconstructing what the platform did when that doing has become legally contentious. Otherwise the business becomes peculiarly safe for the supplier, since it has authorization to change, harm its consumers, explain nothing and still invoke trade secrets as a kind of generalized excuse to avoid any possibility of being held liable. The model does not hold up.

The fifth mechanism concerns remedies and protection proper. Here the central point is that the operationalization of open auditability and functional equivalence should not automatically end in losses and damages as the default solution. The consumer-law microsystem prioritizes specific relief, measures capable of ensuring a practical result equivalent to performance and material restoration of the contracted utility Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 856; p. 897). In IOCs, this means that the standards need to guide remedies aimed at restoring the functional and, where appropriate, symbolic integrity of enjoyment. Among these remedies are reversal of a relevant alteration, re-performance, restoration or maintenance of access, preservation of history, restoration of ecosystemic or reputational attributes, equivalent functional substitution, mitigation through alternative versions, correction of parameters p. 662 and proportional compensation when full restoration is not feasible755 Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 529-530).

This orientation is coherent with the very logic of the regime of quality defects in the CDC. The system does not treat inadequacy only as an occasion to compensate; it treats it as an event that triggers duties to restore the utility enjoyed. If, on the classical plane, this already manifests itself in the priority given to replacing the product or service with another in perfect conditions of use, in IOCs the same rationale must be adapted to reconstitute the substantial utility of the digitally governed object. Instead of throwing everything into the monetary blender and calling it protection, it is argued that the remedy most faithful to the system is the one that gives back to the consumer, as far as possible, the legitimately expected enjoyment.

This remedial dimension also connects with specific enforcement and with the mandatory mechanisms of the microsystem itself. The integration between the CDC and collective redress allows the imposition of obligations to perform, correction protocols, maintenance of adequate supply, continuity of services and other measures capable of giving material effectiveness to consumer rights Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 111; p. 385; p. 783). When the dispute reveals a repeated pattern of opacity, abusive reconfiguration or unverifiable suppression of functional value, the response need not be limited to the individual case. It may take on a structural form: a duty to preserve minimum trails, to formalize qualified communication for relevant alterations, to organize intelligible challenge procedures, to maintain minimum functional impact reports or to adjust the supply regime to prevent repetition of the problem. This is especially important on platforms, where unlawfulness usually comes in batches, not as a single piece.

The sixth mechanism is coordinated institutional action. Without opening a parallel regulatory front, the subsection shows that open auditability and functional equivalence can also guide the action of protection bodies in platform markets. These standards offer a concrete criterion for conduct adjustment agreements, recommendations, civil investigations and minimum governance requirements: functional impact reports on relevant alterations, protocols of verifiable transparency, preservation of essential records, comprehensible p. 663 challenge procedures and minimum criteria of restoration in case of relevant degradation of enjoyment. First, the debate is kept from remaining confined to the retail level of the individual case. Second, the platform is prevented from continuing to operate under the fiction that each opaque reconfiguration is a mere technical detail and each conflict is just a little bad luck for the user.

All the mechanisms respond to the same requirement: to prevent the supplier's exclusive technical control from becoming a monopoly over the proof of the very unlawful act alleged. In IOCs, enjoyment is not given once and for all; it is continuously administered. If the platform also administers what can be seen, what can be remembered, what can be documented and what can be demonstrated, then the asymmetry ceases to be merely informational and becomes institutional. It is precisely to break this vicious circle that the subsection converts open auditability and functional equivalence into procedures: a duty to communicate adequately, a duty to preserve minimum records, a duty to produce relevant documentation, the possibility of functionally oriented expert examination, redistribution of the burden of proof and remedies of material restoration Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 1319-1320) Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 131; p. 145).

For this reason, in IOCs, open auditability and functional equivalence do not operate on an object taken in abstraction, but on the concretely governed regime of enjoyment. Their proper field of application is the governance that conditions access, alteration, verifiability, challenge and restoration of what the consumer actually acquires and continues to enjoy. Without enabling mechanisms, the standards would be merely dogmatic good intentions. With them, they come to function as legal technology for the controllability of continuous supply. It is necessary to leave the plane of distant ideals to actually enter the terrain where the platform can be legally obliged to explain, show, preserve, restore and stop hiding behind its own black box.
