3.1.5 The techno-media environment as the condition of possibility of the IOC

See this section in the PDF

This section in the layers of the site

  • Foundations · Sections of chapter 3

p. 137

The combined reading of Ellul, Postman, Vanderburg and Feenberg makes it possible to recognize one and the same dynamic from distinct angles. Technical rationality tends to expand as the organizing criterion of everyday life; media ecologies reformat what presents itself as reliable information, acceptable debate and relevant knowledge; and technique incorporated into routines fragments experience, reducing the density of meaning-making and opening space for forms of symbolic steering that do not depend on explicit persuasion. Feenberg completes this picture by preventing the diagnosis from degrading into fatalism: technical rationality is institutionalized in concrete choices, stabilized in design, and therefore remains criticizable, contestable and controllable.

In this scenario, “techno-media order” describes not so much a cultural backdrop as a mode of practical coordination. Systems, interfaces and patterns of circulation come to p. 138 organize routines and expectations, defining what appears as possible, probable and desirable. Technique operates as environment, media structure forms of life, and advertising communication, in a broad sense, acts as an engineering of signs that reorders needs, belongings and promises. When these layers combine, ordinary experience no longer depends chiefly on contextual interpretation and comes to be traversed by mediations that anticipate choices, delimit alternatives and rank visibilities.

Digital consumption thus emerges as insertion into continuous operational architectures. The consumer does not merely receive a finished piece of content. They come to inhabit an arrangement of access points, curations, permissions, frictions and update cycles that exists while the system works and while the user remains integrated into it. Experience becomes inseparable from the conditions of its production. The consumed object takes on a relational and architectural form, since it is constituted by mediations that organize enjoyment, permanence, loss, modification and degradation over time.

For this reason, the consumption decision is rarely formed solely by rational comparison of performance, price and suitability. It is also organized by regimes of attention, reputation and belonging, in which value is staged, reiterated and stabilized by presentation formats. Postman explains how rhythm, impact and fragmentation affect credibility and relevance; Vanderburg makes it possible to understand why, in desymbolized routines, goods and services tend to take the place of the restoration of meaning; and Feenberg offers the material link, by locating practical normativity in design. Defaults, choice hierarchies, recommendation systems, metrics and urgency devices produce an everyday grammar in which certain behaviors are facilitated, others become improbable and many alternatives do not even appear as alternatives.

The vulnerability formed in this environment is not exhausted by lack of information or by classic economic asymmetry. It bears on the capacity to interpret, verify and contest the very field in which choice takes place. The decisive gap now opens between promise and the actual conditions of enjoyment, between sign and delivery, between the performance of belonging and the infrastructure of monetization. Even when there is no textual omission, steering may operate through curation and architecture, before reading and often outside it.

Platforms function as everyday governance. Feenberg describes constraints materialized in systems capable of guiding actions with greater efficacy than political authority, because they bear on reflexes, skills and attitudes, and not only on formal declarations. Normativity ceases to depend solely on explicit commands and comes to operate through architecture. What is permitted, encouraged or punished may be inscribed in interface, flow p. 139 and metric, not only in a clause. Terms and notices remain relevant, but cease to be the main place in which power is exercised.

Seen in this key, contracts, private policies, technical standards and property regimes over signs and accesses appear as overlapping layers of one and the same governance. The offer is simultaneously narrative and infrastructure: it mobilizes belonging and recognition while administering visibility, repetition and reward. The question ceases to be only the content of the promise and comes to include the way in which it becomes operative, as well as the mediations that shape enjoyment without allowing themselves to be fully verified by the addressee. This is the conceptual terrain in which the teleology inscribed in design can be reconstructed as a structure of purpose and in which requirements such as auditability and functional value equivalence will come to operate as minimum parameters of legitimacy for exchanges mediated by systems.
