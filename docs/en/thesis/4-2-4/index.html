4.2.4 Private rules, enforcement and platform governance

p. 364 Enjoyment in digital ecosystems depends on continuous adherence to rhythms, metrics and standards of recognition administered by the environment itself. Conformity tends to stabilize without overt coercion, as an effect of the management of symbolic risk and of the cost of misalignment. In this context, the relevant normativity is not exhausted by the contractual text, since choices, access and trajectories of permanence result from the combination of internal rules, architecture and enforcement mechanisms. Lex Plataformae designates this regime in operation, in which rule, implementation and enforcement organize participation, visibility and sanction, with direct effects on the position of enjoyment that economically constitutes the IOC. For this reason, proportional parameters of qualified transparency, minimum stability, verifiable reasoning, contestability and restoration are required.

The central point is to describe a system for directing conduct endowed with functional coercion inside the ecosystem, exercised through internal rules, architecture and application at scale. Eros Grau’s formulation391 offers direct conceptual support for this framing by arguing that “law” can mean a “system of principles (norms) coercively imposed on a given social group by any social organization endowed with power to do so” Grau(Grau, 2008, p. 15-16). This vocabulary allows internal rules, implementation architecture and enforcement mechanisms to be treated as the effective law of the environment in an operational sense, since they organize access, permanence and sanctions with concrete capacity to impose standards and produce collective effects, without neutralizing the phenomenon as a merely formal exercise of contractual autonomy.

For present purposes, Lex Plataformae does not designate a rhetorical label for “terms of use”, but an integrated normative regime that operates inside the ecosystem and defines, with practical effectiveness, the conditions of enjoyment, permanence and recognition. Its structure can be described in three layers that presuppose one another. The first is the internal normative text, formed by terms of service, conduct policies, moderation rules and declared criteria of participation, which state permissions, prohibitions and expectations of p. 365 use. The second is the layer of architecture and implementation, in which such statements cease to be mere declarations and are converted into a configuration of possibilities through interface, flows, access patterns, visibility parameters and technical restrictions that delimit the range of the possible before any interpretive dispute. The third is the layer of enforcement and sanctions, composed of application mechanisms that grade consequences, apply restrictions, lower visibility, suspend features and, in extreme cases, exclude the user, reconfiguring their position in the environment.

The decisive distinction is between the statement and the effective norm. The internal rule only gains normative density when it is converted into conditions of possibility by architecture and into consequences by enforcement. Reading Lex Plataformae as practical normativity gains precision when the platform ceases to be treated as a mere intermediary and comes to be described as an instance of governance by infrastructure, that is, as an arrangement that defines the space of action and the terms of participation by technical and organizational means.

This framing engages with the perception, already consolidated in Brazilian Digital Law doctrine, that the digital environment strains the practical monopoly of state sources and favors solutions founded on repetition, adaptation, analogy and speed of decision. Patricia Peck Pinheiro392 argues that Digital Law "brings the opportunity to apply, within a uniform legal logic, a series of principles and solutions that were already being used diffusely – principles and solutions that lie at the root of so-called Customary Law" Pinheiro(Pinheiro, 2026, p. 20-21).

Pinheiro characterizes this arrangement as a hybrid system between codified law and customary law, in which elements such as generality, uniformity, continuity, durability and notoriety are reorganized in an environment in which the time factor profoundly alters the way legal answers are produced, circulated and become obsolete Pinheiro(Pinheiro, 2026, p. 20- 21). The practical consequence is that, according to the author, "we are living in an Internet governed by contracts", in which the principle of pacta sunt servanda takes on amplified prominence, with Terms of Use of expanded practical effect and technology companies acting as managers of international, multicultural, cross-border communities endowed with their own rules Pinheiro(Pinheiro, 2026, p. 21).

This reading offers relevant descriptive support to the category of the Lex Plataformae, but requires two adjustments. The first concerns scale. In the platform ecosystem, private p. 366 normativity ceases to operate as a diffuse practice recognized among multiple agents and takes on a denser, more concentrated and infrastructural form. The second concerns composition. Contracts do not operate alone. They are articulated with code, interface, choice design, selective frictions, algorithmic curation systems, opaque prioritization regimes and unilateral update mechanisms, composing a normative arrangement thicker than the contractual text taken in isolation. The platform does not merely participate in a fluid regulatory environment, since it itself designs it, organizes its points of entry, defines criteria of permanence, structures systems of reporting, moderation and reputation, administers flows of evidence and executes immediate sanctions through articulated technical and contractual means. It is this twist that justifies shifting the description from an "Internet governed by contracts" to an ecosystem governed by architecture, in which the contract is only one of the operating layers.

Cohen makes explicit that platforms use technical protocols and centralized control to define networked spaces and structure that space in favor of ease of use, operating modularity as an instrument to manage the tension between heterogeneity and usability, insofar as platform protocols impose a modular structure that allows certain kinds of flexibility and, at the same time, blocks others Cohen(Cohen, 2019, p. 41-42).

This arrangement shifts authority to standards, protocols and compliance procedures that come to act as effective operators directing conduct. Cohen observes that networked organization under conditions of legal or practical standardization signals a de facto shift of legal authority to the standard, whose authority can be quite demanding as to forms of compliance and, at the same time, may open new opportunities for the exercise of economic and political power Cohen(Cohen, 2019, p. 218).

When authority shifts to infrastructure and standards, a mismatch arises with traditional models of accountability and contestation, because practical normativity tends to resist visibility and the conventional rites of control. Cohen formulates this problem by noting that the disconnects between network- and standard-based governance and the ideals of the rule of law point to a project of institutional design aimed at confronting arbitrary power and seeking accountability to networked global publics, while having to deal with the growing practical sovereignty of platforms Cohen(Cohen, 2019, p. 237-238). Here the point is not to convert the platform into a State, but to recognize that practical sovereignty, when exercised through architecture and protocols, produces normative effects with enough density to demand control strategies compatible with the kind of power exercised. This lens allows the Lex Plataformae to be understood as government by infrastructure and, simultaneously, prepares the ground to discuss proportional duties of qualified p. 367 transparency, contestability and restoration, without moralism and without simplifying fictions about market neutrality.

The recourse to Brandeis393 (2011) at this point serves to structurally reinforce the diagnosis already established of the Lex Plataformae as practical normativity, avoiding both the moralization of private power and the statist leap. The usefulness of the exercise lies in making explicit an institutional parallel between two forms of concentration that operate through position and through the integration of functions. In the arrangement described by Brandeis, domination does not impose itself as formal public command, but as gradual control of essential circuits and as the capacity to coordinate decisions from the administration of other people's money Brandeis(Brandeis, 2011, [p. 27]).

In the platform ecosystem, the direction of conduct stabilizes through the integration of internal rule, implementation architecture and scalable enforcement, combined with mechanisms of ranking, visibility and reputation that make enjoyment dependent on opaque and mutable criteria. The comparison with Brandeis has a methodological function by shifting the focus from one’s own wealth to power exercised by position. In this register, decisive influence is not born of wealth itself, but of the capacity to command third parties' resources and, with that, direct conduct and economic trajectories Brandeis(Brandeis, 2011, [p. 37]). The usefulness of the parallel lies in showing that the legal problem is not reduced to a lack of information in the classic consumer law sense, but involves the capacity to order the possible through internal standards, with low institutional reciprocity for those who bear the effects, which authorizes demanding proportional counterweights p. 368 in operational terms, especially qualified transparency, effective contestability and verifiable auditability.

The consolidation of this power occurs through the gradual unification of functions essential to economic life. Brandeis describes how distinct functions indispensable to business end up gathered in the figure of the investment banker, and attributes to this gathering the formation of the Money Trust Brandeis(Brandeis, 2011, [p. 26-27]). From there, the historical form of the phenomenon becomes comparable to that of political despotism, since it is established by progressive usurpation, with incremental advance and not by violent acts of rupture Brandeis(Brandeis, 2011, [p. 26-27]). The point here is functional capture through gradual occupation of essential roles until effective direction of the system is concentrated by position. This kind of capture is institutionally dangerous because it does not depend on visible rupture, since it reorganizes the field of the possible from within, altering who decides, who validates, who selects and who becomes dependent, while the external appearance may remain that of a free market with individual choices.

The mechanism of functional concentration produces a specific decisional pathology: the coincidence between who provides the service and who governs the object of the service, eliminating the distance needed to preserve the rationality and integrity of the decision. Brandeis is explicit as to the requirement of separation between banking activity and the financed business, because the accumulation of functions produces a structural conflict of interest Brandeis(Brandeis, 2011, [p. 196]).

When the same agent acts as director of a railroad and, simultaneously, as the banker in charge of structuring the issue of securities, he controls, on one side, the business decision to issue and, on the other, the financial conditions under which these securities will be placed on the market, and may even direct them to himself as financial intermediary. In this configuration, deliberation ceases to be oriented by the best interest of the enterprise and comes to follow the rationality of the financial transaction and of value extraction.

The absence of this separation is not an episodic moral failure. It is a structural defect of the regime, because it converts the very architecture of the decision into a mechanism of asymmetry and opacity, hinders rational contestation of the grounds and naturalizes internal choices that respond to misaligned incentives.

The next step of the diagnosis completes the parallel with governance by information, because oligarchic regimes tend to govern not only by direct control, but by administration of perception and value, which shifts decisions to a logic of signals, reputation and visibility. The author describes this kind of rationality by showing how the decision-maker may orient his action by the probable effect on market value, keeping p. 369 permanent attention to how facts will be read by investors and by the street Brandeis(Brandeis, 2011, [p. 200]).

The institutional result is that decisions necessary for the good of the assets may be postponed to avoid unfavorable readings, preserving an appearance of value in the short term. Here a decisive model of opacity appears, because, when the rationality of the regime is oriented by signals and reactions of external observers operating with imperfect information, power comes to be exercised through control of the evaluation circuit, and not through public justification of the grounds of the decision. Contestability is compressed because the conflict ceases to bear on verifiable criteria and comes to orbit results and expectations modulated by internal mechanisms.

The usefulness of the parallel with Lex Plataformae lies not in equating banks and platforms as identical phenomena, nor in shifting the thesis to economic history. It lies in fixing an analytical grammar of private power capable of ordering conduct and distributing opportunities with low visibility of the grounds. Just as the Money Trust is formed by functional encroachment394 and by control of the circuits of financing and validation, platform ecosystems stabilize when functions that could previously be distributed come to be integrated in a single decision center.

This center defines internal parameters, incorporates these parameters into the architecture of the system and applies consequences at scale, with the capacity to administer access, visibility, reputation and permanence. Just as the “banker-director” comes to govern decisions from the standpoint of the market and of apparent value, governance by ranking, reputation, metrics and algorithmic screening comes to govern the possible and the probable from the standpoint of signal and p. 370 legibility. In both cases, the problem is not reduced to “lack of transparency” understood as simple disclosure, because the core is a power to classify, select and direct that derives from the position occupied, produces collective effects and meets low institutional reciprocity for those affected.

The historical reinforcement helps establish a point of coherence. Informational domination here should not be confused with the “duty to inform” typical of the CDC. The problem is not reduced to an absence of communication to the consumer. The problem is the capacity to govern the ecosystem by internal criteria, by functional unification and by operational opacity, making access to the grounds of decisions structurally difficult and contestation, in ordinary terms, impracticable. The dogmatic consequence follows the same logic as the diagnosis.

If power is concentrated through the unification of functions and exercised through control of circuits and signals, the adequate response is neither nationalization nor a formal “public mandate”, but the imposition of proportional and procedural counterweights that make the regime controllable. It is a matter of separating and managing relevant conflicts, defining standards of accessible reasoning, and of effective contestability and auditable mechanisms that allow the coherence, proportionality and grounds of decisions to be examined. It is in this sense that Brandeis functions here as reinforcement for the transition from diagnosis to the dogmatic demand for verifiable parameters, compatible with a regime in which enjoyment and position depend on private governance in continuous operation.

The normativity of Lex Plataformae is not limited to applying rules already given, because it includes the function of continuously producing and updating the very parameters that structure participation in the ecosystem. The platform defines internal categories of conduct, delimits what counts as acceptable use, formulates hypotheses of infringement, establishes metrics of performance and reputation and sets practical conditions of access and permanence that operate not only as statements, but as structuring criteria of the ordinary life of the environment. This power of normative production does not manifest itself as an exceptional event, since it stabilizes as an administrative routine in which policies, parameters and operating standards are recalibrated under the logic of maintaining the ecosystem, reorganizing what will be recognized as regular behavior, what will be treated as deviation and what will come to be penalized by downgrading, restriction or exclusion.

The passage from “rule production” to “rule existence” occurs when the normative statement ceases to depend on explicit command and comes to operate as a configuration of the environment itself. In this register, what counts is not only what the internal p. 371 terms say, but what the infrastructure permits, encourages, blocks and makes costly, because it is in this layer that the norm is converted into an objective condition of possibility and into a practical range of conduct. The point drawn is that the rule is not exhausted by the text, since it is realized as an architecture that defines, with granularity, the permitted, the improbable and the practically impossible.

This lens is decisive for Lex Plataformae because it explains why the ecosystem governs without needing to present government as an isolated act of will. The implemented norm comes to operate before the conflict, shaping ordinary experience and delimiting, by design, the field of the possible. With this, the analysis can advance from the plane of internal declarations to the plane of implementation as the rule's mode of existence, showing how flows, permissions, frictions and mediations of visibility translate internal policies into practical conditions of enjoyment and, in this movement, reconfigure the very position of enjoyment that economically constitutes the IOC.

Implementation is the point at which Lex Plataformae ceases to be a textual promise and comes to exist as a practicable world. The internal rule, by itself, describes expectations and hypotheses of infringement, but it is architecture that converts these expectations into ordinary paths of behavior, delimiting entrances, exits and deviations with asymmetric costs. Defaults, interface flows, confirmation sequences, permission layers and selective frictions operate as the grammar of the environment, orienting the user toward predictable paths and stabilizing the experience as a governed routine. The result is that the permitted is not only what is said to be permitted, but what is easy, what is sustained by defaults and what remains available without continuous friction.

This implemented normativity is also realized through the administration of time and visibility. Access windows, event calendars, return cycles and update rhythms distribute urgency and functional scarcity, turning the future into an operational resource of the ecosystem. In parallel, mediation by ranking, recommendation, feed and prestige metrics translates the rule into hierarchies of attention, converting recognition into a condition of permanence and reputation into a governed variable. Thus, implementation does not function as a simple neutral means of execution, because it defines what will be found, what will be comparable, what will be rewardable and what will be pushed to the periphery.

In the IOC regime, this point is decisive because the position of enjoyment is continuously shaped by these conditions of possibility, and not only by the declared content of the contract. The relevant economic object is not a one-off performance delivered at the moment of acquisition, but a trajectory of enjoyment sustainable over time, dependent on continued p. 372 access, public legibility and the preservation of commensurability within the environment. When architecture reorganizes flows, alters patterns of visibility, recalibrates frictions and redesigns paths, it redefines enjoyment in practice without needing to “alter the contract”, since it modifies the conditions under which the acquired position remains viable, recognizable and valuable. It is for this reason that Lex Plataformae must be treated as practical normativity in operation, since the implemented rule becomes the real mode of existence of the object and, consequently, the point at which the conflict ceases to be a dispute over clauses and becomes a dispute over the governance that defines the possible in the ecosystem.

The integration between rule and architecture is completed when compliance ceases to be a question of interpretation and comes to be measured and applied by the environment itself. At this point, technical enforcement does not function as a later and neutral stage of execution, because it defines what counts as regular conduct, which deviations will be detected and which corrections will be demanded, replacing normative gradations with operational criteria that need to “fit” into code. Cohen draws attention to the institutional cost of this shift by observing that automated processes, although efficient, may not align well with legal requirements formulated in “shades of gray” and that removing human judgment from the enforcement loop raises difficult questions about how to define and audit compliance itself Cohen(Cohen, 2019, p. 192). The payoff for Lex Plataformae is direct. By converting standards into technical checks and shifting judgment into the system that measures and applies, the ecosystem comes to govern by criteria whose rationality tends to become opaque to those who suffer the effects, while making the sanction predictable and scalable.

This framing prepares the consequence that matters for the IOC. If the position of enjoyment depends on remaining eligible according to internal parameters, automated enforcement becomes a constitutive element of the object in practice, because it calibrates access, permanence and visibility through mechanisms that operate before any public dispute over meaning, proportionality and grounds. From there, the analysis can advance to the decomposition of the concrete forms of enforcement in the ecosystem, showing how blocks, restrictions, downgrades and exclusions operate as gradual sanctions that degrade enjoyment without needing to announce themselves as punishment.

Enforcement, under the regime of Lex Plataformae, does not present itself as a single and clear act, equivalent to the classic sanction applied after an uncontroversial violation. It is distributed in distinct layers and times, combining preventive barriers, subsequent responses and downgrading mechanisms that operate by adjusting access and visibility. With this, the norm ceases to depend on the moment when someone “declares” the infringement and comes to exist p. 373 as a regime of administered enjoyment, in which the environment continuously anticipates deviation, filters conduct and reorganizes the experience to make certain behaviors improbable and others inevitable.

A first form is ex ante enforcement, in which the sanction operates before the conflict as a restriction of possibility. Locks, interaction limits, feature blocks, barriers to accessing certain spaces and requirements of verification or additional steps function as preventive filters that, under the appearance of technical management, delimit ranges of permitted conduct. In these cases, the user does not properly encounter an explained prohibition, but a design that makes certain actions impracticable, risky or excessively costly. The consequence is discipline by architecture, because obedience is obtained by making things impossible and not by persuasion.

The second form is ex post enforcement, in which the ecosystem applies declared or partially declared sanctions after a moderation event or automatic detection. Temporary suspensions, bans, loss of access to content and gradual restrictions of use reorganize the trajectory of enjoyment and may break the continuity that sustained the value of the IOC. Here, the sanction is not only the interruption of a service, since it bears on the position acquired in the ecosystem. A suspension may disrupt cycles, events and reputation milestones; a ban may cut the trajectory of belonging; a functional restriction may downgrade the user to a residual experience that preserves the formal bond, but degrades effective enjoyment.

The third form is enforcement by visibility and reputation, which tends to be the most efficient and the most difficult to contest because it does not necessarily present itself as punishment. Reductions of reach, loss of prominence, degradation of ranking, fewer recommendations and algorithmic silencing may operate as sanctions that keep the user “inside” the environment, but in an inferior condition, with less access to recognition, fewer opportunities for public comparison and less capacity to sustain the socially legible position that gave value to enjoyment. This modality is central to the IOC regime, since it directly affects the positional component of the object. The relevant punishment is often not to expel, but to downgrade, because remaining inside, yet less visible, less eligible and less recognizable, is equivalent to degrading the position of enjoyment and corroding the value that depended on commensurability and validation in circuit.

This third form of enforcement can be clearly illustrated by the Honor System in League of Legends, which operates as a mechanism of gradual sanction by eligibility, reward and communication, modulating reputation and permanence without needing to p. 374 formally terminate the bond. In institutional terms, Riot itself describes Honor as a system that “rewards sportsmanlike conduct” and, at the same time, “penalizes inappropriate behavior” through “reduced rewards” and “limited communication privileges” Riot Games(Riot Games, 2025a). The relevant sanction, in this design, is not only to prevent access to the game, but to shift the player to an inferior regime of enjoyment, in which the account remains active and productive, but loses access to goods, rites and signals that sustain the positional value of the experience.

When the platform lowers Honor and “locks” progression, this is not a symbolic gesture. It is a functional cut in the reward routines and in the promises of continuity that tie investment of time and consumption across seasons and content cycles. In direct language, Riot explains that, after a penalty, the user may enter a “reform period”, during which he “will not be eligible to receive Chests and Keys” and will have his “Honor progress” locked, with a duration dependent on behavior Riot Games(Riot Games, 2017a). Governance by sanction, here, acts as discipline by friction. The player is kept in the environment, but the restitution of eligibility is conditioned on an opaque and gradual form of rehabilitation, which demands long conformity to recover reputational legibility395.

This kind of downgrading operates as a “temporary civil incapacity” from the standpoint of the IOC. The subject remains inside the ecosystem, but in a lower band of recognition, with less access to rewards and with reduced interaction channels, which lowers both his capacity to sustain prestige and his capacity to participate fully in the everyday rituals of the game. The official description itself makes explicit that certain Honor levels remove communication features and benefits associated with seasonal rewards, which directly ties behavioral conformity to access to goods and to participation in the validation circuit Riot Games(Riot Games, 2025a). When this mechanism is observed alongside the structure of rewards by levels and checkpoints, it becomes even clearer that the loss of Honor is not a “moral demotivation” of the environment, but a material reconfiguration of eligibility and trajectory League of Legends Wiki(League of Legends Wiki, [202-?]a).

p. 375 The same ecosystem reveals a second decisive dimension of enforcement, which is the passage from human judgment to a regime of scalable application, in which the control of conduct becomes fast, systematic and, therefore, structural. In the Instant Feedback System, Riot states that users may be punished “within 15 minutes after the end of the match”, and describes a range of consequences that goes from restrictions to permanent suspensions Riot Games(Riot Games, 2026i). In this logic, enforcement ceases to be an exceptional moment and becomes an ordinary feature of enjoyment. The legal consequence is that the sanction comes to compose the object itself, since it defines the practical stability of the acquired position and conditions the continuity of access on criteria whose detailed functioning is not fully revealed to the user, under justifications of efficiency, prevention of circumvention and management of scale396.

This dynamic prepares the contrast with the extreme gesture of definitive expropriation. While Honor downgrades the account, restricts rewards and degrades enjoyment without formal expulsion, suspension and termination break the digital biography built under the regime of the ecosystem. The contract assumes this possibility as a permanent risk and, above all, defines effects that go beyond the simple interruption of the service. If the account is terminated, access is lost not only to the “game”, but to the set of data and virtual content that structured the experience, besides asserting the absence of refund in cases of just cause Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e)397. The ban, in this framework, is not only punishment for conduct. It is the final form of reorganization of positional value, in which years of biographical, reputational and economic investment can be condensed into a total disconnection from the environment, with low institutional reciprocity for those who bear the effect.

Enforcement in platform ecosystems does not only apply a previously intelligible rule, because it operates on an environment in which the very criteria of decision and of distribution of opportunities tend to remain inaccessible to those who bear its effects. The consequence is that the asymmetry ceases to be reducible to an information deficit correctable by notice or generic disclosure, since it involves operational secrecy about intermediation and ranking mechanisms that define visibility, access and permanence. It is in this register that Cohen p. 376 describes the user as an agent acting in an environment marked by operational secrecy, in which he cannot easily monitor the quality of the algorithmic matching service he has acquired nor verify whether the platform engages in undisclosed forms of preferential positioning Cohen(Cohen, 2019, p. 43). When the decision circuit is structured this way, the user's experience is reduced to the result, while the effective grounds of the result, that is, the combination of criteria, weights and priorities, remains out of reach, compressing the possibility of discussing the coherence, proportionality and rationality of the act.

The compression of contestability becomes even more evident when one considers that these systems not only choose, but also classify, rank and stabilize practical patterns of normality, converting categorization into a mechanism of social ordering. In the formulation mobilized by Cohen, these disciplines characterize, classify and specialize, distribute subjects along a scale around a norm, rank individuals and, when necessary, disqualify and invalidate Cohen(Cohen, 2012, [p. 108]).

The relevant point lies not in importing a general theory of surveillance, but in establishing that, when the position of enjoyment depends on signals of recognition, reputation and eligibility produced by such mechanisms, the conflict comes to bear on a regime of classification whose functioning is barely verifiable and whose review becomes impracticable in ordinary terms. It is a matter of understanding that the controversy falls not only on a specific result, but on the very grammar that defines what counts as acceptable conduct, who is perceived as a legitimate user and which trajectories are made probable or improbable inside the ecosystem. Depending on how ranking criteria, trust thresholds, performance metrics and automated screening operate, the platform not only distributes visibility, but also produces effects of normalization, reinforcing dominant patterns and penalizing deviations through downgrading, blocking or loss of access.

For this reason, in the domain of Lex Plataformae, the opacity of criteria is not an external detail, but a constitutive element of an institutional asymmetry, since it prevents identification of the grounds that orient the decision, restricts the possibility of an informed response and hinders the construction of rational challenges that could circulate beyond the individual case. In dogmatic terms, this opacity bears directly on the capacity to contest the exercise of private power, because it makes the very existence of a reviewable parameter uncertain and renders unfeasible, in practice, minimum forms of adversarial hearing, justification and control. Without these counterweights, the operative norm remains an effective command, but is not converted p. 377 into a stable object of scrutiny, and the review of its effects tends to be limited to episodic and casuistic interventions, insufficient to rebalance the position of those affected.

From the moment internal rules, implementation architecture and enforcement mechanisms are integrated into a normative regime with practical effectiveness, but with operational criteria protected by opacity and functional secrecy, the central problem ceases to be the simple lack of information and becomes a deficit of institutional reciprocity. Operative normativity produces collective effects, distributes opportunities, defines eligibility and applies sanctions that reconfigure the position of enjoyment, but the affected subject tends to access only the results, not the grounds that produce them, which compresses contestability and prevents the conflict from being organized in verifiable rational terms, demanding proportional counterparts of verifiable reasoning and intelligible avenues of contestation.

The methodological usefulness of the article by Warren and Brandeis ([201-?]) is to record that technical and organizational changes alter the mode of production of harm and may make insufficient categories previously apt to capture the conflict Brandeis(Warren; Brandeis, [201-?], [p. 3]). When new capacities of recording and circulation invade the private sphere, protection needs to reposition itself to preserve its effectiveness Brandeis(Warren; Brandeis, [201-?], [p. 3]). In this context, it is not enough to base protection on contract or trust, and it is necessary to resort to tort liability as a basis of protection Brandeis(Warren; Brandeis, [201-?], [p. 14]). This reconstruction demands calibration regarding relevant violations and mechanisms of harm Brandeis(Warren; Brandeis, [201-?], p. 33).

Lex Plataformae can thus be synthesized as a regime of practical normativity because it articulates, in an integrated way, rule production, architectural implementation and scalable enforcement, operating with high effectiveness in directing conduct and, simultaneously, with low contestability for those who bear its effects. By producing mutable internal parameters, converting these parameters into conditions of possibility through flows, permissions and mediations of visibility, and applying consequences through restrictions, downgrades and exclusions, the platform governs the range of the possible in the ecosystem and turns conformity into a requirement of permanence and recognition. In this scenario, the conflict ceases to be a peripheral dispute over abstract clauses and comes to bear on a system that classifies, ranks and sanctions trajectories of enjoyment, continuously reconfiguring what counts as a sustainable position in the environment. For the IOC regime, the economically relevant object is not reduced to a one-off delivery, but consists of a position of enjoyment dependent on public legibility, technical eligibility and temporal continuity, all administered by this normative complex. For this reason, the decisive question is not p. 378 only the existence of internal rules, but the way operative normativity organizes participation and distributes positional value, with collective effects that demand an institutional reading of the subject submitted to such government, a theme that imposes itself in the next step.

Recognizing that the Lex Plataformae operate as practical normativity does not depend on turning the platform into a State, but on admitting that the direction of conduct can be exercised by social organizations endowed with power, producing functional coercion and collective effects inside an ecosystem. In Grau, this shift is made explicit when he states that “the State is not the only social organization capable of positing law, that is, of producing posited law”, breaking “definitively with the conception according to which law is necessarily a creature of the State” Grau(Grau, 2008, p. 78). The usefulness of this lens is methodological and critical at the same time, because it allows the platform to be described as an instance that institutes rules, administers sanctions and imposes conformity without naturalizing this order as destiny or market neutrality.

In this sense, the warning that “the legal order of the market prevails”, understood not as something the market ontologically is, but as “the order it institutes” Grau(Grau, 2008, p. 326), blocks readings that would treat private normativity as a mere spontaneous outcome of exchanges. The consequence is to keep open the possibility of control and of demanding proportional counterweights, since what is described is an order effectively produced and sustained by decisions of design, implementation and enforcement.

At the same time, when law is understood as “a level of the social whole […], in which social relations are expressed” Grau(Grau, 2008, p. 54), it becomes possible to treat internal rules, architecture and enforcement not as an external representation of the conflict, but as the very institutional materiality in which digital relations are constituted, stabilized and acquire meaning. This justifies directing legal analysis to the effective plane of operation of the ecosystem, without confusing the description of normative power with the automatic legitimation of its exercise.

If the direction of conduct can be exercised by organizations endowed with power, describing Lex Plataformae requires attention to the way internal rules, architecture and enforcement are realized in the ordinary routines of the ecosystem. This is the point at which access restrictions, visibility downgrades and eligibility changes come to bear directly on the position of enjoyment and on the positional value that depends on it.

If Lex Plataformae operates as practical normativity, integrating rule production, architectural implementation and scalable enforcement to organize participation, visibility and sanction, the subject submitted to this regime appears not only as a contracting party, but as a p. 379 governed participant under conditions of belonging, eligibility and recognition that define his own position in the ecosystem. It is in this sense that the transition to the figure of the consumer-citizen imposes itself, since the consumption experience comes to include, inseparably, submission to private rules, to classifications and to correction mechanisms that structure social life in the environment. At the same time, once the normative functioning of the ecosystem and the deficit of contestability that accompanies it have been described, it becomes inevitable to advance toward instruments capable of converting this description into operational criteria of control, bearing on qualified transparency, minimum stability, avenues of contestation and restoration of enjoyment in terms compatible with the density of the power exercised.

Of interest here is only the characterization of the legal-institutional mechanism described by Brandeis. Interlocking directorates designates, for Brandeis, the practice of intertwining boards and interests in which the same person, or the same group, occupies directing positions in different companies, including rivals or companies that contract with each other, which expands conflicts of interest and facilitates anticompetitive coordination. In this sense, the “curse of bigness” is Brandeis's critique of the idea that size, by itself, generates efficiency. For him, when bigness is obtained through combinations and mergers and comes to be administered remotely, the result tends to be concentration of power with loss of decisional quality and practical inability to govern operational complexity without degradation, which turns “bigness” into a source of inefficiency and systemic risk Brandeis(Brandeis, 2011, [p. 166]). ↩

Functional encroachment designates, in Brandeis, the way a financial oligarchy is consolidated not by a single act of conquest, but by gradual, discreet and often barely visible usurpation, which keeps shifting to the same group functions that, when exercised separately, tend to produce reciprocal controls and competitive balance Brandeis(Brandeis, 2011, [p. 25-28]). It is not about moralizing the agent, but about describing an institutional mechanism of accumulation. The investment banker ceases to be a mere intermediary and comes to concentrate, at the same time, the structuring of financing, the issuing and organization of securities, the placement of these securities on the market and influence over the main buyers and depositaries of funds, such as insurers, banks and trust companies. The formation of the Money Trust stems, above all, from this union of distinct functions in a single position, because accumulation reduces friction between interests, captures the routes of validation and turns corporate decisions into decisions governed by the logic of the financial circuit. For this reason, encroachment is described as a subtle and long-lasting process, comparable, in its form, to the concentration of powers in political despotism, since the loss of economic freedom can occur without open rupture, merely through the recombination of competences in a single center (Brandeis, 2011, [p. 25-28]. In practical terms, it is a shift from “roles” to “position”, in which whoever controls the position comes to direct railroads and industrial companies, influences insurance companies and also comes to command banks and fiduciary institutions, creating structural dependence and reducing the need to compete under symmetrical conditions.
