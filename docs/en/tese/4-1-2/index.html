4.1.2 The society of the spectacle and the gamification of everyday life

See this section in the PDF

p. 281

So far, the IOC has been described through the feature that it only becomes economically operational within an administrable regime of signification, in which the offer depends on public intelligibility and on criteria of comparison and recognition. The next step consists in shifting the focus from the sign to the environment, because, on platforms, the promise does not circulate only as meaning, but as scene. Enjoyment involves a publicized and verifiable position, in which reputation, social proof and legible hierarchies cease to be a side effect and become part of the offer itself.

It is worth describing this mechanism as part of the teleology of extraction already outlined, because the continuity of consumption comes to depend on recurring mediations that convert enjoyment into conduct and return into a practical condition for maintaining value. In this key, it becomes necessary to resort to Guy Debord's304 diagnosis of the spectacle in order to isolate the p. 282 conceptual instruments that allow visibility and comparison to be treated as structural forms of governing desire and permanence in immaterial consumption.

Here, spectacle does not designate media excess nor a mere addition of images to the real, but a social form of mediation in which relations operate through representations and reorganize experience under a logic of visibility. Debord formulates this perspective when he states that the spectacle is not a set of images, but a social relation among people mediated by images Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 26]). Such a definition prevents the reduction of the concept to mass media, since the author refuses to treat it as a mere product of the techniques of image diffusion and describes it as a worldview that has become effective and has been materially objectified305 Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 27]). Thus, the spectacle can be mobilized to understand platforms as environments in which the promise of the IOC circulates as a continuous social scene, that is, as a dynamic of visibility and comparison that stabilizes desire and permanence as part of value itself.

Debord denounces the ideological maneuver that seeks to neutralize social critique by replacing the concept of “spectacle” with the technocratic and reductionist term “media”306. Twenty years later, the author maintains that this reduction conceals domination by making the infrastructure of power look like a mere technical information service. Debord (2013, [p. 120]) demystifies the supposed professional neutrality of the press and television by describing modern mass communication not as a space of dialogue, but as a vector of unilateral purity, in which discourse functions as administrative command. This framing reinforces the use of the expression “techno-media order”, capable of describing the opaque form of operation that digital platforms tend to adopt.

This is a form of mediation in which experience ceases to present itself as direct lived experience and comes to be organized as representation. Debord begins this diagnosis by stating that, under modern conditions of production, social life announces itself as an immense accumulation of spectacles and that everything that was directly lived has moved away into a p. 283 representation Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 26]). The point, however, is not a communicational “noise” that the system would seek to correct. The separation is structural, because the spectacle functions as the common language of the split itself, bringing individuals together without overcoming isolation, to the point of reuniting the separated and, even so, reuniting it as separated Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 33]). In other words, the spectacle produces an appearance of common life by placing everyone before the same signs and references, but keeps the structure of isolation intact, since the relation between people remains mediated and does not turn into a direct bond.

Mediation, therefore, stabilizes a false unity that preserves the form of estrangement, converting life into scene and the social bond into the circulation of images. In this structure, exteriority is not limited to the content seen, since it reaches agency itself, insofar as one's own gestures are no longer one's own and come to be presented by an other, which makes the spectacle ubiquitous and causes the subject not to feel at home anywhere Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 33]). This framing makes it possible to make explicit how visibility is organized as a legible hierarchy and as a material condition of permanence.

If the spectacle reunites the separated by making social experience dependent on mediations, the point to be drawn for platforms is that mediation does not operate only as the circulation of images, but as the organization of the field of what can be seen, compared and recognized as valuable. The platform institutes a regime of legibility in which positions are made public through selection, ordering and highlighting, so that differences come to have economic value because they become measurable in displayed hierarchies. Thus, the offer of the IOC does not present itself as the isolated property of an item, but as access to a position whose relevance depends on the way the environment produces visibility and provides parameters of comparison.

The governance of value, in this context, is not exhausted by price or technical availability. It includes controlling which traits count as distinction, which signals will be read as rarity or prestige and which scripts of recognition will be reactivated. The consumer's experience comes to take place within this architecture of exposure, so that enjoyment and validation become intertwined, and permanence in the ecosystem becomes a practical condition for maintaining the social value of the acquired position. From here on, it is important to make explicit how the spectacle operates as an infrastructure of value, converting visibility and comparison into a mechanism of extraction.

The mediation produced by the spectacle is not only a form of isolation, but also a form of social equivalence. Debord condenses this point when he states that the spectacle is the other face of money, as the abstract general equivalent of all commodities, and that in it the p. 284 totality of use has already been exchanged for the totality of abstract representation Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 39- 40]). Thus, what has value comes to have value through a public grammar of recognition and comparison, in which the visible functions as a unit of social measure.

The descriptive gain of this formulation is to separate appearance from ornament. It is not a matter of denying enjoyment, but of recognizing that the circulation of value comes to depend on a public regime of evidence, in which visibility produces the confirmation of what has value. When recognition is organized as a criterion of social measure, it becomes possible for delivery to be sustained by maintaining the conditions of comparison and validation, even if the concrete experience associated with the good is altered from within. Mediation ceases to be a backdrop and comes to function as economic infrastructure.

If the spectacle operates as an abstract social equivalent, the consequence for IOCs is that value depends on conditions of recognition that the platform itself organizes and distributes. The acquired position of enjoyment fully performs its economic function only when it can be perceived, compared and validated in a public circuit, so that social proof becomes part of delivery itself. In this key, reputation, belonging and prestige do not appear as individual psychological effects, but as structural results of an environment that makes hierarchies legible and continuously updated.

The platform governs, therefore, not only access to the item, but the regime of visibility that allows differences to be perceived as relevant differences, signals to circulate as signals of value and comparison to turn into a continuous incentive to permanence. Extraction stabilizes when this regime turns validation into routine, since the consumer comes to depend on the ecosystem to keep the acquired position operative, which shifts permanence from the technical plane to the social and economic plane.

When comparison is organized as an administered hierarchy, visibility takes the form of a socially naturalized system of measurement. Everyday life comes to be governed by continuous progressions and repositionings, in which remaining is not only continuing to use, but staying measurable and recognizable on a scale updated in real time. Gamification, here, operates as a discipline of the present.

Social proof, in this framework, is not a mere adornment of consumption, since the validation of what counts as valuable comes to depend on what becomes visible and incontestable in the environment. Debord condenses this mechanism when he states that the spectacle presents itself as an enormous indisputable and inaccessible positivity and that its implicit rule is that what appears is good and what is good appears, demanding as a basic attitude a passive acceptance sustained by the p. 285 monopoly of appearance307 Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 28]). In economic terms, this means that comparison need not operate through substantive criteria of enjoyment, since it can be organized as competition for administered distinctions, a “false choice” that develops into a “struggle of phantasmagorical qualities” intended to “impassion adherence” and to transfigure into superiority the occupation of “hierarchical places in consumption”308 Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 45]).

The ranking system in League of Legends makes the economy of the visible clearly explicit. It is not only a matter of “competing”, but of existing socially under a regime of legibility in which the player's position is transformed into public evidence of value. The hierarchy from Iron to the top, with its divisions, restrictions and rites of rising and falling, converts enjoyment into verifiable performance and, for that reason, into social proof. In this scene, prestige is not an internal quality of the good, but an effect of the way the platform exposes, orders and makes differences comparable, so that “what appears” presents itself as good without needing to demonstrate it, because it appears.

The institutional language itself describes the path as a continuous climb, with steps and mandatory stops, and makes permanence a discipline. In explaining how ranked play works, the official support formalizes the existence of tiers and divisions as a general structure of progression, indicating that passing from one level to another presupposes completing internal stages and admits the risk of regression309 Riot Games(Riot Games, 2026j). This is not a detail. When value is organized as a position on a displayed scale, the everyday experience of the p. 286 consumer comes to be governed by successive repositionings. The game does not merely “show” performance, it institutes the social measure of performance and makes it the horizon of recognition.

In the highest tiers, this mechanism takes an even more transparent form, because the platform does not merely record the position, it places it under permanent risk and conditions it on routine. The official support describes a daily decay for inactivity and even acknowledges that this leads most players at the top to play practically every day to keep their position Riot Games(Riot Games, 2026g)310. The stability of prestige, here, is not rest, but vigilance. Enjoyment, which could be thought of as leisure, appears as a recurring task, because the visibility of the place occupied only remains positive while it is updated against the threat of falling.

This spectacle, however, is neither spontaneous nor natural. It is governed. The company itself recalibrates the signals of prestige and reconfigures the filters of frustration and reward to keep the circuit “fun and rewarding”, which includes modifying demotions and removing protections that softened the fall between divisions, making the transition more “fluid” Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024f)311. The promise of visible merit does not depend only on playing, but on accepting the frame of measurement that is offered and, when necessary, redesigned. Thus, what Debord calls “indisputable positivity” appears as a produced attribute, because the form of recognition is administered as part of the offer.

The dogmatic anchor of this device is that the displayed position, with its emblems, divisions and places in rankings312, does not constitute stable property of the consumer. It exists p. 287 while the legal bond of access remains operative. In the Terms of Service, the use of the services and of virtual content is granted by a limited and revocable license, conditioned on compliance with the contract, and the termination of the account automatically cancels the license Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e)313. Moreover, the contract itself makes explicit that, upon termination, access to the account and to the associated data and virtual content is lost Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e)314. The result is that rank, honor and reputation behave as attributes of an administered position. They depend on the service, on the registration and on the rules in operation. Visibility, therefore, is not only a language of comparison, it is an economic and legal infrastructure of permanence, because it sustains the very possibility of existing socially in the ecosystem.

In this key, the word “community” tends to operate as a spectacular mask of a harsher phenomenon. It is the formation of an improvised public, mobilized by signs of belonging and by routines of adherence, whose cohesion does not depend on deliberation, but on ritualized repetition. The “community” does not operate as an instance of control over the platform's power; it functions as a showcase of legitimation. Participation in events, campaigns, collective goals and cycles of hype315 produces the sensation of common life, while, in practice, it consolidates an environment of validation that reinforces the private sovereignty of the ecosystem over what has value, over who appears and over who counts.

This mechanism intensifies when the user base itself comes to perform functions of symbolic enforcement. Criticism is isolated not only by formal moderation, but p. 288 by diffuse disciplining, in which users act as agents of validation, policing and exclusion, defending the integrity of the spectacle as if they were defending a public good. The platform does not need to impose everything through direct sanction, because it obtains, through mediation, a kind of voluntary policing. Thus, the defense of the “atmosphere”, of the “community” and of the “game” functions as a defense of the regime of visibility that converts adherence into proof. The result is a circle in which adoration seems spontaneous, but operates as the economic and political infrastructure of the ecosystem, shielding it against substantive contestation and lowering dissent to antisocial noise.

This passage is important because it establishes that the hierarchy does not arise from intrinsic criteria of the good, but from a comparative field produced as evidence. What is disputed is not only the possession of an item, but the occupation of a recognizable place in an order of distinctions that the environment itself makes legible. Comparison, therefore, is not a simple cognitive operation of the consumer, but a mechanism of government, since it distributes relevance, determines which differences count and converts adherence to the circuit into a practical necessity of permanence.

Visibility thus functions as a device of hierarchization and disciplining, converting displayed difference into relevant difference and stabilizing belonging and prestige as requirements of permanence. When this dynamic is updated as a regime without reply316, the very terrain of public verification contracts, since the simple fact of being, from then on, without counterpoint confers a new quality on the false, reduces the true to a hypothesis that can never be demonstrated and makes public opinion disappear as an autonomous force of validation Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 124]).

When visibility and comparison are organized as an administered hierarchy, permanence tends to turn into a practical condition for maintaining value; the next movement shows how platforms turn this regime into routine through cycles of participation, goals and update milestones that convert recognition into a recurring task.

p. 289 Consistent with the reading of Ellulian technique, the conversion of the environment into a legible hierarchy tends to produce a logic of continuous metrification of experience. Practical life ceases to be merely lived and comes to be accompanied by indices of position, signals of performance and parameters of progress that make comparison permanent and cumulative. In this regime, permanence takes the form of a task, because remaining comes to mean staying under ranking, subject to updating and legible within a scale that the ecosystem itself administers. The gamification of everyday life, in this key, is not a playful artifice added to consumption, but the translation of value into scoreboards of relevance, in which what matters is being situated and updated in real time.

The temporality of the spectacle appears, in Debord, as a structural mechanism of government that depends on constant renewal to prevent a solid stabilization of meaning and value. The spectacle presents itself as indisputable and as if it were perpetual, but this claim is only sustained because it is founded on change and must change with its base, so that, for the author, nothing remains inert and this permanent movement constitutes its natural state317 Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 49]). In the later formulation of the integrated spectacle, this logic takes the form of a continuous present produced by the circular and incessant passage of information, which revolves around a few banalities reiterated as discoveries, creating speed without historicity and demanding constant repositioning within what is recognized as relevant Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 124]). In this regime, what gains the status of importance is the instantaneous, because the important tends to be so in the following instant and replaces another instantaneous importance, which produces a paradoxical eternity of successive unimportances, noisy and empty of fixation Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 125]).

From the perspective of IOCs, this temporal design describes why permanence ceases to be an accident of use and becomes part of the economic infrastructure of value, since what has value needs to be continuously updated within the flow. On this basis, it becomes possible to explain how the platform turns the governance of time and visibility into practical urgency, by organizing windows and opportunities as a recurring pressure to decide.

The economic density of this regime becomes explicit when Debord states that “the spectacle is capital to such a degree of accumulation that it becomes image” Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 34]). Visibility and circulation do not appear as superstructure, but as the final form of accumulated value, so that administering the field of what appears, of what is recognized and of p. 290 what becomes comparable functions as the administration of value in the strict sense, with direct effects on permanence, distinction and the renewal of consumption.

Urgency, in this regime, is neither a spontaneous emotion nor a simple subjective reaction to a “promotion”. It is an effect of the governance of time and visibility, that is, of an environment that organizes opportunities as windows and treats relevance as something that expires. The platform produces decision pressure when it makes the preservation of a socially valuable position depend on the now, so that comparison ceases to be a calm evaluation and comes to function as a risk of losing one's place. It is not only a matter of accelerating purchases, but of converting the continuous flow into a discipline of return, because the value of the IOC, being positional and publicly recognized, demands presence to be reiterated as valid and not to be downgraded by the very dynamic of updating of the ecosystem. In this key, urgency directly reinforces the teleology of extraction outlined in the previous item, since repetition and permanence become practical requirements for maintaining value, not merely side effects of consumption. The next movement, still in Debord's vocabulary, shows that this social direction of time does not act merely as an external calendar, but as an active organization of desire and conduct, naturalizing adherence to the circuit as if it were an autonomous choice.

The spectacle, in Debord's vocabulary, does not operate as an external layer of persuasion, but as a social form that offers the very model of the dominant life and, with this, guides conduct by ratifying choices already made at the level of production. It is in this sense that, in all its particular forms, the spectacle constitutes the present model of socially dominant life, functioning as an omnipresent affirmation of the productive arrangement and having consumption as its corollary318 Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 27]). This key prevents treating adherence as an isolated decision before a message. What imposes itself is a regime in which desire and everyday practice are channeled into the circuit, naturalizing the identification between value and commodity and shifting satisfaction to the register of a continuously administered survival.

Debord condenses this active organization of wanting when he states that the spectacle wages a “permanent opium war” to make the identification of goods with commodities and of satisfaction with survival accepted, a survival that must always increase precisely because it “never ceases p. 291 to contain privation”319 Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 38]). This formulation makes it possible to describe the platform as an environment that not only presents offers, but governs rhythms of expectation and patterns of adherence, so that permanence stabilizes as recurring conduct and the continuity of consumption presents itself as a practical condition for keeping the acquired position operative. From there, the argument can describe how this direction of desire turns into displaced trust, since the consumer's belief comes to rest less on a one-off delivery and more on the apparent stability of the ecosystem that validates, in real time, their position and their value.

The trust formed in this regime does not rest primarily on the verification of an isolated delivery, but on the practical belief that the ecosystem will continue to sustain the intelligibility and recognition of the acquired position. Since the enjoyment of the IOC depends on visibility, comparison and validation in a public circuit, the consumer's security tends to shift from the item to the environment that makes it operative, that is, to the stability of the signals, hierarchies and scripts through which that good “has value” socially. The platform thus comes to function as a source of informal institutional trust, because it appears as the guarantor of the continuity of the game of recognition in which reputation, belonging and prestige remain legible and updated. This trust is exploitable by continuous governance, since the same environment that offers confirmation also controls the criteria of relevance and the parameters of repositioning, and can recalibrate value without explicitly breaking the minimum delivery of the item. In this logic, permanence ceases to be merely habit and becomes a defensive strategy, since remaining active and attached to the flow appears as a requirement for preserving the symbolic conditions that make the position of enjoyment socially operative.

The normalizing effect produced by the spectacle does not depend on one-off persuasion, because it operates as a form of evidence that transforms a historical arrangement into an apparently objective environment. Debord describes this mechanism as an “uninterrupted discourse” of the present order about itself, a monologue that, through the “fetishistic appearance of pure objectivity”, hides its character as a relation between men and between classes and makes it seem that “a second nature” dominates the social environment “with its fatal laws”320 Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 31]). p. 292 Naturalization, thus, is not simple belief, but a reorganization of the field of what can be recognized as reality and of what can be treated as contestable, since the order comes to be lived as an environmental given. Moreover, the spectacle operates through circular self-justification, converting its own means into an end321 Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 28]).

With this, it imposes itself as a totalizing environment that covers social experience and tends to perpetuate itself. For IOCs, this matters because visibility and comparison come to organize enjoyment as if they were natural givens, while the criteria of value, prestige and belonging remain defined internally by the ecosystem and perceived as self-evident, not as governance.

If the value of the IOC depends on visibility, comparison and validation, and if this regime presents itself as natural evidence of the environment, the relevant asymmetry cannot be reduced to a deficit of contractual information. The structural point is that the platform controls the parameters by which something appears as relevant, rare, prestigious or “important”, and this control governs value itself, since it determines what can be recognized as delivery and what can be lived as loss without presenting itself as rupture.

Under these conditions, the consumer faces not only opacity about clauses, but a practical limitation of verification, since the correspondence between promise and experience depends on curations, orderings, cuts of visibility and scripts of recognition that can be recalibrated without clear announcement and without ostensible alteration of the item. It is in this sense that it becomes intelligible to speak, further on, of semiotic vulnerability, as a form of exposure to a regime of signs and public validation administered by an opaque, partial and reconfigurable environment, in which apparent continuity can coexist with relevant changes in the conditions that sustain value and belonging.

p. 293 The power of the spectacle, in Debord, consists in organizing the social totality as a field of evidence, in which the parameters of what can be recognized as real, relevant and legitimate are defined Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 28]). For this reason, its efficacy is that of an indirect command, exercised through mediation, because the operation falls on the conditions of visibility, comparison and validation that make experience intelligible and socially operative. In this regime, the lived gives way to a selection of images that imposes itself as sensible evidence and presents itself as the sensible par excellence322 Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 35]). At the same time, this mediation stabilizes models of conduct and identification, through which the subject on stage aligns with a general law of obedience to the course of things323 Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 44]). In the integrated stage, the dynamic intensifies as unilateral management of the flow of images, producing a concrete experience of permanent submission and sustaining generalized adherence to what is given Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 132]). For IOCs, the analytical gain is direct: governing the visible is not peripheral, because controlling what appears, how it appears and which models of identification circulate implies controlling practical conditions of permanence in the ecosystem.

If the spectacle manages to produce value through mediation, comparison and validation, the consequence is that the economic continuity of the IOC can be preserved even when the relevant enjoyment is reconfigured. The platform keeps the acquired position operative when it preserves the circuit of public recognition, that is, when it sustains signals, categories and forms of display capable of ensuring that the good continues to be read as equivalent within the hierarchies of the ecosystem. In this logic, stability does not depend only on what the item allows one to do, but on the way it continues to appear as valuable, commensurable and prestigious before third parties, since it is this regime of visibility that ensures the social function of the p. 294 good and stabilizes acceptance. Restoration becomes especially effective when it preserves the appearance of equivalence and keeps the conditions of comparison intact, even if it alters restrictions, practical effects or the concrete experience associated with the object. The result is that delivery can be maintained as social evidence without this corresponding, in a transparent way, to the preservation of the position of enjoyment that was at stake.

The closing of the argument makes it possible to make explicit why, in immaterial consumption, appearing can fulfill the economic function of delivering without this presenting itself as rupture. Debord formulates this transition by indicating that, after the degradation of being into having, the present phase leads to a generalized sliding of having into appearing, so that all effective having must draw from appearing its immediate prestige and its ultimate function324 Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 29]). In this key, appearance ceases to be a communicational accessory and comes to operate as a practical condition of value, because it is in appearance that the position becomes recognizable and commensurable before third parties. This does not describe a layer of “decoration” superimposed on neutral commodities, since, “as the indispensable adornment of the objects produced today, as the general exposition of the rationality of the system, and as the advanced economic sector that directly shapes a growing multitude of image-objects, the spectacle is the main production of present-day society”325 Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 29]).

For IOCs, this primacy of appearing explains how imagistic restorations and symbolic equivalences can preserve the circuit of validation and comparison even when the relevant enjoyment changes, since maintaining the regime of recognition may suffice to keep positional value operative. It is precisely this point that requires addressing the simulacrum in order to describe how substitution by images of equivalence becomes autonomous and how the platform can sustain value through signs that no longer depend, in a transparent way, on the preservation of the acquired experience.

The contribution of the spectacle to the legal question, at this point, is not to offer a moral critique of the media, but to delimit a specific form of asymmetry of verifiability. When the platform controls the showcase, the ordering, the apparent metrics, the criteria for highlighting and the scripts of recognition, it controls which evidence will be socially valid as proof of value p. 295 and of delivery. The controversy ceases to revolve only around the isolated item and comes to involve the regime of visibility that makes the acquired position comparable and confirmable, because changes in this regime can downgrade, raise or neutralize a promise without producing an unequivocal technical event of rupture. For this reason, the spectacle matters as a material layer of the evidentiary problem, since it describes how the evidence of what has value can be administered and naturalized as if it were an objective given of the environment.

The text does not describe only cosmetic changes; it tampers with the experience of progression and with the way the fall is felt, which means intervening in the affective and social economy of prestige. Among the changes, the post reports that “Demotion protection between Divisions […] will be removed. The transition between Divisions will be fluid” Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024f). The evidence here is valuable because it shows that displayed merit is not “naturally” generated by the game, but administered as a design of rules and visibility, and that the platform can tighten or loosen the risk to keep recognition circulating. ↩
  9. The entry Sistema de Ligas helps to name, in technical language, the logic of ranking as structured scarcity, in which prestige is sustained by displacement. In explaining the top as a limited space, the text records that “every time someone gets enough LP [(League Points)] to move up [...] a summoner will be pushed [...] down.” League of Legends Wiki(League of Legends Wiki, [202-?]l). It thus becomes evident, in terms of functioning, why visibility tends to turn into anxiety and routine, since keeping one's place depends not only on one's own individual performance, but on the continuous competitive pressure that the system formalizes, inside and outside the game. At this point, the mechanics by which position becomes an unstable spectacle are described with precision. ↩
  10. In the Terms of Service, clause 3.1 objectively ties down the dogmatic point of greatest importance for the discussion. Since enjoyment is not possession, it is conditioned use, the contract qualifies access as a “limited license” and subordinates this use to compliance with the Terms, which makes permanence in the ecosystem a legal condition of “having” within it. The passage is crystal clear in stating that the license is “expressly conditioned” on contractual compliance and that, if the account is terminated, the consequence is not symbolic, it is automatic, since “If we terminate your account [...], your license [...] will be automatically canceled.” Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e). This converts the displayed attributes, including those that function as social proof, into effects of a revocable bond, hence dependent on an administered continuity. ↩
  11. Clause 2.3 makes explicit the material dimension of this revocability by describing what happens when the bond is broken. It does not speak of “losing an item” or “losing a good”, but of losing access to the environment itself and to its traces, which helps to understand why reputation, elo, frames and signs of prestige behave as a sustained position, and not as property. The contract states that, once the account is terminated, “you will no longer have access to your account, including the associated data or the Virtual Content (including the Game Currency)” Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e). The wording is important because it includes “associated data” in the same package as virtual content, which shows the infrastructural nature taken on by the object. Thus, what gives meaning to value, that is, the signs, the records and the displayed markers, ceases to exist for the user when the account ceases to exist. ↩
  12. In this research, “hype” designates the collective frenzy and the sudden rise of expectation around an object or event, generally driven by social media and detached from its real value. It is a technique of attention governance used by the platform to produce urgency and to guide the economic conduct of the consumer through practices of hypercuratorial anticipation. ↩
  13. “Generalized secrecy stands behind the spectacle, as the decisive complement of what it shows and, if we go deeper into things, as its most important operation. The simple fact of being from now on without reply has given the false a completely new quality. At the same time it is the true that has ceased to exist almost everywhere or, at best, has been reduced to the state of a hypothesis that can never be demonstrated. The false without reply has ended up making public opinion disappear, which at first found itself unable to make itself heard; then, quickly afterward, even to form. This evidently entails important consequences in politics, in the applied sciences, in justice, in artistic knowledge.” Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 124]). ↩
  14. “What the spectacle presents as perpetual is founded on change, and must change with its base. The spectacle is absolutely dogmatic and, at the same time, cannot lead to any solid dogma. For it nothing stops; this is the state that is natural to it and, nevertheless, the most contrary to its inclination.“ Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 49]). ↩
  15. “The spectacle, understood in its totality, is at the same time the result and the project of the existing mode of production. It is not a supplement to the real world, its added decoration. It is the heart of the unreality of real society. In all its particular forms, information or propaganda, advertising or direct consumption of entertainment, the spectacle constitutes the present model of socially dominant life. It is the omnipresent affirmation of the choice already made in production, and its corollary consumption. Form and content of the spectacle are, identically, the total justification of the conditions and ends of the existing system. The spectacle is also the permanent presence of this justification, as the occupation of the main part of the time lived outside modern production.” Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 27]). ↩
  16. “The spectacle is a permanent opium war to make accepted the identification of goods with commodities; and of satisfaction with survival, increasing according to its own laws. But if consumable survival is something that must always increase, it is because it never ceases to contain privation. If there is no beyond for increased survival, no point where it could cease its growth, it is because it itself is not beyond privation, but is rather privation made richer.” Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 38]). ↩
  17. “The spectacle is the uninterrupted discourse that the present order makes about itself, its laudatory monologue. It is the self-portrait of power in the epoch of its totalitarian management of the conditions of existence. The fetishistic appearance of pure objectivity in spectacular relations hides their character as a relation between men and between classes: a second nature seems to dominate our environment with its fatal laws. But the spectacle is not that necessary product of technical development regarded as a natural development. The society of the spectacle is, on the contrary, the form that chooses its own technical content. If the spectacle, considered under the restricted aspect of the “mass media”, which are its most overwhelming superficial manifestation, may seem to invade society as a mere instrumentation, this is in fact nothing neutral, but the very instrumentation that suits its total self-movement. If the social needs of the epoch in which such techniques develop can find satisfaction only through their mediation, if the administration of this society and all contact between men can no longer be exercised except through the intermediary of this power of instantaneous communication, it is because this “communication” is essentially unilateral; so that its concentration translates into accumulating in the hands of the administration of the existing system the means that allow it to pursue this determined administration. The generalized split of the spectacle is inseparable from the modern State, that is, from the general form of the split in society, product of the division of social labor and organ of class domination.” Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 31]). ↩
  18. “The fundamentally tautological character of the spectacle stems from the simple fact that its means are at the same time its end. It is the sun that never sets on the empire of modern passivity. It covers the entire surface of the world and bathes indefinitely in its own glory.” Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 28]). ↩
  19. “It is the principle of commodity fetishism, the domination of society by ‘suprasensible yet sensible things’ that is absolutely realized in the spectacle, where the sensible world is replaced by a selection of images that exist above it, and which at the same time has made itself recognized as the sensible par excellence.“ Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 35]). ↩
  20. “The agent of the spectacle put on stage as a star is the opposite of the individual, the enemy of the individual, in himself as much as, evidently, in others. Passing into the spectacle as a model of identification, he has renounced every autonomous quality, in order to identify himself with the general law of obedience to the course of things. The consumption star, while being outwardly the representation of different types of personality, shows each of these types as having equal access to the totality of consumption and finding there, in the same way, its happiness. The decision star must possess the complete stock of what has been admitted as human qualities. Thus, among these, the official divergences are annulled by the official resemblance, which is the presupposition of their excellence in everything. Khrushchev had become a general to decide the battle of Kursk, not on the ground, but on its twentieth anniversary, when he found himself master of the State. Kennedy had remained an orator, to the point of pronouncing his eulogy over his own tomb, since Théodore Sorensen continued, at that moment, to write for the successor the speeches in that style which had so contributed to making the personality of the deceased recognized. The admirable people in whom the system is personified are well known for not being what they are; they became great men by descending below the reality of the smallest individual life, and everyone knows it.” Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 44-45]). ↩
  21. “The first phase of the domination of the economy over social life led, in the definition of all human realization, to an evident degradation of being into having. The present phase of the total occupation of social life by the accumulated results of the economy leads to a generalized sliding of having into appearing, from which all effective "having" must draw its immediate prestige and its ultimate function. At the same time, all individual reality has become social, directly dependent on social power, shaped by it. Only in what it is not is it allowed to appear.” Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 29]). ↩
  22. “As the indispensable adornment of the objects produced today, as the general exposition of the rationality of the system, and as the advanced economic sector that directly shapes a growing multitude of image-objects, the spectacle is the main production of present-day society.” Debord(Debord, 2013, [p. 29]). ↩
