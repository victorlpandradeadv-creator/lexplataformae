5.1 On the structuring concepts of the legal regime of the Immaterial Object of Consumption

See this section in the PDF

p. 394

Having set the general frame of the IOC regime, the next step is to present its structuring categories in a dogmatic key, so as to make manageable what has so far been stated as apparatus. The central question, at this point, is not to repeat the map of the regime, but to show how the categories connect to one another and why this connection is what sustains the legal controllability of continuous platform-governed consumption. Without this internal articulation, each category could be read in isolation, as a conceptual label for a specific problem, and would lose the capacity to operate as a unified language for abuses by design, relevant degradations and proportional responses.

The starting point is not the object, but the position of the consumer that this object meets. Semiotic vulnerability names, in a legal key, the structural inequality of intelligibility and agency that defines this position in environments in which value, belonging and recognition are mediated by signs, visibility regimes and internal metrics administered by the supplier itself. Presenting it first is not an arbitrary expository choice, but a recognition that it functions as a key for reading what follows. Without understanding that the consumer inhabits an environment whose intelligibility is partially reserved to the supplier, the definition of the Immaterial Object of Consumption as an enjoyment bundle sustained over time would appear as a mere technical description of the digital good, and not as a category that organizes attribution, proof and restoration under the Consumer Protection Code.

Once the position of the consumer and the object that this position sees have been identified, there follows the description of the unlawful act that may operate between the two. Abusive teleology occupies this place as an unlawful act of architecture, that is, as a mode of attribution that reaches exploitative purposes inscribed in the technical-organizational design of the service, and not only in textual contractual commands. Its formulation depends on the two previous categories. There is no legally capturable abusive teleology p. 395 without the structural asymmetry that it exploits, nor without the enjoyment bundle that it degrades, which is why the test of five cumulative requirements, developed in its own subsection, only becomes applicable when semiotic vulnerability and the IOC have already been dogmatically fixed.

Next, personal integrity comes in to name the legal interests whose erosion constitutes the relevant injury. It is a category with two faces that operate together, and not as separate categories. The functional face protects the performance, use and utility actually delivered. The identity and symbolic face protects belonging, reputation, distinction and the promise of recognition mediated by signs of the environment. The two faces must be considered together because the enjoyment of the IOC is not realized by separating one from the other, and because relevant degradation in continuous consumption usually falls on both at once, as happens when a unilateral reconfiguration preserves technical access but downgrades the consumer's reputational or symbolic position in the ecosystem.

Finally, open auditability and functional value equivalence close the regime as operational criteria without which the whole apparatus would remain merely declaratory. Open auditability requires minimum verifiability about what was promised, about what was changed and about the criteria of visibility, restriction and sanction, without confusing this requirement with total transparency or with indiscriminate disclosure of industrial secrets. Functional value equivalence provides the substantive yardstick to distinguish ordinary variation of digital dynamics from significant degradation of the enjoyment bundle, preventing every update from being treated as an unlawful act and every restoration from being reduced to economic restitution that ignores what was really contracted and lived.

The gearwork formed by these five categories is not a catalog of independent problems nor an inventory of topics related by affinity. It is a dogmatic articulation in which semiotic vulnerability describes the condition, the IOC describes the object, abusive teleology describes the unlawful act, personal integrity describes the protected legal interests and open auditability with functional value equivalence describe the operational criteria of verification and restoration.
