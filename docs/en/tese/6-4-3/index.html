6.4.3 On framing under quality defects, abusive clauses and abusive practices

View this section in the PDF

This section in the layers of the site

  • Dogmatics · Sections of chapter 6

p. 674

Framing abusive teleology within the categories of the CDC requires sufficient precision to avoid two symmetrical errors. The first would be to dilute the exploitative program into atomized episodes, as if each manifestation exhausted its meaning in an isolated occurrence and the architecture of capture disappeared in the sum of small facts. The second would be to turn abusive teleology itself into a generic label, incapable of indicating which door of the system was actually triggered, which legal interest was affected, what minimum evidence is required and what legal response is adequate. The work of this subsection is precisely to escape these two deformations. Structural exploitation needs to be preserved as a legally intelligible unit, but this unit only gains real usefulness when brought back to the dogmatic paths already available in the consumer-law microsystem.

The first axis of framing is the quality defect, whenever the service's operating program produces a relevant loss of adequacy, continuity or functional equivalence of enjoyment. With IOCs, the question is not resolved by asking whether the object p. 675 is still formally accessible, whether the item is still listed in the account or whether the performance remains nominally in force. The criterion is functional. What matters is whether the service, as governed, remains capable of meeting the ends the consumer legitimately expects or has come to frustrate them through a relevant downgrading of utility, performance, ecosystemic role or integrity of the experience. The CDC already offers this door without the need for a parallel category, because the quality defect derives precisely from the absence of properties or characteristics that allow the performance to achieve the reasonably expected ends, including when the nonconformity results from the breach of accessory duties capable of impairing, even partially, the utility of the service Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 523-524). The same logic is reinforced in the classical doctrine of the Code, which treats unfitness, reduction in value and inadequacy to legitimate expectation as material forms of defect, including when the frustration manifests itself through a shorter useful life, downgraded performance or impaired utility Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 348; p. 369)764.

This framing as a quality defect requires a minimally objective evidentiary standard. A diffuse impression that the experience “got worse” is not enough. The before and after must be reconstructed through version records, documentation of the offer, interface evidence, a history of relevant alterations and a demonstration of impact on the core of enjoyment. It is at this point that functional value equivalence operates as a transversal yardstick. When the object survives only in appearance, but ceases to perform, at a substantially equivalent level, the function that justified the consumer's acquisition and continued engagement, the inadequacy can already be legally recognized. Here the system does not protect the nominal survival of the performance. It protects its material capacity to satisfy the useful interest legitimately incorporated into the relationship.

There are, however, cases in which abusive teleology goes beyond functional inadequacy and approaches the safety defect of the service. This occurs when the governance program not only downgrades enjoyment, but produces relevant risk or actual harm to legal interests of a personal dimension, especially when the combination of opaque sanctions, severe reputational exclusions, erasure of history, permanent threat of loss and practical impossibility of p. 676 challenge affects personal integrity, biographical continuity or symbolic identity. The distinction is important because the CDC already separates, in its architecture, the protection of safety and the protection of adequacy. If the core of the problem lies in downgraded enjoyment, the main lens will be that of the quality defect. If it lies in the production of intolerable risk or harm in a personal dimension, the case shifts to the logic of the safety defect. The very structure of the system prevents treating all forms of abusive governance as if they were homogeneous phenomena, and this differentiation is one of the advantages of its technical use Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 134-135; p. 521; p. 273)765.

The second axis of framing falls on abusive practices, and it is there that abusive teleology appears most forcefully as a repeated method of exploitation. When artificial scarcity, manufactured urgency, cycles of promise, manufacture of risk, asymmetric frictions and hindered reversibility operate predictably and repeatedly to exploit semiotic vulnerability and govern the consumer's choice, the problem no longer lies only in the final quality of the performance, but in the very way the supplier organizes the relationship. The CDC does not require literal falsity to react to this kind of mechanism. It is enough that the conduct, seen in its pattern of functioning, produces irregular conditions of negotiation and relevantly affects good faith, loyalty and balance, even if the service remains formally available and the exploitation presents itself with a varnish of technical neutrality Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 469; p. 627; p. 612)766. The point of attribution, then, is the pattern, not the isolated event.

Therefore, proof of the abusive practice cannot become a prisoner of the micro-event. It requires a combination of evidence of flow, documentation of campaigns, ecosystem rules, screenshots or recordings of the decision path, as well as demonstration that the essential was systematically obscured or subordinated to architectural pressure. The case does not need to show that a single consumer was “psychologically defeated” by the system. It needs p. 677 to show that the environment was designed to compress deliberation, hinder exit, increase tolerance to loss and induce permanence in a context of structural asymmetry. When this pattern proves stable and replicable, the abusive practice ceases to be an impressionistic adjective and becomes an effective dogmatic door of the CDC.

The third axis is that of abusive clauses, whenever the exploitative program gains contractual institutionalization in predisposed terms, policies and acceptances. Here abusive teleology manifests itself not only in the flow or in the interface, but in the very private normative engineering that the supplier builds to reserve to itself broad powers of alteration, exclusion, discontinuation, reconfiguration and sanction without sufficient auditability, without real reciprocity and without intelligible challenge mechanisms. Contractual abusiveness, under the CDC regime, is not reduced to extravagant or openly caricatural clauses. It is also recognized when the drafting party structurally improves its contractual position, transfers risks, unilaterally broadens its power of control and breaks the minimum reciprocity that must sustain the consumer relationship Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 1045)767. If terms and policies serve as a basis for arbitrary mutations or for strategic opacities intended to preserve the supplier's superiority, they cease to be a simple contractual frame and come to function as the legal institutionalization of the exploitative program.

This control becomes even more evident when the contract reserves to the supplier excessive unilateral prerogatives to define, alter, readjust, cancel or reinterpret the very reality of enjoyment. The unilateral conception of the adhesion relationship favors, by its very structure, clauses that break material balance and weaken the consumer's contractual position. The CDC responds to this not with contractual sentimentalism, but with objective nullity of excessive unilateral advantages, of the lack of minimum transparency and of the arbitrariness of one party over the other Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 161; p. 1319-1320; p. 1242)768. The p. 678 same perception appears in the classical reading of the system, which identifies abusive clauses as mechanisms of undue broadening of the supplier's power, assessable by the objective harm suffered by the consumer and by the material imbalance produced by the predisposed provision Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 581; p. 613; p. 643)769. When the exploitative program gains a contractual badge, the CDC does not retreat. It only changes the entrance door.

These three doors do not function as watertight compartments. The same case may simultaneously reveal a quality defect, an abusive practice and an abusive clause, and in borderline cases may also approach the logic of the safety defect. The decision matrix, therefore, does not serve to parcel out the problem artificially, but to guide its treatment without losing sight of the functional unity of the program. In each case, what matters is identifying which mechanism predominates, which legal interest was affected, which door of the system offers the main fit, what minimum evidence is required and which remedy is most adequate. What cannot be done is to let the plurality of manifestations destroy the intelligibility of structural exploitation. When the pattern is repeated, legal classification must preserve the program as a relevant unit, and not pretend that everything comes down to small, disconnected deviations.

It is precisely at this point that open auditability and functional value equivalence operate as transversal filters of the framing. The absence of auditability does not create a fourth autonomous door, but structurally reinforces any of the others. When the platform does not preserve minimum trails, does not offer verifiable reasons, concentrates evidence and prevents reconstruction of what it altered or why it imposed a sanction, this opacity intensifies attribution for abusive practice, aggravates the reading of the predisposed clause that shields arbitrariness and weakens the defense that there was merely a legitimate adaptation of the performance. Likewise, the breach of functional equivalence acts as a strong signal of relevant inadequacy, guiding the framing as a quality defect in particular. In both cases, the preservation and display of records, the redistribution of the burden of proof and the institutional reconstruction p. 679 of the supply cease to be procedural accessories and become part of the typical response to the unlawful act.

This response cannot be purely declaratory. The dogmatic fit must lead to a practicable consequence, because the CDC was not made to recognize exploitation and then regard it with a grave expression. When the problem lies in the downgrading of enjoyment, priority tends to fall on the functional restoration of the lost utility. When it lies in contractual shielding engineering, the consequence must reach the very clause or policy that institutionalizes the imbalance. When the case reveals a repeated pattern of architectural exploitation, the system also calls for procedural correction and injunctive relief, so as to interrupt the reproduction of the program. Specific relief, in this context, is not a decorative procedural appendix, but a central instrument for materially restoring the legality of the supply, capable of ordering the cessation of practices, the exclusion of abusive clauses, the fulfillment of offers and an effective change in the supplier's behavior Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 473; p. 828; p. 678)770.

The advantage of this framing is its sobriety. It does not require leaving the CDC or inventing a parallel law of platforms. Quality defect, safety defect, abusive practice and abusive clause remain the doors of the system. What changes is how the object on which they bear is read. Instead of being limited to the stable thing, the isolated clause or the one-off event, they come to reach the regime of enjoyment that the platform continuously organizes. This is how legal control ceases to aim only at the episode and comes to reach the pattern of governance that produces, reinforces or normalizes the harm. Abusive teleology, then, does not compete with the CDC. It merely makes visible the type of exploitative program that the CDC itself is already technically able to contain.

Notes

  1. The relevant downgrading of enjoyment can be classified under the CDC without need for a parallel category, because consumer-law dogmatics itself already treats the quality defect and the inadequacy of the performance in a material key. The supplier is liable for quality defects that render services unfit for consumption, diminish their value or result from disparity with the indications of the offer, services being considered unfit when inadequate for the purposes reasonably expected of them Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 348). This structure is reinforced when the useful life of the good is treated as an internal element of quality, so that the sale of a durable good with a useful life shorter than that legitimately expected constitutes a defect of adequacy, breach of the duty to inform and non-realization of the very object of the contract Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 369). Frustration of the useful interest and impairment of utility already constitute entry doors offered by the Code itself. ↩
  2. The architecture of the CDC already distinguishes the different dogmatic doors of control, which prevents treating all problems of enjoyment under a single generic label. Liability for the product-caused or service-caused accident protects the consumer's safety, while liability for the quality defect protects the purpose sought in the performance of the contract; in the first case, there is breach of a duty of safety, in the second, of a duty of adequacy Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 134-135; p. 521). Alongside this, good faith projects protection against the supplier's abusive conduct, manifested in the prohibition of abusive advertising, abusive practices and abusive clauses, all marked by the exploitation of a dominant position over vulnerability Miragem(Miragem, 2020, p. 134-135; p. 273). If the core of the problem lies in downgraded enjoyment, one looks to quality defect and inadequacy; if it lies in the exploitation of vulnerability by conduct or stipulation, one looks to abusive practices and clauses. ↩
  3. Abusive practices, in the CDC, function as a category able to classify mechanisms of exploitation incompatible with good faith, loyalty and balance, even when the conduct does not present itself as a frontal lie. These are “irregular conditions of negotiation” capable of wounding the foundations of the legal order, often presenting a “high dose of economic immorality and oppression” Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 469). Article 51, IV, simultaneously incorporates the principles of objective good faith and economic balance, prohibiting clauses that impose exaggerated disadvantage Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 627). The list of Article 51 functions as an open concept able to classify abusiveness that offends the balance between the parties Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 612). Abusive teleology does not compete with the CDC; it allows itself to be classified through the system's internal doors intended to repress mechanisms of exploitation incompatible with the material protection of enjoyment. ↩
  4. Under the CDC regime, abusive clauses are instruments of structural imbalance incompatible with objective good faith and with the minimum reciprocity required in consumer relations. Their legal relevance lies in the fact that they can institutionalize, in predisposed terms and policies, a contractual program oriented to the transfer of risks and to the unilateral expansion of powers of control over the bond. Abusive clauses share the aim of improving the contractual situation of whoever holds the preponderant position and the effect of unbalancing the contract through lack of reciprocity Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 1045). It matters little whether the predisposing party presents itself as technically neutral: the system represses the contractual result that breaks commutativity and compromises the balance of the bond to the detriment of the consumer. Terms and acceptance mechanisms can be read as the institutionalization of abusiveness whenever they serve as a basis for arbitrary mutations or strategic opacities intended to preserve the supplier's power. ↩
  5. The control of excessive unilateral prerogatives is one of the cores of the CDC's logic at the contractual level, especially when the supplier reserves for itself exclusive powers to define, alter or readjust the performance. The unilateral conception and drafting of the contract invites clauses that privilege the unilaterality of the rights secured, breaking the balance and weakening the consumer's contractual position Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 161). Clauses that grant the supplier unilateral advantages such as varying the price or cancelling the bond without the same right being secured to the consumer are null, the nullity arising from the very unilaterality of the advantage granted Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 1319-1320). Abusiveness rests on objective factors such as unilaterality, lack of minimum transparency and the arbitrariness of one party over the other Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 1242). An adhesion contract is not a license for the supplier to exercise absolute discretionary power over the reality of enjoyment. ↩
  6. Abusive clauses function, in the CDC, as a legal mechanism for containing negotiating arrangements that unduly expand the supplier's unilateral power and degrade the consumer's material position. Vulnerability shows itself clearly in the contractual field, where adhesion contracts predominate whose clauses invariably seek to safeguard only the economic interests of the business Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 581). Abusiveness can be assessed by an objective approach, centered on the “serious harm objectively suffered by the consumer” and on the “imbalance resulting from the imposed clause” Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 613). Provisions that leave the supplier the option of concluding the contract or not while binding the consumer are null, prohibiting a situation of manifest contractual imbalance Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 643). If the exploitative program has earned a contractual badge, the entry door for confronting it remains the CDC. ↩
  7. Specific relief, in the CDC, is not a mere procedural accessory, but an instrument for effectively restoring the supply regime, able to correct the unlawful act and prevent the continued reproduction of the abusive practice. The judge may, on the basis of Article 84, order the abstention from or performance of conduct under a coercive injunction, precisely because the market needs to be cleaned up in favor of the consumer and of competition Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 473). Collective protection may require the cessation of abusive advertising, the exclusion of an abusive clause from a standard contract and the fulfillment of an offer Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 828). Once the claim is upheld, the supplier is bound to modify the challenged behavior Bessa(Benjamin; Marques; Bessa, 2021, p. 678). The system was made to order a halt, to correct and to restore materially the legality of the performance. ↩
