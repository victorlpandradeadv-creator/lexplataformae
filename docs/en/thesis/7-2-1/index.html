7.2.1 On IOCs of cognitive functionality and the information regime

See this section in the PDF

This section in the layers of the site

  • Platforms · Platform family

p. 757

The incidence of the regime of the Immaterial Object of Consumption on services of cognitive functionality is not defined by the label of “artificial intelligence”, “assistant”, “chat” or “productivity tool”, but by the governance structure that modulates, over time, the utility delivered and the possibility of reconstructing promise, change and functional impact through accessible evidence.

In this family, provision is not exhausted in the one-off issuing of a response, in the isolated display of content or in the supply of a singly considered piece of data. What is offered is an environment of cognitive orientation. It is a functional infrastructure that organizes how the user understands the problem submitted, trusts the response received, incorporates it into their decision-making, reviews it, corrects it or reassesses it in the face of error, oscillation or change in performance. The legally relevant core of the subtype therefore lies in the informational integrity of the enjoyment regime, in the epistemic trust that sustains the functional use of the service and in the institutional possibility of reconstructing the link between promise, delivery, change and impact.

This configuration rules out, from the outset, two inadequate reductions. The first is to treat the conflict as a mere discussion about an isolated factual error, as if the utility of the service came down to the abstract correctness of each individual response. The second is to reduce the analysis to the generic contractual vocabulary of software licensing or of the technical provision of informational support. Neither reading reaches the central problem.

p. 758 In services of this nature, the utility consumed consists precisely in offering practical orientation in conversational, synthetic, contextual or responsive form, so that the user's experience comes to depend on an environment whose real intelligibility, operational limits and patterns of change are not mastered by them. What is at stake is not only the content issued, but the regime of functional trust in which this content is received, used and converted into conduct.

Enjoyment, here, must be read as governed cognitive enjoyment. The user does not consume information in raw form alone. They consume a private arrangement of selection, formulation, prioritization, synthesis, interface language and presentation of answers that organizes the very experience of useful knowledge. This governance is especially sensitive because it operates on an object whose usefulness depends on practical trust. The service is valuable not only because it answers, but because it induces the user to treat the answer as a relevant point of orientation.

Whenever this environment is structurally opaque as to the operating criteria that condition error, instability, changes in performance, contextual limitation or later reconstruction of the interaction, a typical field of incidence of the IOC arises. The central vulnerability, here, is not mere technical ignorance on the user's part. It is semiotic vulnerability intensified by epistemic dependence, that is, by subjection to an environment that administers the signs of the answer's reliability, usefulness and sufficiency without granting the recipient, to the same extent, means of verification and audit.

A reading of the official terms reinforces precisely this nature of governed service. OpenAI's Terms of Use state that they apply to individuals' use of ChatGPT and of the company's other services, together with associated applications, software and websites, and make this use subject to compliance with supplementary policies, documentation and guidelines, which shows a continuous provision organized by private rules for the operation and modulation of the environment OPENAI(OpenAI, 2026).

In the case of Gemini, Google's own official page of services and specific terms indicates that the Gemini Apps are subject to Google's general Terms of Service and to the Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy, which likewise reveals an environment of continuous supply governed by its own normative layer, and not a simple instantaneous delivery of neutral content GOOGLE(Google, [202-?]). In both cases, the contractual frame does not describe mere access to a static tool. It describes entry into an ecosystem of regulated use, subject to conditioning, limitation and reorganization by private governance.

This point matters dogmatically because it allows the subtype to be brought within the IOC regime without relying on fragile analogies. The cognitive utility supplied does not present itself p. 759 as an autonomous thing, fully inspectable outside the environment. It depends on the continuity of the system, the relative stability of the interface, the maintenance of the conditions of access, the model for presenting answers, the warnings, the contextual limits, the memory or history mechanisms where they exist, and the private rules that structure the very field of use. In other words, enjoyment lies not only in the individual answer, but in the functional arrangement that makes it possible to trust, use, review and contest the provision. In this light, the legal problem ceases to be the abstract quality of the information and becomes the controllability of the informational regime as a form of supply.

Relevant inadequacy may, in this subtype, take forms of its own. There may be informational noise when the architecture of the answer projects an appearance of security, completeness or sufficiency incompatible with the real limits of the provision. There may be functional opacity when the user cannot reconstruct, in minimally verifiable terms, why a given output was presented to them, to what extent the system's behavior changed in a relevant way, or which elements of the environment conditioned the answer that guided their conduct. There may also be acute evidentiary asymmetry when the functional impact of the provision depends on history, interactional context, internal parameters or records that remain concentrated in the supplier's hands. In these scenarios, the injury need not appear as a direct lie to be legally relevant. It is enough that the environment replaces legibility with a performance of trust, shifting onto the user the cost of bearing error, review, fluctuation or functional loss without adequate institutional trails for reconstruction.

The consequence is that, in this family, open auditability plays an even more central role than in other subtypes. The point is not to require unrestricted opening of the model, the code or the internal architecture. It is to recognize that, in services whose usefulness consists in guiding the user cognitively, there must be minimum institutional verifiability over relevant aspects of the provision. Depending on the case, this includes preservation of accessible history, minimum traces of interaction, sufficient disclosure of materially relevant limits, intelligibility about changes in the regime of use and the possibility of reconstructing the link between the functional promise of the service and the impact produced on the user's experience. Without this, the platform retains not only the power to provide guidance, but also the power to define unilaterally what may be narrated and proven when that guidance turns out to be inadequate, inconsistent or harmful.

The evidentiary standard compatible with this subtype therefore cannot naively reproduce the logic of the isolated event. The relevant evidence tends to require reconstruction of the informational journey, the context of use, the available interactional history, the p. 760 warnings present or absent, the form in which the answer was presented and the practical function it performed in the user's decision. It also tends to require caution regarding recurrent blind spots. The first is the tendency to reduce the conflict to the sentence produced, ignoring the semiotic architecture that made it reliable or operational. The second is the tendency to presume that generic warnings about the system's limitations would suffice, by themselves, to neutralize any problem of informational adequacy. The third is the tendency to treat the model's variability as a raw technical fact, and not as a governed element whose opacity must be legally bearable.

The possible dogmatic consequence, in this type of environment, usually revolves around a defect in the informational quality of the supply, abusive practice through functional opacity, insufficient compliance with the duty to inform and the strengthening of verifiability duties compatible with the cognitive enjoyment promised. In more serious cases, especially when the architecture of the service repeatedly administers practical trust without proportionate instruments of reconstruction and contestation, there may be room for a reading in terms of abusive teleology, particularly if the opacity is not a mere technical residue but a stable part of the way the environment is exploited. The most adequate institutional response will tend to favor procedural correction, the strengthening of verifiable trails, review of the communicational arrangement and functional restoration of the injured trust, rather than mere abstract monetization of the conflict.

The subtype thus clearly shows the expansive usefulness of the IOC regime. Even outside the original laboratory in which the thesis was developed, a structure of supply remains recognizable in which utility, trust, opacity and evidence are administered by the supplier in continuous and governed form. What changes is the matter on which this governance bears. Here, it falls on cognitive orientation and on the very functional intelligibility of the provision. Precisely for this reason, enjoyment cannot be treated as the simple reception of content, but as a governed informational experience, dependent on private operating criteria that must be brought back under auditability, adequacy and institutional controllability.
