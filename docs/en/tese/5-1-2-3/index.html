5.1.2.3 On the recognition criteria of the IOC and the limits of application of the category

p. 427

Controllability presupposes an elementary discipline of method, which requires identifying which examination is being applied and separating planes that cannot be treated as synonyms. Ávila warns that confusing distinct concrete examinations makes the correct application of law unfeasible, because each one mobilizes its own relations and parameters and may lead to different results Ávila(Ávila, 2018, p. 223). In the regime of the IOC, this warning prevents heterogeneous discussions from being compressed into generic labels such as “service failure”, “policy change” or “moderation”, without clarification of what is being tested and what needs to be demonstrated. The category only remains operable when it forces the separation of the qualification of the object, the identification of the promised enjoyment bundle, the recognition of relevant degradation, the attribution of responsibility and the criteria of restoration, preventing these planes from being confused and generating unstable conclusions.

The institutional usefulness of this separation lies in exposing the structure of reasoning and allowing rational control of the way in which other norms are applied. Ávila observes that, in the application of law, a reasoning aimed at the incidence of other norms of the legal order is always carried out, and that these examinations investigate the way in which such norms should be applied438 Ávila(Ávila, 2018, p. 225). With this, the role of methodological categories as instruments of verifiability is established. In terms of the IOC, each criterion must correspond to an identifiable test, with recognizable parameters and controllable results, so as to allow assessing whether the decision followed a justifiable path or merely covered a discretionary choice with technical vocabulary.

p. 428 The IOC only fulfills a dogmatic function when it operates as a filter and as a roadmap. As a filter, it defines what enters and what remains outside the field of incidence, preventing any digital relationship with an appearance of continuity from being absorbed by the category and also preventing the supplier from expelling the conflict from the CDC through strategic labeling. As a roadmap, it forces qualification to follow a controllable sequence, making explicit what needs to be demonstrated for the language of the IOC to make sense and for the discussion not to dissolve into vague impressions about “experience”, “community” or “moderation”.

The first field covered by the criteria is the promised object, understood as an enjoyment bundle. The relevant question is not whether some “content” was available at some point, but what utility was economically offered in terms of functionalities and significations that, in the regime of the IOC, constitute what was acquired. This requires identifying the core of the bundle from the relevant promise and from the elements that, in the concrete arrangement, make that utility exist as an enjoyable thing. The criterion is not satisfied by ornamental lists of functionalities or by generic advertising slogans. It requires identifying which central functions organize the legitimate expectation of enjoyment and, consequently, delimiting which alterations may reach the core of the object even when the formal continuity of access is preserved.

The second field is the position of sustained enjoyment. In continuous consumption, the acquisition inaugurates a position in the ecosystem and not a delivery exhausted in an instant. This position is the mode of existence of the object over time, since the effective possibility of use, circulation and permanence in the promised form depends on it. Recognizing it means treating as legally relevant the elements that define the consumer's permanence in the regime of enjoyment, without converting mere ordinary discomforts into legal losses. The incidence of the legal regime of the IOC presupposes that utility is enjoyed as a continued bond and that the execution of supply is inseparable from the maintenance of that position.

The third field is infrastructural governance as the mode of execution of supply. The IOC does not merely describe an environment around the object. Infrastructure is incorporated as a constitutive dimension of delivery, because it is through it that internal rules, curation, modulations of visibility, parameters of participation and sanction mechanisms are implemented. Qualification requires demonstrating that enjoyment depends on a technical-organizational arrangement that continuously administers conditions relevant to the promised utility. The filter here is decisive, since the presence of technical mediation in a trivial sense is not enough. What matters is the existence of internal criteria and executive mechanisms that structurally shape the experience of enjoyment and distribute consequences to the consumer.

p. 429 The fourth field is the typical possibility of relevant degradation without total rupture. The IOC is only justified if it can see losses that manifest themselves as persistent erosion of the enjoyment bundle, even if nominal access is preserved. The qualification roadmap must require the demonstration of a pattern of degradation that is not confused with ordinary variations, one-off instabilities or neutral interface adjustments. It is a matter of delimiting the zone in which enjoyment continues to exist formally, but is lowered, emptied or reconfigured so as to frustrate the core of offered utility or to reorder the consumer's position in the ecosystem in terms incompatible with the promise that justified the acquisition.

With these fields, the IOC ceases to be an explanatory label and comes to function as an instrument for screening and disciplining the debate. Incidence is decided not by technological intuition or by automatic deference to the contract, but by the cumulative presence of verifiable elements of the regime, which makes it possible to stabilize a path of qualification.

The construction of incidence criteria is neither a classificatory luxury nor an attempt to invent new law for the digital. It responds to the need to make recurring conflicts decidable in a stable way, without depending on variable intuitions about technology and without resorting to generic labels that erase the object. The filter of the IOC cannot ignore the structural traits of platform-governed consumption nor, at the opposite extreme, aim to capture each and every discomfort of digital use. Incidence criteria cannot depend on full access to the algorithm, logs and internal rules as a condition of possibility, since that would turn the IOC into an impracticable category, reserved for exceptional cases.

Practicability, however, requires dogmatic rigor, and not relaxation. Under two simultaneous pressures, the binding to norms and the need to decide, legal discourse demands specialized technique and the production of doubts oriented to action Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 47]). Under this horizon, already established earlier as a functional cut aimed at the decidability of conflicts, the institutional gain is to stabilize expectations counterfactually, since norms express expectations whose duration is maintained regardless of the fulfillment or non-fulfillment of the empirically expected action Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 104]). For the IOC, the criteria must operate with possible proof and controllable inferences, capable of stabilizing expectations about the core of the enjoyment bundle even when the ecosystem tries to naturalize opacity and reconfigurability as if they were indisputable facts.

This requirement of possible proof separates the inaccessible as a technical detail from the demonstrable as a structured effect of supply. What matters for incidence is not proving “how” the algorithm decides at a microscopic level, but demonstrating that there is infrastructural governance relevant to the acquired utility and that it operates with stability p. 430 sufficient to produce patterns of enjoyment and of relevant degradation without total rupture of access. With this, opacity ceases to function as a condition of unenforceability.

Recognizing infrastructural governance as a constitutive element of the IOC requires admitting that, in certain ecosystems, an effective system of rules and sanctions operates that bears on a delimited group and organizes the very possibility of enjoyment. Along the lines of Grau (2008), this institutional datum can be described as practical normativity, that is, a way of guiding conduct and imposing conformity that produces concrete effects without depending on state status. This reading does not attribute to the platform the condition of a State nor presume unlawfulness. It serves, rather, to qualify effective normativity as a component of supply and, therefore, as a relevant element for assessing the mode of execution of the promise to the consumer, especially when rules, architecture and enforcement mechanisms come to define, in practice, the continuity, integrity and meaning of what was acquired.

The same idea is expressed when it is stated that the State is not the only social organization capable of positing law, that is, of producing posited law, and that it is necessary to break with the conception according to which law is necessarily a creature of the State Grau(Grau, 2008, p. 78). In platform-governed consumption, utility is not realized despite internal rules, curation, modulations of circulation and sanction, but through these mechanisms. This allows treating governance as a component of the legally relevant fact, without converting every form of governance into abuse.

Nor should this normativity be naturalized as “spontaneous order”. Grau makes explicit that the expression “legal order of the market” indicates that the market posits its legal order, not the order that it is, but the order that it establishes Grau(Grau, 2008, p. 324). With this, governance ceases to be presented as a technical fatality and comes to be treated as an instituted arrangement. Moreover, the reading avoids an unproductive sociologization by recognizing that law is not a simple external representation of social reality, but a functional level of the social whole and a constitutive element of the mode of social production Grau(Grau, 2008, p. 19-20). In the regime of the IOC, this authorizes reading material and organizational layers as constitutive of the acquired utility, without shifting the argument to a broad sociological diagnosis and without presuming unlawfulness.

The mere existence of moderation, rankings, community rules or interface adjustments is not enough to trigger the operator of the IOC. Ordinary updates, regular curation, anti-abuse interventions and changes that do not reach the functional core of the enjoyment bundle remain in the normal field of execution and do not constitute relevant degradation by definition. What matters is the structural dependence of utility on a continuous executive p. 431 governance, in intensity and centrality sufficient for nominal access not to exhaust delivery and for the experience to be degradable without total rupture.

The cut is also not sustainable if “signification” enters as interpretive cosmetics. On platforms, part of the value offered may be anchored in regimes of meaning that organize conduct, expectation and recognition. Umberto Eco439 (1997) provides the technical key for treating sign and code as operative dimensions of experience. By indicating that connotation, because it is shared, is “translated into a system of expectations” and “will be foreseen by a connotative lexicon”440Eco(Eco, 1997, p. 29), it is established that the sign coordinates practical predictions. This matters when the promised utility depends on visibility, reputation, status, ranking, belonging and forms of recognition administered by the ecosystem. In these cases, signification is part of the enjoyment bundle as a condition of use.

This point gains density when the code is understood as a structure of acceptability and intelligibility. Eco maintains that the acceptability of an expression depends on the code and that it would be an exaggerated reduction to state that the code fixes only content Eco(Eco, 2000, p. 145). In platform-governed enjoyment, this key describes how grammars of interaction and regimes of classification modulate valid participation, legitimate visibility and admissible circulation. Thus, the code structure operates as a condition of intelligibility of use and, in certain regimes, as a controllable dimension of the object.

The third piece is the reading of signs as forces with social effects. Eco observes that a theory of codes is mainly interested in signs as social forces Eco(Eco, 2000, p. 109), which makes it possible to recognize relevant losses that manifest themselves as a persistent lowering of recognition, reach or belonging when these elements were offered as part of the value. At the limit, Eco maintains that subjects are shaped by the p. 432 possibilities that the form of the world produced by signs allows them to occupy Eco(Eco, 1991, p. 62). The argument reinforces that the environment administers subject positions and conditions of participation, which can be sold as utility and, when they are, can be part of the enjoyment bundle.

The consequence is to circumscribe when the semiotic dimension enters and when it should stay out. It is part of the bundle when it makes up the core of utility economically offered and enjoyed as a position in the ecosystem, that is, when the platform sells, explicitly or functionally, effects of recognition, visibility, circulation, reputation, belonging or status as part of the value of what was acquired. The dimension is not part of it when it is a matter of aesthetics, marketing, generic advertising narrative or diffuse satisfaction without an identifiable functional core and without a structural link to the position of sustained enjoyment. The intention is to avoid two abuses: seeing semiotics in everything and dissolving the filter; or always treating it as peripheral and blinding the concept to regimes in which the object is a socially operative position sustained by rules of visibility and sanction.

Such a filter needs, then, a connection with the dogmatic basis of consumption, without requiring, for that purpose, the inauguration of a new microsystem. Cláudia Lima Marques describes the contemporary shift toward massified, adhesion-based and long-term contractual forms, marked by symbiosis between products and services and by technological intermediation on consumer platforms Marques(Marques, 2024, [p. 2]). Her doctrine makes it possible to recognize that the form of performance depends on continuous technical mediations, which reinforces the need for incidence criteria capable of separating ordinary variations from relevant degradations of the bundle. The author also highlights that vulnerabilities and harms may arise from the design and the conduct of the intermediary, who operates as a gatekeeper and influences the time, manner and outcome of the bond, including through data manipulation Marques(Marques, 2024, [p. 3]). This fit reinforces the thesis of structural vulnerability and the need for criteria that do not depend on total transparency of the black box.

The consumerist anchoring also resolves the risk of absolutizing design. When it is recognized that it is possible to sanction cases of deceptive design and the provisions of Article 39 of the CDC441 are linked to dark patterns, design is put back in its correct place, as an economically oriented and legally qualifiable commercial practice, and not as aesthetic neutrality Bergstein(Marques; Mendes; Bergstein, 2023, [p. 4]). In the same sense, the statement that, in the digital society, vulnerability may assume an architectural character, and that such p. 433 vulnerabilities are a product of the digital consumer markets themselves, reinforces that the architecture of the environment is part of the economic mechanism of supply Mucelin(Marques; Mucelin, 2022, p. 18). This does not presume abuse, but justifies the need for an operator that identifies when architecture appears as a constitutive condition of the acquired utility.

Here, Rizzatto Nunes offers dogmatic support for essential locks of the filter. Technical vulnerability appears as structural asymmetry, since the consumer is the weak link of the relationship, especially because they do not have access to the information that makes up the productive process, so that their choice is already born reduced Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 122]). Presentation and visual impact also enter as legally relevant conduct, insofar as suppliers may resort to visual impact and catchphrases to conceal relevant aspects of the offer Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 370]). Moreover, in interpreting Article 30442, the author recognizes that the offer may arise from the mere presentation of the product in the shop window Nunes(Nunes, 2018, [p. 312]), which supports reading the digital shop window as a binding offer. With this, the IOC remains within the CDC, without demonizing the interface and without allowing the interface to be treated as irrelevant.

Thus, the incidence criteria of the IOC can be formulated as a qualification roadmap in a controllable sequence. The first element is economic-functional. An identifiable core of utility is required, promised and enjoyed as acquired, reconstructible from the relevant offer and from the effective mode of enjoyment. The second element is structural. Utility is realized as a position of enjoyment sustained over time, and not as an exhausted event. The third element is infrastructural. Utility depends on governed conditions, such as access, permanence, visibility, circulation and sanction, administered by continuous execution. The fourth element is one of legal relevance. There is a typical possibility of relevant degradation of the bundle without total rupture of access, in a persistent and substantive way, and not as a one-off discomfort.

With the incidence criteria outlined, the limits of exclusion fulfill an equally institutional function. They prevent the IOC from becoming a language “of atmosphere” for digital disputes and ensure that the concept operates as an instrument of demarcation with precision and legitimacy.

The first limit is one-off access without a position of sustained enjoyment. When performance is episodic and exhausted in the act, there is no reason to mobilize an operator built p. 434 to describe continuity and administration of permanence, since the classic dogmatics of service is sufficient to resolve the dispute.

The second limit is the hypothesis in which the platform functions as a channel or shop window, but does not govern, in structural terms, the acquired utility, lacking the infrastructural centrality characteristic of the IOC. This is the case that excludes, in principle, the marketplace that provides positioning and customer acquisition services by advertising means, but does not exercise relevant material interference over the effective governance of subsequent commercial operations. This limit, however, cannot be read as a clause removing marketplace platforms from the regime of the IOC. Once the applicability of Article 29 of the CDC443 to qualify legal entities in a factual position of vulnerability is admitted, the regime proves fully pertinent when the immaterial object in question is the enjoyment bundle in a sustained position exploited as a product in the B2B444 market by the holder and offered, through the marketplace platform, to the seller (vendedor).

The third limit falls on situations in which the symbolic or semiotic value is functionally accessory and is not part of the core of the promised enjoyment bundle. In these hypotheses, signification is not reconstructible from traits of supply and of infrastructural execution, degrading into an indeterminate expectation about social reaction. The function of this limit is to block the unmotivated use of the regime of the IOC to contest alterations incapable of depredating the core of the value offered. Therefore, as a rule, predominantly aesthetic alterations fall outside the reach of the concept, except when, in the concrete case, they make up a convergent set of signals able to sustain objective recognition of an unlawful act of architecture through structural indications of abusive teleology.

The fourth limit covers ordinary variations of the ecosystem that do not constitute relevant and persistent degradation of the bundle, such as updates, interface adjustments, anti-abuse measures, regular moderation and one-off instabilities. It is a protocol limit that, in general terms, introduces a reasonableness clause. At the same time, the design of the concept must allow recognizing when the ordinary exacerbates its own function and comes to collaborate with macrostructures responsible for practices of structural exploitation of the semiotic vulnerability of consumer users.

p. 435 The fifth limit is the case in which the core of the conflict is something else, such as a classic one-off quality defect, undue charging, localized misleading advertising or specific non-performance of an offer. In these hypotheses, common consumer dogmatics tends to resolve the dispute without real gain in mobilizing the IOC.

The negative edge is not an aesthetic caution, but a condition of operability of the concept. Larenz (1997, p. 311) recalls that open standards are not purely and simply devoid of content and are not to be confused with empty pseudo-normative formulas compatible with any concrete form of behavior.

The requirement of a limit is also dogmatic. Ferraz Jr. (2003, [p. 172]) observes that dogmatics grasps the legal order systematically to meet the requirements of the decidability of conflicts and that this set needs to present reasonably precise contours. From this perspective, system implies a notion of limit, that is, an abstract line that makes it possible to identify what is inside, what enters, what leaves and what remains outside Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 172]). The negative edge preserves the system from collapse by overreach and prevents the IOC from invading conflicts whose core is already resolvable by traditional keys of the CDC.

In these terms, the IOC ceases to be an elegant description of platforms and comes to operate as a technology of qualification. The category imposes a controllable sequence of questions about promise, position of enjoyment, infrastructural mode of execution and relevant degradation, with demonstrable and contestable parameters, keeping the conflict within the CDC without inaugurating a microsystem and without accepting strategic escape through labeling of supply. At the same time, the limits of exclusion prevent ordinary variations and regular curation from being treated as degradation by definition. The IOC comes to function as a language of proof and of screening, and not as a vocabulary of atmosphere.

With this design structured, it becomes possible to move on to relevant losses and criteria of attribution without contaminating the discussion with doubts about what was acquired and what the legal mode of existence of that utility is over time. Criteria and exclusions thus fulfill the institutional function of turning the IOC into an applicable operator, with a roadmap and limits, capable of guiding the reconstruction of supply and preparing the analysis of what counts as legally relevant degradation and what remains as an ordinary variation of the regime.
