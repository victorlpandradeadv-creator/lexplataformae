5.1.4 On personal integrity, symbolic identity and digital citizenship

p. 456

Beyond functional utility, the IOC projects a layer of personal integrity and symbolic identity, because the user's position in the ecosystem is produced by regimes of visibility, recognition and belonging. The task now is to define relevant losses that can be neither downgraded to “displeasure” nor treated as a side effect. In digital ecosystems, the value offered and enjoyed is not exhausted by instrumental utilities, because it depends on regimes of visibility, recognition, belonging and reputation that p. 457 form part of the very experience of enjoyment and condition the subject's practical position in the environment.

For this reason, the dogmatic reconstruction of the immaterial object of consumption must incorporate, as a dimension of relevant loss, the consumer's personal integrity and symbolic identity, understood as functional components of enjoyment in private spaces that operate, to a large extent, as environments of social participation and status projection. The problem is to make them legally tractable through objective delimitations and criteria of controllability, including in order to capture losses that affect the enjoyment bundle even when there is no total interruption, but rather a persistent downgrading of the conditions of use and recognition.

In Reale's thought, this type of construction is neither an arbitrary gesture nor a later moralization by the interpreter. The legal norm operates as an integration of facts and values, without being reduced to an empty logical form and without being confused with a mechanical reflection of the world. It is this integration that authorizes treating platform-governed enjoyment as a legally modelable experience, because “the normative structures, which constitute Objective Law, are not mere empty logical forms, but forms of a concrete experience, whose dominant or essential lines were abstracted from social reality to operate as an instrument of social discipline, that is, as ‘legal models’” Reale(Reale, 1994, p. 109- 110).

In scenarios of technical mutation, legal meaning may shift without the response depending on moral rhetoric, since “an unforeseen circumstance of a technical order can completely alter the signification and content of a legal text” Reale(Reale, 2001, p. 164). This adjustment remains controllable because valuation and decision remain bound to facts and values, imposing methodological limits against voluntarism. With this grounding, personal integrity and symbolic identity can be formulated as functional dimensions of supply in digital ecosystems, provided they are defined by criteria of relevance and verifiability that preserve decidability.

Personal integrity, in the regime of the Immaterial Object of Consumption, designates neither an emotional state nor subjective discomfort arising from ordinary market frustrations. It designates the preservation of minimum conditions of agency, continuity and practical orientation of the consumer within a technical-organizational environment that permanently manages the possibilities of enjoyment. When the acquired utility is realized as a position of enjoyment sustained by a platform, the subject is not merely the recipient of content, but a participant in an ecosystem that organizes access, priorities, visibilities and sanctions.

p. 458 Integrity is affected when these conditions are downgraded persistently or structurally, to the point of converting the experience of use into chronic instability, functional disorientation or loss of control over one's own way of existing in the environment, even if formal access remains available. Legal relevance, therefore, lies not in measuring internal disturbances, but in identifying when the continuous management of the environment significantly alters the level of predictability, consistency and governability of enjoyment, eroding the core of utility stabilized in the bond.

This cut only holds if protection is not captured by a resigned empirical description of the functioning of the ecosystem. The counterfactual stabilization of normative expectations, already established as a methodological lock of the IOC regime, is decisive at this point because it makes it possible to affirm normative expectations about continuity, loyalty in performance and preservation of minimum conditions of enjoyment even under opacity and mutability. With this, the persistent downgrading of these conditions can be reconstructed as relevant loss without requiring proof of sociological “normality” nor depending on moral reproach of the technical arrangement.

Dogmatics, however, can only deal with this type of loss if it is able to delimit the phenomenon and make it decidable, avoiding descriptive paralysis in the face of the environment's complexity. Under the technological function already established, dogmatic delimitation operates through a cut in reality that isolates the problems relevant to decision-making Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 92]) and organizes uncertainties into a specialized technique compatible with normative binding and with institutional pressure for decision Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 86-87]).

Personal integrity thus ceases to be a subjective appeal and becomes matter that can be delimited by criteria, making it possible to locate typical losses in the gap between formally preserved enjoyment and materially degraded enjoyment. Protection no longer depends on the crude figure of total interruption and comes to reach situations in which access remains nominal, but the experience of use is downgraded by persistent alterations in the conditions of participation, predictability and governability of the environment.

Relevant degradation may take the form of cumulative restrictions, gradual reconfigurations of functionalities, persistent changes in access rules, selective frictions and reiterated instability that makes enjoyment unpredictable or structurally disadvantageous. Even when there is no immediate collapse of access, the aggregate effect may erode the capacity to orient one's own action, plan permanence and exercise use according to stabilizable normative expectations, delimiting the point at which modulation ceases to be p. 459 an ordinary variation of supply and comes to constitute a significant downgrading of the enjoyment bundle.

The argument must, then, make explicit that part of the value enjoyed in digital ecosystems is not merely instrumental, but symbolic and socially structured. To that end, it is necessary to set aside the reading that symbolic value is a mere private emotional projection, immune to control and, for that reason, legally irrelevant. Under the semiotic apparatus already established, the sign is not a subjective ornament that accompanies experience, since it operates as a public structure that organizes conduct and produces verifiable social effects, regardless of its physical truth or of individual mental states. In this light, interpretation can be understood as an objective datum, since interpretants, that is, the relations of interpretation, do not necessarily depend on individual mental representations and are collectively verifiable Eco(Eco, 1991, p. 112).

Under the semiotic lock already established, symbolic identity ceases to be an introspective appeal and comes to designate a socially instituted position in systems of signs that structure expectations, recognition and belonging in the ecosystem. Meaning is not reduced to a mental image, but operates as a relation instituted by the code, and even connotation becomes predictable through a shared lexicon, which makes it possible to describe symbolic losses by public criteria.

Symbolic identity, in the IOC regime, designates the consumer's public position in an ecosystem of signs managed by a platform, in which visibility, reputation, belonging and distinction function as practical conditions of enjoyment and as components of the economically experienced value. This identity is formed and stabilized by socially operative criteria of recognition, frequently coded in metrics, badges, rarities, rankings, participation history, relationship networks and regimes of access to spaces and functionalities. When the utility of the object depends on these mediations, the enjoyment bundle structurally includes the continuity of this position and the possibility of exercising it over time. The platform may formally preserve access to the content and, even so, reconfigure the criteria that define status, reach, belonging and recognition, degrading the socially instituted core of utility that sustained enjoyment as a practice.

An especially didactic example of this symbolic identity as a public position appears in the end-of-season ranked rewards and, in particular, in the “Victorious” skins and in the borders associated with the tier460. These signs do not operate as mere p. 460 cosmetics, because they become social proof immediately legible in the ecosystem, stabilizing a form of reputation that is simultaneously historical and displayable, since the player comes to carry, on the very symbolic body of the account, a mark of performance and temporal dedication recognized by third parties Riot Games(Riot Games, 2026b). The consequence is that enjoyment ceases to be merely access to the game and comes to include the permanence of this visible biography as a component of the economically experienced value, because it organizes expectation, distinction and belonging in internal circuits of recognition.

The intelligibility of this public position can also be reinforced by the sociology of sport, insofar as institutionalized competitive practices produce not only performance, but stable forms of community, integration and reciprocal recognition. Helal461 observes that sport is one of the most solid social institutions of the modern world and that its presence deeply penetrates everyday life, influencing habits, customs and modes of social insertion Helal(Helal, 1990, p. 11-12; p. 14). Along the same lines, treating sport as a social fact, the author stresses that it imposes itself on individuals from an early age, so much so that those who do not take to the sport of their social group often experience the condition of the non-integrated, while sporting taste itself is formed in the collectivity, and not as a purely private or spontaneous preference Helal(Helal, 1990, p. 12-14).

This perspective is particularly useful for understanding continuous competitive ecosystems such as that of League of Legends462. When competition is stabilized by seasons, p. 461 rankings, rewards, public circuits, shared memory and recognizable signs of prestige, enjoyment no longer consists of a mere succession of matches and comes to operate as insertion into a socially structured space, in which belonging, reputation and biographical continuity are produced collectively. Within this frame, the value of the IOC is not exhausted by the instrumental utility of access, because it includes permanence in a competitive community organized by shared codes, mutual recognition and public criteria of distinction. For this reason, exclusions, persistent restrictions or relevant downgrades of the conditions of participation affect not only the user's functional conveniences, but may bear directly on their symbolic identity and on the continuity of their socially recognized position in the ecosystem.

This same arrangement makes clear why symbolic identity, when institutionalized by a platform, can produce identity captivity. Obtaining the sign of prestige depends not only on competitive performance, but also on maintaining a state of eligibility managed by disciplinary technologies of conduct, in which low Honor levels bar the player from seasonal rewards Riot Games(Riot Games, 2025a)463. In this dynamic, the subject comes to manage their own behavior with a view to preserving recognition and narrative continuity, because the relevant threat is not p. 462 only the loss of a match or of a functional convenience, but the frustration of a rare reward that consolidates status and that, once missed, cannot simply be repurchased as an equivalent substitute. Conformity ceases to be mere “following the rules” and becomes a mechanism of self-preservation of a socially operative position in the ecosystem, in which the cost of leaving is not limited to time or money, but includes the loss of displayable historicity.

The legal point that this example illuminates is that accumulated symbolic capital is maintained under a regime of disciplined permanence, since the platform not only describes standards of conduct, but reserves to itself a power of intervention capable of downgrading enjoyment through functional limitations, restrictions on interaction, redefinitions of progress and, in extreme cases, the very termination of participation, with measures that may be triggered even when the specific conduct is not previously listed as inappropriate Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e)464. In this way, the risk of sanction bears not only on formal access, but on the integrity of agency and on the maintenance of symbolic identity as a public position, since the system of rewards and eligibility turns prestige into something both desirable and vulnerable to private governance. In practical terms, what the environment recognizes as merit becomes, at the same time, an instrument of capture through disciplinary asymmetry, since preserving the possibility of receiving and sustaining signs of prestige comes to require continuous self-adjustment to administratively controlled criteria and determinations Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024k)465.

Within this frame, relevant downgrading may consist of loss of position, persistent disqualification, erasure of signs of distinction, systematic instability of p. 463 recognition or restrictions that make participation in internal circuits of value unfeasible. Symbolic identity thus functions as a lens that makes visible losses which do not take the form of total interruption, but directly affect the position of enjoyment and its continuity as utility.

Personal integrity and symbolic identity become fully intelligible as dimensions of the IOC when it is recognized that, in platform-governed environments, agency and identity are not located outside supply as mere psychological phenomena, but can be configured by architectural design and by continuous modulation. Cohen synthesizes this point by stating that technologies and artifacts mediate the embodied perception of reality and that, over time, they come to constitute and define the lived world, so that networked information technologies not only empower the networked self, but configure it Cohen(Cohen, 2012, [p. 36]).

This configuration is carried out through layers of protocols and structural choices that simultaneously enable certain flexibilities and block others, while producing legibility and intermediation compatible with the platform's own priorities Cohen(Cohen, 2019, p. 42). In the same movement, flows of personal data are refined to produce operational representations of the user, structured to make behaviors calculable, predictable and profitable at scale Cohen(Cohen, 2019, p. 67). The legal consequence is that agency comes to be treated as a governable variable of supply and that motivational patterns can be reinforced in pre-deliberative layers through techniques of choice architecture and algorithmic anticipation Cohen(Cohen, 2019, p. 77). Thus, the enjoyment bundle may include architecturally produced conditions of visibility, recognition and participation, and the degradation of these conditions may constitute relevant loss without depending on the consumer's introspection.

The identification of relevant losses in the IOC can, then, be structured as a matrix that captures the harm without depending on total interruption or subjective assessment. The common axis is the impairment of the position of enjoyment over time, as a combination of practical conditions of use and agency in the environment with conditions of recognition, visibility and belonging that form part of the socially instituted utility.

Relevant losses, in this regime, include exclusion or significant limitation of participation when the platform prevents the normal exercise of the enjoyment bundle through persistent blocks, restrictions or downgrades. They include substantial degradation of the conditions of use when gradual reconfigurations, selective frictions, reiterated instabilities or rule changes erode the predictability and governability of the experience. They include the p. 464 devaluation or erasure of signs of distinction and reputation when metrics, badges, rarities, histories or visibility criteria are altered so as to disfigure the stabilized symbolic utility. They include, finally, the structural reconfiguration of access and recognition when the consumer remains within the ecosystem, but durably loses the capacity to occupy the position of enjoyment that made the object economically and socially valuable.

Organized in these terms, the problem ceases to be described as diffuse discomfort and comes to separate ordinary variations of service from significant downgrades of the enjoyment bundle, distinguishing alterations compatible with continued performance from those that transform, to the consumer's detriment, the minimum conditions of agency, participation and recognition that sustained the acquired utility.

The incorporation of symbolic identity as a component of the enjoyment bundle is also sustained because legal rationality assimilates social structures of recognition, roles and history as conditions of coordination and of limitation of meaning. In the sense indicated by Tércio Sampaio Ferraz Jr., elements such as reputation and stereotypes function as material topoi that can be mobilized in legal discussion, allowing the parties to present themselves as upright people, in good faith, aware of duties and rights and bearers of recognized public merit, in opposition to a minority or to interests described as deviant Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 1997, p. 84). The same mechanism appears in the idea of role. For the author, assuming a role means becoming entangled in actions that the agent comes to have to represent as part of himself, and this assumption functions as a presupposition of continuous interaction, producing a paradoxical cooperation, since the parties recognize each other as such even in a condition of conflict Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 1997, p. 75). The communicative situation also delimits meaning through its own history, since generating one's own history means differentiating one's own behavior in reference to the more complex history of the surrounding world. In this dynamic, every behavior that enters the communicative situation contributes to limiting it, because it excludes and enables alternatives and ensures other discursive behaviors Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 1997, p. 59). On platforms, internal positions of user, participant, creator, moderated or sanctioned are equally organized by roles and recognitions that condition enjoyment as a social practice, so that persistent downgrades of status and participation may affect the core of utility even without total interruption of access.

The reference to digital citizenship operates neither as a constitutionalizing shift nor as an inflated metaphor, but as a functional description of the way certain private ecosystems have come to concentrate relevant conditions of social participation, recognition and p. 465 circulation of reputation. Consumer relationships structure access to spaces of interaction, mechanisms of visibility and internal circuits of value that, in practice, function as everyday infrastructure of belonging, public exposure and the exercise of social roles. The argument is put in strict terms. The platform operates as a private environment of public relevance insofar as it structures practical opportunities for expression, participation and recognition, and continuous consumption places the consumer in a position of functional dependence on these conditions. Within this frame, private sanctions, reconfigurations of visibility and persistent downgrades cease to be indifferent operational variations when they substantially alter the consumer's capacity to occupy socially relevant positions within the environment, with effects that extend beyond the instrumental use of the service.

For this impairment to be legally assimilable, the content of what is understood here as personal integrity in the IOC regime must be made explicit. The term is employed as an umbrella concept compatible with the theory of quality in the CDC466, which already operates through categories broad enough to encompass harms arising from the fact of consumption without requiring the crude figure of total interruption or of immediate material injury. Personal integrity includes, therefore, classic dimensions of physical, psychological, patrimonial and even aesthetic integrity, but now also comes to incorporate an operational facet proper to continuous p. 466 consumption in an environment. It is integrity as to the capacity for agency and predictability, understood as the minimum set of conditions that allows the consumer to orient their own action, understand practical consequences, plan permanence and exercise use according to stabilizable normative expectations in an ecosystem governed by architecture and by private sanctions.

This integrity of agency does not correspond to a private emotional state or to a psychological parameter of protection. It designates a functional condition of existence in the system, observable in patterns of governability of the environment, stability of rules and legibility of consequences. In ecosystems of governed enjoyment, the consumer may remain formally “inside” and, even so, lose control over how they exist in the system, when a regime of chronic instability sets in, produced by repeated alterations of rules, selective frictions, gradual reconfigurations of functionalities, opacification of visibility criteria and unpredictability of sanctions. What deteriorates is the capacity to maintain enjoyment as a practice orientable over time. Relevant loss appears when modulation ceases to be an ordinary variation of supply and comes to persistently erode the possibility of acting with minimum predictability and of sustaining a position of use endowed with practical meaning.

In this same register, symbolic identity and personal integrity are connected by two effects of dependence that can be named symbolic captivity and identity captivity. The first designates the platform's capacity to replace public repertoires of signification with proprietary signs and internal grammars that come to organize value, belonging and recognition, reducing external comparability and reordering the user's interpretable world under managed metrics, rarities, badges, rankings and regimes of visibility. The second designates the form of dependence in which the cost of exit is not exhausted in financial or convenience dimensions, because it implies loss of narrative continuity and of positions of recognition whose existence takes place, decisively, within the ecosystem, through networks, histories, roles and internal circuits of value. In both cases, it is not a matter of hypnosis of the consumer nor of psychologization of the bond, but of a functional description of how architecture, curation and sanction can produce dependence through exit costs and through asymmetry of governance, directly affecting agency and the position of enjoyment.

Fixing a protective core that includes personal integrity, in its facet of agency and predictability, and symbolic identity, as a socially instituted public position, does not stand as a license for axiological voluntarism, but as a p. 467 controllable cut anchored in objective requirements of valuation and in structural limits of legal experience itself.

In Reale's thought, the evaluative dimension does not operate as a moral supplement external to Law. Decision-making authority does not detach itself from the axiological horizon that makes normativity intelligible, since the binding force of Law is linked to axiological requirements and to a set of options processed in the social milieu, from which decision-making authority is not separated. Along this line, every norm enunciates something that ought to be because a value was recognized as the determining reason for a behavior declared obligatory Reale(Reale, 2001, p. 33). When legal experience identifies fundamental values as conditions of possibility of the system itself, valuation takes the form of a limit and not of rhetoric. Reale maintains that, in the dialogical process of history, man becomes aware of certain fundamental values, such as the inviolability of the human person, which, once brought to the light of historical consciousness, come to be considered intangible. For the author, these values act as transcendental axiological constants of Law, since it was they that made legal experience possible Reale(Reale, 1994, p. 108).

Accordingly, the protection of integrity and identity, when operated as functional dimensions of enjoyment in private environments of public relevance, can be formulated as a protective core without abstract moralism, provided it remains bound to criteria of relevance and to standards of verifiability capable of converting this core into decidable losses in the IOC regime.

This incorporation requires, however, explicit delimitation to prevent personal integrity, symbolic identity, symbolic captivity, identity captivity and digital citizenship from turning into expansive labels capable of absorbing any discomfort arising from ordinary market frictions. It is not a matter of elevating the consumer's experience to a psychological parameter or of transforming trivial frustrations, aesthetic preferences or normal fluctuations of service into matters of reinforced protection. Nor is it a matter of attributing unlawfulness to technology as such or of replacing dogmatic criteria with moral reproach of the business model. The cut is functional. In platform-governed enjoyment, part of the economically acquired value depends on architecturally produced conditions of participation, visibility, recognition and governability of the environment, and the relevant degradation of these conditions may objectively affect the enjoyment bundle without taking the form of total interruption. For this reason, the categories only operate when the downgrading is significant and persistent, when it substantially alters the level of agency and the position of the p. 468 consumer in the ecosystem, and when it can be described by verifiable criteria of loss in the IOC regime, under the filters of relevance and controllability.

The passage to standards of proof and restoration finally requires making explicit why losses so delimited must be converted into decidable alternatives even when platform governance operates under opacity and mutability. Under the methodological locks already consolidated, legal technique does not suspend its action in the face of opacity, since it organizes criteria to make it bearable under normative binding and institutional pressure for decision, increasing uncertainties in a manner oriented toward action Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 87]).

In this key, the absorption of insecurity justifies transforming initially diffuse losses into controllable cuts, since the act of deciding converts undecidable incompatibilities into decidable alternatives, even if, at a subsequent moment, it generates new incompatibilities, sometimes more complex than the previous ones, without this transformation being confused with a promise of harmony Jr.(Ferraz Jr., 2003, [p. 312-313]). The matrix of losses relating to personal integrity and symbolic identity, thus outlined, does not purport to exhaust the phenomenon, but to make it decidable by criteria, opening the way for the formulation of standards of verifiability and restoration compatible with continued enjoyment.

Thus, the protective core outlined for personal integrity and symbolic identity ceases to be a rhetorical ornament and comes to function as an instrument of attribution and restoration compatible with the IOC's mode of existence. Platform-governed enjoyment cannot be read only through the lens of formal access nor through the crude category of total interruption, because what is acquired and lost is frequently a position of enjoyment that is realized over time and under architectural governance. When this position is degraded by persistent reconfigurations of conditions of use, participation, visibility and recognition, relevant loss may occur without collapse of access and, even so, affect the core of utility economically experienced and socially verifiable in the ecosystem. The result is a reading rule that ties together the three planes already opened: the object remains intelligible as an immaterial product, the relevant conduct can be reconstructed in architectural patterns oriented by exploitative purpose, and relevant losses can be described by criteria that capture substantial degradations of the enjoyment bundle, including in its symbolic dimension. This conceptual architecture provides controllable cuts that make decidable what, under abstract labels or narrow categories of defect and interruption, would tend to remain invisible, and prepares the shift of the diagnosis to the standards of proof, control and redress capable of dealing with gradual losses, persistent downgrades and architectural reconfigurations without p. 469 requiring total transparency or reducing protection to the binary logic of “works or does not work” or of an object “intact or defective”.

The use of “personal integrity” as an umbrella concept to encompass the binomial “health and safety” native to consumer protection does not propose the creation of a new legal interest, but an operation that engages directly with the dogmatics already stabilized in the CDC. In Claudia Lima Marques's reconstruction, consumer protection in the market is organized, at least, by the protection of their physical and psychological integrity and their economic integrity, in addition to the concern with the moment at which this protection applies in contractual dynamics Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 13). This frame engages directly with the theory of quality in the CDC, understood as a legal duty of quality-adequacy and quality-safety, by which quality defects due to inadequacy and to lack of safety are qualified according to reasonable expectations of supply, as a functional substitute for the classic categories of the redhibitory defect in the previous regime Marques(Marques, 2016, p. 1349). In this framing, the inclusion of an operational facet linked to agency and predictability in environments of platform-governed enjoyment can be presented as an extension of the adequacy of the supply regime itself, and not as a normative innovation. The recent literature that describes so-called digital vulnerability as the power of commercial actors to affect decisions, desires and behaviors in a way that the consumer tends not to tolerate, but is not in a position to prevent, helps to bring out the reverse of this integrity of agency, as a practical loss of the capacity for orientation and containment of the environment Mucelin(Marques; Mucelin, 2022, p. 2). This loss is associated with a structural, architectural aspect, in which the environment is designed to change behaviors, anticipating biases and operating through digital choice architectures, which reinforces the centrality of governance by design for the legal and consumer-law qualification of the problem Mucelin(Marques; Mucelin, 2022, p. 16). Likewise, in treating successive-performance digital contracts as relationships driven by the search for security, future performance, status or a certain quality, and in describing the position of dependence as captivity in the context of current relationships, the author offers a dogmatic point of support to articulate permanence, exit cost and expectation of quality as elements inherent to the integrity of continuous consumption Mucelin(Marques; Mucelin, 2022, p. 23-24). The objective, therefore, is not to replace the grammar of the CDC with an environmental label, but to bring these mechanisms back to criteria of quality and protection of integrity, preserving the instrumental character of the vocabulary and preparing the critique of the expression digital vulnerability as a polysemic category that tends to describe the scenario more than the operator of power. ↩
