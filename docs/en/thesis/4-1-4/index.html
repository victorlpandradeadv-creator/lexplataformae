4.1.4 Curated promise, rarity and value capture

See this section in the PDF

Concepts in this section

  • Hypercuratorial anticipation

  • Foundations · Sections of chapter 4

p. 313

On platforms, the IOC exists as an offer whose economic form depends on shared reading, public ranking and recognition within a circuit, capable of keeping value in circulation even when enjoyment shifts. From here on, the decisive axis becomes time as a governed dimension, because, in games-as-a-service regimes and continuous digital services, the promise of the IOC no longer behaves as a one-off delivery event and presents itself as administered continuity, in which value is sustained only by rhythms and milestones that the platform itself organizes.

Hypercuratorial anticipation designates an economic-semiological technique by which the platform produces a consumable future through continuous curation, defining what will come, what will disappear, what will return and what will be raised to the status of an “event”. This production governs value itself by deciding rhythms, relevances and expectations within the ecosystem, converting calendar and presentation into instruments of legible distinction and relative measurement. It is the governance “of what is to come” as an operative part of the offer, with the p. 314 administered future serving as an economic resource and as a device for steering conduct in the present.

The economic functioning of this regime can be described as the deliberate maintenance of continuous readiness. The consumer is kept in continuous readiness for “the next cycle”, “the next update”, “the next opportunity”, that is, for the next piece of meaning that reorders the positional value of the ecosystem. The present ceases to be the place of a stabilized delivery and becomes a governed antechamber, in which the promise is renewed through announcements, teasers356, roadmaps357 and “seasons” that function as advance delivery in the sphere of recognition. Thus, the platform does not sell only the “now”, since it also sells permanence oriented toward the “to come”, converting expectation into an asset and turning waiting into a material component of enjoyment.

This technique can be seen clearly when the company itself publicly structures the “to come” as a communicable object, in layers and in the language of promise, forming an administered horizon of announcements, corrections and returns. Rather than appearing as the consumer's mere expectation, the future is produced as an institutional calendar and as a repertoire of signals, with release windows, schedule forecasts, teasers and updates that become, in practice, part of what sustains permanence and decision. In this register, development updates function as explicit management of the “to come”, by presenting what is being prepared for the year and by condensing announcements into a sequence, with time frames, forecasts and references to longer pieces of context Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024q)358. The promise is thus not limited to “having something new”, because it is organized as direction, agenda and milestones of attention, so that the consumer's presence comes to be oriented by a series of imminences.

p. 315 This serialization becomes even more tangible when the competitive design is reconfigured as a program of stages. By announcing and justifying three splits in each ranked season, the company converts the year into smaller units of participation, with rewards and tracks tied to the completion of these units Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024h)359. As a result, enjoyment no longer refers only to access to the game and comes to depend on showing up for windows, on completing cycles and on remaining in cadences that the ecosystem makes operative so that positional value does not degrade. The institutional insistence on continuity throughout the season and on consistency in each split frames time as organizable raw material, not as a neutral background.

The government of time gains precision when temporalization is converted into executable dates and times, as in the patch notes that announce the end of one split and the beginning of another with time stamps and a reference to the local time zone, turning “missing a window” into an administratively delimited event Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024o)360. What theoretical discourse describes as continuous readiness here appears as practical infrastructure. The cycle expires on a set day and time and restarts at another, so that the consumer's permanence in the circuit is calibrated by an official regime of deadlines that disciplines return, intensifies urgency and reorganizes the meaning of “being up to date”.

This design, in turn, does not lack institutional backing, for the contract frames instability as governable normality and recognizes as legitimate the reprogramming of content, access and rhythm by unilateral decision, including through updates and modifications that may occur with or without notice Riot Games(Riot Games, 2024e). The “consumable future” becomes economically relevant because the promise need not be fulfilled in full as stable delivery, it is enough that it remain credible and mobilizing, while the operability of the ecosystem is preserved by signals and calendars that the platform itself administers. Anticipation, in this sense, ceases to be mere persuasion and comes to function as a technique of value capture, since it turns waiting into economically oriented conduct and makes permanence a practical response to the risk of expiration.

p. 316 The immediate economic effect is the replacement of a logic “of delivery” by a logic “of directed trajectory”. The promise is always reframed as a succession of stages, events and returns, so that enjoyment becomes structurally incomplete without the next cycle. The future need not be realized in full; it is enough that it remain credible and mobilizing. Delivery can thus be continually postponed without appearing as failure, because the system keeps the user in motion, rewarding adherence with sufficient signals of continuity and with calibrated temporal scarcity. Hypercuratorial anticipation, in this sense, is not a marketing ornament, but a technique for governing time that monetizes expectation as a regular means of maintaining value.

This anticipation is economically relevant for IOCs because it sustains the conversion of expectation into present value, altering the very logic of the consumer's decision and permanence. When acquiring an immaterial good in this regime, one adheres not merely to an isolated item, but to a place within a governed flow, in which what “has value” is also the capacity to continue to count in the ecosystem as the calendar unfolds. As a result, price, urgency and convenience come to incorporate the to come as a component of evaluation, since the consumer's situation comes to be measured by access to cycles, by presence at temporal milestones and by permanence on a public scale that is continually updated.

When time is governed in this way, it ceases to be a background and becomes an instrument of command, because the platform comes to produce not only content, but administered instability. Predictability, which in traditional consumption functions as a minimum condition for planning and comparison, is replaced by a regime in which rhythms, returns, disappearances and reclassifications are continually reprogrammable, and this reprogramming operates as a technique for steering conduct in the present. The practical effect is not only the intensification of desire or urgency, but the compression of the space of agency of the consumer, who loses the capacity to orient conduct, anticipate consequences and stabilize legitimate expectations about the permanence of what was promised. In this key, the “to come” becomes a functional component of the immaterial good and a sensitive point of verifiability, because shifts in the calendar and in presentation may produce relevant losses without a single, easily identifiable event of non-performance.

An operational analogy helps make the concept more concrete. In an urban transit subscription plan, the value lies not only in the card acquired, but in the stability of the system that makes it usable, lines, schedules, transfers and access rules. If the operator, without formally breaching the contract, begins to change schedules frequently, eliminate transfers, reclassify routes and introduce variable validation obstacles, the p. 317 consumer may continue “having access to the service”, but loses the capacity to plan trips and to foresee practical consequences, which affects the adequacy of the service as a continuous regime. In platform consumption, hypercuratorial anticipation works in a similar way, only with calendars, progression milestones, promises of return and public forms of visibility that are reprogrammed as part of governance. The point is that the strategic mutability of the to come reconfigures, in the present, the space of agency and the minimum stability of legitimate expectations that make up the value of the IOC.

This curated future materially integrates the offer and materializes through a recurring set of devices that organize consumption as flow. The “to come” ceases to be a simple subjective expectation and becomes part of what is put into circulation, because the platform sells the promise of access to sequences, milestones and returns as if they were properties of the good itself, capable of generating socially effective recognition already in the present. In this regime, the IOC is not only what one obtains now, but also what one is authorized to expect and to claim as probable continuity within the ecosystem, which shifts the offer to a model in which the calendar and the presentation of the to come function as operative components of value.

These devices are recurring and recognizable. Seasons and roadmaps structure expectation as a horizon in chapters, converting continuity into meaning and relevance. Passes and progression tracks condition presence and return by tying value to goals and temporal milestones. Events and collections fabricate belonging and public memory by transforming acquisition into situated participation. Availability windows produce temporal scarcity by making access a problem of timing, and not only of price. Returns and relaunches recalibrate prestige by reorganizing what counts as rare, current or “back”. Editions and variations establish an internal scale by grading differences and transforming nuances into legible hierarchy.

With these devices, the promise is no longer structured as closed delivery and comes to operate as a serialized promise, in which the sequence becomes the relevant economic unit. What is bought is not only a result, but the maintenance of a circuit that unfolds through stages, updates and planned returns, so that “delivering” comes to mean sustaining the operability of value over time. The offer is thus reorganized as administered continuity, because the immaterial good remains economically active insofar as it is reinscribed in cadences and milestones that reactivate expectation, renew comparison and preserve the consumer's position within the flow.

p. 318 When the promise is serialized and the sequence constitutes the economic unit, adequacy comes to depend on the good remaining operative in the circuit that gives it value, that is, continuing to be recognizable, measurable and socially confirmable in the ecosystem throughout the governed cadences. In this regime, the relevant delivery is not measured only by residual access, but by the practical preservation of the position of enjoyment within a flow in which value and recognition are updated over time.

At this point, expectation ceases to be a psychological by-product of consumption and comes to function as produced infrastructure, because the platform fabricates, administers and distributes the state of “almost delivery” as the regular mode of existence of the promise. Anticipation does not appear as an empty interval between purchases, but as an operative track that sustains interest, an internal scale of value and social insertion while the object has not yet been realized, or even when it is never realized in a fully stable way. The ecosystem organizes waiting as a governed experience, in which signals of the future, milestones and announcements function as material elements of the circuit, keeping value active through the continually reactivated promise.

Structured as a track, it is converted into routine through cycles of participation, goals and milestones that demand recurring presence for the position to remain operative. Tasks, progressions, checkpoints and temporal rewards transform enjoyment into conduct, because value comes to depend on completing stages and keeping up with the rhythm imposed by the ecosystem, and not only on “having” an item. Conduct, in turn, is converted into permanence, since returning ceases to be a neutral choice and becomes a practical condition for not losing sequence, not losing access to milestones and not losing the continuity of the public recognition that sustains the acquired position.

The urgency produced by this regime is not diffuse emotion, because it operates as the administration of the risk of losing one's place in a legible hierarchy. Missing a window, failing a milestone or ignoring a cycle is not equivalent to “missing a chance”, but to dropping in position in the public regime of comparison that the ecosystem updates in real time. Time is instrumentalized to convert absence into visible disadvantage, so that “being left out” means positional demotion and a socially observable loss of relevance. Decision and permanence are thus driven by structured risk, not by spontaneous affect.

This temporal regime only works because it is coupled to a regime of visibility that converts time into public distinction. The calendar does not organize only access and opportunity, for it transforms dated participation into a displayable mark of being current, belonging and prestige, making being up to date a comparable differential. Time, scene and position operate as one and the same mechanism, insofar as the curation of the to come defines what p. 319 matters, public exposure turns this into a criterion of hierarchy and the consumer's position remains operative only while it receives continuous social validation. Anticipation, here, is not an internal rhythm of the service, but a mechanism of exposed stratification.

In this key, governed time takes the form of a continuous present produced by flow, in which novelties and repositionings replace one another without allowing interpretive rest. What matters is always the now that expires, and the continuity of the experience rests less on the stability of the object than on the obligation to keep up with what has been made relevant. Attention is maintained by recurrence, and permanence is normalized as a practical response to an environment that ceaselessly redefines what must be seen, compared and pursued.

Likewise, signals of the future function as symbolic equivalences sufficient to sustain adherence and conduct, even if enjoyment is unstable, degradable or reconfigurable. Promises of return, of appreciation, of an imminent event or of a next phase operate as anticipated delivery on the plane of social validity, for they keep the circuit active through expectation and commensurability even when the concrete referent has not yet been realized or has already been altered. The ecosystem comes to operate through promises that are worth something in themselves in everyday practice, insofar as the announced to come becomes part of what is bought, of what is justified and of what is accepted as continuity of value.

When the offer takes the form of a temporal program, the link between promise and delivery can no longer be verified by simple inspection, because it comes to depend on continuous decisions that do not present themselves as fixed “content” of the item. Curation of rhythm, selection of what appears and what disappears, relevance criteria, regimes of visibility, comparison parameters and repositioning rules operate as structural layers of delivery and can be recalibrated without an identifiable event of rupture. Opacity, therefore, is neither communication noise nor a lack of attention on the consumer's part: it is the effect of a reconfigurable architecture in which what is at stake is the maintenance of the conditions that keep the promise holding over time.

In this scenario, trust migrates from the item to the ecosystem that makes it operative, because what matters is not only access, but the continuity of the public validity of the acquired position. The expectation of stability comes to fall on the platform as the private guarantor of the circuit: the instance that will preserve cadences, relevances and the parameters of measurement and endorsement by which the acquisition made sense. Delivery, in practice, is experienced as the institutional stability of the environment, and not as the objective permanence of an isolated object, which makes the consumer dependent on the continuous governance of the very system that defines what has value, what appears and what continues to count as “the same”.

p. 320 The teleology of extraction is reinforced because hypercuratorial anticipation monetizes recurrence: it converts time and attention into the raw material of value and turns permanence into an economic input. By serializing the promise and treating the to come as an operative component of the offer, the platform shifts the center of monetization to repetition, return and the maintenance of presence in cadences that it administers itself. Consumption ceases to be an event and becomes a regime, in which the continuous governance of the ecosystem is the condition for extraction to be effective.

The sensitive point is that this same ecosystem may preserve labels, signals and public commensurability while shifting, from within, the real conditions of the position of enjoyment. Anticipation facilitates this exchange because the future promise functions as a buffer: the announced to come takes the place of the present loss and sustains the sense of continuity even when restrictions, practical effects, modes of access or performance conditions change in a relevant way. Thus, restoration may operate as a socially legible nominal equivalence, preserving prestige, belonging and comparability, while the experience that justified that position is degraded or reconfigured without a clear marker of rupture.

If the value of the IOC depends on controlled semiosis, on administered visibility, on operative symbolic equivalences and on time governed as a program, then the central problem ceases to be only the content of the item and comes to be the demonstrability of the link between promise and enjoyment in a reconfigurable environment. The consequence is that the relevant exposure is not only informational, for it falls on the possibility of proving what sustained the promise at the moment of acquisition and what was altered in the experience over time. This is what requires naming and delimiting semiotic vulnerability as a structural loss of verifiability when experience and value are produced by regimes of signs, of scene and of calendar unilaterally controlled by the platform.
